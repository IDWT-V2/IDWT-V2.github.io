Projected Electron Orbits in the 6D Sector

Follow a literal deterministic electron trajectory around a nucleus. The displayed candidate uses four spatial directions within the electron’s six-dimensional setting. A connection to the nucleus’s depth-three and depth-four quarks motivates that choice; the attractive interaction must still derive the directional constraint. The physical idea

The internal common-frequency portions of a particle are distinct from this external electron trajectory. The current particle-geometry and sector-depths account explains the count clock, spin, color and charge constructions and their remaining physical questions.

The attractive interaction between an electron and a nucleus may constrain the external orbit to four spatial directions. The nucleus’s depth-three and depth-four quark patterns motivate this hypothesis, but their depth numbers do not establish a common alignment or the direction of the force. The displayed law explicitly assumes four-direction restoring and gyroscopic terms. Its exact trajectory turns at one rate while passing between two orthogonal planes at a second rate. The electron’s distance from the nucleus stays constant. A three-coordinate projection has an apparent radius that contracts and expands as the angular position advances, giving a ring or petals.

The electron itself remains a six-dimensional pattern. Its other two position coordinates are zero in this preparation; that does not show that those directions are dynamically inert or unreachable by the nuclear interaction. A projected force alone would leave free motion in its complement unless initial data or another mechanism confines it. Both nuclear and electron–electron responses need the coupled field dynamics. The relation between this orbit and three-dimensional bulk matter is examined in a separate note with scoped energy and orientation checks.

The default Flat Action display evaluates this motion exactly. The external position trajectory is distinct from the electron’s internal classical field oscillations. Its gyroscopic and restoring coefficients are prescribed inputs, and the closed examples use integer frequency ratios chosen by hand. The equations below make the picture reproducible and state the open test: whether the electron–nucleus interaction produces the selected motion, its rate and the measured response.
