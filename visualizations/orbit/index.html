Projected Electron Orbits in the 6D Sector

Explore a four-direction orbit construction within the six-dimensional electron setting. The default three-plane readout shows lobes with smaller nubs between them; its phase offsets are adjustable. Choose Spatial (Signed) to view coordinates of the baseline flat-action trajectory instead. The nuclear interaction still needs to derive the proposed directional constraint.

The physical idea

The internal common-frequency portions of a particle are distinct from this external electron trajectory. The current particle-geometry and sector-depths account explains the count clock, spin, color and charge constructions and their remaining physical questions.

The attractive interaction between an electron and a nucleus may constrain the external orbit to four spatial directions. The nucleus’s depth-three and depth-four quark patterns motivate this hypothesis, but their depth numbers do not establish a common alignment or the direction of the force. The displayed law explicitly assumes four-direction restoring and gyroscopic terms. Its exact trajectory turns at one rate while passing between two orthogonal planes at a second rate. The electron’s distance from the nucleus stays constant. A three-coordinate projection has an apparent radius that contracts and expands as the angular position advances, giving a ring or petals.

The electron itself remains a six-dimensional pattern. Its other two position coordinates are zero in this preparation; that does not show that those directions are dynamically inert or unreachable by the nuclear interaction. A projected force alone would leave free motion in its complement unless initial data or another mechanism confines it. Both nuclear and electron–electron responses need the coupled field dynamics. The relation between this orbit and three-dimensional bulk matter is examined in a separate note with scoped energy and orientation checks.

The Flat Action law supplies the baseline trajectory and clock rates. The default display applies a separate three-plane construction to those rates, producing the nubbed readout described below. That readout is not itself an established solution of the flat action. The external trajectory remains distinct from internal field oscillations; the coefficients and integer frequency ratios are supplied inputs.

The electron and the displayed object

The electron follows literal deterministic motion in six ordinary spatial dimensions. Physical spacetime has infinitely many ordinary spatial dimensions plus time. Matter occupies up to ten spatial dimensions; all remaining spatial directions are unbound for every particle. The working matter-domain geometry is flat. Particle depth describes dimensional involvement of the same field.

The Spatial (Signed) setting shows three real coordinates of the baseline external position around the nucleus, with an adjustable display-height scale. The default Folded setting instead shows a normalized three-plane readout. The chosen trajectory uses four directions and has two remaining position coordinates set to zero. It embeds in six or more containing dimensions without selecting the electron’s physical depth. Here LLL labels the chosen frequency ratio between the orbit and its precession. Their relation to observed atomic states, energies and transition strengths requires the physical interaction and observer response. Squared coordinates and ray overlaps do not assign a probability cloud to the electron.

One Plane restores the simpler Folded or Smooth readout described above. Spatial (Signed) bypasses the three-plane readout and uses the baseline position coordinates; its phase-spread control therefore has no effect. The Ring also bypasses the three-plane construction. State-distance information refers to the baseline trajectory, even while the canvas shows the nubbed readout.
