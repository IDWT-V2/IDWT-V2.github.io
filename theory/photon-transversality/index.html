Photon transversality at every coordinate depth

Physical premise (IDWT): There is one infinite-dimensional physical manifold. All sectors form one nested chain of submanifolds within it. Particle depth counts literal macroscopic spatial dimensions. The electron's CP3 sector has six; nuclear 3+3 locking constrains ordinary matter and observers to an experienced three-dimensional sector. Unobserved spatial directions remain physical. See spatial meaning and mathematical scope.

1. The claim to explain

The photon is an excitation bound in two macroscopic spatial dimensions of the nested manifold:

Ξ2⊂Ξ3⊂Ξ4⊂⋯⊂Ξ∞. \Xi_2\subset\Xi_3\subset\Xi_4\subset\cdots\subset\Xi_\infty. Ξ2​⊂Ξ3​⊂Ξ4​⊂⋯⊂Ξ∞​.

The single time coordinate is shared at every depth. Ordinary observers occupy the r=3r=3r=3 level of this nesting.

The transversality problem is therefore:

Why must the ambient momentum of a photon bound in two spatial dimensions be perpendicular to its two directions of oscillation at every depth that contains it?

Photon masslessness and the complete electromagnetic field equations are the neighbouring dynamical problems.

3. The depth-independent transversality theorem

This is a conditional geometric theorem: assigning polarization to the physical spatial plane and propagation momentum to its normal space already enforces their orthogonality. The depth-two assignment alone does not derive those assignments, select the separated mode below, or establish the electromagnetic constraint equations. Free outer dynamics also allow wavepackets; constant outer intensity describes the chosen momentum eigenmode, not every photon.
