Current Reading and Scope — 1 November 2026

A collection of internal common-frequency portions is not an identification of observed quarks as constituents of every particle. Composite mass requires complete rest energy, relative motion, support and reciprocal interaction. The compact cluster law supplies a new conditional mechanical comparison; its count offset and source normalization are premises, and it does not yet predict a nucleon mass.

The new particle-geometry and sector-depths case provides the current derivations, shared assumptions, glossary and reproduction instructions. The twenty-volume research map connects the complete account. Calculations below retain their own stated mathematical premises and do not inherit results from a different action.

1. Question

The quark sectors in V2 are assigned different spatial depths:

Q4for up-type quarks, Q_4 \quad \text{for up-type quarks}, Q4​for up-type quarks,

and

Q3for down-type quarks. Q_3 \quad \text{for down-type quarks}. Q3​for down-type quarks.

The cross-depth geometry is asymmetric. A four-dimensional mode projected into a three-dimensional hadronic sector loses one independent spatial oscillator direction. The three-dimensional state does not gain one.

This raises a specific dynamical question:

Can the large difference between light current-quark masses and observed hadron masses be interpreted as the energy cost of constraining a four-dimensional oscillator into three dimensions?

The calculation below separates what follows mathematically from dimensional constriction from what remains an empirical or dynamical input.
