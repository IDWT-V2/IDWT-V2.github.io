67. Frequency-domain correction: the hadronic problem must be stated spectrally

Re-audit convention: in Sections 67--76, ϖd\varpi_dϖd​ denotes the spatial sector frequency unit μd/ℏ\mu_d/\hbarμd​/ℏ. Frequency ratios are the targets; no independently fitted field amplitude is introduced.

The preceding normalization discussion drifted into amplitude-domain quantities. That is not the correct observable language for the present V2 investigation.

The measurable content of the theory is spectral: frequencies, masses interpreted as frequencies in natural units, and dimensionless ratios of those frequencies. Field amplitudes, profile normalizations, and current magnitudes may appear in an intermediate representation of an operator, but they are not the quantities the hadronic calculation is trying to predict.

Accordingly, the cross-depth problem should be written directly in terms of spectral eigenvalues.

No independent MeV scale has to be generated. Once the dimensionless ratio to ωe\omega_eωe​ is known, expressing the result in MeV is merely a human unit conversion.

69. Frequency targets without an amplitude fit

The target observables are frequencies and their electron-relative ratios. No independent field-amplitude measurement is introduced.

An eigenvector's arbitrary overall normalization does not affect the eigenvalues of a fixed linear operator. This allows a frequency calculation without solving for an experimentally calibrated amplitude. It does not make geometric matrix elements, operator coefficients, boundary conditions or induced interactions disappear.

The earlier factorized-current example remains only a diagnostic of a particular operator representation. Couplings and spatial-profile overlaps are not all field amplitudes. Any dependence they create in a proposed generator must be derived, eliminated exactly, or proved to cancel before making a frequency prediction.

69.1 What changing units and projecting a state do not prove

Writing an unknown coefficient as the electron reference times an unknown dimensionless ratio changes its units, not whether that ratio has been determined.

Likewise, projecting a state and then renormalizing it does not automatically justify replacing its generator by a compressed matrix. Section 74 gives an exact example in which a projection factor changes spectral weight but not the surviving frequency.

The normalized-marginal example showed no universal 7/67/67/6 multiplier for that example. Its failure is neither erased merely by using frequency notation nor promoted into a universal theorem about a different operator.

The required endpoint is a derived frequency ratio, with no fitted amplitude. In a nonlinear theory, any background dependence of the linearized frequency operator also has to be specified or eliminated; eigenvalue notation alone does not establish independence from it.
