31. Self-consistent Gaussian curvature closure

There is a stronger route to the 7/37/37/3 coefficient than the raw reciprocal dimension trace.

The canonical normalized reciprocal 4→34\to34→3 calculation gave the inverse symmetric weight

C34=76. \boxed{ C_{34}=\frac76. } C34​=67​.​

Under the Gaussian profile and local harmonic self-consistency ansatz below, a factor of two appears. It is not a universal mean-field result and does not survive as an automatic doubling when the exact-profile comparisons later in this ledger replace that ansatz.

Take a normalized one-dimensional oscillator mode in the lost normal coordinate,

ψ(y)=1(πℓ2)1/4exp⁡(−y22ℓ2). \psi(y) =\frac{1}{(\pi\ell^2)^{1/4}} \exp\left(-\frac{y^2}{2\ell^2}\right). ψ(y)=(πℓ2)1/41​exp(−2ℓ2y2​).

Its density is

ρ(y)=∣ψ(y)∣2=1πℓexp⁡(−y2ℓ2). \rho(y)=|\psi(y)|^2 =\frac{1}{\sqrt\pi\ell} \exp\left(-\frac{y^2}{\ell^2}\right). ρ(y)=∣ψ(y)∣2=π​ℓ1​exp(−ℓ2y2​).

Suppose an attractive local self-consistent interaction produces a normal mean-field well proportional to that density,

V(y)=−Gρ(y). V(y)=-G\rho(y). V(y)=−Gρ(y).

Define its central depth

A=Gρ(0). A=G\rho(0). A=Gρ(0).

Then

V(y)=−Aexp⁡(−y2ℓ2). V(y) =-A\exp\left(-\frac{y^2}{\ell^2}\right). V(y)=−Aexp(−ℓ2y2​).

Expanding around the minimum,

V(y)=−A+Aℓ2y2+O(y4). V(y) =-A +\frac{A}{\ell^2}y^2 +O(y^4). V(y)=−A+ℓ2A​y2+O(y4).

Matching the quadratic term to a harmonic oscillator,

V(y)=−A+12m⊥ω2y2+⋯ , V(y) =-A+\frac12m_\perp\omega^2y^2+\cdots, V(y)=−A+21​m⊥​ω2y2+⋯,

gives

12m⊥ω2=Aℓ2. \frac12m_\perp\omega^2 =\frac{A}{\ell^2}. 21​m⊥​ω2=ℓ2A​.

For the oscillator ground state written above,

ℓ2=1m⊥ω \boxed{ \ell^2=\frac{1}{m_\perp\omega} } ℓ2=m⊥​ω1​​

in natural units. Substitution eliminates both the inertial parameter and the width:

12m⊥ω2=Am⊥ω, \frac12m_\perp\omega^2 =A m_\perp\omega, 21​m⊥​ω2=Am⊥​ω,

so for the nonzero localized solution,

ω=2A. \boxed{ \omega=2A. } ω=2A.​

This factor of two follows within the stated field-intensity ansatz: the self-consistent potential is proportional to ∣ψ∣2|\psi|^2∣ψ∣2, whose Gaussian exponent is twice that of the mode amplitude.

31.1 Combination with the canonical reciprocal normalization

If the normalized reciprocal 4→34\to34→3 contact gives the normal well depth

A34=76μ3g33, \boxed{ A_{34} =\frac76\mu_3g_{33}, } A34​=67​μ3​g33​,​

then Gaussian self-consistency yields

ℏωcommon=2A34=276μ3g33=73μ3g33. \hbar\omega_{\rm common} =2A_{34} =2\frac76\mu_3g_{33} =\boxed{\frac73\mu_3g_{33}}. ℏωcommon​=2A34​=267​μ3​g33​=37​μ3​g33​​.

Thus the previously unexplained coefficient decomposes as

73=2×76. \boxed{ \frac73 =2\times\frac76. } 37​=2×67​.​

The two factors have distinct origins:

  1. 7/67/67/6 is the canonical inverse symmetric reciprocal normalization of the 4→34\to34→3 shared phase-space carrier;
  2. 222 is the exact curvature factor generated when a Gaussian oscillator mode produces a mean field proportional to its squared amplitude.

Numerically,

μ3g33=99.5195753 MeV, \mu_3g_{33}=99.5195753\ {\rm MeV}, μ3​g33​=99.5195753 MeV,

so

A34=76μ3g33=116.106171 MeV, A_{34} =\frac76\mu_3g_{33} =116.106171\ {\rm MeV}, A34​=67​μ3​g33​=116.106171 MeV,

and therefore

ℏωcommon=232.212342 MeV. \boxed{ \hbar\omega_{\rm common} =232.212342\ {\rm MeV}. } ℏωcommon​=232.212342 MeV.​

Fixed-action dimensional constriction then gives

ℏω3=43ℏωcommon=309.616456 MeV. \boxed{ \hbar\omega_3 =\frac43\hbar\omega_{\rm common} =309.616456\ {\rm MeV}. } ℏω3​=34​ℏωcommon​=309.616456 MeV.​

This is exactly the previous nucleon-scale candidate, but the 7/37/37/3 coefficient no longer has to be postulated as a raw dimension-incidence ratio.

31.2 What remains unproved

This calculation substantially reduces the closure problem, but one identification is still required from the V2 master action:

A34=76μ3g33. \boxed{ A_{34}=\frac76\mu_3g_{33}. } A34​=67​μ3​g33​.​

The factor 7/67/67/6 is already supplied by canonical reciprocal normalization, and the factor 222 follows from self-consistent Gaussian curvature. What is not yet derived is that the interaction scale entering that normalized reciprocal well is specifically the product

μ3g33. \mu_3g_{33}. μ3​g33​.

Accordingly, the new proof target is narrower than before. One no longer needs to derive the full 7/37/37/3 coefficient. One needs only to show that the normalized 4→34\to34→3 normal mean-field depth is

A34=C34μ3g33,C34=76. \boxed{ A_{34}=C_{34}\mu_3g_{33}, \qquad C_{34}=\frac76. } A34​=C34​μ3​g33​,C34​=67​.​

32. Schur-complement derivation of the surviving g33g_{33}g33​ factor

The previous question was whether a genuine 4→34\to34→3 interaction should involve the cross-sector coupling g34g_{34}g34​ rather than the surviving-sector self-coupling g33g_{33}g33​. A two-channel quadratic model resolves this distinction.

Let xxx denote the surviving depth-three normal amplitude and yyy the depth-four amplitude that is removed by constriction. The most general positive quadratic interaction block is

E(x,y)=12(xy)(g33g34g34g44)(xy). E(x,y) =\frac12 \begin{pmatrix}x&y\end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} g_{33} & g_{34}\\ g_{34} & g_{44} \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix}x\\y\end{pmatrix}. E(x,y)=21​(x​y​)(g33​g34​​g34​g44​​)(xy​).

Explicitly,

E(x,y)=12g33x2+g34xy+12g44y2. E(x,y) =\frac12g_{33}x^2+g_{34}xy+\frac12g_{44}y^2. E(x,y)=21​g33​x2+g34​xy+21​g44​y2.

32.1 Relaxed higher-dimensional state

If the fourth-direction amplitude is allowed to relax freely, minimization gives

∂E∂y=0⟹yrel=−g34g44x. \frac{\partial E}{\partial y}=0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad y_{\rm rel}=-\frac{g_{34}}{g_{44}}x. ∂y∂E​=0⟹yrel​=−g44​g34​​x.

Substitution gives the Schur-complement energy

Erel(x)=12(g33−g342g44)x2. E_{\rm rel}(x) =\frac12 \left( g_{33}-\frac{g_{34}^2}{g_{44}} \right)x^2. Erel​(x)=21​(g33​−g44​g342​​)x2.

Thus ordinary integration-out or adiabatic relaxation produces the effective coefficient

g33rel=g33−g342g44. \boxed{ g_{33}^{\rm rel} =g_{33}-\frac{g_{34}^2}{g_{44}}. } g33rel​=g33​−g44​g342​​.​

32.2 Constricted state

Dimensional constriction is different. The removed coordinate is not allowed to relax to yrely_{\rm rel}yrel​; it is forced onto the lower-dimensional subspace

y=0. y=0. y=0.

The constrained energy is therefore

Econ(x)=12g33x2. E_{\rm con}(x) =\frac12g_{33}x^2. Econ​(x)=21​g33​x2.

The energetic cost of constriction relative to the relaxed higher-dimensional state is

ΔEcon(x)=Econ(x)−Erel(x)=12g342g44x2. \Delta E_{\rm con}(x) =E_{\rm con}(x)-E_{\rm rel}(x) =\frac12\frac{g_{34}^2}{g_{44}}x^2. ΔEcon​(x)=Econ​(x)−Erel​(x)=21​g44​g342​​x2.

Hence the positive stiffness generated specifically by suppressing the fourth direction is

gcon=g342g44. \boxed{ g_{\rm con} =\frac{g_{34}^2}{g_{44}}. } gcon​=g44​g342​​.​

This explains why a 4→34\to34→3 process can be governed by the surviving-sector self-coupling even though the transition itself is mediated by g34g_{34}g34​.

32.3 Rank-one coupling closure

The V2 coupling hypothesis uses the rank-one relation

g342=g33g44. \boxed{ g_{34}^2=g_{33}g_{44}. } g342​=g33​g44​.​

Therefore

gcon=g342g44=g33. \boxed{ g_{\rm con} =\frac{g_{34}^2}{g_{44}} =g_{33}. } gcon​=g44​g342​​=g33​.​

Even more strongly, the relaxed Schur complement becomes

g33rel=g33−g342g44=0. g_{33}^{\rm rel} =g_{33}-\frac{g_{34}^2}{g_{44}} =0. g33rel​=g33​−g44​g342​​=0.

So under the rank-one condition the coupled higher-dimensional mode has a flat relaxed direction, while constraining the fourth component to zero restores the full depth-three stiffness g33g_{33}g33​.

This gives a concrete geometric interpretation of the self-coupling:

g33=stiffness cost of forbidding the depth-four relaxation channel. \boxed{ g_{33}=\text{stiffness cost of forbidding the depth-four relaxation channel}. } g33​=stiffness cost of forbidding the depth-four relaxation channel.​

That is precisely the structure required by the hadronic constriction mechanism.

33. Combined closure chain

The previous sections supplied two other ingredients.

Canonical reciprocal normalization gives

C34=76. C_{34}=\frac76. C34​=67​.

The Schur-complement constriction calculation gives

gcon=g33. g_{\rm con}=g_{33}. gcon​=g33​.

Therefore, if the depth-three contact-energy unit μ3\mu_3μ3​ converts this dimensionless constriction stiffness into the central normal mean-field depth, then

A34=C34μ3gcon=76μ3g33. \boxed{ A_{34} =C_{34}\mu_3g_{\rm con} =\frac76\mu_3g_{33}. } A34​=C34​μ3​gcon​=67​μ3​g33​.​

For a self-consistent Gaussian density well, the local curvature relation derived above gives

ℏωcommon=2A34=73μ3g33. \boxed{ \hbar\omega_{\rm common}=2A_{34} =\frac73\mu_3g_{33}. } ℏωcommon​=2A34​=37​μ3​g33​.​

ℏω3=43ℏωcommon=289μ3g33. \boxed{ \hbar\omega_3 =\frac43\hbar\omega_{\rm common} =\frac{28}{9}\mu_3g_{33}. } ℏω3​=34​ℏωcommon​=928​μ3​g33​.​

Numerically,

ℏω3=309.616456 MeV. \boxed{ \hbar\omega_3=309.616456\ {\rm MeV}. } ℏω3​=309.616456 MeV.​

Multiplying them gives

76×2×43=289. \boxed{ \frac76\times2\times\frac43 =\frac{28}{9}. } 67​×2×34​=928​.​

33.1 Remaining assumption

The principal remaining assumption is now narrower still: that the dimensionless quadratic constriction stiffness is converted to physical normal-well depth by the same depth-three contact-energy unit μ3\mu_3μ3​,

A34=76μ3gcon. \boxed{ A_{34}=\frac76\mu_3g_{\rm con}. } A34​=67​μ3​gcon​.​

Unlike the earlier unexplained product μ3g33\mu_3g_{33}μ3​g33​, the factor g33g_{33}g33​ now arises from an explicit constrained-versus-relaxed quadratic calculation. What remains to be derived from the master action is the normalization identifying μ3\mu_3μ3​ as the energy unit of this normal quadratic block.
