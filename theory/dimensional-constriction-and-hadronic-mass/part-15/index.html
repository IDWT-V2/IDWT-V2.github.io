64. Action and frequency are distinct normalization questions

The fixed-radius oscillator construction describes one band. It does not supply a conservation law for a change in depth. Even if total action were conserved, Section 2 shows that this would imply 3Iper(3)=4Iper(4)3I_{\rm per}^{(3)}=4I_{\rm per}^{(4)}3Iper(3)​=4Iper(4)​, not a frequency ratio.

Consequently the trial quantity

149μ3g33=154.808228 MeV \frac{14}{9}\mu_3g_{33}=154.808228\ {\rm MeV} 914​μ3​g33​=154.808228 MeV

is not a derived constriction quantum, nor an established half of a nucleon contribution. It combines three independent, unclosed identifications:

  1. a scalar energy normalization μ3g33\mu_3g_{33}μ3​g33​;
  2. a chosen reciprocal normalization 7/67/67/6;
  3. a frequency multiplier 4/34/34/3.

The static rank-one Schur identity alone does not make any product of these factors a spectral pole. The companion trial value (7/6)μ3g33=116.106171 MeV(7/6)\mu_3g_{33}=116.106171\ {\rm MeV}(7/6)μ3​g33​=116.106171 MeV is likewise not an independently established physical energy. Sections 66--76 examine those remaining normalization and spectral questions.

An action-transfer law and a stationary Hamiltonian must be specified separately. No transfer of action alone fixes the missing frequency.

65. Symplectic-reduction test: canonical projection does not generically produce the 4/34/34/3 action transfer

The assumed frequency-rescaling hypothesis can be tested at the level of classical phase-space geometry.

Start with four oscillator directions with canonical coordinates

(qi,pi),i=1,2,3,4, (q_i,p_i), \qquad i=1,2,3,4, (qi​,pi​),i=1,2,3,4,

Ω4=∑i=14dqi∧dpi. \Omega_4 =\sum_{i=1}^4dq_i\wedge dp_i. Ω4​=i=1∑4​dqi​∧dpi​.

Let the corresponding action variables be

Ii=12π∮pi dqi. I_i =\frac{1}{2\pi}\oint p_i\,dq_i. Ii​=2π1​∮pi​dqi​.

H4=ω4∑i=14Ii. H_4 =\omega_4\sum_{i=1}^4I_i. H4​=ω4​i=1∑4​Ii​.

Now perform the simplest canonical reduction to three surviving directions by restricting to the constraint surface

q4=0,p4=0. q_4=0, \qquad p_4=0. q4​=0,p4​=0.

The pullback of the symplectic form is

ι∗Ω4=∑i=13dqi∧dpi=Ω3. \iota^*\Omega_4 =\sum_{i=1}^3dq_i\wedge dp_i =\Omega_3. ι∗Ω4​=i=1∑3​dqi​∧dpi​=Ω3​.

But its action is

Ired=I1+I2+I3, I_{\rm red} =I_1+I_2+I_3, Ired​=I1​+I2​+I3​,

I1+I2+I3+I4. I_1+I_2+I_3+I_4. I1​+I2​+I3​+I4​.

I1=I2=I3=I4=I, I_1=I_2=I_3=I_4=I, I1​=I2​=I3​=I4​=I,

I4⟶ΔI1+ΔI2+ΔI3, I_4 \longrightarrow \Delta I_1+\Delta I_2+\Delta I_3, I4​⟶ΔI1​+ΔI2​+ΔI3​,

with

ΔI1+ΔI2+ΔI3=I4. \Delta I_1+\Delta I_2+\Delta I_3=I_4. ΔI1​+ΔI2​+ΔI3​=I4​.

Equal redistribution would give

ΔIi=13I4, \Delta I_i=\frac13I_4, ΔIi​=31​I4​,

but neither symplecticity nor dimensional reduction forces that sharing rule.

65.1 Adiabatic invariance does not rescue the transfer automatically

For a slowly varying Hamiltonian with a fixed number of independent oscillator modes, each action variable is an adiabatic invariant to leading order:

Ii≃const. I_i\simeq\text{const}. Ii​≃const.

That statement does not imply that when one degree of freedom disappears its action is reassigned to the others. In fact, ordinary adiabatic invariance favours preservation of the surviving IiI_iIi​ rather than the specific transfer

I4→(I1,I2,I3). I_4\to(I_1,I_2,I_3). I4​→(I1​,I2​,I3​).

A change in the number of degrees of freedom typically involves a singular limit, constraint activation, mode coupling, or separatrix-type dynamics, at which ordinary adiabatic-invariant arguments require separate analysis.

Hence

neither canonical symplectic reduction nor generic adiabatic invariance derives the 4/3 factor. \boxed{ \text{neither canonical symplectic reduction nor generic adiabatic invariance derives the }4/3\text{ factor.} } neither canonical symplectic reduction nor generic adiabatic invariance derives the 4/3 factor.​

65.2 Action transfer would still not determine the frequency

To derive action transfer, V2 would need explicit coupled projection dynamics. Schematically one needs a Hamiltonian

H(qi,pi;λ) H(q_i,p_i;\lambda) H(qi​,pi​;λ)

with a constriction parameter λ\lambdaλ, together with mode-coupling terms such that evolution from the four-dimensional regime to the three-dimensional regime satisfies

lim⁡λ→λ3D(I1+I2+I3)=I1in+I2in+I3in+I4in. \lim_{\lambda\to\lambda_{3D}} \left(I_1+I_2+I_3\right) = I_1^{\rm in}+I_2^{\rm in}+I_3^{\rm in}+I_4^{\rm in}. λ→λ3D​lim​(I1​+I2​+I3​)=I1in​+I2in​+I3in​+I4in​.

Only after such a transfer theorem is established does isotropy imply

3Iout=4Iin 3I_{\rm out}=4I_{\rm in} 3Iout​=4Iin​

for the per-direction action ratio. This does not imply ω3/ω4=4/3\omega_3/\omega_4=4/3ω3​/ω4​=4/3. The Hamiltonian and energy exchange must additionally determine the frequency; conserving total energy as well as total action in this isotropic model instead gives equal frequencies.

The present V2 action contains no such explicit constriction Hamiltonian or transfer map.

Therefore the current status is stronger than merely "unproved":

the 4/3 factor is not the generic result of symplectic 4→3 reduction. \boxed{ \text{the }4/3\text{ factor is not the generic result of symplectic }4\to3\text{ reduction.} } the 4/3 factor is not the generic result of symplectic 4→3 reduction.​

It requires additional mode-transfer dynamics.

65.3 Consequence for the hadronic scale

Dropping the unsupported frequency multiplier leaves the numerical trial (7/6)μ3g33≈116.106171(7/6)\mu_3g_{33}\approx116.106171(7/6)μ3​g33​≈116.106171 MeV, not an established physical scale. The scalar-unit and reciprocal-normalization identifications also remain unclosed.

A complete calculation must derive the interacting spectral operator, including its normalization and response to constriction. Neither total-action transfer nor proximity to a constituent residual fixes that operator. There is no established half-contribution that the calculation is obliged to double.

66. Reciprocal-normalization audit: 7/67/67/6 is canonical, but not forced by the current cross-depth action

The remaining reciprocal factor was audited against the independent V2 cross-depth modules rather than against its repeated use in this hadronic note.

The V2 cross-depth theorem establishes three relevant facts:

  1. the higher-depth density enters through its complete marginal over the extra fibre;
  2. the lower-depth density is cylindrically extended over that fibre;
  3. functional differentiation of a symmetric interaction action produces reciprocal response in both sectors.

Schematically,

Sab=gab∫dt Iab S_{ab}=g_{ab}\int dt\,I_{ab} Sab​=gab​∫dtIab​

with

δSabδρa∝ρˉb→a,δSabδρb∝ρa. \frac{\delta S_{ab}}{\delta\rho_a} \propto\bar\rho_{b\to a}, \qquad \frac{\delta S_{ab}}{\delta\rho_b} \propto\rho_a. δρa​δSab​​∝ρˉ​b→a​,δρb​δSab​​∝ρa​.

These statements establish full contact and exchange symmetry, but they do not specify a unique averaging functional over the dimensions of the two phase-space carriers.

For a 4→34\to34→3 phase-space comparison, the real quadrature counts are

N4=8,N3=6. N_4=8, \qquad N_3=6. N4​=8,N3​=6.

If one chooses the symmetric arithmetic normalization of the shared carrier, the shared fraction is

C34=2N3N4+N3=1214=67, C_{34} =\frac{2N_3}{N_4+N_3} =\frac{12}{14} =\frac67, C34​=N4​+N3​2N3​​=1412​=76​,

C34−1=76. \boxed{ C_{34}^{-1}=\frac76. } C34−1​=67​.​

However, the written V2 action does not contain an independent theorem requiring this particular arithmetic normalization. Full marginalization fixes the total normalized contact weight, and self-adjointness/exchange symmetry constrain the cross-sector blocks, but neither condition uniquely selects

This is stronger caution than the earlier statement that 7/67/67/6 is merely preferable to 7/37/37/3. The audit shows that current V2 decisively rejects the naive 7/37/37/3 double count, but it does not yet prove that 7/67/67/6 is the only allowed normalized reciprocal coefficient.
