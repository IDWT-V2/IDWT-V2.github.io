Particle depth as spatial geometry

Literal macroscopic spatial dimensions

IDWT has one infinite-dimensional physical manifold, M∞M_\inftyM∞​. Every particle sector is a nested spatial submanifold of that same manifold:

Qa↪Qb↪M∞,a<b. Q_a\hookrightarrow Q_b\hookrightarrow M_\infty,\qquad a<b. Qa​↪Qb​↪M∞​,a<b.

This inclusion relates spatial sectors. Individual particles are field structures associated with the sectors; it does not place one particle inside another or make it a constituent of another. In particular, an electron state need not be the restriction of a tau state. A restriction of bundles compares the available field components across sectors; identifying particular particle solutions through that restriction is an additional dynamical assumption.

Depth ddd counts ordinary, literal macroscopic spatial dimensions of QdQ_dQd​. They are all the same kind of physical dimension. Their coordinates specify position, separation, and motion. A sector is not an internal state space, a compactified auxiliary space, or a configuration-space substitute for physical space. There are no additional physical dimensions outside M∞M_\inftyM∞​.

For the electron and tau, this means Q6↪Q10Q_6\hookrightarrow Q_{10}Q6​↪Q10​: the electron's six spatial dimensions are six of the tau's ten. All ten are the same kind of dimension for the tau. The electron is “flat” relative to it, meaning lower-dimensional, not that the tau has an inherent six-plus-four division. The same relationship holds between three-dimensional nuclei and six-dimensional electrons. “Flat” here describes relative dimensionality, not a claim of zero metric curvature.

The photon-transversality construction illustrates this at every containing depth. The photon retains two directions of bound oscillation. There is one perpendicular direction within a three-dimensional sector, four within a six-dimensional sector, and eight within a ten-dimensional sector. These are descriptions relative to the photon; they do not create different kinds of dimensions in the containing sector.

IDWT attributes the three-dimensional experience of ordinary matter and observers to the three-dimensional nuclear sector. This general premise does not require a physical division of any higher-depth particle into different kinds of dimensions. The observer map must describe what the observer's interactions register; its dynamics remains to be derived.

For example, the electron has six ordinary macroscopic spatial dimensions. In the proposed geometry, Q6=CP3Q_6=\mathbb{CP}^3Q6​=CP3 is its actual spatial submanifold. Three of those dimensions are shared with us. That fact makes none of the six physically different in kind, and does not give the electron an inherent 3+33+33+3 split. Grouping coordinates by an observer's access is a choice of description, not a division in the electron. Frame-lock models that use such a grouping have additional assumptions; they do not establish the general origin of three-dimensional experience.
