Theory

Bound within, free without

Physical premise (IDWT): There is one infinite-dimensional physical manifold. All sectors form one nested chain of submanifolds within it. Particle depth counts literal macroscopic spatial dimensions. The electron's CP3 sector has six; nuclear 3+3 locking constrains ordinary matter and observers to an experienced three-dimensional sector. Unobserved spatial directions remain physical. See spatial meaning and mathematical scope.

1. Claim and status

IDWT has one infinite-dimensional physical manifold. Each particle's sector is a nested submanifold, and its depth counts the macroscopic spatial directions in which it has bound structure. A depth-aaa mode has localized structure in Ξa\Xi_aΞa​, while the same physical manifold continues beyond it:

Ξa↪Ξb↪M∞,a<b. \Xi_a\hookrightarrow\Xi_b\hookrightarrow M_\infty, \qquad a<b. Ξa​↪Ξb​↪M∞​,a<b.

The founding kinematic statement is:

A particle is bound within the dimensions in which it has localized spatial structure and spans every additional dimension in which it has no localized motion.

Here spans means coordinate independence: the sector mode has equal support across the additional coordinate and is localized in none of its values. This is the content of bound within, free without.

That statement is a premise of the sector ontology. Together with coordinate nesting, it yields the contact theorem below by pullback, pushforward, and Fubini integration.

A depth-aaa mode has a normalizable bound factor χa(x)\chi_a(x)χa​(x) on its own sector. Its continuation to depth bbb is the cylindrical pullback

(πba∗χa)(x,y)=χa(x). (\pi_{ba}^{*}\chi_a)(x,y)=\chi_a(x). (πba∗​χa​)(x,y)=χa​(x).

∂yj(πba∗χa)=0(j=1,…,b−a). \partial_{y^j}(\pi_{ba}^{*}\chi_a)=0 \qquad (j=1,\ldots,b-a). ∂yj​(πba∗​χa​)=0(j=1,…,b−a).

The lower-depth particle has no profile that must be aimed at a point of the extra fibre. It spans that fibre because nothing in its spatial mode distinguishes one fibre point from another.

The same state is compatible with, but not forced by, a free outer Hamiltonian. In a direction with no binding potential,

3. The cross-depth contact theorem

Let ρa(x)\rho_a(x)ρa​(x) be a density or current carried by the depth-aaa mode, and let ρb(x,y)\rho_b(x,y)ρb​(x,y) be one carried by the depth-bbb mode. From the higher-depth side, the lower-depth quantity is cylindrical:

ρa↑b(x,y)=ρa(x). \rho_a^{\uparrow b}(x,y)=\rho_a(x). ρa↑b​(x,y)=ρa​(x).

For an allowed interaction local on Ξb\Xi_bΞb​, the overlap is

Iab=∫Ξadμa(x)∫Fb/adμF(y) ρa(x)ρb(x,y). I_{ab} = \int_{\Xi_a}d\mu_a(x) \int_{F_{b/a}}d\mu_F(y)\, \rho_a(x)\rho_b(x,y). Iab​=∫Ξa​​dμa​(x)∫Fb/a​​dμF​(y)ρa​(x)ρb​(x,y).

Define the pushforward, or marginal, of the higher-depth density:

ρˉb→a(x)=(πba)∗ρb(x)=∫Fb/aρb(x,y) dμF(y). \bar\rho_{b\to a}(x) = (\pi_{ba})_*\rho_b(x) = \int_{F_{b/a}}\rho_b(x,y)\,d\mu_F(y). ρˉ​b→a​(x)=(πba​)∗​ρb​(x)=∫Fb/a​​ρb​(x,y)dμF​(y).

Fubini's theorem then gives

Iab=∫Ξaρa(x)ρˉb→a(x) dμa(x). \boxed{ I_{ab} = \int_{\Xi_a} \rho_a(x)\bar\rho_{b\to a}(x)\,d\mu_a(x) }. Iab​=∫Ξa​​ρa​(x)ρˉ​b→a​(x)dμa​(x)​.

This is the cross-depth contact theorem. The higher-depth particle contributes through its complete marginal over the extra coordinates, and the cylindrical lower-depth extension covers the entire fibre. The overlap therefore carries the full marginal weight.

If ρb\rho_bρb​ is a normalized nonnegative density, its whole weight survives the pushforward:

∫Ξaρˉb→a dμa=∫Ξbρb dμb=1. \int_{\Xi_a}\bar\rho_{b\to a}\,d\mu_a = \int_{\Xi_b}\rho_b\,d\mu_b =1. ∫Ξa​​ρˉ​b→a​dμa​=∫Ξb​​ρb​dμb​=1.

This is the precise sense in which contact is full: the interaction integrates the extra coordinates over the complete fibre.

For a nonlocal or filtered interaction, replace the pointwise product by a kernel on the shared coordinates:

Iab[K]=∫Ξa×Ξaρa(x)Kab(x,x′)ρˉb→a(x′) dμa(x)dμa(x′). I_{ab}[K] = \int_{\Xi_a\times\Xi_a} \rho_a(x)K_{ab}(x,x')\bar\rho_{b\to a}(x') \,d\mu_a(x)d\mu_a(x'). Iab​[K]=∫Ξa​×Ξa​​ρa​(x)Kab​(x,x′)ρˉ​b→a​(x′)dμa​(x)dμa​(x′).

Unequal depth determines how the modes meet. The kernel, coupling constant, and representation content determine what they do when they meet.

4. Noncompact normalization

On an infinite fibre, the cylindrical pullback πba∗χa\pi_{ba}^{*}\chi_aπba∗​χa​ can be used as a translation-invariant generalized state or as a field/observable. These uses need distinct normalizations:

  • a cylindrical field or observable pulled back from Ξa\Xi_aΞa​;
  • a generalized momentum eigenstate with delta normalization; or
  • a box-normalized approximation, whose density contains an explicit inverse fibre-volume factor.

The particle's normalization over its spatial support remains

∫Ξa∣χa(x)∣2dμa(x)=1. \int_{\Xi_a}|\chi_a(x)|^2d\mu_a(x)=1. ∫Ξa​​∣χa​(x)∣2dμa​(x)=1.

The cross-depth interaction of a cylindrical field is finite when the higher-depth density supplies an integrable fibre factor. This does not make it equal to the overlap of two probability-normalized states. In a box of fibre volume VFV_FVF​, a normalized uniform extension has density ρa(x)/VF\rho_a(x)/V_Fρa​(x)/VF​; its overlap with a fixed normalized ρb\rho_bρb​ is Iab/VFI_{ab}/V_FIab​/VF​, tending to zero as the box grows. Cancelling that factor requires a specified flux, source-density, or coupling normalization in the action. It does not follow from Fubini's theorem. The contact formula above uses the cylindrical-field convention, normalized on the physical spatial sector.

5. Reciprocity

Let the interaction part of the action be

Sab=gab∫dt Iab. S_{ab}=g_{ab}\int dt\,I_{ab}. Sab​=gab​∫dtIab​.

For the local form, functional differentiation gives

δSabδρa(x)=gabρˉb→a(x), \frac{\delta S_{ab}}{\delta\rho_a(x)} =g_{ab}\bar\rho_{b\to a}(x), δρa​(x)δSab​​=gab​ρˉ​b→a​(x),

and

δSabδρb(x,y)=gabρa(x). \frac{\delta S_{ab}}{\delta\rho_b(x,y)} =g_{ab}\rho_a(x). δρb​(x,y)δSab​​=gab​ρa​(x).

The lower-depth mode feels the higher-depth mode through the latter's complete marginal. The higher-depth mode feels the lower-depth mode uniformly across the additional fibre. These are two variations of the same interaction term, so reciprocity follows when the action and kernel have the corresponding exchange symmetry. Both objects remain in their respective sectors.

In IDWT's one-field language, ρa\rho_aρa​ and ρb\rho_bρb​ are sector components of one field Ψ∞\Psi_\inftyΨ∞​. A cross-sector term is that field coupling two of its own depth-resolved structures on their shared coordinates.

6. Coupling conditions

An interaction requires three ingredients:

  1. Coordinate incidence. An embedding or shared-coordinate map must identify the active lower-depth structure inside the higher-depth sector.
  2. Coupling filter. The modes must carry compatible charge, representation, chirality, or another interaction handle. The projected current may be zero despite full contact.
  3. Dynamics. The action supplies a coupling constant and an interaction kernel or connection.

In a fixed nested gauge the inclusion Ξa↪Ξb\Xi_a\hookrightarrow\Xi_bΞa​↪Ξb​ is explicit. Rotating spatial frames require the corresponding embedding or projector in the interaction. Coordinate independence supplies complete fibre coverage; the polarization or representation projector on the shared coordinates determines whether the contact channel is nonzero. Numerical coupling ratios come from that projector and the normalized action.

7. Causal evolution

Uniform support specifies the mode state. A disturbance evolves through the retarded Green function of the local field equation, whose causal cone governs the propagation of controllable changes. Marginalization specifies the coordinates sampled by the interaction at an event. Thus “already there” supplies coverage while the field equation supplies causal evolution.

8. Photon–electron example

x∈Ξ2,y∈F6/2. x\in\Xi_2, \qquad y\in F_{6/2}. x∈Ξ2​,y∈F6/2​.

Jˉe→2(x)=∫F6/2Je(x,y) dμF(y). \bar J_{e\to2}(x) = \int_{F_{6/2}}J_e(x,y)\,d\mu_F(y). Jˉe→2​(x)=∫F6/2​​Je​(x,y)dμF​(y).

or its covariant analogue on the full manifold. The photon's coordinate independence across the four additional electron directions supplies the cylindrical extension, and the electron supplies the complete fibre marginal at the shared d=2d=2d=2 interaction. Electric charge and the normalized U(1)U(1)U(1) connection determine the interaction coefficient eee.

The same geometry underlies photon transversality. The photon oscillates in its physical spatial two-plane, while outer coordinates carry free phase or zero variation. Higher-depth embedding therefore preserves the spatial polarization plane.

10. Mathematical domain

Marginalization also does not by itself make a point particle appear smeared. For an instantaneous point trajectory,

ρb(x,y,t)=δ(x−X(t))δ(y−Y(t)),∫ρb(x,y,t) dy=δ(x−X(t)). \rho_b(x,y,t)=\delta(x-X(t))\delta(y-Y(t)),\qquad \int\rho_b(x,y,t)\,dy=\delta(x-X(t)). ρb​(x,y,t)=δ(x−X(t))δ(y−Y(t)),∫ρb​(x,y,t)dy=δ(x−X(t)).

An extended visible density instead requires visible extent in the joint state, an ensemble or time average of the trajectory, or detector response. Consequently, observing a spread alone does not distinguish the cylindrical ontology from an unobserved-localized source. Cylindrical extension remains the selected V2 premise here; the claim that smearing uniquely selects it is withdrawn.

Coordinate nesting, outer translation invariance, full-coverage coupling, higher-to-lower marginalization, reciprocal response, and coupling filters form one consistent geometric mechanism.

On a compact outer fibre, uniform support admits ordinary normalization. On an infinite fibre, it is a cylindrical or generalized state. Coupling magnitudes are computed from the action, kernel, connection, and representation geometry.
