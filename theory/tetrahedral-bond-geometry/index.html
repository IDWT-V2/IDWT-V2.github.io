Tetrahedral bond geometry from the projective electron sector

1. Claim and status

This module is a conditional occupation-simplex and hybrid-state construction. It is not a calculation of actual multi-nucleus electron trajectories. The four-state bonding-space identification and molecular energy are additional premises; its tetrahedral angle is not independent evidence of electron depth or a solution of the reciprocal orbit problem.

The ideal tetrahedral bond angle is the angle of the centred occupation simplex of CP3\mathbb{CP}^3CP3.

The same result appears in the observable bonding subspace

Vsp=span⁡{∣s⟩,∣px⟩,∣py⟩,∣pz⟩}≅C4. \mathcal V_{sp} =\operatorname{span}\{|s\rangle,|p_x\rangle,|p_y\rangle,|p_z\rangle\} \cong\mathbb C^4. Vsp​=span{∣s⟩,∣px​⟩,∣py​⟩,∣pz​⟩}≅C4.

Four equivalent orthogonal hybrids in this space point toward the vertices of a regular tetrahedron, giving

θtet=arccos⁡(−1/3)=109.4712206…∘. \boxed{\theta_{\rm tet}=\arccos(-1/3)=109.4712206\ldots^\circ.} θtet​=arccos(−1/3)=109.4712206…∘.​

This angle follows exactly from equivalence, orthogonality, and the stated observer-space identification.

Ammonia and water are less symmetric because one or two of the four occupied states are lone-pair states. Orthogonality then proves how an unequal sss-occupation changes the bond angle and proves that greater lone-pair sss-occupation compresses it. A molecular energy minimization determines the amount of redistribution and therefore the numerical angles.

2. Why chemistry belongs to the same physics

The single-electron orbit module uses four homogeneous coordinates for the electron's projective orientation:

[z1:z2:z3:z4]∈CP3. [z_1:z_2:z_3:z_4]\in\mathbb{CP}^3. [z1​:z2​:z3​:z4​]∈CP3.

At a molecular centre, the lowest observable sigma-bonding space also has four complex components: one sss state and three ppp states. Together they form one four-dimensional state space, and a molecule chooses an orthogonal basis adapted to its surrounding nuclei.

Axial symmetry about a neighbouring nucleus produces the bond-directed form, while fermionic occupancy permits an orthonormal basis of the occupied subspace. The projective construction adds a geometric statement: the four equivalent directions are the vertices of the same simplex that is the moment polytope of CP3\mathbb{CP}^3CP3.

Both derivations express the geometry of one copy of C4\mathbb C^4C4. The pure-spinor bridge makes the proposed physical identification precise: under Spin⁡(3)obs×Spin⁡(3)hid\operatorname{Spin}(3)_{\rm obs}\times\operatorname{Spin}(3)_{\rm hid}Spin(3)obs​×Spin(3)hid​ the chiral four is (2,2)(\mathbf2,\mathbf2)(2,2), and diagonal frame locking gives 2⊗2=1⊕3=s⊕p\mathbf2\otimes\mathbf2=\mathbf1\oplus\mathbf3=s\oplus p2⊗2=1⊕3=s⊕p. Until the bound-state action derives that diagonal locking, equality of the two copies of C4\mathbb C^4C4 is the conditional intertwiner used in this module.

6. The continuous occupation family

Completeness and orthogonality admit a continuous family of δ\deltaδ values.

Let a=(a1,a2,a3,a4)a=(a_1,a_2,a_3,a_4)a=(a1​,a2​,a3​,a4​) be any positive vector satisfying

∑iai2=1. \sum_i a_i^2=1. i∑​ai2​=1.

The Gram matrix of the remaining ppp components of four orthonormal hybrids is

Gp=I−aaT. G_p=I-aa^T. Gp​=I−aaT.

It is positive semidefinite and has rank three. Consequently it always admits a realization by four vectors in R3\mathbb R^3R3. Dividing the iiith vector by 1−ai2\sqrt{1-a_i^2}1−ai2​​ produces unit bond/lone-pair directions satisfying the exact overlap formula of the previous section.

Therefore a continuous family of sss-share assignments, including a continuous family of δ\deltaδ values, is compatible with completeness and orthogonality. The geometry determines the angle once δ\deltaδ is known. Selection of δ\deltaδ belongs to the molecular energy functional:

7. What must select the distortion

The physical angle is a minimum of the Born--Oppenheimer energy surface. In the one-parameter reduction it has the form

E(δ,R)=Eone centre(δ)+Etwo centre(δ,R)+VNN(δ,R). E(\delta,R) =E_{\rm one\ centre}(\delta) +E_{\rm two\ centre}(\delta,R) +V_{NN}(\delta,R). E(δ,R)=Eone centre​(δ)+Etwo centre​(δ,R)+VNN​(δ,R).

The complete occupied s+3ps+3ps+3p space makes the trace of every one-electron operator on that space basis-independent. Selection of δ\deltaδ therefore comes from geometry-dependent multi-centre terms.

A Slater-orbital estimate finds that the one-centre electron--electron contribution changes by less than 0.0040.0040.004 atomic units over the tested interval. The hydrogen--hydrogen nuclear repulsion increases as the bond angle closes, so that term resists positive δ\deltaδ. The selecting contribution is the geometry-dependent two-centre electronic energy: nuclear attraction, Coulomb repulsion, exchange, and correlation of orbitals extending between the heavy atom and the hydrogen nuclei. A quantitative derivation must minimize these terms simultaneously in δ\deltaδ and the bond lengths. In the marginal-exactness regime, this is the ordinary three-dimensional molecular Hamiltonian expressed in the projective-orbit basis.

The closure target is

∂E∂δ=0,∂E∂R=0, \frac{\partial E}{\partial\delta}=0, \qquad \frac{\partial E}{\partial R}=0, ∂δ∂E​=0,∂R∂E​=0,

with measured angles excluded from both equations. The current result derives the tetrahedral value and the angle--occupation map. Numerical molecular angles await this minimization.

8. What this says about a six-dimensional electron

The result gives the chain

CP3⟶four-state occupation simplex⟶centred tetrahedron⟶109.471∘. \mathbb{CP}^3 \longrightarrow \text{four-state occupation simplex} \longrightarrow \text{centred tetrahedron} \longrightarrow 109.471^\circ. CP3⟶four-state occupation simplex⟶centred tetrahedron⟶109.471∘.

It explains why four equivalent bonding channels of a depth-six electron can present themselves as four tetrahedral directions to a three-dimensional observer. It also joins the orbital-lobe construction and molecular geometry to the same projective state space.

The model identifies this four-state structure as the observable bonding face of the electron's underlying CP3\mathbb{CP}^3CP3 sector. A specific empirical discriminator for the larger identification remains an open target.
