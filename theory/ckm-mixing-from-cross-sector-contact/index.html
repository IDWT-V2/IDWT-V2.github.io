CKM mixing from the cross-sector contact operator

Physical premise (IDWT): There is one infinite-dimensional physical manifold. All sectors form one nested chain of submanifolds within it. Particle depth counts literal macroscopic spatial dimensions. The electron's CP3 sector has six; nuclear 3+3 locking constrains ordinary matter and observers to an experienced three-dimensional sector. Unobserved spatial directions remain physical. See spatial meaning and mathematical scope.

1. Result

The present V2 weak-transition operator is

P3D∞P4⊃g2γμWμ−T−P(1,1)⊗C34, P_3D_\infty P_4 \supset g_2\gamma^\mu W_\mu^-T^-P_{(1,1)}\otimes\mathcal C_{34}, P3​D∞​P4​⊃g2​γμWμ−​T−P(1,1)​⊗C34​,

The existing construction fixes the weak polarization intertwiner and the occupied quark modes, but it does not yet define a nontrivial operator on the three-dimensional generation space.

Under the additional assumption that the leading band-blind weak vertex identifies aligned up- and down-type mass eigenbases, the interaction is generation diagonal. This aligned benchmark gives

VCKM(0)=I3. \boxed{V_{\rm CKM}^{(0)}=I_3.} VCKM(0)​=I3​.​

The CKM problem isolates a missing piece of the projected master operator:

V2 requires a nontrivial generation-space component of P3D∞P4. \boxed{ \text{V2 requires a nontrivial generation-space component of } P_3D_\infty P_4. } V2 requires a nontrivial generation-space component of P3​D∞​P4​.​

4. Leading-order no-go theorem

Assume the following particular leading ansatz. Generation preservation is an extra interaction assumption, not a consequence of the diagonal mass law:

  1. each occupied quark mass eigenstate is localized on its own coherent mode line;
  2. the scalar contact coefficient is band blind;
  3. the weak polarization intertwiner I34pol\mathcal I_{34}^{\rm pol}I34pol​ acts only on the weak/color polarization factors;
  4. the leading mode contact does not mix distinct occupied generation bands;
  5. no generation-dependent contribution from R34\mathcal R_{34}R34​ is retained;
  6. the kinetic-normalized charged current satisfies the isometry condition in Section 3.

Then on the physical generation space

C34∝∑i=13∣di⟩⟨ui∣, \mathcal C_{34} \propto \sum_{i=1}^3 |d_i\rangle\langle u_i|, C34​∝i=1∑3​∣di​⟩⟨ui​∣,

up to the kinetic metrics and removable phases. Physical generation-dependent vertex strengths cannot simply be declared state normalizations.

After canonical normalization,

C34⟶I3, C_{34}\longrightarrow I_3, C34​⟶I3​,

hence

VCKM(0)=I3. \boxed{V_{\rm CKM}^{(0)}=I_3.} VCKM(0)​=I3​.​

The Jarlskog invariant is then

J(0)=0. \boxed{J^{(0)}=0.} J(0)=0.​

This conditional diagonal, isometric ansatz cannot reproduce inter-generation transitions or CKM CP violation. Without the isometry assumption, it gives a diagonal vertex, not necessarily I3I_3I3​; diagonality still supplies no mixing. Band blindness alone proves neither diagonality nor isometry.

5. Why the mass hierarchy does not determine CKM

The simplex mass laws determine diagonal mass eigenvalues:

Mu=μ4diag⁡(S(3,4),S(20,4),S(72,4)), M_u =\mu_4\operatorname{diag} \left( S(3,4), S(20,4), S(72,4) \right), Mu​=μ4​diag(S(3,4),S(20,4),S(72,4)),

while the down sector has the schematic form

Md=μ3diag⁡(S(1,3),S(4,3),S(16,3)S(17,3)). M_d =\mu_3\operatorname{diag} \left( S(1,3), S(4,3), \sqrt{S(16,3)S(17,3)} \right). Md​=μ3​diag(S(1,3),S(4,3),S(16,3)S(17,3)​).

These spectra fix eigenvalues but not the relative orientation of the up and down eigenbases.

For any unitary matrices UuU_uUu​ and UdU_dUd​,

Mu↦UuMuUu†, M_u \mapsto U_uM_uU_u^\dagger, Mu​↦Uu​Mu​Uu†​,

preserves the eigenvalue spectra. The charged-current mixing matrix is

VCKM=Uu†Ud. V_{\rm CKM}=U_u^\dagger U_d. VCKM​=Uu†​Ud​.

This is the usual up-row/down-column W+W^+W+ convention. The down-row/up-column matrix V−V_-V−​ used for the preceding cross-sector kernel is its adjoint, V−=Ud†UuV_-=U_d^\dagger U_uV−​=Ud†​Uu​; the two must not be given the same matrix label.

Therefore the successful simplex mass hierarchy alone cannot determine CKM. A separate geometric object fixing the relative orientation of the two three-dimensional flavor frames is mathematically necessary.

Any proposal that constructs CKM solely from the six quark masses, with no independently derived cross-sector operator, adds an extra ansatz rather than following from the present V2 mass theorem.

9. Falsification criteria

The CKM sector falsifies or constrains V2 in several clean ways.

Failure A: exact band diagonality

If the kinetic-normalized current in the physical mass bases is diagonal,

Kij=0(i≠j), K_{ij}=0 \qquad(i\neq j), Kij​=0(i=j),

it gives no generation mixing. If it also satisfies the isometry condition, its removable diagonal phases give VCKM=I3V_{\rm CKM}=I_3VCKM​=I3​, which is experimentally false. A diagonal nonisometric vertex is not a unitary CKM matrix at all.

Failure B: real transition matrix only

If every element of the normalized mass-basis charged-current matrix can be made simultaneously real by rephasing the quark states, then

J=0, J=0, J=0,

and V2 fails to reproduce observed quark CP violation.

Failure C: generic order-one mixing

If the normalized off-diagonal elements are generically of the same order as the diagonal ones, the observed hierarchical CKM structure is not reproduced.

Failure D: fitted generation rotation

If an arbitrary U(3)U(3)U(3) rotation must be introduced after the projected operator is calculated, then CKM has not been derived. The model has merely reintroduced the Standard Model mixing parameters.

Success criterion

A successful V2 derivation must calculate a unique or discretely selected KKK from the master projection and recover both the approximate CKM hierarchy and a nonzero rephasing-invariant CP phase.

It does falsify the stronger claim that the already-written leading band-blind weak contact is sufficient to explain quark mixing.

Reality of an unnormalized matrix alone is not a CP test if the other required operators are complex.
