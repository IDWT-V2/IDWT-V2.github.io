Spectral counts in a responsive cavity

Updated September 12, 2026

Summary: A gapped spatial band retains its cumulative count while its projector and coupling change; a reciprocal quasiperiodic phase gives a complete spatial response and nonlinear field evolution that test a low-frequency inertia reduction.

What the integrated count protects

The bounded potential keeps the instantaneous spectral count at fifteen. Unitarity separately preserves the rank of the occupied density matrix. During motion that density need not equal the instantaneous spectral projector. Equating them would discard the response being calculated.

Connection to cavity mode selection

Laser mathematics supplies examples of nonlinear, self-consistent mode selection in spatial cavities. Ge, Chong and Stone develop such equations, including gain saturation and changes in which modes lase. Their gain and threshold conditions are part of the stated laser system. Using that mathematics for IDWT would require the relevant energy transport and confinement to arise from its same field; laser mode selection does not itself give the particle mass law.

The useful connection here is precise. The cumulative count can survive spatial changes, while the actual projector, local compatibility and reciprocal dynamics change. The frequency-resolved cavity response calculates the corresponding spatial and transition weights. The common-size balance states what additional energy and kinetic scaling is needed before this count becomes the proposed mass spectrum.
