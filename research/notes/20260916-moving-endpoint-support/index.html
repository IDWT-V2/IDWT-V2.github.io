Research / Mass and normalization

Moving support, nearby frequency zeros and the simplex branch

Updated September 16, 2026

conditional calculation

A continuously moving supporting pattern is a legitimate alternative to a frozen spatial profile. The question is whether its equations preserve the selected mass-frequency. The completed radial operator gives enough information to make a precise local test.

Two results follow. First, the small wave frequency can be taken to zero by an exceptionally small allowed complex shape deformation. Second, a positive dynamical model of that deformation admits bounded periodic motion, but its frequency is determined by an additional conserved orbit action. The original simplex coefficient becomes a displacement of the orbit center. This model therefore does not select the particle masses.

The first result concerns the complete infinite normal plane at one fixed surface eigenvalue. The second is an explicitly specified local effective action. Neither is a solution of the full curved particle, a prediction of zero particle inertia, or a derivation of an additional interaction field. All mode coordinates describe patterns of the same proposed field. Mass continues to mean the selected particle frequency in equivalent units.

What the calculation establishes

The later periodic endpoint and temporal-frame calculation identifies a different moving possibility. A perpendicular periodic deformation preserves the leading simplex ratios through one common multiplier, with a uniform analytic error bound. Its reciprocal force still has a nonzero static component and higher temporal harmonics. Exact temporal transport can preserve all frequencies in a stated positive inverse action, but its physical connection and supporting energy remain to be derived. These results extend the moving test without turning the flat local action above into a frequency-selecting particle solution.

The completed radial mass polynomial remains a valid conditional spectral result. Its nearby allowed shape zero is now explicit. Positive periodic support is possible in a stated local action, but does not by itself retain that polynomial as a selected spectrum. Ordinary curvature pinning has a quantified energy limitation in the same regime.

The next supporting law must therefore preserve the relevant spatial spectrum under its own motion, with the actual component frame, complete energy and common normalization retained. This is a requirement on the joint same-field action, beyond merely choosing a cavity radius.
