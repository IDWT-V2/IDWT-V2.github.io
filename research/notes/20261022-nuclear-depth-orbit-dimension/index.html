This calculation retains its stated assumptions and action. For the current ten-space-plus-time physical picture, common-frequency portions and later developments, see the new particle-geometry and sector-depths case.

1. Result and Scope

The orbit demonstration uses a literal deterministic electron trajectory through four directions of the surrounding physical space. Its flat candidate law is

The default L=2L=2L=2 example has β=1\beta=1β=1 and k=L2−1=3k=L^2-1=3k=L2−1=3. This is a prescribed restoring and gyroscopic law. It supplies an exact precessing trajectory, but does not derive the attractive interaction between an electron and a nucleus or its directional restriction.

The calculations below establish constant radius and an isotropic second-moment tensor for specified single-centre preparations. They also establish bound motions at every fixed angle in a prescribed two-centre extension. The aligned extension has a free fifth coordinate that must be treated explicitly. The static potential minimum is independent of the angle; the complete potential and mode frequencies are angle-dependent. These results leave open how the coupled field dynamics selects nuclear orientations and the familiar three-dimensional structure of bulk matter.

The electron's position is the deterministic trajectory throughout. A time average of its squared coordinates describes registration along that trajectory, not a probability cloud. Its external motion around nuclei is distinct from its six-dimensional internal classical field structure.

2. Nuclear Depth as a Directional Hypothesis

The maintained down-sector and up-sector assignments are depth three and depth four. One possible nuclear geometry puts their patterns in a common four-direction subspace, with a triple shared by all its quarks. Their depth numbers alone do not establish that alignment.

The force direction does not follow just from the source's dimensional extent. An interaction with a source supported on a subspace can still change when the electron moves transversely to that subspace. For example, an overlap with a localized translated profile can depend on its transverse displacement, giving a transverse derivative of the energy. A projected force must therefore be derived or stated as an additional assumption.

4. The Empirical Far-Field Constraint

An effective IDWT interaction must reproduce the established electrostatic and gravitational force measurements in their applicable source preparations and distance ranges. For example, Lee et al., Physical Review Letters 124, 101101 (2020) tested the gravitational inverse-square law between 52 micrometres and 3.0 millimetres and found agreement with Newtonian gravity. Electrostatic monopole comparisons refer to appropriate charged sources; arbitrary neutral bodies need not have a monopole electrostatic force.

For an isotropic radial field with conserved outward flux in nnn spatial dimensions, the sphere area gives

F(r) area⁡(Sn−1)rn−1=constant. \mathcal F(r)\,\operatorname{area}(S^{n-1})r^{n-1} =\text{constant}. F(r)area(Sn−1)rn−1=constant.

The familiar exponent 1−n1-n1−n follows under those flux, symmetry and constitutive assumptions. Identifying F\mathcal FF with a measured force requires the corresponding response law. An observed inverse-square law does not alone determine the full containing dimension of an anisotropic or constrained IDWT field. A construction in the shared higher-dimensional physical space must derive the effective response that measurements test.

The harmonic restoring force used in this note is not an inverse-square force. Its bound trajectories neither reproduce that empirical law nor constitute a failure of a higher-dimensional theory: they test a separate, explicit candidate for the orbit. The link between the candidate orbit and the measured long-range interaction remains missing.

5. Orientation and Polarization Have Different Assumptions

A pattern's bound directions and its extension through other directions can motivate a geometric model. Its orientation is a continuous spatial subspace, not a selection among named coordinate axes. The space of ddd-planes in Rn\mathbb R^nRn has dimension d(n−d)d(n-d)d(n−d). Changing that orientation is distinct from changing polarization within a fixed subspace.

The working photon picture is a structure spanning source and target with phase accumulating along its extent. It is not a point object travelling along an initially empty line. Its proposed two bound directions supply a useful transverse comparison, but this picture does not by itself derive its polarization constraints or their massive counterparts.

7. What Is Established and What Remains

  • Under the stated preparation and force law, the single-centre trajectory has constant radius and, for the specified nonexceptional ratios, isotropic second moments. Higher moments retain directional structure.
  • Under the projected two-centre law, conserved positive energy gives bound motions at every fixed nonzero relative angle; the aligned free direction has the explicit zero-velocity qualification above.
  • The static potential minimum is angle-independent. The complete potential, normal frequencies and preparation-dependent spread are angle-dependent.
  • Independently oriented fourth axes above a shared triple are a conditional geometric proposal. A reciprocal same-field interaction, nuclear orientation dynamics and the observer response must determine whether it produces the observed bulk geometry and force laws.
