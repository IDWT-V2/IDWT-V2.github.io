Updated September 10, 2026

Summary: The same spatial moment interaction drives reciprocal deformation of complete complex Dirac profiles in an exact CP3–CP5 symmetry class, including a generated two-form component and phase-dependent changes in interaction overlap.

The physical comparison and its scope

All spatial directions remain ordinary macroscopic dimensions of the same manifold. The projective topology, common metric scale, spin twists and current-family decomposition are explicit assumptions; no compactification is introduced.

Take the lower spinor bundle to be Λ0,∗⊗O(L)\Lambda^{0,*}\otimes\mathcal O(L)Λ0,∗⊗O(L) and the higher one Λ0,∗⊗O(K)\Lambda^{0,*}\otimes\mathcal O(K)Λ0,∗⊗O(K), with their FS/Chern Dirac operators. This calculation tests an aligned-center spatial symmetry class while retaining its complete complex field profiles. It is not a generic relative-orientation calculation or a replacement for the evolving electron–nucleus orbit. Its purpose is to determine whether the stated common kernel can actually change both spatial patterns under one action.

What has been established

The common kernel can drive reciprocal changes in complete spatial profiles within this exact symmetry class, with conserved norms and energy. Both signs of changing scalar overlap occur without an imposed steering rule. This is a spatial Dirac counterpart to the finite coherent-mode deformation example, with its own action and different constraints.

The aligned embedding and profile symmetry remain restrictions. Stable occupied particles, arbitrary relative orientations, phase-sensitive cross-current terms from the master field, the actual electron orbit, and observed electromagnetic and weak vertices have not been derived. The calculation supplies a reproducible full-equation test for a common kernel, rather than a depth-only interaction coefficient.
