Boundaries needed for the next spatial calculation

The uniform-polarization restriction is substantive. Allowing z(x) to vary changes the nonlocal functional and may permit lower-energy textures. The subsequent frozen magnetic texture test now provides explicit lower-energy textures in predeclared stronger-coupling cases, while the inherited weak benchmark survives the limited family tested. The original uniform-sector square remains valid; it must not be extended to unrestricted spatial-polarization stability. The square above does not prove a global minimum over all spinor fields. Likewise n(x) was fixed: the magnetic attraction alone does not prove a stable localized profile, and the orbital/electric/kinetic terms must be included before testing localization or collapse.

This model concerns magnetization and its field on the assumed local six-dimensional space. It does not by itself identify polarization rays with the six spatial coordinates. The full sector reduction and spatial observable map remain distinct from the established collective CP3 result.

The new next step is well specified: evaluate the constrained kernel with the actual electron/nuclear profile and the remaining kinetic and current terms. Replacing its projector by the identity would discard precisely the field equation that this bridge has now supplied.
