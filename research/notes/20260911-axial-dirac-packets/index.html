An ordinary spatial family with explicit angular dependence

The full local equation closes exactly

The scalar is independent of transverse angle, but retains the complete complex interference:

Verification and limits

axial_scalar_checks.py reconstructs the complete Cartesian spinor and differentiates it in every ordinary direction at D=3,5,7,9D=3,5,7,9D=3,5,7,9. It checks (6)–(8), physical rotation covariance, the normalized radial basis and its independent derivatives, full complex source variation, exact quartic quadrature, conserved norm, axial momentum and the matrix (11).

Independent full matrix exponentials verify the free propagator and complete positive-frequency projection implemented by axial_scalar_packets.py. The diagnostic runs 192 checks, including full nonlinear evolution of complex spatial textures and a five-dimensional localized comparison with both zero and nonzero scalar sources. Spatial, radial and temporal refinement remain necessary for any particular collision result.

The family provides transverse localization and several spatial polarizations with a tractable full-source equation. It supplies no bound-particle depth assignment or electric and weak channel embedding. With dimensionless QQQ and ℏ=c=1\hbar=c=1ℏ=c=1, [g]=lengthD−1[g]=\mathrm{length}^{D-1}[g]=lengthD−1. Choosing equal numerical ggg values at different DDD does not by itself define one physically normalized interaction across nested sectors.

Changing compatibility of localized spatial packets uses this full angular family to compare both outgoing complex patterns after removing their known free evolution. A precisely specified future encounter has either increased or decreased scalar contact for different five-dimensional preparations under the same local dynamics.

A separate spherical vector family works in both odd and even dimensions, including six and ten. Its full radial polarization vectors retain tangential current while scalar density is radial. It has one radial center and does not extend the present axial encounter ansatz to a general even-dimensional collision.
