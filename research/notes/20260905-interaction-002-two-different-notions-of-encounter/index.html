2. Two different notions of encounter

Particles can interact through their extended field tails while their centres remain separated. The changes in mutual influence must propagate no faster than light over the separation. A local product of fields samples their values at the same spatial point; it does not require the centres of the two patterns to coincide.

The pair must retain each pattern's spatial profile, its multiple orientations and their relative arrangement, phase and response. The centre is a position associated with that pattern, not a complete description of its interaction.

Within the ten-dimensional matter domain, a three-dimensional observer leaves seven spatial directions unresolved. Infinitely many additional directions are unbound for every particle. These counts do not imply independently randomized offsets or a power-law encounter penalty. A particle's bound depth, the directions through which its tails extend, and the coordinates an apparatus resolves are different data.

The physical calculation is the reciprocal evolution of both complete patterns under the same field law. A reduced projection is usable when derived from that law for the specified preparation and observable.
