2. Two different notions of encounter

A crucial distinction is required.

2.1 Full-depth direct encounter

For two particles of the same depth ddd, let their relative coordinate be

ΔXd∈Qd. \Delta X_d\in Q_d. ΔXd​∈Qd​.

A genuinely local same-depth interaction samples the full relative geometry. In a locally Euclidean model,

Rd2=∑i=1d(Δxi)2. R_d^2=\sum_{i=1}^d(\Delta x_i)^2. Rd2​=i=1∑d​(Δxi​)2.

If the interaction is central,

V=V(Rd), V=V(R_d), V=V(Rd​),

then

Fi=−dVdRdΔxiRd. F_i=-\frac{dV}{dR_d}\frac{\Delta x_i}{R_d}. Fi​=−dRd​dV​Rd​Δxi​​.

Thus all nonzero components of the one separation vector evolve together. The uncontrolled coordinates are not automatically independent random variables.

The correct probabilistic object for an observer resolving only a subspace is therefore

P(ΔX⊥∣ΔXobs), P(\Delta X_\perp\mid\Delta X_{\rm obs}), P(ΔX⊥​∣ΔXobs​),

not the unconditional P(ΔX⊥)P(\Delta X_\perp)P(ΔX⊥​).

A correlation law such as

E ⁣(∣ΔX⊥∣2∣r)=αr2 \mathbb E\!\left(|\Delta X_\perp|^2\mid r\right) =\alpha r^2 E(∣ΔX⊥​∣2∣r)=αr2

would imply

E(Rd2∣r)=(1+α)r2, \mathbb E(R_d^2\mid r)=(1+\alpha)r^2, E(Rd2​∣r)=(1+α)r2,

so full coincidence follows as the visible separation r→0r\to0r→0.

2.2 Interaction through a lower-depth carrier

The current V2 cross-depth theorem gives a different mechanism. Let two matter sectors have depths bbb and ccc, and suppose an interaction carrier or shared interaction base has depth

a≤min⁡(b,c). a\leq\min(b,c). a≤min(b,c).

Push both higher-depth currents to the common base:

Jˉb→a=(πba)∗Jb,Jˉc→a=(πca)∗Jc. \bar J_{b\to a}=(\pi_{ba})_*J_b, \qquad \bar J_{c\to a}=(\pi_{ca})_*J_c. Jˉb→a​=(πba​)∗​Jb​,Jˉc→a​=(πca​)∗​Jc​.

Then a carrier-mediated interaction can have the form

Ibc(a)=∫Qa⟨Jˉb→a(x),KaJˉc→a(x)⟩ dμa(x). \boxed{ I^{(a)}_{bc} = \int_{Q_a} \left\langle \bar J_{b\to a}(x), K_a\bar J_{c\to a}(x) \right\rangle \,d\mu_a(x). } Ibc(a)​=∫Qa​​⟨Jˉb→a​(x),Ka​Jˉc→a​(x)⟩dμa​(x).​

For normalized nonnegative densities, complete marginalization preserves total weight:

∫Qaρˉb→a=1,∫Qaρˉc→a=1. \int_{Q_a}\bar\rho_{b\to a}=1, \qquad \int_{Q_a}\bar\rho_{c\to a}=1. ∫Qa​​ρˉ​b→a​=1,∫Qa​​ρˉ​c→a​=1.

Therefore the b−ab-ab−a and c−ac-ac−a extra coordinates do not generate a naive coincidence penalty of the form

(a/L)(b−a)+(c−a). (a/L)^{(b-a)+(c-a)}. (a/L)(b−a)+(c−a).

They have already been integrated out of the interaction channel.
