Energy constraints from scalar response

Updated September 12, 2026

Summary: The same scalar response fixes the energy-frequency defect, the curvature of energy with canonical norm, and the sensitivity to an interaction change, giving joint inverse constraints on mass ratios and coupling without selecting the physical particle norm.

A common mass scale can survive an energy-frequency difference

The inverse scalar-law construction tests whether complete spatial fields can obey one causal local interaction. Its frequency target needs a further check: the same action determines total energy, and stationary frequency is generally an energy derivative rather than energy per unit norm.

That distinction does not by itself spoil the mass relationships. A common conversion factor can preserve their ratios. The question is whether the factor is common on the actual particle branches after their shapes respond. This note derives tests for that question and connects them to the scalar coupling derivative already present in the full field equation.

Use a fixed physical spatial geometry, measure and canonical time-kinetic normalization. Let the self-adjoint spatial Dirac operator be D\mathscr DD, with β†=β\beta^\dagger=\betaβ†=β and β2=I\beta^2=Iβ2=I. The candidate action has energy and conserved canonical norm

where UUU is real, U(0)=0U(0)=0U(0)=0, and F=U′F=U'F=U′. Its equation is

Only the sectors are nested; the two particle fields need not be restrictions of each other.
