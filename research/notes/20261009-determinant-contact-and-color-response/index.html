Geometric Color Channels, Pair Response and Charge Consistency

Updated October 9, 2026

The middle-Hodge construction gives a complete three-component channel only at depths three and four. Combining that channel with its volume tensor gives a pair operator with the standard color decomposition. Combining its rank with the stated chiral weak field content gives charge fractions and an additional parity test.

These calculations use equivalent relative modes of the same spatial field. The frame and spin factors must have the meaning already specified in the Hodge construction. A three-component spatial spin-one representation alone does not give an independent color symmetry.

1. One Geometric Rank Enters Several Calculations

For d=2m−1d=2m-1d=2m−1 or 2m2m2m, the complete middle channel has complex rank

km=(2m−1m−1). k_m=\binom{2m-1}{m-1}. km​=(m−12m−1​).

The nontrivial rank-three case is exactly m=2m=2m=2. With a Hermitian metric and parallel complex volume, the maximal mixing group is SU(3)SU(3)SU(3). The complete relative-mode space then has eight traceless generators, fundamental Casimir 4/34/34/3 and a scalar alternating trilinear amplitude. These are consequences of the same carrier, conditional on realizing that unitary mixing as a physical response.

6. What Has Been Connected

One candidate rank now controls the complete color carrier, its eight generators, a volume-to-pair interaction, conditional electric fractions and a global weak-consistency test. The same-field vertex and occupation law remain explicit physical inputs to derive. The calculation gives several linked restrictions on that completion rather than independent tunable assignments.

The finite-sawtooth extension adds an explicit temporal source under a separable preparation. Its positive pair overlap multiplies I−SI-\mathsf SI−S, preserving the symmetric kernel and antisymmetric channel while making the scalar response depend on relative phase. The higher-rank three-form contraction still carries k−2k-2k−2; temporal weighting alone supplies no additional exclusion of higher-sector forces.

The common checker contracts volume tensors explicitly through rank six, checks the three-form extension through rank eight, verifies SU(3)SU(3)SU(3) generator and pair identities, and tests charge polynomials and geometric parity across depths 2 through 16.
