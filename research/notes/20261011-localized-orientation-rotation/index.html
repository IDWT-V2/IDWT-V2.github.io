Localized Rotation and Constrained Stability in the Orientation Law

Updated October 13, 2026

Result and Added Structure

One explicit extension of the determinant orientation action supports nonconstant stationary rotating radial profiles at every occupied nonempty normal carrier. The common finite calculation covers ordinary spatial depths two, three, four, five and six, with depth ten recorded as an empty carrier. It tests full spatial localization instead of extending a two-dimensional defect uniformly along additional directions.

The extension adds a smooth bounded potential and a selected ordinary normal rotation generator H. They are constitutive premises, whose same-field origin remains to be derived. No coefficient is retuned between depths. The profiles are solutions of an orientation-field candidate; they are not identified observed particles, complete Dirac spinors, or a derived simplex frequency family.

Boundary and Residual Verification

The script first brackets regular shooting data, then solves the complete boundary problem by collocation. At the outer radius it uses the exact linear decaying radial-tail Robin coefficient sqrt(m squared−omega squared) times K_(d/2) divided by K_(d/2−1). It compares radius 80 at collocation tolerance 1e−8 with radius 110 at tolerance 2e−10, recomputing energy, charge, stresses and the center. This does not replace a continuum existence proof; it provides a bounded, residual-controlled numerical construction with analytic exterior behavior.

The refined calculation additionally solves the linear branch-sensitivity equation, including the frequency derivative of the tail boundary condition. Independent derivatives of the complete energy and charge obey dE/domega=omega dQ/domega. This tests the normalization and continuation without fitting energy to a desired frequency.

Stability Test Rather Than Principal Causality Alone

The derivative-free potential leaves the determinant principal cone unchanged. For this radial ansatz its radial speed squared is B/A and every tangential speed squared is B. All target polarizations share that cone and stay causal throughout the tested profiles.

A translation mode F' belongs to the spatial angular-momentum-one amplitude operator and has eigenvalue zero. Since the observed radial profiles are monotone, this mode has no interior node. Single-Field and Frequency Limits

These radial states have no spatial orientation winding. A local normalized spinor lift (cos(F/2),sin(F/2) exp(i omega t)) tends to a constant at spatial infinity and has finite spatial and temporal gradient integrals. It avoids the phase-energy obstruction of the earlier degree-one defect. Nevertheless adding the lift's phase and amplitude kinetics changes the J equation and its supporting stress. The J-only action is not thereby a complete spinor action.

The additional fixed normal axis, restoring potential, continuous conserved rotation charge and continuous frequency window remain explicit. The calculation does not give the occupied simplex harmonics, their shared sector units, spin statistics, physical source forces, or the depth-ten charged-lepton branch. It supplies a geometrically consistent localized orientation construction and tests its complete inertia and constrained stability, without treating those unresolved mechanisms as already derived.

The fixed H also reduces the target's global rotation symmetry. Its polarization gaps cannot simultaneously be presented as unbroken SU3 color dynamics. Their value is a conditional cross-sector comparison, not evidence that the chosen axis or restoring law is physically selected.

Context and Reproduction

Rotation-supported localization is an established Q-ball mechanism. Kuniyasu, Sakai and Shiraishi, Q-balls in DBI type k field theory (2016), study a square root of a kinetic trace with polynomial amplitude potentials. That kinetic law differs from the full multifield determinant here. Its existence and stability results are not imported. The contribution of this calculation is the explicit determinant construction on the retained normal-orientation geometry, with its complete transverse forms and common occupied-sector comparison.

The single reproducible script and recorded report contain 47 exact checks and ten localized numerical cases, plus the empty depth-ten control. The exact checks cover the radial equation, complete energy and stress, every vacuum target weight, normalized-ray transverse jets and actual quaternionic curvature. Run with system Python, SymPy, NumPy and SciPy available. A new timestamped report is written beside the executed source, retaining its SHA-256. Boundary refinement, branch sensitivity and both spectral meshes are run in one execution under common inputs.
