Phase-Complete Coefficient Rotation and Polarization Positivity

Updated October 13, 2026

This construction retains the complete phase of one normal-spinor-valued coefficient field. It is a positive second-order wave model on field values, not a Lorentz Dirac kinetic law. Its fixed norm, selected normal generator and restoring potential are constitutive hypotheses. It tests localization and polarization without claiming observed fermionic spin, statistics or the simplex frequency law.

Field and Common Action

Let the even normal rank be 2q and use the full positive-chiral normal Spin carrier S_plus=Lambda_even(Cq), with its half-determinant Spin shift. Its complex dimension is 2^(q−1) for q positive. Write the coefficient field as Psi=R0 U with U dagger U=1 and unknown constant R0. Fixing the norm is an added target constraint, not an amplitude measurement. The constant R0 enters the kinetic coefficients and is not fitted independently for individual particles.

The ordinary normal generator used in the orientation calculation acts as i(N1+N2−1)/2 on this carrier. Combine it with the common phase generator i/2 to define iG=i(N1+N2)/2. This leaves the vacuum coefficient stationary and gives Hermitian G eigenvalues 0, 1/2 and 1. The phase combination is an explicit additional choice; it is not the unchanged Spin generator or a derived electric charge.

Take T,lambda,R0,m squared positive and 0<b<m squared. The potential is smooth and nonnegative everywhere on the full coefficient sphere. The complete determinant cone and positive energy proof applies to its full induced Riemannian metric. Target coordinates still describe field values, not additional physical spatial dimensions.

The full coefficient sphere includes the phase omitted by a projective ray. At q3 its seven real directions are exactly the six orientation directions plus one phase. At q4 its fifteen real directions also include the two nonpure ray directions; purity is not imposed in this comparison. At q2 it is a three-dimensional coefficient target. These dimensions are not physical spatial depths.

Radial Branch and Changed Equations

Choose U=(cos F(r),sin F(r) exp(i omega t),0,...), where the first coefficient has G weight zero and the second has weight one. This real three-coordinate sphere is a totally geodesic subtarget. The potential's transverse first derivatives vanish there. Its induced metric is dF squared+sin squared F d alpha squared. Consequently A=1+beta F-prime squared, B=1−beta omega squared sin squared F and the complete radial equations, energy and stress have the same functional form as the orientation calculation, now with beta=16 lambda R0 squared.

This is a change of field and potential, not an inheritance of the old J-only solution. The projected root orientation angle is 2F, and its metric alone would have angular coefficient sin squared F cos squared F. The retained phase contributes the additional sin to the fourth F. The chosen full-coefficient potential uses the full Killing norm z=sin squared F; the old projective Killing variance is sin squared(2F). They agree in their small-field mass after their canonical factors are retained, but differ nonlinearly.

The full U tends to the constant vacuum at spatial infinity. Both its phase kinetic energy and spatial gradient energy are finite for an exponentially decaying F. Its first coefficient is static, so its separate phase current vanishes; the rotating second coefficient carries the conserved G charge. This supplies a complete coefficient-field ansatz with no missing projective phase energy. The physical amplitude remains constrained, and a finite-stiffness amplitude completion would require another equation.

A Common Positivity Identity

The full radial equation gives L0 h0=0 and L1 h1=0, where La=−div(Cr radial derivative)−Ct angular Laplacian+Wa. Both functions are strictly positive at finite radius.
