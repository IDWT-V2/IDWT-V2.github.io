Local contact instability on proposed CP3

Updated September 10, 2026

Summary: Every positive-degree coherent branch of the specified massless local scalar Dirac action on physical CP3 has a small-coupling oscillatory instability in its complete radial Dirac complement.

Precise statement

Use the metric, twist, normalization and full spatial equation of the specified CP3 scalar branch. The physical space is CP3, the bare mass is zero, and the interaction is local ε(φ†βφ)βφ\varepsilon(\varphi^\dagger\beta\varphi)\beta\varphiε(φ†βφ)βφ. For each fixed integer L≥1L\ge1L≥1, its branch through the coherent holomorphic section is spectrally unstable for every sufficiently small nonzero ε\varepsilonε.

The proof retains both Dirac signs and conjugate variations. The unstable mode belongs to an exact invariant class of spatial spinors, so it is also an instability of the full specified field equation. The statement does not extend automatically to other kernels, additional field terms, other branches or arbitrary coupling strengths. It does not make a particle–antiparticle identification from Dirac signs.

Why this is not an artifact of a finite cutoff

In the complete radial class, the unperturbed frequencies λn=2n(n+L+3)\lambda_n=2\sqrt{n(n+L+3)}λn​=2n(n+L+3)​ are discrete and separated. The cluster at +λ1+\lambda_1+λ1​ has dimension two in the doubled equation (1). The smooth background on the specified global CP3 geometry makes the perturbation a bounded analytic operator on the same Dirac domain. Its Riesz projection is therefore an analytic finite-dimensional cluster, with first-order matrix exactly (4).

The two nonreal eigenvalues supplied by (8) are distinct at first order. They persist for sufficiently small nonzero coupling; higher orders cannot cancel their leading imaginary parts. This proves the spectral statement for the complete symmetry class. The class is invariant under the full spatial equation, so these eigenmodes are valid spatial perturbations even when all other angular and spinor components are admitted.

No convergence of the largest growth rate over all modes is needed for this proof. One unstable isolated pair is sufficient. Numerical near-zero roots associated with the phase Jordan block are also not the modes used in (8)–(10).

Relation to the projected coherent calculation

Restricting the action to holomorphic zero forms gives the smooth quartic with coefficient DL2/D2LD_L^2/D_{2L}DL2​/D2L​. Its finite holomorphic coherent set is orbitally stable within that finite action, taking its projective complex dimension to be three here. The missing radial one-form partners create the instability just proved.

The comparison therefore distinguishes a stable projected pattern from a stable solution of the spatial Dirac equation. The smooth moment-kernel calculation changes the actual scalar interaction and tests which part of this resonance survives; it does not reinterpret the local-contact result.
