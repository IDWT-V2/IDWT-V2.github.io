Inheriting the projective mass candidate

Updated September 11, 2026

Summary: The normal bundle supplies the connection needed by the CP3 simplex candidate, but complete form degrees and normal Dirac derivatives prevent an automatic common mass construction at depths six and ten.

The required connection is present

The local simplex polynomial on physical CP3 uses the bundle

S3(2)=Λ0,∗T∗CP3⊗O(2),M6=X(X+4)(X+6)720,X=R2D224.(1) \mathcal S_3(2)=\Lambda^{0,*}T^*\mathbb{CP}^3\otimes\mathcal O(2), \qquad \mathcal M_6=\frac{X(X+4)(X+6)}{720}, \qquad X=\frac{R^2\mathscr D_2^2}{4}. \tag{1} S3​(2)=Λ0,∗T∗CP3⊗O(2),M6​=720X(X+4)(X+6)​,X=4R2D22​​.(1)

Its paired zero-form and one-form family has exactly the coefficient S(n,6)S(n,6)S(n,6). A natural question is whether the line degree two can come from the spinor bundle on the containing ten-dimensional sector.

For the specified linear inclusion CP3⊂CP5\mathbb{CP}^3\subset\mathbb{CP}^5CP3⊂CP5, the answer is yes at the level of bundles with connection: it occurs as the top normal exterior component of the canonical ambient Spinc^cc bundle. This result does not select that component under the full spatial equation.

The inclusion relates sectors, not individual particles. It does not place an electron inside a tau or require an electron solution to be the restriction of a tau solution. The simultaneous direct inheritance of their mass families tested below is an additional construction, not a requirement of sector nesting. Failure of that construction therefore does not invalidate the sector-nesting premise or exclude other interactions between independently occupied particle states.

Both projective spaces here are conditional actual physical spatial geometries, with a common induced FS scale RRR. No spatial direction is compactified. Tangent and normal directions are defined relative to the lower submanifold; they are equivalent kinds of spatial direction. The intermediate-depth obstruction also applies: this particular global inclusion cannot provide all the intervening spatial depths. It remains a conditional direct nesting test.

A simultaneous unshifted spectrum is not inherited directly

The polynomial classification in the simplex note gives a necessary and sufficient condition on the r,r+1r,r+1r,r+1 paired form family:

L=m−1+r(5) L=m-1+r \tag{5} L=m−1+r(5)

for a polynomial in R2DL2/4R^2\mathscr D_L^2/4R2DL2​/4 to have exactly S(n,2m)S(n,2m)S(n,2m) with the same unshifted spectral integer nnn. All form families, rather than just the first, are included in (5).

Suppose a full lower paired family comes from a fixed normal degree sss of a higher paired family by the direct restriction (4). The line connections and the two form degrees must then satisfy

La=Lb+s,ra+s=rb.(6) L_a=L_b+s,\qquad r_a+s=r_b. \tag{6} La​=Lb​+s,ra​+s=rb​.(6)

Demanding (5) at both depths gives

a−1+ra=b−1+rb+s⟹2s=−(b−a)=−q.(7) a-1+r_a=b-1+r_b+s \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \boxed{2s=-(b-a)=-q.} \tag{7} a−1+ra​=b−1+rb​+s⟹2s=−(b−a)=−q.​(7)

Since q>0q>0q>0 and s≥0s\ge0s≥0, this is impossible. The result is algebraic for all depths under the stated direct connection and full-pair inheritance, not a finite scan of mode labels.

At depths six and ten the degree check is even more explicit. The lower allowed line degrees are 2+ra2+r_a2+ra​, with ra=0,1,2r_a=0,1,2ra​=0,1,2; the higher ones are 4+rb4+r_b4+rb​, with rb=0,…,4r_b=0,\ldots,4rb​=0,…,4. Equation La=Lb+sL_a=L_b+sLa​=Lb​+s has only one permitted match:

(ra,rb,s)=(2,0,0).(8) (r_a,r_b,s)=(2,0,0). \tag{8} (ra​,rb​,s)=(2,0,0).(8)

Its lower form degrees are 2,32,32,3, whereas the higher family has degrees 0,10,10,1. The direct component map is zero even before imposing the coexact and exact spatial conditions. A scalar identity restriction cannot bridge this mismatch.

This result tests one proposed geometric origin for the new polynomial. It does not rule out a common master field with different spatial responses, a derived interaction changing form channels, or different geometric data. It also does not identify the global ambient Dirac operator with its tangential part: ambient eigenfields still have normal derivatives.

Allowing mathematical index offsets changes (5), but supplies an additional mode identification. For complete pairs satisfying (6), the offsets obey

Normal derivatives do not preserve one normal degree

The standard inclusion is totally geodesic, so its tangential Dirac operator has precisely the connection decomposition (4). The full ambient operator additionally differentiates in all 2q2q2q ordinary normal spatial directions.

Use Hermitian spatial Clifford matrices α\alphaα with {αi,αj}=2δijI\{\alpha_i,\alpha_j\}=2\delta_{ij}I{αi​,αj​}=2δij​I. In a local adapted frame the complete graded tensor product is

α10,i=α6,i⊗I4,α10,6+ν=β6⊗α4,ν,β10=β6⊗β4.(10) \alpha_{10,i}=\alpha_{6,i}\otimes I_4,\qquad \alpha_{10,6+\nu}=\beta_6\otimes\alpha_{4,\nu}, \qquad \beta_{10}=\beta_6\otimes\beta_4. \tag{10} α10,i​=α6,i​⊗I4​,α10,6+ν​=β6​⊗α4,ν​,β10​=β6​⊗β4​.(10)

Let Πs\Pi_sΠs​ project onto normal exterior degree sss. Normal Clifford multiplication creates or removes one exterior factor. For a real normal covector ppp, it follows that

Πsα4(p)Πs=0,Πsα4(p)(I−Πs)α4(p)Πs=∣p∣2Πs.(11) \Pi_s\alpha_4(p)\Pi_s=0,\qquad \boxed{ \Pi_s\alpha_4(p)(I-\Pi_s)\alpha_4(p)\Pi_s =|p|^2\Pi_s. } \tag{11} Πs​α4​(p)Πs​=0,Πs​α4​(p)(I−Πs​)α4​(p)Πs​=∣p∣2Πs​.​(11)

Thus a nonzero normal plane-wave derivative of a field in the selected grade drives complementary components with the full derivative norm. This holds for every complex spinor polarization and every normal orientation. The projectors are covariant under common unitary normal frame rotations; changing that frame does not remove (11).

In particular, the s=2s=2s=2 summand that supplies La=2L_a=2La​=2 is preserved by the tangential connection but is not an invariant sector of the unrestricted ambient Dirac equation. The nonzero commutator with normal Clifford matrices occurs in its principal derivative part. Connection terms cannot cancel it for arbitrary normal derivatives.

Special differential constraints can make several normal derivatives cancel. In the full rank-eight top component at one point, the four independent complex normal first derivatives have 323232 complex entries; their Dirac source map has rank 161616 and a 161616-dimensional kernel. Equation (11) is therefore not a theorem excluding every ambient extension. Such a constrained extension still needs its equation, norm and physical selection.

Eliminate the complete local normal coupling

A local calculation makes the missing normal dynamics concrete. Freeze the coefficients at one physical point and retain the full scalar Dirac symbol

H=α10(pt,pn)+Mβ10.(12) H=\alpha_{10}(p_t,p_n)+M\beta_{10}. \tag{12} H=α10​(pt​,pn​)+Mβ10​.(12)

Here MMM is a fixed real scalar coefficient. Freezing it tests the local linear operator; it does not solve a nonlinear scalar-current background or the global projective field. Retain the eight top-normal-degree components and eliminate all other 242424 components at a frequency ω\omegaω away from their poles. The retained diagonal block is

A=α6(pt)+Mβ6,A2=(∣pt∣2+M2)I8.(13) A=\alpha_6(p_t)+M\beta_6,\qquad A^2=(|p_t|^2+M^2)I_8. \tag{13} A=α6​(pt​)+Mβ6​,A2=(∣pt​∣2+M2)I8​.(13)

Normal Clifford multiplication maps the retained component isometrically into a middle-degree subspace. After conjugation by that map, the complementary diagonal action is −A-A−A. The exact Schur contribution is therefore

Σ(ω)=∣pn∣2(ω+A)−1,K(ω)=ω−A−Σ(ω).(14) \boxed{ \Sigma(\omega)=|p_n|^2(\omega+A)^{-1},\qquad K(\omega)=\omega-A-\Sigma(\omega). } \tag{14} Σ(ω)=∣pn​∣2(ω+A)−1,K(ω)=ω−A−Σ(ω).​(14)

This comes from varying the full real quadratic action with respect to every complex complementary amplitude. The eliminated amplitudes are components of the same spinor field, with no new mediator field.

The same reduction determines the norm. For a normalized retained eigenvector of AAA with eigenvalue aaa, the full field has squared norm

Za=1+∣pn∣2(ω+a)2,∂ωK=I8+∣pn∣2(ω+A)−2.(15) \boxed{ Z_a=1+\frac{|p_n|^2}{(\omega+a)^2}, \qquad \partial_\omega K=I_8+|p_n|^2(\omega+A)^{-2}. } \tag{15} Za​=1+(ω+a)2∣pn​∣2​,∂ω​K=I8​+∣pn​∣2(ω+A)−2.​(15)

The positive additional term is the norm in the eliminated components. It cannot be omitted when interpreting the reduced action. The complete dispersion relation is

ω2=∣pt∣2+∣pn∣2+M2.(16) \boxed{\omega^2=|p_t|^2+|p_n|^2+M^2.} \tag{16} ω2=∣pt​∣2+∣pn​∣2+M2.​(16)

Both signs have multiplicity sixteen in the original rank-thirty-two symbol. At these frequencies the discarded block itself has a decoupled eigenspace, so its unrestricted inverse is singular. The contraction in (14) has a removable pole there: the retained source is orthogonal to that eigenspace. Solving in the sourced subspace, or using the minimum-norm complementary solution, gives (15) and reconstructs full eigenvectors. The independent decoupled eigenvectors remain in the full spectrum.

Normal motion contributes to the frequency and the reduced norm; this local elimination does not produce the polynomial (1). Curvature, spatial variation and a normal binding law would require a further global calculation.

The normal extension analysis demonstrates the same need to derive the kinetic norm in an actual spatial profile problem. The nonlinear reduction also retains the complementary scalar response when the background depends on the field itself.
