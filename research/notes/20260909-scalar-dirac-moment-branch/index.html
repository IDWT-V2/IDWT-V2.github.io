Self-consistent scalar Dirac moment modes

Updated September 9, 2026

Summary: The scalar moment kernel admits self-consistent radial Dirac modes with a calculable source and virial law, while the complete conjugate response detects an instability in several massless-background branches.

A spatial profile derived from the existing source term

A coupling vertex needs actual spatial field profiles. This note applies the nonlocal scalar moment interaction to a full first-order Dirac field, retaining both radial components and the change in its own source.

The model is a conditional flat spatial calculation on Rd\mathbb R^dRd within the one nested macroscopic manifold. It does not replace a particle's proposed global spatial geometry or the actual Coulomb-plus-precession electron orbit. The computed modes are stationary solutions of the stated model; their existence alone does not establish stable physical particles.

The complete radial fluctuation problem

The source changes when the spatial field changes. On the fixed-norm tangent space, ϕTx=0\phi^Tx=0ϕTx=0; quotienting the phase direction removes the associated zero-mode numerical ambiguity. It does not remove growing physical radial perturbations.

For the m=0m=0m=0, a=1a=1a=1 branch, converged radial growth rates include 1.0284044471.0284044471.028404447 at depth three, 1.0270651721.0270651721.027065172 at four, 0.9542813030.9542813030.954281303 at five, and 0.8161715050.8161715050.816171505 at six, in the model's time units. These are finite nonzero rates, not small numerical real parts of a phase zero mode. Direct nonlinear evolution of the depth-five field reproduces the growing linearized perturbation, with the remainder decreasing when the initial perturbation is reduced. Norm and the full nonlinear energy remain conserved during that growth.

Consequently these massless-background branches cannot be identified as stable particles on the strength of their stationary profiles. They remain well-defined source and response calculations for the stated model. A radial calculation alone also does not establish stability against every spatial angular perturbation, even where it detects no radial growth.

Polarized Dirac shape response calculates the quadrupole motions of these same profiles. Translational Dirac response retains their moving center and derives its zero mode and inertial response.

Self-consistent nested Dirac pair lets both a lower and a containing spatial field determine their profiles through one common scalar coefficient.

Radial dynamics of two-frequency fields embeds this entire radial equation into the full opposite-frequency spatial family, retaining its source, kinetic weight and any radial instability.

Verification

spatial_dirac_mode_checks.py checks the basis and kinetic matrices by independent Gauss–Laguerre quadrature, compares positive frequencies at several basis sizes and widths, and substitutes the reconstructed functions directly into both equations (6). It checks self-consistency, the dilation identity, the distinction between total energy and frequency, and nonlinear perturbation evolution.

As an independent spectral control, the same radial method is tested with equal scalar and vector quadratic backgrounds. Eliminating the lower component there gives the analytic equation (E−m)2(E+m)=2a(4n+d)2(E-m)^2(E+m)=2a(4n+d)^2(E−m)2(E+m)=2a(4n+d)2 for radial level nnn. This control belongs to the solvable class described by Ginocchio; it tests the discretization and is not substituted for the scalar source in (3).
