Rotation-neutral spatial modes

Updated September 9, 2026

Summary: A common rotation of two spatial planes has an exact neutral spinor subspace, while three equal planes have none; full spatial motion and dynamical selection determine whether this can become a particle coupling rule.

A concrete cancellation between spatial polarizations

An interaction can vanish because a spatial pattern is unchanged by the particular geometric operation to which it couples. This is stronger than zero overlap of two unperturbed sources. The calculation below gives an explicit example involving two oppositely polarized rotation planes.

All coordinates are ordinary macroscopic spatial coordinates in the one nested manifold. Spinor components describe fields on those coordinates. The calculation uses a flat spatial chart and a specified rotation probe; it does not identify a spinor parameter space with the particle's spatial submanifold. Nor does it identify the probe with electromagnetism in advance.

The construction first works separately at each containing depth. Combining these probes into one action on the entire nested chain requires compatible embeddings and their variations. A common formula for several local probes is not by itself one globally defined conserved charge.

Rotations of nested spatial sectors derives the fixed-chain constraint and the moving-embedding terms required to pursue this candidate through the whole hierarchy.

Five-dimensional cancellation as an operator identity

A geometric origin for the two equal plane rates

The equal-plane source and its surviving complement are both fixed by the same six-dimensional geometry. Which component the photon current samples, and whether the mixed component acts directly or through other spatial modes, remain dynamical questions.

Spatial transport of a neutral doublet derives another response directly from this projector: variation of its physical null axis produces a nonzero traceless geometric curvature and a fixed positive contribution to the spatial kinetic energy.

Which higher depths guarantee this cancellation

The equal-rate guarantee in (6a) uses a codimension-one hyperplane. For a lower spatial subspace of general codimension, write an orthogonal complex structure on the containing space as

J=(BC−CTD),B2=−I+CCT.(6e) J=\begin{pmatrix}B&C\\-C^T&D\end{pmatrix},\qquad \boxed{B^2=-I+CC^T.} \tag{6e} J=(B−CT​CD​),B2=−I+CCT.​(6e)

When neutrality survives dynamics

F[U(θ)Φ]=U(θ)F[Φ].(9) \mathcal F[U(\theta)\Phi]=U(\theta)\mathcal F[\Phi]. \tag{9} F[U(θ)Φ]=U(θ)F[Φ].(9)

Other couplings and imperfect cancellation

An operator R=−iγ1γ5R=-i\gamma_1\gamma_5R=−iγ1​γ5​ acts nontrivially on the neutral example above: ∥Rz∥=1\|Rz\|=1∥Rz∥=1 and Q5Rz≠0Q_5Rz\ne0Q5​Rz=0. Thus neutrality under Q5Q_5Q5​ is compatible with transitions under another spatial operation. This is the kind of channel distinction needed in a neutrino calculation; it does not identify RRR with an observed weak vertex or calculate a scattering rate.

Equal plane rates matter. If

A dynamical symmetry selecting equal rates could protect the cancellation; choosing them by hand leaves it conditional. Under a common physical rotation of the probe and the field, the annihilation condition is unchanged. Rotating just one changes their relative preparation and must be evaluated afresh.

Taking d=5d=5d=5 and z∈ran⁡P0z\in\operatorname{ran}P_0z∈ranP0​ therefore supplies a normalized neutral spatial solution with nonzero extent in all five directions. But (12) also supplies equally minimal solutions in the two nonzero rotation-weight subspaces. Their energy and shape width are identical. The scalar density kernel has no preference between them. This is an auxiliary spatial field solution, not the actual electron orbit or a derived Dirac particle solution.

The derivation supplies actual neutral modes and a definite six-dimensional comparison. It does not force a five-dimensional excitation to occupy the neutral subspace: its two nonzero weights also exist. A spin-independent radial self-binding energy gives the same energy to all constant normalized spinors, as the full spatial example now demonstrates. An initial constant spinor polarization is preserved by this scalar evolution, but there is no attraction of an arbitrary polarization toward the neutral subspace.

Applying this result to electromagnetic neutrality requires deriving the probe from the one field's action, including the orbital contribution, photon current, kinetic normalization, physical boundary conditions, and the selected particle solutions. Consistency along the nested spatial chain is part of that derivation. The existence of a neutral eigenspace is a concrete mechanism candidate, not a completed charge assignment.
