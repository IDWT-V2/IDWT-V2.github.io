Physical spatial profile

Complete local scalar response also separates rank from occupied-ray self-contact at an actual physical point. Its scalar-null source derivative has one real responsive direction, but that direction is orthogonal to the occupied complex ray and gives zero after projection onto it. This does not derive the graph kernel or its simplex normalization.

Local scalar normalization tracks the remaining spatial equation: virial and fixed-norm constraints make the nonlinear coefficient and its frequency distinct. In the flat massless local reduction, the standard regular radial ansatz has no nonzero finite-norm stationary solution. The actual physical geometric and bundle terms must be retained when testing a particle profile.

The direct derivative calculation gives the local scalar term before adding the graph pairing. Its coefficient count repeats local source eigenvalues; it does not generate the factor in (3). This makes the extra interaction premise explicit even when the graph ray is described by field derivatives.
