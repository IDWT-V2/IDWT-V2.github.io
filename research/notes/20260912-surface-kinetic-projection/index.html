Research / Mass and normalization

Projecting the field kinetic term onto a surface

Updated September 13, 2026

Summary: The canonical field kinetic term gives a boundary expression for the two-normal surface coefficient; common background circulation can make it profile-independent, while a fully localized normal field has a cancelling outer contribution.

A kinetic coefficient with a stated field origin

The surface-bending frequency calculation uses a geometric kinetic coefficient Γ\GammaΓ. It is possible to test its origin directly from the canonical field action, independently of the curvature energy.

This addresses a normalization coefficient, not a new way of solving for mass. The selected frequency already specifies mass in equivalent units. A field's fixed-point equation may determine the common background and the profile used in the kinetic projection. Their existence as solutions of the same spatial action remains necessary.

Take the canonical time term

Ltime=iℏ2∫(Ψ†Ψ˙−Ψ˙†Ψ)ddx.(1) L_{\mathrm{time}}=\frac{i\hbar}{2}\int \left(\Psi^\dagger\dot\Psi-\dot\Psi^\dagger\Psi\right)d^dx. \tag{1} Ltime​=2iℏ​∫(Ψ†Ψ˙−Ψ˙†Ψ)ddx.(1)

All complex spinor components are retained. Their number does not add physical dimensions. The following comparison singles out two ordinary coordinates normal to a proposed field feature; it does not distinguish two fundamental directions in the containing sector.

Let y=(y1,y2)y=(y_1,y_2)y=(y1​,y2​) denote those normal coordinates. Locally approximate a translated core by Ψ(y−X)\Psi(y-X)Ψ(y−X), with slow dependence along the surface. The sign follows the order of the normal coordinates and the convention that the symplectic form is the exterior derivative of the kinetic one-form. Reversing their orientation reverses the sign. Equation (2) is a pullback of the existing kinetic term, rather than an independently chosen field or pair coupling. The approximation that a curved feature is represented by these translated normal profiles, and the contributions of its other field variations, still require checking.

A common winding and background density therefore supply a coefficient independent of the occupied surface harmonic, under these assumptions. The transverse core width in this calculation is different from the round surface radius or a particle's proposed common cavity scale. Equation (8) establishes no equality between those spatial lengths.

A localized outer field cancels the coefficient

If the complete normal field decays so that its outer current circulation vanishes, (5) gives Γ12=0\Gamma_{12}=0Γ12​=0. For example, a smooth localized profile f(r)=r∣k∣exp⁡[−r2/(2a2)]f(r)=r^{|k|}\exp[-r^2/(2a^2)]f(r)=r∣k∣exp[−r2/(2a2)] has positive and negative radial contributions that cancel. Keeping only the rising core would miss the returning outer contribution.

Thus a nonzero coefficient of the form (8) needs the stated nonvanishing surrounding field or a different complete kinetic reduction. A nonzero background also requires its own finite relative energy, spatial stress and global consistency analysis; (5) by itself does not provide them.

This conditionally supplies the origin of one common normalization factor. It does not derive the bending energy coefficient κ\kappaκ, the particle indices, the common radius or the full inertial response. In particular, Γ\GammaΓ is a first-order geometric kinetic coefficient with different units from mass; it must not be relabelled as inertia.

The next dynamical requirement is a same-field fixed point and reduction that produce the needed curvature energy and its supporting terms together with (2). The unrestricted bending evolution's propagation limitation also remains. A boundary identity cannot resolve that limitation.
