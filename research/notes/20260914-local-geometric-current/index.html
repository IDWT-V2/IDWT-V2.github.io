Locality tests for the geometric current source

Updated September 14, 2026

conditional calculation

Replacing a mode average by a pointwise current changes the full source, even when the same coherent state remains exact. This distinction can be tested without fitting any particle amplitudes. A local oscillator square has an explicit growing harmonic. A different first-order Dirac current law avoids that particular growth in its two-endpoint limit, but does not inherit the result when arbitrary fast carrier bands are added.

These are inverse local comparisons on an ordinary two-dimensional oscillator plane. That plane tests proposed normal dynamics; it has not been identified with the linked carrier's complete spatial mode expansion. Appending another physical plane to that carrier would change the model's dimensionality. A valid embedding must use the same physical coordinates and canonical field. Form and spinor indices below are component labels.

Fast bands are a separate test

The proof above uses the specific two-endpoint mass matrix. It cannot be transferred to arbitrary carrier bands. A structural control keeps four signed masses +1,−1,+M,−M+1,-1,+M,-M+1,−1,+M,−M and one active normal current. The occupied state couples to the −1-1−1 branch with vertex one and to +M+M+M with vertex 0.10.10.1, through the same reciprocal local law.

Without the fast vertex, the primary coupling shifts the negative disturbance frequencies. The crossing must be found using those shifted frequencies, not the bare carrier values. With the fast vertex retained, the complete invariant matrix has growing modes. For example, M=5M=5M=5, normal degree 17 gives rate approximately 0.01967890.01967890.0196789; M=20M=20M=20, degree 8268 gives 0.01297670.01297670.0129767. The corresponding controls with the fast vertex set to zero are stable.

This establishes a limitation of a proposed generalization. These assigned four-band controls are not the full linked physical geometry. Their distant resonances may also lie outside a specified effective projection's valid range. The complete physical carrier and that range must be evaluated before making a physical stability claim.
