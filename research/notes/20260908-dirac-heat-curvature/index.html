Curvature is not an Euler characteristic

Rescaling a metric by a2a^2a2 changes its scalar curvature by a−2a^{-2}a−2, whereas the Euler characteristic is unchanged. On a closed surface,

∫R dμ=4πχ. \int R\,d\mu=4\pi\chi. ∫Rdμ=4πχ.

This is an integrated identity. It does not make R(x)=χR(x)=\chiR(x)=χ. For the round sphere, their numerical equality at radius one is a choice of units and metric. In particular, the round three-sphere has χ(S3)=0\chi(S^3)=0χ(S3)=0 but R=6/a2R=6/a^2R=6/a2, so its local Dirac heat correction is nonzero.

Applications to finite mode bands or mixing vertices require the associated operator, physical scale, and normalized matrix element. Numerical use at a particular nonzero ttt additionally requires control of the asymptotic remainder.
