An Introduction to Infinite Dimensional Wave Theory

Updated October 17, 2026

Why do particles fall into families with similar properties but very different masses? Infinite Dimensional Wave Theory, or IDWT, investigates whether their physical dimensionality helps explain both features.

The proposal is one field in a shared higher-dimensional physical space, with particles confined to layers of different dimensionality. The full space has at least ten spatial dimensions; the total could be ten, larger or infinite. Our familiar three-dimensional view does not exhaust that space. The proposed electron has six spatial dimensions and the tau ten. All those directions are physical spatial directions of the same kind as the familiar three, without compactification.

The layers can be described as nested submanifolds of the same space. An electron is lower-dimensional relative to a tau, but is not a constituent inside it. The current containing-space hypothesis is flat. The field dynamics that confines and sustains a particle pattern remains to be derived; the layer picture states the geometry being investigated.

The case for investigating the sector structure is the main research article. It connects the mass comparisons to dimensional criteria and explicit higher-dimensional constructions, with detailed derivations linked throughout. The dimensions remain proposed assignments, not experimentally measured extra spatial directions.

One Classification Across Several Questions

“Depth” means the dimensionality involved in a particle's bound structure. It is used in the mass rule, family assignments and proposed spin, charge and orbit constructions. A pattern's bound directions need not be the only directions in which its center can move through the containing space.

The six-dimensional electron itself moves around the nucleus on a literal deterministic trajectory using four spatial directions. That external orbit is distinct from motion within the electron's own field structure. Its position, internal field oscillations and time-averaged detector image are different quantities.

The difference exceeds measurement uncertainty, so the uncorrected expression is not an exact mass law. Changing the common scale moves both particles together.

The non-neutrino scale prescription uses the electron as its measured dimensional reference when evaluating the formula. Numerical sector coefficients relate the other units. Those coefficients were developed with the masses already known; their physical derivation remains unfinished. Neutrinos have a separate absolute-scale problem. Their mass-squared splitting ratio can be tested without choosing that scale.

Alternative assignments must move grouped families together, retain fixed measured targets and test the same scale constraints across all families. The assignment study proves conditional uniqueness within its specified model and search domain. That result does not establish uniqueness among arbitrary geometries or alternative scale laws.

What the Recent Mathematics Adds

Some calculations distinguish dimensions; others show that particles in different dimensions can share a property. Both are relevant.

Within depths one through ten, that criterion allows exactly two, three, four, five, six and ten. A larger common carrier can restore clocks at other depths, so the minimal-carrier premise matters. Its chosen carrier, frequency units, occupied indices and overall normalization A0A_0A0​ are inputs. Its controlled reference perturbations are not a proof of nonlinear lifetime or physical pair interactions. Complete action and derivation.

This advances the construction of a particle candidate without completing the physical mass operator. The remaining task is a common same-field law that selects and sustains the observed branches, fixes their units and normalization, and yields their reciprocal responses. Persistence must also match the particle: electron longevity and a tau's finite lifetime are different targets. The decay-tree study supplies kinematic constraints on that work.

The proposed dimensional classification is worth testing because the same depths now enter several explicit mathematical questions. Their shared premises make a combined physical explanation possible; they do not make the results statistically independent. The expanded sector case sets out the evidence and the calculations that could change its assessment.
