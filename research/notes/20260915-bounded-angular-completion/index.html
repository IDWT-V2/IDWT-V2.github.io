Research / Mass and normalization

Preserving the simplex factors in the full radial operator

Updated September 16, 2026

conditional calculation

The simplex numerator can survive the complete radial normal field, rather than only its finite kernel projection. An explicit bounded completion of the spatial operator achieves this at the leading order in the normal-to-tangential scale ratio. Its radial coefficient is common to all particle indices at one depth.

The completion is an inverse construction from the target angular structure. It is not a forward derivation from the master action. It retains the original highest-derivative part, but changes specified lower terms. Finite-scale corrections, the occupied supporting geometry and the physical shared scales remain to be derived. Mass is the resulting frequency in equivalent units throughout.

The later spatial dimension and frame audit establishes the positive-dimensional reference surface formula, while identifying a missing surface harmonic tower at depth two. Its depth-two radial calculation therefore still uses a prescribed surface parameter. The same audit specifies how the angular operator must be transported under a local component-frame change.

The remaining physical calculation

Equation (14) gives a common frequency unit at each depth, not its absolute physical value or the shared six/ten unit. The normal and tangential lengths and coupling must follow from the occupied same-field action. The pressure calculation shows that bare geometric tension alone does not maintain the required common radius.

The coefficient ggg mixes the normal spatial modes; it is not already an observed interaction strength or the proposed self-coupling mass unit. At fixed R,LR,LR,L, its small-ggg, large-separation behavior gives

∂log⁡ω∂log⁡g=−(d−1)+O(g2)+Od,g,n(Λ−2).(17) \frac{\partial\log\omega}{\partial\log g} =-(d-1)+O(g^2)+O_{d,g,n}(\Lambda^{-2}). \tag{17} ∂logg∂logω​=−(d−1)+O(g2)+Od,g,n​(Λ−2).(17)

Increasing this mixing lowers the small frequency under that fixed-geometry comparison. A proposed relation between small mass and weak interaction must therefore include the same-field geometry and normalization, or a different identified coupling coefficient. Numerical smallness of ggg alone does not explain a small neutrino mass in this operator. The comparison requires γ=gΛ\gamma=g\Lambdaγ=gΛ to remain large; it is not the limit g→0g\to0g→0 at a fixed finite Λ\LambdaΛ.

There is also a direct stationarity test. For one occupied band with fixed positive canonical phase charge Q\mathcal QQ, the trial energy is E=ZG+QωE=ZG+\mathcal Q\omegaE=ZG+Qω. The flat linked geometric cost is stationary along its complex modulus αd\alpha_dαd​, whereas (15) gives

in the controlled large-separation regime. The canonical phase charge is the wave action's normalization, not an electric charge or a mass-fitting amplitude. Equation (18) follows by differentiating the magnitude of the complex endpoint coupling. Choosing its relative phase can make one real derivative vanish, but cannot make both vanish while κd(g)≠0\kappa_d(g)\ne0κd​(g)=0.

Thus a single occupied band does not hold this bare radial geometry stationary. The common leading source across particle indices is useful for a common supporting law, but that law has not been derived. A complete same-field contribution must balance this shape source as well as the radius pressure, with its energy and kinetic normalization retained.

The moving-support calculation strengthens this local test: under the stated smoothness and gap assumptions, an allowed complex shape deformation gives a nearby exact zero of the complete separated normal operator. That wave-frequency zero is not a massless complete particle. A positive local moving action does admit stable periodic support, but loses the particle-index dependence unless an additional orbit-action selection law is supplied. Ordinary curvature pinning also has a quantified supporting-energy cost in the tested regime.

The completion also changes the full shape-dependent operator. Previous finite-carrier stiffness calculations cannot be adopted unchanged: the new two-step term and the normal dressing must enter that source and stability calculation. A positive norm and a preserved mass numerator are necessary progress, but do not establish a complete particle solution. The physical rotation module must also be supplied: the geometric fluctuation and ordinary spinor representations discussed in the original carrier note are not identified merely by sharing a differential expression.
