Charge Neutrality and Handed Spatial Modes

Updated October 1, 2026

Summary: A revised conserved geometric generator makes the lowest handed normal mode neutral and preserves neutrality of the complete spin-half profile through orbital compensation; its electric interpretation remains conditional.

1. The Connection Between Charge and Handedness

A candidate geometric charge connects the spin calculation to the depth-five helicity construction. In the complete depth-five Dirac representation, the sign controlling tangent handedness is the product of two geometric signs. The proposed charge counts their sum. Opposite signs give the selected handedness and zero charge together.

The generator uses the Clifford orientation blocks retained in the helicity calculation and the complete rotation in its normal spatial plane, including orbital motion. Both commute with ordinary observer spin rotations. This changes the generator from a sum of fixed spin-plane rotations, which need not have that property. The rotation-neutral construction explains the earlier cancellation and its scope. No independent charge-label space is introduced here.

Three findings follow under this revised rule:

  1. The handed, lowest-winding normal mode used in the existing helicity construction is exactly neutral. Its neutral state need not be selected separately from its handed state.
  2. A complete five-dimensional nonlinear Dirac bubble retains exact neutrality and spin-half covariance. Opposite-handed spatial components do not spoil the cancellation, because their orbital motion contributes.
  3. The normal angular sector can accommodate massive three-dimensional kinematics without changing its charge, if the spatial dynamics supplies a suitable nonzero normal eigenvalue.

The rule is a candidate conserved geometric charge. Its identification with electromagnetism is an additional physical hypothesis. A different conserved generator can give the same handed mode a nonzero charge. Also, the bubble and the textured normal mode are not yet solutions of one combined equation. These limits matter when judging whether the charge-selection gap has been filled.
