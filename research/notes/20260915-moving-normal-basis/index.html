Canonical response of the actual moving normal modes

Updated September 15, 2026

conditional calculation

A spatial eigenbasis changes when its supporting geometry changes. The canonical response therefore contains more than the change of the finite carrier's eigenvectors. The missing contribution can be computed from the actual normal modes and their complementary spatial inverses, without introducing a fitted kinetic coefficient.

This calculation uses the ordinary unbounded normal plane, the linked geometry and both the original and geometric carriers at depths 3, 4, 5, 6 and 10. It supplies a wave-induced geometric curvature. Its sign is not a sign of particle inertia.

Translation, phase and temporal connection

A common translation has a useful independent check. The coefficient α1\alpha_1α1​ translates the profile center by −α1/d-\alpha_1/d−α1​/d. The normal contribution is exactly

Fx1y1normal=−id2[Pp1P,Pp2P].(7) F^{\rm normal}_{x_1y_1} =-\frac{i}{d^2}[Pp_1P,Pp_2P]. \tag{7} Fx1​y1​normal​=−d2i​[Pp1​P,Pp2​P].(7)

For a complete translated wave, the retained basis contribution cancels (7). The full canonical translation curvature is zero, as also follows from commuting full spatial translation generators.
