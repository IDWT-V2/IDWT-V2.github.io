Orbit drive origin

Updated September 10, 2026

Physical premise (IDWT): There is one infinite-dimensional physical manifold. All sectors form one nested chain of submanifolds within it. Particle depth counts ordinary, literal macroscopic spatial dimensions. “Observed” and “unobserved” describe an observer's access to those dimensions, not different kinds of physical dimension. See spatial meaning and mathematical scope.

Summary: A scalar geometric potential reproduces the orbit generator after compression but necessarily leaks from the holomorphic sector, while frame changes alone do not establish physical motion.

Physical evolution versus a moving description

The precession mechanism predates this diagnostic. In the visualization history, ce71f75 explicitly assigns precession rates and locks them to the orbital frequency; 1f8a54a replaces that recipe with Hamiltonian exchange and clock evolution, and c6d6bcf–4701c3a place both in one representation action. This note tests a possible physical source for that existing flow. It does not introduce precession for the first time, nor show that a new scalar potential is necessary to obtain petals. The Coulomb-binding-plus-precession construction remains the baseline; the compression below is an inverse diagnostic, not its derived electron–nucleus force law.

The selected-line current construction added ℏνĜ as a candidate physical drive. It did not establish whether the master action supplies this term relative to the nucleus and electromagnetic observer.

For a time-dependent unitary change of description b = W(t)c, the energy Hamiltonian and measured operator transform together:

h′=WhW†+iℏW˙W†,D′=WDW†,b†D′b=c†Dc. h'=WhW^\dagger+i\hbar\dot W W^\dagger,\qquad D'=WDW^\dagger,\qquad b^\dagger D'b=c^\dagger Dc. h′=WhW†+iℏW˙W†,D′=WDW†,b†D′b=c†Dc.

Starting with h = 0 and W = e^{-iνGt} produces h' = ℏνG, but no changing measurement: the transformed D' cancels the apparent orbit. Conversely, evolving c by e^{-iνGt} while keeping the physical D fixed generally gives a changing measurement. Transforming that active evolution to a stationary state makes D time-dependent; it does not remove the signal.

Thus the ability to remove G by a time-dependent basis change is not a no-motion theorem. The physical test is relative evolution of electron, nuclear frame, and probe. The action must specify all three. A transformed coordinate current cannot be inserted into an inertial radiation formula while leaving the observer and electromagnetic field untransformed.

A scalar potential can reproduce the compressed generator

Consider the candidate spinor line carrying H⁰(CP3,O(L)), with its unit-volume FS measure and orthogonal holomorphic projector P_L. This section tests a prescribed nonconstant position-dependent potential, not the earlier scalar self-contact operator that was proportional to identity on a selected ray.

Let g = diag(1,−1,−1,0), μ_g = Z†gZ/(Z†Z), and Ĝ_L = N₁−N₂−N₃ on degree-L monomials. The exact integrals in the geometric probe note imply

Consequently the real smooth multiplication potential

VL(u)=ℏν[(L+4)μg(u)+1] V_L(u)=\hbar\nu[(L+4)\mu_g(u)+1] VL​(u)=ℏν[(L+4)μg​(u)+1]

has P_L V_L P_L = ℏνĜ_L. It reproduces the actual orbit generator on the four selected monomials, including the even/odd amplitude exchange, without adding a first-order differential drive at the microscopic level.

This is an exact compression identity, not an exact invariant field solution or a derived nuclear potential. The choice of g already inserts the desired orientation and eigenvalue pattern. Moreover, one universal potential vμ_g instead gives rate ν_L = v/[ℏ(L+4)], up to a common energy shift. Holding ν fixed across all degrees requires a degree-dependent amplitude or additional dynamics. The inverse construction cannot count as independent selection of g, ν, or the field scale.

Why exact preservation fails

Suppose a real smooth multiplication potential V maps a nonzero holomorphic section s back to a holomorphic section. Then

∂ˉ(Vs)=(∂ˉV)s=0. \bar\partial(Vs)=(\bar\partial V)s=0. ∂ˉ(Vs)=(∂ˉV)s=0.

Away from the zero set of s, ∂̄V = 0. That set has dense complement, so smoothness extends the equality everywhere. On connected CP3 a real holomorphic function is constant. In particular, a nonconstant V cannot preserve the entire holomorphic sector, or even send one nonzero holomorphic section exactly back into it. This does not exclude a compressed effective potential or a corrected field with nonholomorphic components.

The same reasoning constrains a smooth algebraic spinor endomorphism if it is required to map the selected vacuum line into itself and preserve its holomorphic sections: its restriction is a scalar multiplication function. It does not constrain arbitrary operators acting outside that line, nor first-order differential generators such as a Killing lift.

A controlled illustrative correction, not a V2 prediction

For full energy Hamiltonian h, eliminating Q at spectral parameter E gives h_eff(E) = h_PP + h_PQ(E−h_QQ)^{-1}h_QP directly from the two block equations. This is the usual projection method for effective Hamiltonians; see second-order perturbation theory notes. No fixed sign can be assigned without knowing the omitted spectrum.

There is a useful sharper result for the bare massive Dirac baseline itself. In units ℏ = c = 1, write h₀ = D + mβ, with D self-adjoint, {D,β} = 0, and the retained holomorphic zero modes satisfying Dψ = 0, βψ = ψ. Assume D² is invertible on the omitted space Q. Since h₀² = D²+m²,

Q(m−h0)−1Q=−Q(m+h0)(D2)−1Q. Q(m-h_0)^{-1}Q=-Q(m+h_0)(D^2)^{-1}Q. Q(m−h0​)−1Q=−Q(m+h0​)(D2)−1Q.

A real multiplication perturbation preserves chirality. Sandwiching this resolvent between its positive-chirality leakage sources removes the odd D term and gives the exact second-order coefficient

δh(2)=−2mPVQ(DQ2)−1QVP. \delta h^{(2)}=-2mPVQ(D_Q^2)^{-1}QVP. δh(2)=−2mPVQ(DQ2​)−1QVP.

It is negative semidefinite for m > 0. Thus opposite-energy Dirac branches do not freely cancel this shift in the specified massive zero-mode baseline; they combine as shown. At m = 0 this particular second-order coefficient vanishes, without proving all-order protection. A nuclear background that changes h₀ or its chiral structure needs a new resolvent calculation. This identity fixes a sign, not the matrix values of (D_Q²)^{-1} or the correction to the amplitude/clock ratio. A finite chiral-block check verifies it independently below.

Consequence for current derivation

An algebraic scalar potential adds no bare minimal-coupling spatial current. If it drives approximate motion in the holomorphic sector, the corrected Dirac field on physical space and effective probe/current must include the eliminated components. Using P_L V_L P_L as the dynamics while declaring the pure vacuum-line Dirac current zero is inconsistent. Nor may the current of an independently postulated Killing term simply be substituted without deriving the effective source from the scalar model.

The next physical calculation is the nuclear-projected potential and the omitted-mode resolvent, with the electromagnetic field included during elimination. This route can produce an effective orbit drive, but its selection, errors, and source are not closed by the compression identity.

The spatial scalar branch evaluates a complementary self-consistent test on the same specified geometry and twist. Restoring the radial Dirac modes yields a nonzero cubic frequency correction despite the vanishing massless second-order term. It does not derive the nuclear drive or replace the moving orbit.
