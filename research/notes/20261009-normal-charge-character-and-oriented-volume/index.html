Normal Charge, Rotation Alignment and Oriented Volume

Updated October 9, 2026

The normal bundle is made of ordinary spatial directions in one containing manifold. Its complex component ranks describe those directions and the field on them. This note adds consequences of the shared normal geometry; it does not select physical particles, calibrate electric response or derive an action. In the maintained four-base/ten-domain construction, the complex normal rank is three. Comparisons below keep the same carrier prescription at other ranks.

6. Contribution And Verification

The concrete contribution is a shared geometric reading: the rank-three carrier's first charge asymmetry is cubic; the same normal dimension has a cubic oriented volume polynomial; and its complete chiral polarizations give rotation structures whose definite-grade alignment reproduces the existing charge weights. Higher rank controls fail the universal polarization-to-rotation property. This supplies further conditional structure for investigating the four/six/ten geometry. It does not favor four physical particle-sector assignments or settle electric charge, neutral-state occupation, physical handedness or antiparticles.

The single exact checker and results use rational arithmetic and finite Gaussian-integer Clifford preparations. They compare ranks one through sixteen, independently check character values, verify multivariate leading terms, rotation/reflection covariance of the Pfaffian, and the correlated-frame and charge-alignment controls. The character theorem and embedded higher-rank counterexample extend the relevant conclusions beyond the finite comparison. No particle simulation is used.
