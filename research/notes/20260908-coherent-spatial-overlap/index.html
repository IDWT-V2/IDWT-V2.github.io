Coherent spatial overlap

Updated September 8, 2026

Summary: Exact complex Gaussian overlaps retain both objects' shapes, polarizations, and spatial phases, while normalized opposing lobes give a concrete low-momentum source cancellation.

Spatial source patterns, before averaging

After deriving a source or response operator, a local overlap may take the form

A(k)=∫⟨Fa(x),Fb(x)⟩e−ik⋅x dDx.(1) \mathcal A(k)=\int \langle F_a(x),F_b(x)\rangle e^{-ik\cdot x}\,d^Dx. \tag{1} A(k)=∫⟨Fa​(x),Fb​(x)⟩e−ik⋅xdDx.(1)

Both FaF_aFa​ and FbF_bFb​ are polarized spatial patterns. Their components must be expressed in compatible frames and normalized according to the action. Equation (1) adopts a local identity pairing; a derivative or nonlocal kernel must be retained when the action supplies one.

The coordinates belong to one common physical chart of the nested manifold. This benchmark uses finite test functions or wavepackets after the spatial mode maps have been specified. It does not replace a lower field's cylindrical continuation by a thin sheet, or identify wavepacket localization in a free direction with a new bound dimension.

For an actual nonlinear source, first calculate FbF_bFb​ from the higher field as in descending photon source. Substituting a bare wavefunction or a positive density for a different source would change (1).

Two structured objects

For finite patterns

Fa=∑iaiψaiTai,Fb=∑jbjψbjTbj, F_a=\sum_i a_i\psi_{ai}T_{ai}, \qquad F_b=\sum_j b_j\psi_{bj}T_{bj}, Fa​=i∑​ai​ψai​Tai​,Fb​=j∑​bj​ψbj​Tbj​,

linearity gives the exact amplitude

A(k)=∑i,ja‾ibj⟨Tai,Tbj⟩Iai,bj(k).(4) \boxed{\mathcal A(k)= \sum_{i,j}\overline a_i b_j \langle T_{ai},T_{bj}\rangle I_{ai,bj}(k).} \tag{4} A(k)=i,j∑​ai​bj​⟨Tai​,Tbj​⟩Iai,bj​(k).​(4)

Both objects' orientations and phases occur in every term. Individual nonzero overlaps can cancel in the sum. The full Gram matrix of the component profiles determines the normalization of aaa and bbb; the lobes are not assumed orthogonal merely because their centers differ. If physical dynamics makes some outputs distinguishable, sum their intensities only after separating those outputs.

General moment cancellation

Suppose an integrable scalar or tensor source s(x)s(x)s(x) has finite absolute moment of order mmm and

∫xαs(x)dDx=0for every multi-index ∣α∣<m. \int x^\alpha s(x)d^Dx=0 \quad\text{for every multi-index }|\alpha|<m. ∫xαs(x)dDx=0for every multi-index ∣α∣<m.

Subtract the degree-m−1m-1m−1 Taylor polynomial of e−ik⋅xe^{-ik\cdot x}e−ik⋅x under the integral. Its real-variable integral remainder is bounded by ∣k⋅x∣m/m!|k\cdot x|^m/m!∣k⋅x∣m/m!, which proves

∥s~(k)∥≤∣k∣mm!∫∣x∣m∥s(x)∥dDx.(7) \boxed{ \|\widetilde s(k)\| \leq\frac{|k|^m}{m!} \int |x|^m\|s(x)\|d^Dx.} \tag{7} ∥s(k)∥≤m!∣k∣m​∫∣x∣m∥s(x)∥dDx.​(7)

This gives a calculable low-momentum suppression from vanishing source moments. The moments must vanish for the source produced by the actual field equation. Squaring an odd field can remove its odd parity, so an odd bare shape alone does not establish the moment conditions.

The same construction is available at many depths. A distinctive particle prediction requires the field dynamics to select the relevant shape, scale, and source symmetry, and requires the coupling to allowed response tangents to be checked as well. A zero source does not by itself exclude a nonzero susceptibility.
