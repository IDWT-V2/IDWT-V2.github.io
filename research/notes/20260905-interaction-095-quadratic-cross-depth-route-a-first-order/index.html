95. Quadratic cross-depth route: a first-order 3↔63\leftrightarrow63↔6 transition can generate the full (3,3)(\mathbf3,\mathbf3)(3,3) lock after Schur elimination

Section 94 ruled out an overly direct identification of the ordinary Levi--Civita mixed connection with the desired full-rank frame-lock operator. That does not rule out the actual cross-depth block

D36=P3D∞P6. D_{36}=P_3D_\infty P_6. D36​=P3​D∞​P6​.

The important distinction is that a transition operator from the electron sector into a depth-three channel need not itself transform as (3,3)(\mathbf3,\mathbf3)(3,3). The effective operator obtained after composing the transition with its adjoint can have richer representation content.

95.1 Representation content of the first-order transition

Relative to the nucleus-selected split,

Spin(3)O×Spin(3)H, Spin(3)_O\times Spin(3)_H, Spin(3)O​×Spin(3)H​,

the electron chiral four is

E=(2,2). E=(\mathbf2,\mathbf2). E=(2,2).

A depth-three spinor channel carried by the observed factor has representation

N=(2,1). N=(\mathbf2,\mathbf1). N=(2,1).

Therefore the vector space of first-order transition operators is

Hom⁡(E,N)≅N⊗E∗. \operatorname{Hom}(E,N) \cong N\otimes E^*. Hom(E,N)≅N⊗E∗.

Since the SU(2)SU(2)SU(2) doublet is pseudoreal,

Hom⁡((2,2),(2,1))=(1,2)⊕(3,2). \boxed{ \operatorname{Hom}((\mathbf2,\mathbf2),(\mathbf2,\mathbf1)) =(\mathbf1,\mathbf2)\oplus(\mathbf3,\mathbf2). } Hom((2,2),(2,1))=(1,2)⊕(3,2).​

So a bare 3↔63\leftrightarrow63↔6 transition does not contain a (3,3)(\mathbf3,\mathbf3)(3,3) representation. This agrees with the warning from Section 94 that one must not simply relabel a first-order mixed coefficient matrix as the final lock Hamiltonian.

95.2 The electron effective operator does contain (3,3)(\mathbf3,\mathbf3)(3,3)

The electron endomorphism algebra is

End⁡(E)≅(2⊗2,2⊗2), \operatorname{End}(E) \cong (\mathbf2\otimes\mathbf2,\mathbf2\otimes\mathbf2), End(E)≅(2⊗2,2⊗2),

hence

End⁡(2,2)=(1,1)⊕(3,1)⊕(1,3)⊕(3,3). \boxed{ \operatorname{End}(\mathbf2,\mathbf2) =(\mathbf1,\mathbf1) \oplus(\mathbf3,\mathbf1) \oplus(\mathbf1,\mathbf3) \oplus(\mathbf3,\mathbf3). } End(2,2)=(1,1)⊕(3,1)⊕(1,3)⊕(3,3).​

Thus a quadratic combination such as

D63D36 D_{63}D_{36} D63​D36​

can contain exactly the full mixed tensor sector needed by the frame lock even though D36D_{36}D36​ itself does not.

This changes the correct target from

D36∼?TQ D_{36}\stackrel{?}{\sim}T_Q D36​∼?TQ​

to

D63 R3 D36∼?PQ or TQ, \boxed{ D_{63}\,\mathcal R_3\,D_{36} \stackrel{?}{\sim}P_Q\text{ or }T_Q, } D63​R3​D36​∼?PQ​ or TQ​,​

where R3\mathcal R_3R3​ is the appropriate depth-three resolvent/propagator in the eliminated channel.

95.3 Explicit rank-one transition already produces the exact oriented lock projector

Let

∣SQ⟩ |S_Q\rangle ∣SQ​⟩

be the maximally entangled electron ray associated with an oriented frame map QQQ, and let

∣n⟩ |n\rangle ∣n⟩

be one selected normalized depth-three channel. Consider the rank-one transition

BQ=β ∣n⟩⟨SQ∣. \boxed{ B_Q=\beta\,|n\rangle\langle S_Q|. } BQ​=β∣n⟩⟨SQ​∣.​

Then

BQ†BQ=∣β∣2PQ,PQ=∣SQ⟩⟨SQ∣. \boxed{ B_Q^\dagger B_Q =|\beta|^2P_Q, \qquad P_Q=|S_Q\rangle\langle S_Q|. } BQ†​BQ​=∣β∣2PQ​,PQ​=∣SQ​⟩⟨SQ​∣.​

Although BQB_QBQ​ has rank one, the projector PQP_QPQ​ has a full-rank (3,3)(3,3)(3,3) correlation tensor.

At the aligned lock,

PS=∣S⟩⟨S∣ P_S=|S\rangle\langle S| PS​=∣S⟩⟨S∣

has the Pauli expansion

PS=14(I−∑a=13σa⊗σa). \boxed{ P_S =\frac14 \left( I-\sum_{a=1}^3\sigma_a\otimes\sigma_a \right). } PS​=41​(I−a=1∑3​σa​⊗σa​).​

Its (3,3)(3,3)(3,3) coefficient matrix is

−14I3, \boxed{-\frac14I_3,} −41​I3​,​

whose determinant is

−164≠0. \boxed{-\frac1{64}\neq0.} −641​=0.​

So full mixed rank does not require a full-rank transition matrix. A single coherent cross-depth channel can generate the complete oriented spin-frame correlation after composition with its adjoint.

For general orientation,

PQ=14(I−TQ),TQ=∑a,iQaiσa⊗σi. \boxed{ P_Q =\frac14(I-T_Q), \qquad T_Q=\sum_{a,i}Q_{ai}\sigma_a\otimes\sigma_i. } PQ​=41​(I−TQ​),TQ​=a,i∑​Qai​σa​⊗σi​.​

This is exactly the Section-93 lock operator, now appearing in the quadratic effective electron operator rather than being incorrectly identified with the first-order Levi--Civita connection.

95.4 Schur/Feshbach elimination fixes the favorable sign conditionally on a positive gap

Consider a block Hamiltonian/operator of the schematic form

H=(HeBQ†BQHN). \mathcal H = \begin{pmatrix} H_e & B_Q^\dagger\\ B_Q & H_N \end{pmatrix}. H=(He​BQ​​BQ†​HN​​).

If the relevant eliminated depth-three channel has positive gap

HN=ΔI,Δ>0, H_N=\Delta I, \qquad \Delta>0, HN​=ΔI,Δ>0,

then the static Schur complement on the electron sector is

Heff=He−BQ†HN−1BQ. H_{\rm eff} =H_e-B_Q^\dagger H_N^{-1}B_Q. Heff​=He​−BQ†​HN−1​BQ​.

Therefore

ΔHe=−∣β∣2ΔPQ. \boxed{ \Delta H_e =-\frac{|\beta|^2}{\Delta}P_Q. } ΔHe​=−Δ∣β∣2​PQ​.​

Using

PQ=14(I−TQ), P_Q=\frac14(I-T_Q), PQ​=41​(I−TQ​),

this becomes

ΔHe=∣β∣24ΔTQ−∣β∣24ΔI. \boxed{ \Delta H_e =\frac{|\beta|^2}{4\Delta}T_Q -\frac{|\beta|^2}{4\Delta}I. } ΔHe​=4Δ∣β∣2​TQ​−4Δ∣β∣2​I.​

The last term is a common energy shift. The physically relevant mixed coefficient is therefore

κeff=∣β∣24Δ>0. \boxed{ \kappa_{\rm eff}=\frac{|\beta|^2}{4\Delta}>0. } κeff​=4Δ∣β∣2​>0.​

Consequently the singlet-oriented ray ∣SQ⟩|S_Q\rangle∣SQ​⟩ is lowered relative to the triplet by

ΔE=∣β∣2Δ. \boxed{ \Delta E=\frac{|\beta|^2}{\Delta}. } ΔE=Δ∣β∣2​.​

This is important: if the lock is generated by virtual mixing with a positive-gap depth-three channel, the sign required in Section 93 is not arbitrary. It is the ordinary negative-semidefinite second-order level shift from eliminating that channel.

95.5 Relation to the scattering-selected orientation

The remaining orientation problem may now be phrased at the level of the transition amplitude rather than the Levi--Civita connection.

One needs the cross-depth matrix element to select

⟨SQscat∣ \langle S_{Q_{\rm scat}}| ⟨SQscat​​∣

or, more generally, a coherent family whose dominant singular vector is the electron lock ray associated with the scattering/nuclear frame.

Then

D36⟶BQscat†BQscat⟶PQscat⟶TQscat, D_{36} \longrightarrow B_{Q_{\rm scat}}^\dagger B_{Q_{\rm scat}} \longrightarrow P_{Q_{\rm scat}} \longrightarrow T_{Q_{\rm scat}}, D36​⟶BQscat​†​BQscat​​⟶PQscat​​⟶TQscat​​,

and the positive-gap Schur sign selects that ray as the low-energy electron configuration.

Sections 90--92 then apply directly:

PQscat stationary under Gq⟹q=1⟹1:1:3. P_{Q_{\rm scat}} \text{ stationary under }G_q \Longrightarrow q=1 \Longrightarrow 1:1:3. PQscat​​ stationary under Gq​⟹q=1⟹1:1:3.

95.6 What is now proved and what remains

The following statements are exact:

  1. the bare transition space is (1,2)⊕(3,2), (\mathbf1,\mathbf2)\oplus(\mathbf3,\mathbf2), (1,2)⊕(3,2), not (3,3)(\mathbf3,\mathbf3)(3,3);
  2. quadratic electron endomorphisms contain a (3,3)(\mathbf3,\mathbf3)(3,3) sector;
  3. a rank-one transition BQ=β∣n⟩⟨SQ∣B_Q=\beta|n\rangle\langle S_Q|BQ​=β∣n⟩⟨SQ​∣ gives exactly BQ†BQ=∣β∣2PQ; B_Q^\dagger B_Q=|\beta|^2P_Q; BQ†​BQ​=∣β∣2PQ​;
  4. PQP_QPQ​ has the full mixed correlation tensor required for frame lock;
  5. eliminating a positive-gap channel produces the sign that lowers the selected singlet ray.

The unresolved physical step is now highly specific:

does P3D∞P6 actually have a dominant singular channel whose electron right-singular vector is ∣SQscat⟩? \boxed{ \text{does }P_3D_\infty P_6\text{ actually have a dominant singular channel whose electron right-singular vector is }|S_{Q_{\rm scat}}\rangle? } does P3​D∞​P6​ actually have a dominant singular channel whose electron right-singular vector is ∣SQscat​​⟩?​

Equivalently, one must derive the singular-vector structure of the cross-depth transition operator from the master interaction and the nuclear/scattering background.

This is a substantially narrower problem than deriving an arbitrary full-rank mixed connection. The full (3,3)(3,3)(3,3) lock can emerge automatically at second order from a much smaller first-order transition space.
