Domain of the interaction result

Equation (11) is exact for constant gradients. Equation (14) is its leading local term with four first derivatives, with its coefficient also fixed by the static response calculation. It is not the complete scattering law of arbitrary finite pulses.

The correction (16) has a 1/r1/r1/r tail. Its static quadratic-form energy is finite, but its L2L^2L2 norm grows logarithmically with transverse cutoff. The original sigma model also has a gapless transverse continuum. An arbitrary time-dependent elimination consequently needs boundary conditions and a retarded spatial response; a gapped-mode approximation cannot simply be assumed. A finite collision must retain the additional profile deformation and any emitted field.

The equal-strength result in (7) and (14) is specific to this two-dimensional profile and this component action. It establishes a coherent geometrical interaction calculation with both wave polarizations, but neither a measured electromagnetic coefficient nor an interaction with the occupied electron or neutrino geometry has been derived.

Retarded displacement response constructs that causal spatial inverse and derives its low-frequency logarithm, positive spectral normalization and outgoing radiation.

Polarized pulse collisions solves the local induced-area equations with both full vector waveforms and derives their reciprocal energy-dependent delays.
