72. Beam-axis spinor decomposition: the orbit generator requires a bilinear lock term

Section 71 showed that a beam axis explains the twofold transverse degeneracy but not the longitudinal factor three. The spinor branching gives a sharper test.

Under the nucleus/scattering-induced split,

Spin(6)⊃Spin(3)O×Spin(3)H, Spin(6)\supset Spin(3)_O\times Spin(3)_H, Spin(6)⊃Spin(3)O​×Spin(3)H​,

the electron homogeneous-coordinate representation is

4=(2,2). \mathbf4=(\mathbf2,\mathbf2). 4=(2,2).

If the scattering state selects one axis in each three-plane, each spinor doublet acquires an axial Pauli generator. In the product basis

∣++⟩, ∣+−⟩, ∣−+⟩, ∣−−⟩, |++\rangle,\ |+-\rangle,\ |-+\rangle,\ |--\rangle, ∣++⟩, ∣+−⟩, ∣−+⟩, ∣−−⟩,

the natural diagonal operator basis is

I,σz⊗I,I⊗σz,σz⊗σz. I, \qquad \sigma_z\otimes I, \qquad I\otimes\sigma_z, \qquad \sigma_z\otimes\sigma_z. I,σz​⊗I,I⊗σz​,σz​⊗σz​.

The desired traceless orbit generator is

G~=14diag⁡(5,−3,−3,1). \widetilde G =\frac14\operatorname{diag}(5,-3,-3,1). G=41​diag(5,−3,−3,1).

72.1 Correct exact decomposition

Direct exact decomposition gives

G~=14 σz⊗I+14 I⊗σz+34 σz⊗σz \boxed{ \widetilde G =\frac14\,\sigma_z\otimes I +\frac14\,I\otimes\sigma_z +\frac34\,\sigma_z\otimes\sigma_z } G=41​σz​⊗I+41​I⊗σz​+43​σz​⊗σz​​

for the displayed product-state ordering.

This corrects the first write of this section, which incorrectly stated coefficients 1/2,1/2,1/41/2,1/2,1/41/2,1/2,1/4. The symbolic calculation itself returned the correct coefficients 1/4,1/4,3/41/4,1/4,3/41/4,1/4,3/4; the prose inserted afterward was wrong and must not be used.

72.2 Axial one-body generators are insufficient

Trying to represent G~\widetilde GG using only

I,σz⊗I,I⊗σz I, \qquad \sigma_z\otimes I, \qquad I\otimes\sigma_z I,σz​⊗I,I⊗σz​

has no solution.

Therefore the beam-selected axes in the observed and hidden spin frames do not by themselves generate the required orbit spectrum. A genuine two-frame bilinear is necessary:

σz⊗σz. \boxed{\sigma_z\otimes\sigma_z.} σz​⊗σz​.​

The missing ingredient is therefore an interaction between the two axial spin-frame labels, not merely another preferred direction.

72.3 Observed--hidden exchange symmetry fixes the bilinear magnitude

The ordering of the four product states is conventional, so all permutations of the four target eigenvalues were tested. If no physical relation between the two spin factors is imposed, the Pauli-basis coefficients depend on that relabelling.

However, the locked construction treats the observed and hidden Spin(3)Spin(3)Spin(3) factors symmetrically before the external scattering axis distinguishes a common direction. Requiring the one-body axial coefficients to respect exchange symmetry,

b=c b=c b=c

in

G~=b σz⊗I+c I⊗σz+d σz⊗σz, \widetilde G =b\,\sigma_z\otimes I +c\,I\otimes\sigma_z +d\,\sigma_z\otimes\sigma_z, G=bσz​⊗I+cI⊗σz​+dσz​⊗σz​,

leaves only exchange-symmetric relabellings whose bilinear coefficient has invariant magnitude

∣d∣=34. \boxed{|d|=\frac34.} ∣d∣=43​.​

Thus, within the observed--hidden exchange-symmetric identification, the required two-frame interaction strength relative to the chosen primitive generator normalization is not 1/41/41/4 but 3/43/43/4.

(54,−34,−34,14). \left(\frac54,-\frac34,-\frac34,\frac14\right). (45​,−43​,−43​,41​).

72.6 Revised binary proof target

The strongest remaining operator-level test is now:

Does the depth-six projected interaction, in the beam-axis-adapted (2,2)(\mathbf2,\mathbf2)(2,2) basis and with observed--hidden exchange symmetry, contain a traceless block proportional to

14σz⊗I+14I⊗σz+34σz⊗σz? \boxed{ \frac14\sigma_z\otimes I +\frac14I\otimes\sigma_z +\frac34\sigma_z\otimes\sigma_z? } 41​σz​⊗I+41​I⊗σz​+43​σz​⊗σz​?​

Equivalently, if the one-body axial normalization is tied to g66=1/4g_{66}=1/4g66​=1/4, does the same projected geometry force the bilinear coefficient to be
