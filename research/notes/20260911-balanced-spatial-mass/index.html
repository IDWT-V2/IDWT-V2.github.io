A spatial solution of the normalization problem

A nonconstant spatial field can produce a constant scalar potential without requiring the interaction kernel itself to be constant. The distinction matters for the source-preservation theorem: its whole-band degeneracy conclusion assumes a coherent holomorphic section. Certain noncoherent holomorphic superpositions instead balance their source moments and preserve an exact linear branch for the already specified smooth spatial moment kernel.

The result below constructs such fields and treats their complete spatial linearization, including the entire holomorphic multiplet and the excited source modes. It solves a conditional spatial normalization and spectral stability problem. The kernel's origin in the master action and its causal completion remain open.

What is now available for the mass calculation

These balanced fields have a common normalized frequency ω=g\omega=gω=g without a profile-dependent cubic or higher-order correction. They therefore satisfy the spatial source-preservation requirement identified by the nonlinear reduction, and their complete linear spectrum passes the stability test above.

The frequency-energy distinction remains visible in (17). The observed rest mass, physical metric scale, occupied section degree, causal master kernel and nonlinear orbital stability are not determined here. That larger field bundle has a different spatial response, so the linear stability theorem above is not automatically its theorem.
