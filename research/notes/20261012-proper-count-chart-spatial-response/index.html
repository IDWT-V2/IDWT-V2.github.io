Spatial Response of Proper-Current Count Charts

Updated October 12, 2026

The geometric envelope gives exact nonlinear spatial persistence when its affine charts use the preferred-time density rho. Replacing rho with the Lorentz-scalar proper current n=sqrt(j_mu j^mu) preserves the stationary clock, but changes the spatial response. Neither flattening the scalar-ratio curvature nor reducing the carrier columns automatically removes that response.

This note compares the two carriers under the same proper-current chart at every occupied depth. Ground states are preparation controls, not the assigned excited electron, muon, tau or other particle states. Their growth does not negate the exact count solutions, observer transformation or local rotation-form theorems, and is not an observed lifetime.

4. Reproduction and Consequences

An exact polynomial continuation now proves closure of a smaller eight-dimensional family at depths two, three, four, six and ten. Its depth-ten member is axial and remains closed despite the larger family leakage. At the stated ground preparation, every real scalar curvature retains growth at six and in this explicit depth-ten family. Depth five needs a larger coupled space. These results constrain the proper-current continuation, not the preferred-time nonlinear theorem or assigned excited particles.

proper-envelope-ground-response.py and its report pass 90 independent exact/numerical comparisons on the full matrix. minimal-carrier-ground-response.py and its report retain the closure residuals and all reported time-generator eigenvalues.

The test narrows a proposed simplification: replacing preferred-time density with the field's own proper current changes dynamics that must be retained. The existing preferred-time nonlinear theorem remains valid, as do the exact mass clocks and geometric forms. The responding same-field clock construction remains a distinct covariant option with its stated load and ground linear theorem. None of these comparisons establishes decay, assigned excited-state persistence, localization or new empirical preference for the occupied sector depths.
