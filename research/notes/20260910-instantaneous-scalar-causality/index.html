Causal limit of an instantaneous scalar moment interaction

Updated September 10, 2026

Summary: Smooth spatially separated initial data show that the instantaneous scalar moment equation changes a local current before causal contact, so its physical use requires a controlled retarded reduction.

Consequence for the candidate interaction

The reciprocal spatial pair remains a valid calculation of its specified mathematical action. Its overlap changes and full-spinor deformations cannot yet be interpreted as a causal fundamental particle interaction.

An effective instantaneous approximation could still be useful in a regime derived from local same-field dynamics. It must specify the retained spatial modes, response frequencies, initial conditions and error from neglecting propagation. The local Dirac retarded response supplies one explicit calculation in which both propagation channels follow from the written scalar term and signals have the required delayed support. Its static approximation has a frequency bound. That calculation does not derive the projective moment action or identify a physical background.

Verification

local_dirac_response_checks.py computes both normalized smooth source moments with independent quadrature refinement, verifies the unchanged total source, and differentiates the local current independently of (2). Its separate full nonlinear local Dirac propagation control has zero receiver response before causal arrival. The contrast tests the equations' domain of dependence, without assigning electric charge, weak interaction rates or an antiparticle interpretation.
