Coherent orbit readout

Updated September 8, 2026

Physical premise (IDWT): There is one infinite-dimensional physical manifold. All sectors form one nested chain of submanifolds within it. Particle depth counts literal macroscopic spatial dimensions. The electron's CP3 sector has six; nuclear 3+3 locking constrains ordinary matter and observers to an experienced three-dimensional sector. Unobserved spatial directions remain physical. Summary: A selected-monomial readout of the coherent degree-L lift maps an actual CP3 trajectory to the demo's four coefficients, with explicit preparation and omitted-mode boundaries.

An explicit nonlinear map, not equality of spaces

Recorded 2026-09-06 17:21 UTC. A normalized geometric Z in C4 has the coherent symmetric lift v_L(Z)=Z tensor-power L in Sym^L(C4), of norm one. In the normalized monomial basis, select the same four modes used by the demo. Their coefficients before the even/odd basis change are

fL(Z)=(Z1L,Z2L,LZ3Z4L−1,Z4L). f_L(Z)=\left(Z_1^L,Z_2^L,\sqrt L Z_3Z_4^{L-1},Z_4^L\right). fL​(Z)=(Z1L​,Z2L​,L​Z3​Z4L−1​,Z4L​).

Every component has the same degree, so [f_L(Z)] is independent of the homogeneous representative. It is defined wherever f_L is nonzero. For L>=2 the base point [0:0:1:0] is excluded; at L=1 the map is the identity up to the stated basis choice. The clock and radial readout impose their additional nonzero-denominator restrictions as in the demo.

Under the existing base generator G=diag(1,−1,−1,0), these components have weights (L,−L,−1,0). Their subspace is invariant under this action. Consequently applying f_L after the base evolution gives exactly the same four-coefficient evolution as applying the selected representation generator after f_L. The even/odd first-pair transformation gives the demo's L sigma_x block; the common central phase remains irrelevant.

This is an explicit nonlinear map from an actual proposed sector point to the coefficient ray used by the displayed readout. It is not an identification of CP3 with the full section space or with a Dirac field.

Preparation reproducing the demo

For its A,B>0 with A²+B²=1, define

r=(A/B)1/L,D=2r2+1/L+1,Z0=1D(r,r,1/L,1). r=(A/B)^{1/L},\qquad D=2r^2+1/L+1,\qquad Z_0=\frac1{\sqrt D}(r,r,1/\sqrt L,1). r=(A/B)1/L,D=2r2+1/L+1,Z0​=D​1​(r,r,1/L​,1).

For L>1 there are many nonzero omitted coefficients in the full coherent lift. They have not been removed from the physical state by this algebra. If f_L is used as a deterministic detector readout, the detector coupling must justify which components it resolves and their normalization. If it is instead called a projected quantum state, postselection and its retained weight must be accounted for. The two interpretations are not interchangeable.

What this closes and what remains

Given the existing G, preparation, and selected-component readout, the coefficient trajectory and hence the demo's petal formula can now be obtained from one actual CP3 trajectory through an explicit map. This is useful progress on the previously unspecified coordinate-to-coefficient bridge. The map is not generally a symplectic identification and is not claimed to be a uniquely forced detector law.

It does not show that the induced response model generates this particular G. Matching one instantaneous source spectrum remains insufficient. It also does not supply the spatial Dirac solution, the physical electromagnetic current, or the apparatus interaction. The ring's degree-zero obstruction remains; this construction treats L>=1.

Checks

The subsequent dimensional precession test constructs the same displayed formula from a circle in a smaller sector using a different chosen nonlinear readout. This bridge therefore does not establish a minimum particle depth unless the physical readout is restricted and justified independently. That counterexample is about identifiability of the displayed trace, not equality of the underlying trajectories.
