19. Exchange symmetry and Pauli suppression of full-depth contact

Two electrons are identical fermions. The physical two-electron state must be antisymmetric under

(q1,s1)↔(q2,s2), (q_1,s_1)\leftrightarrow(q_2,s_2), (q1​,s1​)↔(q2​,s2​),

where sis_isi​ denotes the remaining spin/polarization labels.

Consequently a pointlike symmetric scalar contact at the diagonal can be suppressed or vanish for channels whose sector-coordinate wavefunction is antisymmetric at coincidence.

Schematically, for an antisymmetric relative state

Ψ−(q1,q2)=−Ψ−(q2,q1), \Psi_-(q_1,q_2) =-\Psi_-(q_2,q_1), Ψ−​(q1​,q2​)=−Ψ−​(q2​,q1​),

we have on the diagonal

Ψ−(q,q)=0. \boxed{\Psi_-(q,q)=0.} Ψ−​(q,q)=0.​

Therefore

⟨Ψ−∣δΔ6∣Ψ−⟩=0 \langle\Psi_-|\delta_{\Delta_6}|\Psi_-\rangle=0 ⟨Ψ−​∣δΔ6​​∣Ψ−​⟩=0

for an ideal order-zero point contact.

A symmetric spatial/sector channel paired with the appropriate antisymmetric spin state need not vanish. Finite-width, derivative, or tensor interactions can also survive in channels where the strict scalar delta contact is zero.

This supplies an additional, independent reason why genuine six-dimensional electron-electron contact could be much rarer or more channel-selective than ordinary electromagnetic Moller scattering.

It also predicts that any full-depth signal should be analyzed versus beam polarization rather than only total luminosity.
