48. One- and two-vector outer-product mixed ansatzes

The simultaneous commutant of (J1,J2)(J_1,J_2)(J1​,J2​) admits no mixed map between its canonical

E0(2)⊕E4(4) E_0^{(2)}\oplus E_4^{(4)} E0(2)​⊕E4(4)​

pieces. A collision, however, supplies additional covariant data, beginning with the relative momentum

p=prel. p=p_{\rm rel}. p=prel​.

Decompose it with the pair-defined projectors:

p0=P0p,p4=P4p. p_0=P_0p, \qquad p_4=P_4p. p0​=P0​p,p4​=P4​p.

The simplest real symmetric mixed tensor constructed from the pair and the collision momentum is

Mp=p0p4T+p4p0T. \boxed{ M_p=p_0p_4^T+p_4p_0^T. } Mp​=p0​p4T​+p4​p0T​.​

This is no longer forbidden by the static-pair commutant theorem because ppp breaks the pair stabilizer.

Its off-diagonal block is

Bp=p0p4T:E4→E0. B_p=p_0p_4^T:E_4\to E_0. Bp​=p0​p4T​:E4​→E0​.

Being an outer product,

rank⁡Bp≤1. \boxed{\operatorname{rank}B_p\le1.} rankBp​≤1.​

All 2×22\times22×2 minors vanish identically, and a generic numerical specialization has rank exactly one.

This particular outer-product ansatz opens one mixed direction. The rank bound does not classify all tensors constructed using a vector and the complex structures.

48.1 Adding impact parameter or a second current direction

A scattering state also contains an independent transverse/impact vector bbb. The next mixed tensor can contain

Bp,b=p0p4T+b0b4T. B_{p,b} =p_0p_4^T+b_0b_4^T. Bp,b​=p0​p4T​+b0​b4T​.

Generically this has rank two, the maximum possible rank of a map

E4(4)→E0(2). E_4^{(4)}\to E_0^{(2)}. E4(4)​→E0(2)​.

Therefore the complete pair-native 2+42+42+4 decomposition can at most correlate two independent active combinations directly with its fixed two-plane.

This confirms that the free-pair 2+42+42+4 geometry is not the same structure as the laboratory-visible 3+33+33+3 collision split. The latter requires the observer/beam geometry as additional physical data.

48.2 Consequence for the tested ansatzes

For the restricted commuting class and the displayed outer-product constructions:

(J1,J2)⟹no mixed map in the simultaneous commutant, (J_1,J_2) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \text{no mixed map in the simultaneous commutant}, (J1​,J2​)⟹no mixed map in the simultaneous commutant,

while

(J1,J2,prel)⟹rank-one mixed map, (J_1,J_2,p_{\rm rel}) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \text{rank-one mixed map}, (J1​,J2​,prel​)⟹rank-one mixed map,

and

(J1,J2,prel,b)⟹generically rank-two mixed map. (J_1,J_2,p_{\rm rel},b) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \text{generically rank-two mixed map}. (J1​,J2​,prel​,b)⟹generically rank-two mixed map.

For the original beam question, the natural next step is therefore to include the laboratory-selected observed three-plane O3O_3O3​ and its orthogonal complement H3H_3H3​. A beam experiment physically supplies this split; it need not be generated by the unordered pair alone.
