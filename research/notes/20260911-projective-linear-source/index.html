Preserving a holomorphic section forces a constant potential

Let VVV be a smooth real scalar function on this connected spatial manifold, and let χ\chiχ be a nonzero holomorphic section. Then

Smoothness therefore extends dV=0dV=0dV=0 over that set, and connectedness makes VVV constant globally. The converse is immediate. The argument concerns a scalar multiplication operator on actual spatial sections, not an arbitrary matrix on their coefficient space.

For local contact K=IK=IK=I and L>0L>0L>0, this cannot hold: every nonzero holomorphic section of this line bundle has zeros. Restrict its homogeneous polynomial to a projective line on which it is not identically zero; a positive-degree homogeneous polynomial in two complex coordinates has a projective zero. Its squared norm therefore cannot be a positive spatial constant. The field must acquire a complementary spatial response if it continues as a stationary solution of that local equation.

Equation (2) does not say that the kernel itself must be spatially constant. A nonconstant kernel can send a particular source to a constant. Its action on the full source family is the next question.

Exact scalar linearity and coherent shape selection impose separate requirements. A covariant scalar kernel that preserves a coherent holomorphic branch at degree LLL makes the whole holomorphic band degenerate. A kernel that retains its spatial harmonics instead generates a calculable complementary correction. Noncoherent holomorphic sections with balanced source moments, derivative terms, additional same-field structure or non-holomorphic occupied fields could change these hypotheses; their dynamics and normalization must then be derived. The result constrains this mass-operator route without changing the assigned particle depths or claiming a determined physical geometry.
