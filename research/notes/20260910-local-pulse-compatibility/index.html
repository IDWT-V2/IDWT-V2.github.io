Updated September 10, 2026

Summary: Independent full spatial evolutions of the local scalar Dirac equation show reciprocal pulse deformations that increase or decrease normalized scalar-pattern compatibility while preserving the combined polarization matrix.

An autonomous local encounter

Both complex spatial patterns evolve; there is no prescribed target, externally forced probe or instantaneous spatial convolution. The scalar cross term of the one field is retained at each spatial point. This control uses zero bare mass and fields homogeneous in transverse ordinary spatial directions. It is not a transversely bound particle pair or a derived relation between different depths.

The projective spatial pair tests a different, specified nonlocal action. The present calculation tests changing compatibility in a local equation, while retaining its more restricted flat plane pulse geometry. The uniform-background response instead uses a prescribed probe and a different background.

Interpretation

This calculation supplies a concrete mechanism for reciprocal change of spatial compatibility within the written local scalar equation. It also supplies exact polarization-sensitive noninteractions under that same rule. Both results retain the complete scalar cross term, physical propagation, complex phases and the response of both patterns.

Its limits remain substantial: the pulses are unbound in transverse space, their geometry is flat, their physical particle identity is unspecified, and distinct-depth embeddings and observed electric and weak vertices have not been derived. Successful conservation and convergence establish the conditional field calculation, not experimental support for IDWT.
