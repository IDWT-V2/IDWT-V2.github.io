Updated September 11, 2026

Summary: An inverse polynomial classification gives nine local Hermitian dynamics with exact simplex frequencies on the specified six-dimensional Dirac pair, while canonical normalization fixes their action and the original unchanged kinetic term cannot realize that spectrum through a scalar polynomial alone.

Physical scope of the reconstruction

Each operator (8) is Hermitian on smooth spatial fields and has a self-adjoint realization defined by the complete harmonic decomposition. Its unitary linear evolution preserves the full canonical norm. Finite linear superpositions do not develop exponentially growing amplitudes under this fixed quadratic action.

The complete scalar-response comparison adds the same local interaction to each reconstructed action. Equal frequencies allow different spatial responses; both frequency branches retain a fixed inclusive scalar strength. The complete nonlinear source also drives spatial components beyond a chosen pure occupied mode.

That statement is not nonlinear particle stability. These equations permit every spectral level and do not select the assigned occupied states. They do not by themselves specify a nonlinear interaction between two particle structures. Locality as a differential expression also does not establish a finite propagation cone for a higher-order spatial equation.

The complete curved causality test now proves that every member of (8), used on all spatial modes, lacks a finite propagation speed. The test uses a local spatial commutator and retains both frequency branches. These actions therefore need a controlled effective interpretation or a different causal completion.

The inverse calculation supplies explicit actions that meet the target frequency identity and a complete classification within (8). Their physical coupling and state selection remain additional constraints. This approach is consistent with the distinction between an effective action and its deeper origin in Burgess, Section 3.3. No scale hierarchy or effective cutoff has been derived here.
