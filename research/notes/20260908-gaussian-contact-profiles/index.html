Spatial model

Consider two normalized densities on mmm shared ordinary spatial coordinates of the nested physical manifold. A Gaussian is a specified local flat-space profile, with centre XXX and positive-definite covariance matrix Σ\SigmaΣ:

Thus ⟨x⟩=X\langle x\rangle=X⟨x⟩=X and ⟨(x−X)(x−X)T⟩=Σ\langle(x-X)(x-X)^T\rangle=\Sigma⟨(x−X)(x−X)T⟩=Σ. Covariance eigenvalues have units of length squared. The Gaussian describes spatial extent; an actual orbit or a time-averaged exposure requires its own dynamical specification.

Contact across nested depths

For a higher-depth density on coordinates z∈Rdz\in\mathbb R^dz∈Rd, choose an orthogonal shared-coordinate projection x=Bzx=Bzx=Bz, with BBT=1mBB^T=\mathbf1_mBBT=1m​. Completing BBB to an orthogonal coordinate frame identifies the remaining ordinary spatial coordinates yyy. A Gaussian with mean XBX_BXB​ and covariance ΣB\Sigma_BΣB​ has full marginal

ρˉB(x)=∫ρB(x,y) dd−my=ρBΣBBT, BXB(x). \bar\rho_B(x)=\int\rho_B(x,y)\,d^{d-m}y =\rho_{B\Sigma_BB^T,\,BX_B}(x). ρˉ​B​(x)=∫ρB​(x,y)dd−my=ρBΣB​BT,BXB​​(x).

Indeed its characteristic function is the higher one evaluated at BTkB^TkBTk, which proves the covariance rule even when shared and remaining coordinates are correlated. Apply the contact formula with C=ΣA+BΣBBTC=\Sigma_A+B\Sigma_BB^TC=ΣA​+BΣB​BT and R=XA−BXBR=X_A-BX_BR=XA​−BXB​.

The cylindrical contact premise uses a lower-depth density independent of yyy. Fubini then gives ∫ρA(x)ρB(x,y) dx dy=∫ρA(x)ρˉB(x) dx\int\rho_A(x)\rho_B(x,y)\,dx\,dy=\int\rho_A(x)\bar\rho_B(x)\,dx∫ρA​(x)ρB​(x,y)dxdy=∫ρA​(x)ρˉ​B​(x)dx. This uses a cylindrical field normalized on its supported submanifold. A box-normalized probability extension instead carries its explicit inverse fibre-volume factor.

Every coordinate in this construction belongs to the same nested physical manifold. The projection describes the smaller sector's shared directions; the containing particle's dimensions remain equivalent in physical kind.
