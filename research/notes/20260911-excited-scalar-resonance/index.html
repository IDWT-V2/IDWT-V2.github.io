The inverse interaction must support a responsive spatial field

Consequence for reconstructing the mass dynamics

In an inverse law F(J)F(J)F(J), its values must support the complete stationary profiles. Its derivative controls the ordinary and conjugate feedback in (5), with ggg replaced by the appropriate local F′(J)F'(J)F′(J) in the source derivative. A law that gives a desired frequency can still produce an unacceptable spatial response.

This gives a concrete additional inverse condition: the same interaction must pass the full spatial source test, the common-law test and the required stability test. Changing the scalar response to repair an unstable branch also changes coupling; those adjustments cannot be treated as unrelated parameters. The local quartic law supplies a reproducible example of this constraint, while the physical particle fields remain unidentified.

Verification

These calculations provide numerical convergence evidence; the finite matrix alone is not a continuum stability proof.
