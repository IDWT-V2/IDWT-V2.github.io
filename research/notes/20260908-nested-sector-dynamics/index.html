Nested sector dynamics

Updated September 8, 2026

What nesting supplies

All spatial sectors in V2 belong to one nested chain in the same infinite physical manifold. Their dimensions are ordinary macroscopic spatial directions. Describing their relationship through functions needs two steps: defining the lift of a function and checking the lift of its dynamics.

For an inclusion i:Qa↪Qbi:Q_a\hookrightarrow Q_bi:Qa​↪Qb​, the canonical function map is restriction, in the opposite direction:

i∗:C∞(Qb)⟶C∞(Qa),i∗F=F∘i. i^*:C^\infty(Q_b)\longrightarrow C^\infty(Q_a),\qquad i^*F=F\circ i. i∗:C∞(Qb​)⟶C∞(Qa​),i∗F=F∘i.

To lift functions from QaQ_aQa​ to QbQ_bQb​ cylindrically, specify a smooth projection π:Qb→Qa\pi:Q_b\to Q_aπ:Qb​→Qa​ with π∘i=id\pi\circ i=\mathrm{id}π∘i=id. Then

J=π∗,Jf=f∘π,i∗Jf=f J=\pi^*,\qquad Jf=f\circ\pi,\qquad i^*Jf=f J=π∗,Jf=f∘π,i∗Jf=f

is an injective algebra map. In a product chart (x,y)(x,y)(x,y), this is simply Jf(x,y)=f(x)Jf(x,y)=f(x)Jf(x,y)=f(x). Nested inclusions alone do not supply a global π\piπ. The sector geometry audit already identifies this geometric limitation.

When a lower-sector equation is preserved

For a time-independent lift JJJ, an exact evolution lift requires

HbJf=JHaf H_bJf=JH_af Hb​Jf=JHa​f

on a domain preserved by the relevant evolutions. For unbounded operators, domain compatibility is part of the assertion; a symbolic differential identity alone is not a complete operator theorem.

A direct differential example is

Ha=−Δx+Va(x),Hb=−Δx−Δy+Va(x). H_a=-\Delta_x+V_a(x),\qquad H_b=-\Delta_x-\Delta_y+V_a(x). Ha​=−Δx​+Va​(x),Hb​=−Δx​−Δy​+Va​(x).

Since ΔyJf=0\Delta_yJf=0Δy​Jf=0, the displayed intertwining identity holds on smooth cylindrical fields. If instead HbH_bHb​ contains κxy\kappa xyκxy in a two-coordinate example, then

HbJf−JHaf=κxyf(x), H_bJf-JH_af=\kappa xyf(x), Hb​Jf−JHa​f=κxyf(x),

which depends on yyy and leaves the cylindrical class. Coordinate nesting survives, but this exact autonomous lower-sector evolution does not.

A constant extension is not square-integrable over a noncompact fibre of infinite volume. In that case the preceding calculation is a statement about fields or generalized modes, not a normalized Hilbert-space embedding. For a Hilbert-space version one can instead specify a normalized transverse eigenfunction χ\chiχ and use Jχf=fχJ_\chi f=f\chiJχ​f=fχ; a separated Hamiltonian then gives

HbJχf=Jχ(Ha+ϵ)f,Hyχ=ϵχ. H_bJ_\chi f=J_\chi(H_a+\epsilon)f,\qquad H_y\chi=\epsilon\chi. Hb​Jχ​f=Jχ​(Ha​+ϵ)f,Hy​χ=ϵχ.

Such an eigenfunction and its boundary conditions must actually exist. They are extra model data, not a derivation of cylindrical continuation.

Consequence for interactions

Nesting allows a shared-coordinate description; it does not guarantee that every interacting sector retains its isolated spectrum. A nonzero coupling can introduce backreaction, mix states, or shift energies. Use the explicit intertwining condition when exact inheritance is needed, and an effective reduction when it fails.

The spatial structure checks verify the separated and mixed differential examples.
