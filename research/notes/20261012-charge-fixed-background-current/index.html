The Charge-Fixed Dynamical Background Current

Updated October 12, 2026

The assigned-shell calculation leaves the first coexact background harmonic dynamical because its static field operator is singular. Its current response can nevertheless be calculated. The distinction follows from the equations and conserved matrix charges; it does not require removing the field mode or prescribing a response. Here “charge” denotes a Noether matrix integral, not electric charge.

1. The Conserved Matrix Integrals

On an affine plateau the complete retained equations have Hermitian left-acting Hamiltonian sources. Consequently each matrix block obeys a matrix-valued continuity equation, before taking a trace:

∂t(B†B)+∇i(B†αiB)=0,∂t(Φ†Φ)+∇i(Φ†αiΦ)=0.(1) \partial_t(B^\dagger B)+\nabla_i(B^\dagger\alpha_iB)=0, \qquad \partial_t(\Phi^\dagger\Phi)+\nabla_i(\Phi^\dagger\alpha_i\Phi)=0. \tag{1} ∂t​(B†B)+∇i​(B†αi​B)=0,∂t​(Φ†Φ)+∇i​(Φ†αi​Φ)=0.(1)

The cancellation uses the same source and its adjoint in the two terms; spatial variation of the source does not introduce a derivative here. On the closed conditional sphere,

QB=∫B†B,QΦ=∫Φ†Φ(2) \mathsf Q_B=\int B^\dagger B,\qquad \mathsf Q_\Phi=\int\Phi^\dagger\Phi \tag{2} QB​=∫B†B,QΦ​=∫Φ†Φ(2)

are separately conserved Hermitian matrices. They are the integrals associated with the separate constant right-unitary symmetries of the stated two-block action. The block split remains an additional premise.

For the positive Killing background B0=ρBKB_0=\sqrt{\rho_B}KB0​=ρB​​K, a free zero-shell variation is δB=KC\delta B=K CδB=KC with constant matrix CCC. Its linear charge variation is proportional to C+C†C+C^\daggerC+C†, since K†K=I/rK^\dagger K=I/rK†K=I/r. Fixing the full matrix (2) to first order removes its Hermitian component. The remaining anti-Hermitian component is an actual tangent δB=B0T\delta B=B_0TδB=B0​T to the right-unitary orbit. Variations orthogonal to the background Dirac shell contribute zero to this linear integrated charge. Fixing only the scalar trace would be insufficient.
