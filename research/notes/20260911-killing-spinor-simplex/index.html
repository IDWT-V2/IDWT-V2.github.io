Transfer of the simplex operator to spatial spinors

Updated September 11, 2026

Summary: A complete Killing-spinor frame transfers the scalar simplex operator to a local spinor operator, but its two Dirac branches and the real action distinguish this spectrum from an algebraic scalar mass.

An exact transfer, with the mass question kept explicit

The critical conformal scalar operator has normalized eigenvalues S(ℓ,d)=(ℓ+d−1d)S(\ell,d)=\binom{\ell+d-1}{d}S(ℓ,d)=(dℓ+d−1​). It can be transferred to the full ordinary spinor bundle using Killing spinors. The transferred operator is local and self-adjoint, with coefficients fixed by the specified geometry.

Its direct insertion into the scalar mass position of the canonical Dirac Hamiltonian is not self-adjoint. Taking the real action changes the coefficient to the average of adjacent simplex values. Both facts follow from retaining the two Dirac signs and the full variation.

The projective spinor polynomial supplies a separate local construction on a specified CP3 bundle. Its squared Dirac polynomial has the same simplex value on both signs and admits a Hermitian scalar insertion. The obstruction proved here concerns the stated round-sphere transfer, rather than all spatial spinor operators.

Throughout, SRdS_R^dSRd​ is an assumed actual physical spatial geometry, d=2hd=2hd=2h, with undetermined radius RRR. All ddd directions are ordinary macroscopic spatial directions. The calculation neither compactifies dimensions nor selects physical topology. Spinor components and a mathematical frame do not add spatial dimensions.
