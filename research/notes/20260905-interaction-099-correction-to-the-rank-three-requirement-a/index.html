99. Correction to the rank-three requirement: a generic rank-two orbital bivector uniquely selects an SO(3)SO(3)SO(3) frame by orientation-preserving Procrustes completion

Section 55 correctly proved that the mixed block of one simple orbital bivector has rank at most two,

EL=xOpHT−pOxHT,rank⁡EL≤2. E_L=x_Op_H^T-p_Ox_H^T, \qquad \operatorname{rank}E_L\le2. EL​=xO​pHT​−pO​xHT​,rankEL​≤2.

It then drew the stronger conclusion that rank two is insufficient to determine a complete three-frame relation. That conclusion is false once the desired frame map is restricted to

Q∈SO(3). Q\in SO(3). Q∈SO(3).

Two independent oriented vector correspondences determine the third axis by orientation. Equivalently, a generic rank-two 3×33\times33×3 tensor has a unique orientation-preserving orthogonal Procrustes factor.

This materially strengthens the scattering route.

99.1 Exact factorization of the orbital mixed block

Write

AO=[xO  pO],AH=[xH  pH], A_O=[x_O\;p_O], \qquad A_H=[x_H\;p_H], AO​=[xO​pO​],AH​=[xH​pH​],

and

J2=(01−10). J_2= \begin{pmatrix} 0&1\\ -1&0 \end{pmatrix}. J2​=(0−1​10​).

Then

EL=AOJ2AHT. \boxed{ E_L=A_OJ_2A_H^T. } EL​=AO​J2​AHT​.​

If

xO∦pO x_O\not\parallel p_O xO​∥pO​

and

xH∦pH, x_H\not\parallel p_H, xH​∥pH​,

both AOA_OAO​ and AHA_HAH​ have column rank two and J2J_2J2​ is invertible. Therefore

rank⁡EL=2. \boxed{ \operatorname{rank}E_L=2. } rankEL​=2.​

Its left and right null directions are exactly the two orbital-plane normals:

ELT(xO×pO)=0, \boxed{ E_L^T(x_O\times p_O)=0, } ELT​(xO​×pO​)=0,​

EL(xH×pH)=0. \boxed{ E_L(x_H\times p_H)=0. } EL​(xH​×pH​)=0.​

Thus the missing third axis is not absent geometrically. It is already fixed, up to sign, by the oriented normals of the two rank-two planes.

99.2 Rank-two 3×33\times33×3 tensors have a unique SO(3)SO(3)SO(3) Procrustes maximizer

Let a real rank-two tensor have singular-value decomposition

E=U(σ1000σ20000)VT,σ1,σ2>0. E=U \begin{pmatrix} \sigma_1&0&0\\ 0&\sigma_2&0\\ 0&0&0 \end{pmatrix} V^T, \qquad \sigma_1,\sigma_2>0. E=U​σ1​00​0σ2​0​000​​VT,σ1​,σ2​>0.

Because the third singular vectors correspond to zero singular value, their signs may be chosen so that

U,V∈SO(3). U,V\in SO(3). U,V∈SO(3).

Consider the orientation-preserving Procrustes problem

max⁡Q∈SO(3)tr⁡(QTE). \max_{Q\in SO(3)}\operatorname{tr}(Q^TE). Q∈SO(3)max​tr(QTE).

Write

Q=URVT,R∈SO(3). Q=URV^T, \qquad R\in SO(3). Q=URVT,R∈SO(3).

Then

tr⁡(QTE)=σ1R11+σ2R22. \operatorname{tr}(Q^TE) = \sigma_1R_{11}+\sigma_2R_{22}. tr(QTE)=σ1​R11​+σ2​R22​.

Since every diagonal entry of an orthogonal matrix is at most one,

tr⁡(QTE)≤σ1+σ2. \operatorname{tr}(Q^TE) \le \sigma_1+\sigma_2. tr(QTE)≤σ1​+σ2​.

Equality requires

R11=R22=1. R_{11}=R_{22}=1. R11​=R22​=1.

Orthogonality then forces the first two rows and columns to be the first two coordinate basis vectors, and the condition

det⁡R=1 \det R=1 detR=1

forces

R33=1. R_{33}=1. R33​=1.

Hence

R=I3 R=I_3 R=I3​

is the unique maximizer. Therefore

QE=UVT \boxed{ Q_E=UV^T } QE​=UVT​

is a uniquely selected element of SO(3)SO(3)SO(3) even though

det⁡E=0. \det E=0. detE=0.

So full matrix rank is not required for full orientation selection in three dimensions.

99.4 Consequence for the scattering hierarchy

The ranking in Section 55 must therefore be revised.

A rank-one mixed source leaves a continuous axial ambiguity and is insufficient. But a generic rank-two source already fixes two independent directions, and the orientation condition fixes the third. Thus

rank⁡E=2is generically sufficient to select a unique Q∈SO(3). \boxed{ \operatorname{rank}E=2 \quad\text{is generically sufficient to select a unique }Q\in SO(3). } rankE=2is generically sufficient to select a unique Q∈SO(3).​

The scattering-triad tensor

Etri E_{\rm tri} Etri​

remains a valid full-rank construction, but it is no longer the minimal one.

The simpler orbital bivector

L=x∧p \boxed{ L=x\wedge p } L=x∧p​

already supplies, through its mixed block,

EL=xOpHT−pOxHT, \boxed{ E_L=x_Op_H^T-p_Ox_H^T, } EL​=xO​pHT​−pO​xHT​,​

a generically rank-two bifundamental tensor whose unique SO(3)SO(3)SO(3) Procrustes completion defines

QL. \boxed{Q_L.} QL​.​

This is substantially more economical because xxx and ppp are the ordinary phase-space data of one relative orbit; no independently postulated third dyadic term is needed.

99.5 Relation to the second-order lock theorem

Section 95 showed that the first-order cross-depth transition itself need not contain a full (3,3)(\mathbf3,\mathbf3)(3,3) correlation tensor. A much smaller transition can produce

PQ P_Q PQ​

and hence

TQ T_Q TQ​

at second order.

Section 99 now removes an analogous unnecessary requirement on the classical orientation source. The source tensor need not be full rank either. It only has to determine a unique orientation-preserving frame map.

Therefore the required chain can be weakened from

full-rank Etri⟶Qscat \text{full-rank }E_{\rm tri} \longrightarrow Q_{\rm scat} full-rank Etri​⟶Qscat​

to

generic rank-two EL⟶QL⟶PQL⟶TQL. \boxed{ \text{generic rank-two }E_L \longrightarrow Q_L \longrightarrow P_{Q_L} \longrightarrow T_{Q_L}. } generic rank-two EL​⟶QL​⟶PQL​​⟶TQL​​.​

This is a genuine reduction in the amount of dynamical structure V2 must derive.

99.6 Degenerate cases are now sharply classified

For the orbital tensor

EL=AOJ2AHT, E_L=A_OJ_2A_H^T, EL​=AO​J2​AHT​,

rank falls below two exactly when at least one orbital plane degenerates:

xO∥pO x_O\parallel p_O xO​∥pO​

or

xH∥pH. x_H\parallel p_H. xH​∥pH​.

Then only one or zero independent directions are correlated and a continuous frame ambiguity returns.

Thus the physical distinction is not

rank three versus rank two, \text{rank three versus rank two}, rank three versus rank two,

but

two independent oriented directions versus fewer than two. \boxed{ \text{two independent oriented directions versus fewer than two}. } two independent oriented directions versus fewer than two.​

This matches ordinary three-dimensional frame geometry: two noncollinear axes determine the third by the right-hand rule.

99.7 Revised bottleneck

The source audit of Section 98 remains valid: the current non-note V2 corpus does not yet insert an orbital/scattering bifundamental into the electron--nucleus kernel. But the missing source is now simpler than Section 98 assumed.

One no longer needs to derive a full-rank tensor such as

Etri. E_{\rm tri}. Etri​.

It is enough to derive a nondegenerate mixed orbital block

EL=PO(x∧p)PH \boxed{ E_L=P_O(x\wedge p)P_H } EL​=PO​(x∧p)PH​​

or any equivalent rank-two bifundamental whose SO(3)SO(3)SO(3) Procrustes completion is unique.

The next source-level test is therefore:

does the actual 3↔6 projected interaction retain the mixed orbital bivector PO(x∧p)PH or an equivalent two-direction correlation? \boxed{ \text{does the actual }3\leftrightarrow6\text{ projected interaction retain the mixed orbital bivector }P_O(x\wedge p)P_H\text{ or an equivalent two-direction correlation?} } does the actual 3↔6 projected interaction retain the mixed orbital bivector PO​(x∧p)PH​ or an equivalent two-direction correlation?​

If yes, the orientation problem is much closer to closure than the earlier full-rank criterion suggested. If every source-level cross-depth term removes or averages away this rank-two orbital information, the orientation route still fails.
