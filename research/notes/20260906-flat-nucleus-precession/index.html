Flat nucleus precession

Updated September 8, 2026

Summary: The fixed nuclear three-plane and an invariant CP3 binding energy yield an exact conditional precession flow, with conserved misalignment and no automatically selected petal lock.

Premises: follow the lower-dimensional nucleus

The user emphasizes that a nucleus is “flat” from the six-dimensional electron's perspective. The existing binding module represents its depth-three structure by a fixed projector P onto O in V6 = O direct-sum H. This is not a literal two-dimensional disk, nor an assumption that the nucleus is isotropic in all six directions. The bound/free module extends lower-depth modes cylindrically across their unstructured directions. It does not replace that extension with delta-function sheet support.

This calculation uses the electron's intrinsic sector point, represented by the orthogonal complex structure J in SO(6)/U(3) = CP3. It does not substitute a stationary orbital or a coefficient ray for that point. Three premises are retained explicitly: a fixed nuclear frame, its SO(3) times SO(3) symmetry, and Hamiltonian evolution on the invariant symplectic CP3 orbit. Additional electron kinetic/field variables and nuclear motion are not included in this reduced flow. Flatness alone does not prove this reduction.

From the existing energy to motion

Write

J=(AQ−QTC),JT=−J,J2=−I,P=diag⁡(I3,0). J=\begin{pmatrix}A&Q\\-Q^T&C\end{pmatrix},\qquad J^T=-J,\quad J^2=-I,\quad P=\operatorname{diag}(I_3,0). J=(A−QT​QC​),JT=−J,J2=−I,P=diag(I3​,0).

The binding module gives AQ + QC = 0 and

s=−12tr⁡A2∈[0,1],VN=V(s). s=-\frac12\operatorname{tr}A^2\in[0,1],\qquad V_N=V(s). s=−21​trA2∈[0,1],VN​=V(s).

Use the invariant matrix pairing ⟨X,Y⟩ = −tr(XY)/2. Fix the Hamiltonian sign and normalization by

J˙=1I[∇JV,J],I>0. \dot J=\frac1{\mathcal I}[\nabla_J V,J],\qquad \mathcal I>0. J˙=I1​[∇J​V,J],I>0.

Here the action scale I (written as mathcal I in equations) is a symplectic normalization, not a nuclear mechanical moment of inertia. It is not fitted or identified with a physical constant in this diagnostic. The natural coadjoint-orbit symplectic construction supplies this commutator form; see Karshon's Hamiltonian group-action notes. The following specialization and solution are derived here. Reversing the symplectic sign reverses precession, not the conservation or closure results.

Direct variation gives

δV=−V′(s)tr⁡(AδA),∇JV=2V′(s)PJP. \delta V=-V'(s)\operatorname{tr}(A\delta A),\qquad \nabla_J V=2V'(s)PJP. δV=−V′(s)tr(AδA),∇J​V=2V′(s)PJP.

Consequently

J˙=2V′(s)I[PJP,J],A˙=0,C˙=0,Q˙=2V′(s)IAQ. \dot J=\frac{2V'(s)}{\mathcal I}[PJP,J],\qquad \dot A=0,\quad\dot C=0,\quad \dot Q=\frac{2V'(s)}{\mathcal I}AQ. J˙=I2V′(s)​[PJP,J],A˙=0,C˙=0,Q˙​=I2V′(s)​AQ.

Both s and V(s) are conserved. V'(s) is therefore constant along each trajectory even when V is nonlinear. The exact solution is

Q(t)=exp⁡ ⁣(2V′(s0)IA0t)Q0. Q(t)=\exp\!\left(\frac{2V'(s_0)}{\mathcal I}A_0t\right)Q_0. Q(t)=exp(I2V′(s0​)​A0​t)Q0​.

Equivalently the full J is conjugated by diag(exp(2V'(s0)A0 t/I),I3). Antisymmetry, J² = −I, and the chosen CP3 component are preserved. This is actual motion on the proposed electron-sector manifold, not motion created by changing the observer coordinates.

Exact precession, with an explicit rate

For the binding module's canonical family, A0 = √s0 K and Q0 = diag(√(1−s0),√(1−s0),1), where K generates rotations in the first two axes. For 0 < s0 < 1 and V'(s0) nonzero, Q rotates with signed rate

ωp=2V′(s0)s0I,TJ=2π∣ωp∣. \omega_{\rm p}=\frac{2V'(s_0)\sqrt{s_0}}{\mathcal I},\qquad T_J=\frac{2\pi}{|\omega_{\rm p}|}. ωp​=I2V′(s0​)s0​​​,TJ​=∣ωp​∣2π​.

The nucleus-relative structure can therefore support precession through an orientation-dependent scalar binding energy. No separately inserted magnetic field or comparison to conventional atomic dynamics is used to obtain this conditional result. But the nuclear kernel must still give a nonzero slope:

V′(s)=∫QNρN(u) ∂sKN6(u,J(s)) dμN(u), V'(s)=\int_{Q_N}\rho_N(u)\,\partial_s K_{N6}(u,J(s))\,d\mu_N(u), V′(s)=∫QN​​ρN​(u)∂s​KN6​(u,J(s))dμN​(u),

assuming differentiation under the integral is valid. The lower-dimensional structure permits this dependence; it does not determine its strength.

Endpoint qualifications matter. At s0 = 0, A = C = 0 and this contribution to the flow vanishes. At s0 = 1, Q has rank one and AQ = 0, so J is also stationary even if the matrix exponential rotates its unused plane. If V'(s0) = 0, the motion is stationary at any s0.

What this resolves, and what it does not

  1. There is now an explicit precession equation for the fixed, lower-depth nuclear geometry already written in V2. This is not a new force invented to fit a petal shape.
  2. Positive V'(0) makes the exchanging configuration an energy minimum, but the isolated Hamiltonian flow does not relax toward it: s is conserved. An energy minimum is not an attracting trajectory. This corrects any reading of “selects the lock” as automatic dynamical capture.
  3. A nonzero precession rate depends on the interaction slope and the prepared s0. This reduced model has no separate orbital-angle variable, so it does not yet derive a ratio to the Coulomb orbit clock. Integer L-dependent frequency locking cannot be inferred from its periodicity.
  4. No physical three-coordinate readout has been assigned to J(t) here. Its periodicity alone is not a derivation of the visualization's petals or of its specific 1:1:3 vector-plane generator. The earlier conditional petal construction remains the target to connect to, not a result erased by this calculation.

The next calculation should retain the electron–nucleus relative position and the dependence of K_N6 on it, together with the sector variable J. It must determine whether that same interaction synchronizes the relative orbit and this precession. The choice of a product position–CP3 phase space would itself need justification; J must not silently become a duplicate “internal orientation” if CP3 is being used as the particle's actual space. Nuclear response can subsequently be included reciprocally, without confusing an electric-polarization calculation with magnetic feedback.

A spatial revolution within O does not itself rotate the projector onto O. Making P(t) rotate at an assigned orbital rate would therefore insert a drive, not derive synchronization from “flatness.” The nucleus-to-electron direction inside O may supply additional position-dependent invariants, but those must come from the source map and kernel. The fixed-P calculation deliberately does not prescribe that missing clock through a rotating frame.

Verification

flat_nucleus_precession_checks.py tests the energy gradient by finite differences, the closed-form derivative, independent O/H frame rotations, numerical integration against the exact solution, constraint and energy conservation, both stationary endpoints, and the predicted full-period versus half-period recurrence. Linear, nonlinear, positive-slope, and negative-slope potentials are tested. All conditions use explicit runtime failures, not assert or a literal PASS. The output reports computed counts and residuals. These tests establish consistency of this reduced model, not its physical premises. The normal and optimized runs each complete 1,991 evaluations across 16 conditions; the largest integration-versus-exact residual is approximately 1.4e-11. An in-memory reversal of the velocity sign is rejected at the independent exact-solution derivative check in both modes. No mutated source is saved; this is a test-sensitivity check, not physical validation.
