Nonlinear response of the bending modes

Updated September 12, 2026

Summary: The same surface curvature energy that gives simplex frequencies has nonzero canonical mode couplings, an exact quadratic resonance and a scale response that must enter any nonlinear particle fixed point.

A frequency calculation also needs its interaction

The bending construction gives the exact frequency sequence under a declared geometric action. Its nonlinear curvature energy supplies a way to test interactions without inserting a separate coupling matrix for each pair of modes.

This note uses the four-dimensional round surface in ordinary six-dimensional space. It is a conditional feature of a field, not a replacement of that physical space. The calculation retains both normal directions. Individual zonal harmonics are specified spatial polarizations within the complete harmonic multiplets; they do not exhaust possible particle patterns.

The mass-frequency targets are unchanged. The small displacements below differentiate the geometry and test its nonlinear response. They are not variables used to fit particle masses. Physical magnitude may be needed in a full nonlinear fixed-point solution, after specifying its frequency.

Consequences for a shared coupling response

The same action fixes both the simplex spectrum and the geometric coupling. At a common radius and common canonical normalization, the scale vertices in (17) are proportional to ωℓ\omega_\ellωℓ​. Their muon-to-electron ratio is therefore the simplex ratio, approximately 206.7647206.7647206.7647, in this particular channel.
