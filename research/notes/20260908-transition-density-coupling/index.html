Transition-density coupling

Updated September 8, 2026

Summary: The complete derivative of a bilocal density interaction pairs both objects' transition densities, giving reciprocal mode vertices and an exact signed quadrupole matching law from the existing spatial kernel.

The spatial patterns which must match

A coupling calculation compares the changes that two spatial patterns can undergo. For a density interaction the relevant objects are their transition densities, which can have signs and complex phases even when each original density is positive. Their pairing provides a precise version of shape matching with both objects polarized.

Use one field Ψ(t,x)\Psi(t,x)Ψ(t,x) on a specified finite containing spatial region of the nested macroscopic manifold. The labels x,yx,yx,y below are two points in that same physical space. Let Aα(x)A_\alpha(x)Aα​(x) be fixed Hermitian matrices on its field components, and define real scalar bilinears

ρα(x)=Ψ(x)†Aα(x)Ψ(x).(1) \rho_\alpha(x)=\Psi(x)^\dagger A_\alpha(x)\Psi(x). \tag{1} ρα​(x)=Ψ(x)†Aα​(x)Ψ(x).(1)

For a compatible Dirac scalar channel one may take Aα=βPαA_\alpha=\beta P_\alphaAα​=βPα​ when βPα\beta P_\alphaβPα​ is Hermitian. Positivity of the bilinear is not assumed. The channel labels do not supply spatial embeddings; the appropriate nested maps, transport and measures must be included in the kernel used for a physical application.

The complete coherent mode vertex

Let Φi(x)\Phi_i(x)Φi​(x) be modes normalized in the derived kinetic metric. If they are not orthonormal, apply the canonical normalization before interpreting amplitude magnitudes. Define transition densities

rijα(x)=Φi(x)†Aα(x)Φj(x).(7) r_{ij}^{\alpha}(x)=\Phi_i(x)^\dagger A_\alpha(x)\Phi_j(x). \tag{7} rijα​(x)=Φi​(x)†Aα​(x)Φj​(x).(7)

For Ψ=∑iciΦi\Psi=\sum_i c_i\Phi_iΨ=∑i​ci​Φi​ the exact quartic term is

Eint=12∑i,j,k,lcˉicjcˉkcl Tij;kl,Tij;kl=∑α,β∬rijα(x)Kαβ(x,y)rklβ(y)dμxdμy.(8) E_{\rm int}=\frac12\sum_{i,j,k,l} \bar c_i c_j\bar c_kc_l\,T_{ij;kl}, \qquad T_{ij;kl}=\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\iint r_{ij}^{\alpha}(x)K_{\alpha\beta}(x,y)r_{kl}^{\beta}(y) d\mu_xd\mu_y. \tag{8} Eint​=21​i,j,k,l∑​cˉi​cj​cˉk​cl​Tij;kl​,Tij;kl​=α,β∑​∬rijα​(x)Kαβ​(x,y)rklβ​(y)dμx​dμy​.(8)

Both pairs of modes enter. Reversing a transition changes its density to its complex conjugate, giving

Tij;kl=Tkl;ij,Tij;kl‾=Tji;lk.(9) T_{ij;kl}=T_{kl;ij},\qquad \overline{T_{ij;kl}}=T_{ji;lk}. \tag{9} Tij;kl​=Tkl;ij​,Tij;kl​​=Tji;lk​.(9)

This retains the spatial shapes and relative phases required by the one field. The diagonal densities alone do not determine (8). Likewise, frequency matching does not guarantee a nonzero spatial vertex. A rotating wave approximation can select resonant terms only after this full tensor and the temporal frequencies have been calculated.

Exact factorization for the spatial dot-square kernel

For two prescribed spatial charts and their rectangular identification B:Re→RdB:\mathbb R^e\to\mathbb R^dB:Re→Rd, adopt the scalar term K(x,y)=g(xTBy)2K(x,y)=g(x^TBy)^2K(x,y)=g(xTBy)2. Define complex transition moments

Mij(a)=∫xxTΦˉi(a)Φj(a) dx,Mkl(b)=∫yyTΦˉk(b)Φl(b) dy. M_{ij}^{(a)}=\int xx^T\bar\Phi_i^{(a)}\Phi_j^{(a)}\,dx, \qquad M_{kl}^{(b)}=\int yy^T\bar\Phi_k^{(b)}\Phi_l^{(b)}\,dy. Mij(a)​=∫xxTΦˉi(a)​Φj(a)​dx,Mkl(b)​=∫yyTΦˉk(b)​Φl(b)​dy.

Expansion of the two dot products gives

Tij;kl=gtr⁡(Mij(a)BMkl(b)BT).(10) \boxed{T_{ij;kl}=g\operatorname{tr} \left(M_{ij}^{(a)}B M_{kl}^{(b)}B^T\right).} \tag{10} Tij;kl​=gtr(Mij(a)​BMkl(b)​BT).​(10)

There is no extra complex conjugate in this bilinear expression; the reversal identities are already in (9). The geometric action supplies the identification BBB, and common physical rotations transform all three tensor factors consistently. Centre motion or a position-dependent contact factor must also be varied when they belong to the adopted energy.

The source channel has at most d(d+1)/2d(d+1)/2d(d+1)/2 independent symmetric moment components at fixed depth. These are descriptions of spatial shape, not additional spatial axes. Higher spatial structure can enter other terms of the action even when it is absent from this particular kernel.

Use ordinary flat Gaussian modes with width parameter ω>0\omega>0ω>0,

ϕ0(x)=(ωπ)d/4e−ω∣x∣2/2. \phi_0(x)=\left(\frac\omega\pi\right)^{d/4} e^{-\omega|x|^2/2}. ϕ0​(x)=(πω​)d/4e−ω∣x∣2/2.

In a shared two-plane take B=IB=IB=I and

A=12(100−1),C=R(θ)AR(θ)T. A=\frac1{\sqrt2}\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix}, \qquad C=R(\theta)A R(\theta)^T. A=2​1​(10​0−1​),C=R(θ)AR(θ)T.

Then

T0A;0C=g2ωaωbcos⁡(2θ).(14) \boxed{T_{0A;0C}=\frac{g}{2\omega_a\omega_b}\cos(2\theta).} \tag{14} T0A;0C​=2ωa​ωb​g​cos(2θ).​(14)

The patterns have the same nonzero spatial support and norm, yet this transition is exactly blocked at θ=π/4\theta=\pi/4θ=π/4. At θ=π/2\theta=\pi/2θ=π/2 the amplitude reverses sign. Rotating both objects by the same angle changes neither result. Squaring the amplitude gives a cos⁡2(2θ)\cos^2(2\theta)cos2(2θ) angular factor for an otherwise specified transition probability.

There is also an orientation-independent mismatch: a traceless shape AAA has zero pairing with the breathing shape C=I/dC=I/\sqrt dC=I/d​ for every relative rotation in this isotropic kernel. Other compatible shape transitions can remain nonzero. These are explicit geometric selection rules obtained from the existing interaction, without assigning a separate zero to a selected particle species.

Scope of the selection rule

For harmonic-oscillator basis functions, multiplication by xixjx_ix_jxi​xj​ changes total oscillator number by 000 or ±2\pm2±2. Thus (10) connects only the corresponding mode pairs on each side. This is a rule for the transition density and its moment; it is not a claim that an odd spatial field has zero self-energy. The distinction is derived in angular kernel parity.

Equations (11)–(14) are a conditional flat-profile example. They do not replace the actual Coulomb-plus-precession electron orbit or specify a neutrino profile. Their role is to demonstrate, using an explicit kernel, how polarization becomes a higher-order spatial shape selection rule. The occupied particle solutions, photon map, accessible target motions, kinetic scales and scattering boundary conditions remain to be derived.
