Updated October 13, 2026

This is a candidate continuation of the phase-complete positive wave field. Let U=Psi/R0 now range over the full complex chiral coefficient space, with R0 an unknown physical vacuum norm. Keep the flat positive coefficient metric, the common combined generator G=(N1+N2)/2, beta=16 lambda R0 squared, kappa=T beta and the full determinant kinetic term. The norm is dynamical rather than constrained. The model still has second-order positive wave kinetics and does not claim Lorentz Dirac dynamics, observed spin/statistics or particle-frequency selection.

Globally Controlled Polynomial Potential

It is nonnegative for all z>=1 when M squared>=2b if m squared>=2b. When m squared<2b, the sharp nonnegativity threshold is M squared>=m to the fourth/[2(m squared−b)]. A strict inequality in the latter case avoids an additional zero-potential vacuum at larger norm. Exact differentiation and the quadratic minimum verify these statements. A simple sufficient bound for either range is M squared>=m to the fourth/[2(m squared−b)], which also implies M squared>=2b.

For the numerical comparison m squared=1 and b=1/4, M squared>=1/2 already suffices for global nonnegativity. Localizing solutions may demand much greater stiffness; positivity is not existence or stability.

A Necessary Clock Bound and the Mass-Frequency Limit

The simple radial family has only one rotating temporal frequency, omega, and a static supporting coefficient. Its root frequency window can be bounded without fitting amplitude to a measured mass. At zero spatial slope let v=V/kappa and z=c squared. Its rotating potential is U_omega=V+T(sqrt(1−beta omega squared z)−1). A nonconstant localized radial critical point at d>=2 requires U_omega negative somewhere: if it were nonnegative everywhere, its dilation derivative would be strictly positive. The gradient contribution to that derivative is T sqrt(B)[d(sqrt(A)−1)−(A−1)/sqrt(A)]>=0, strictly positive at a nonzero radial slope.

Negativity requires beta v<1 and

ω2>2v−βv2z,ω2<m2 \omega^2>\frac{2v-\beta v^2}{z},\qquad \omega^2<m^2 ω2>z2v−βv2​,ω2<m2

Thus throughout either tested finite-stiffness action, U_omega<0 implies omega squared>1/3. Any two such rotating clocks under one common m=1 obey omega_high/omega_low<sqrt(3). Numerically minimizing the exact stationary quartic sharpens this upper ratio to approximately 1.28121076 at M squared 400 and 1.28107157 at 1600.
