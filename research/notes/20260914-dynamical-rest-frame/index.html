Retaining the spatial frame's response

Updated September 14, 2026

conditional calculation

The reconstructed localized-current action has an exact frequency and inertia relation. Its earlier spatial-tensor completion imposed an instantaneous identification between the coefficient frame and the field's timelike current. For polarized fields that identification produced a defective principal propagation matrix.

A dynamical frame supplies an explicit inverse completion of that kinetic construction. It preserves the localized frequency and boost solutions, while replacing the high-wave-number defect with a finite frame response. It does not derive the oscillator grades as actual spatial modes, choose their weights, or prove particle stability.

This is a different extension from the normal-band construction in A complete finite normal band. The two have not been combined into a single derived master action. The present calculation extends the maintained Localized oscillation and its inertial response and Spatial mode tensors carried by the field's rest frame.

Let the same-field frame respond

Use natural units c=hbar=1. Keep the complete grade fields Psi_N, their local current j^mu, and s=sqrt(j^mu j_mu)>0. Introduce a future unit timelike orientation u, with u^mu u_mu=1. It represents a retained collective frame degree of the same proposed field. Its realization in the master spinor is an additional inverse premise; it is not an exchanged particle or a Yukawa coupling.

The frame refers to all d ordinary spatial directions of a sector. Its components add no physical spatial dimensions. A constant frame does not mean that the particle's oscillating field is at rest internally.

The existing mass-frequency solutions survive
