Controlling the full source of the reciprocal spinor pair

Updated September 14, 2026

conditional calculation

The reciprocal polynomial evolution has a controlled weak-coupling interpretation in the complete surface equation. Its cubic source contains four temporal frequencies. Each source has finite angular support, including the non-vacuum spinor components and the opposite parent frequencies. Explicit nonzero detunings permit a first spatial correction and a finite-slow-time averaging argument.

This concerns the conditional depth-six surface action in Nonlinear spinor frequencies under one common surface action and Reciprocal shape response in the spinor family. It retains all 48 complex field components on the specified four-sphere. It does not construct the supporting field in the two ordinary normal directions, establish particle stability, or determine the physical coupling coefficient. The surface is a proposed field feature in ordinary space, not compactification.

The complete spectral support is finite

Under a common rotation of the two complex planes, the four source classes have vacuum orbital charges

q=(a,b,2a−b,2b−a).(6) q=(a,b,2a-b,2b-a). \tag{6} q=(a,b,2a−b,2b−a).(6)

The other spinor components carry the compensating orbital charges q+1,q+1,q+2. Their total spinor rotation character is the same within each class. Since a and b differ, the four classes are mutually orthogonal. The free Hamiltonian preserves them.

The polynomial degrees in (5), including the degree-two vector (4), give the following upper bounds on scalar harmonic degree K:

Kmax⁡=(a+2max⁡(a,b)+2, b+2max⁡(a,b)+2,2a+b+2, 2b+a+2).(7) K_{\max}=(a+2\max(a,b)+2,\ b+2\max(a,b)+2, 2a+b+2,\ 2b+a+2). \tag{7} Kmax​=(a+2max(a,b)+2, b+2max(a,b)+2,2a+b+2, 2b+a+2).(7)

In each class K has the parity of q. For the two positive central charges, K is at least q. For either combination class a conservative lower bound is max(0,|q|-2), adjusted to that parity. This bound can include harmonics with zero source; including them only weakens a resolvent estimate.

For each such K, retain both B eigenvalues and both signs of every parent frequency. The smallest positive parent frequency is obtained from the full six-link singular problem. The two signs of B give the same singular frequencies by reflection and diagonal sign changes. The other singular frequencies are at least G-(K_max+4), by the singular-value perturbation bound relative to the gap shift. Consequently, their detuning from a source frequency Omega is at least G-(K_max+4)-|Omega|. This is positive for the cases below.

At each central frequency, remove only its complete resonant polynomial family. Its projection is precisely the force derived from the Hamiltonian in Reciprocal shape response in the spinor family. The opposite parent frequency at that same degree remains forced. For the combination frequencies, the enumerated signed low branches have nonzero detuning in all reported cases. Thus no additional resonant source has been silently omitted.

The positive combination frequency for degrees 14 and 36 illustrates the geometric restriction. Its charge is 58, so K is conservatively at least 56. A frequency comparison that ignores this charge would include lower-degree modes to which the actual source cannot couple.

A finite-slow-time theorem for the complete surface equation

Normalize H and all rapid frequencies by omega_e^0, and time by its inverse. The full equation is

i∂tΨ=H^Ψ+ϵN(Ψ).(13) i\partial_t\Psi=\widehat H\Psi+\epsilon N(\Psi). \tag{13} i∂t​Ψ=HΨ+ϵN(Ψ).(13)

Fix any finite slow interval 0 <= s <= S and an initial pair in the specified resonant polynomial spaces. Suppose its four complete source classes have the nonzero complementary detunings just verified. Then the exact solution of (13), with that initial pair, converges to the reciprocal resonant evolution as epsilon approaches zero, uniformly for 0 <= t <= S/epsilon in H^3 on the four-sphere.

Here is the argument, including the full field space. The chain Hamiltonian is self-adjoint and commutes with the componentwise spherical Laplacian, because B^2=-Delta+9/4. Its group is therefore unitary in every spectral Sobolev norm. H^3 on the four-dimensional surface is an algebra, and the smooth tangent Clifford coefficients make N locally Lipschitz in H^3. The usual product bound follows from Leibniz' rule, H^3 embedding into L-infinity and the applicable H^1-to-L^4 derivative estimates. It applies to all 48 complex components.

The finite polynomial averaged system preserves the two norms, so its solution and every derivative remain bounded on a fixed S. Its source has the finite support (7). In normalized units define

W(t,s)=∑je−iΩ^jt(Ω^j−H^)−1Fj(s),W~(t,s)=W(t,s)−e−itH^W(0,0),Ψapp(t)=Ψ(0)(t,ϵt)+ϵW~(t,ϵt).(14) \begin{aligned} W(t,s)&=\sum_j e^{-i\widehat\Omega_jt} (\widehat\Omega_j-\widehat H)^{-1}F_j(s),\\ \widetilde W(t,s)&=W(t,s)-e^{-it\widehat H}W(0,0),\\ \Psi_{\rm app}(t)&=\Psi^{(0)}(t,\epsilon t) +\epsilon\widetilde W(t,\epsilon t). \end{aligned} \tag{14} W(t,s)W(t,s)Ψapp​(t)​=j∑​e−iΩj​t(Ωj​−H)−1Fj​(s),=W(t,s)−e−itHW(0,0),=Ψ(0)(t,ϵt)+ϵW(t,ϵt).​(14)

The second line enforces the original initial field exactly. The leading residual is minus epsilon times the complementary source; the fast derivative of the correction cancels it. The remaining residual is

ϵ2i∂sW−ϵ{N(Ψ(0)+ϵW~)−N(Ψ(0))},(15) \epsilon^2 i\partial_s W -\epsilon\{N(\Psi^{(0)}+\epsilon\widetilde W) -N(\Psi^{(0)})\}, \tag{15} ϵ2i∂s​W−ϵ{N(Ψ(0)+ϵW)−N(Ψ(0))},(15)

and hence is bounded by A epsilon^2 in H^3, uniformly on the interval. A is finite because the source support, its inverse detunings and the shape derivatives are all bounded there.

Choose a fixed H^3 ball containing the approximate trajectory, and let L be the local Lipschitz constant of N on a slightly larger ball. Duhamel's formula and Gronwall give

∥Ψ(t)−Ψapp(t)∥H3≤AϵL(eLS−1).(16) \|\Psi(t)-\Psi_{\rm app}(t)\|_{H^3} \le\frac{A\epsilon}{L}(e^{LS}-1). \tag{16} ∥Ψ(t)−Ψapp​(t)∥H3​≤LAϵ​(eLS−1).(16)

Taking epsilon sufficiently small closes the ball bootstrap and continues the exact local solution through S/epsilon. The correction in (14) is also O(epsilon), proving the claimed convergence. No harmonic cutoff is imposed on the exact solution. Newly generated modes and subsequent nonlinear responses are covered by the full Sobolev estimate.

This theorem supplies a conditional asymptotic justification for the shape calculation. A coarse Sobolev bound can make its explicit smallness threshold extremely restrictive. It does not certify that an example such as epsilon=0.01 accurately follows the average for slow time 20. The informative L2 first-response coefficients in the table do not replace that nonlinear estimate. Neither result derives the surface, the master-field interaction, a depth-five neutral branch, or the particle's full inertia. Mass remains the selected frequency; the slower shape evolution is its interaction response.
