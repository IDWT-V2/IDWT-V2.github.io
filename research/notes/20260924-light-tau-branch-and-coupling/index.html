A testable version of the self-coupling hypothesis asks whether one canonically normalized same-field interaction supplies both the selected self-frequency and the response between two occupied spatial patterns. Relative polarization and phase can change the pair term for nonorthogonal patterns. This is an algebraic illustration, not a derivation of IDWT's particle profiles.

The neutrino and nucleon examples motivate different checks. Rare neutrino encounters in ordinary matter do not, by themselves, measure the neutrino pattern's self-coupling. Electron and muon weak vertices are closely constrained despite very different lepton masses, so a universal rule equating mass to an external vertex strength is not supported by those observations; the one-field theory would need to derive both quantities from state-dependent spatial patterns. See the Particle Data Group electroweak review. For a nucleon, energy stored in interacting quark and gluon dynamics accounts for most of its rest energy in a lattice QCD mass decomposition. That supports testing a composite field's complete energy against its constituents, but it does not identify an IDWT self-coupling coefficient or imply that every bound composite is heavier than the sum of the isolated constituents' total energies.

The exact inverse current interaction is quadratic-homogeneous in field amplitude, while a pure scalar quartic contact is fourth-order. The fixed linear projection obstruction in the preceding note still applies. A nonzero same-field background, nonlinear reduction, fixed norm, or additional master invariant must supply the needed effective interaction, its positive current curvature, and its spatial support. The rational gap shape can be written as a positive source divided by a positive stiffness, but choosing those radius functions to reproduce the fit is an inverse construction, not their derivation from the master field.

Observable channels

The 86.03 eV value is the equivalent rest energy of the outer candidate. It does not by itself predict a light-emission line at 86.03 eV. A transition from the fitted inner branch to the outer one would release approximately 1776.929914 MeV before recoil and any other products. Production or emission predictions require the same-field coupling vertex, conservation laws, line width, and a channel connecting the states. The current model gives none of those quantities. A spectral feature near 86 eV cannot be assigned to this branch by its energy alone.
