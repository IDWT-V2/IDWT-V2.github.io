Nested sector reciprocity

Updated September 8, 2026

Summary: Compatible spatial marginals and their dual pullbacks give reciprocal coupling equations on arbitrary nested sectors, with exact composition along a chain.

Spatial maps and measures

All sectors are ordinary macroscopic spatial submanifolds in one nested chain within the infinite physical manifold. The calculation uses any finite pair Qa⊂QbQ_a\subset Q_bQa​⊂Qb​, not a particular particle. Inclusion alone supplies neither a global product nor a retraction. Assume a specified map rba:Qb→Qar_{ba}:Q_b\to Q_arba​:Qb​→Qa​ that agrees with the inclusion where required, or work in a product neighborhood on which such a map exists.

Specify measures through a disintegration

dμb(x,y)=dμa(x) dνx(y). d\mu_b(x,y)=d\mu_a(x)\,d\nu_x(y). dμb​(x,y)=dμa​(x)dνx​(y).

The fibre measure dνxd\nu_xdνx​ includes the geometric Jacobian. It is not automatically a probability measure. For integrable real scalar sources jbj_bjb​, define the marginal and the pullback of a scalar test field faf_afa​:

(Rbajb)(x)=∫rba−1(x)jb(x,y) dνx(y),(Rba∗fa)(x,y)=fa(x). (R_{ba}j_b)(x)=\int_{r_{ba}^{-1}(x)}j_b(x,y)\,d\nu_x(y), \qquad (R_{ba}^{*}f_a)(x,y)=f_a(x). (Rba​jb​)(x)=∫rba−1​(x)​jb​(x,y)dνx​(y),(Rba∗​fa​)(x,y)=fa​(x).

Fubini's theorem gives the duality identity

∫QafaRbajb dμa=∫Qb(Rba∗fa)jb dμb.(1) \int_{Q_a}f_a R_{ba}j_b\,d\mu_a =\int_{Q_b}(R_{ba}^{*}f_a)j_b\,d\mu_b. \tag{1} ∫Qa​​fa​Rba​jb​dμa​=∫Qb​​(Rba∗​fa​)jb​dμb​.(1)

Here the star denotes the dual map in this integral pairing. On an infinite fibre, the pullback of a nonzero function need not be in L2(Qb)L^2(Q_b)L2(Qb​). Equation (1) remains valid for an L1L^1L1 source and bounded test field; calling it a bounded Hilbert-space adjoint requires additional domain assumptions. On finite fibres with uniformly bounded volume, the corresponding L2L^2L2 maps are bounded.

For spinor or tensor sources, first specify how their indices are transported to a common bundle and contracted. A bare integral of components in unrelated frames is not a covariant marginal. Those linear transport and polarization maps must be retained in RRR and its dual.

One interaction, two reciprocal responses

Let KaK_aKa​ be a fixed real spatial kernel on the shared sector:

(Kau)(x)=∫QaKa(x,x′)u(x′) dμa(x′). (K_a u)(x)=\int_{Q_a}K_a(x,x')u(x')\,d\mu_a(x'). (Ka​u)(x)=∫Qa​​Ka​(x,x′)u(x′)dμa​(x′).

Its transpose in the measure pairing has kernel Ka(x′,x)K_a(x',x)Ka​(x′,x). Choose the sign and coefficient by writing the interaction energy

Uab=g⟨ja,KaRbajb⟩a,Sint=−∫Uab dt. U_{ab}=g\langle j_a,K_a R_{ba}j_b\rangle_a, \qquad S_{\rm int}=-\int U_{ab}\,dt. Uab​=g⟨ja​,Ka​Rba​jb​⟩a​,Sint​=−∫Uab​dt.

Vary the two sources independently while keeping the geometry and kernel fixed. The result is

Va=δUabδja=gKaRbajb,Vb=δUabδjb=gRba∗Ka∗ja.(2) V_a=\frac{\delta U_{ab}}{\delta j_a}=gK_a R_{ba}j_b, \qquad V_b=\frac{\delta U_{ab}}{\delta j_b} =gR_{ba}^{*}K_a^{*}j_a. \tag{2} Va​=δja​δUab​​=gKa​Rba​jb​,Vb​=δjb​δUab​​=gRba∗​Ka∗​ja​.(2)

Thus the upper-to-lower response is a marginal followed by the kernel. The reverse response is the transpose kernel followed by a cylindrical pullback. These maps generally have different domains and different matrix entries; reciprocity is their dual relation:

⟨δja,gKaRbaδjb⟩a=⟨gRba∗Ka∗δja,δjb⟩b.(3) \langle \delta j_a,gK_aR_{ba}\delta j_b\rangle_a =\langle gR_{ba}^{*}K_a^{*}\delta j_a,\delta j_b\rangle_b. \tag{3} ⟨δja​,gKa​Rba​δjb​⟩a​=⟨gRba∗​Ka∗​δja​,δjb​⟩b​.(3)

If a specified positive scalar model has jd=∣ψd∣2j_d=|\psi_d|^2jd​=∣ψd​∣2 and canonical time derivative, varying ψd∗\psi_d^*ψd∗​ gives

i∂tψa=(Ha+Va)ψa,i∂tψb=(Hb+Vb)ψb. i\partial_t\psi_a=(H_a+V_a)\psi_a,\qquad i\partial_t\psi_b=(H_b+V_b)\psi_b. i∂t​ψa​=(Ha​+Va​)ψa​,i∂t​ψb​=(Hb​+Vb​)ψb​.

Real VdV_dVd​ and self-adjoint HdH_dHd​ preserve each norm. For static geometry and kernels, the complete two-field Hamiltonian preserves total energy. This density coupling can exchange energy while preserving the number in each mode family. Particle-changing vertices are additional interaction structures. A Dirac scalar source ψˉψ\bar\psi\psiψˉ​ψ instead requires its own adjoint and projector variation, as derived in Projected bilocal variation.

Composition through intermediate sectors

For Qa⊂Qb⊂QcQ_a\subset Q_b\subset Q_cQa​⊂Qb​⊂Qc​, assume compatible projections rca=rba∘rcbr_{ca}=r_{ba}\circ r_{cb}rca​=rba​∘rcb​ and their corresponding disintegrations. Iterated integration proves

Rca=RbaRcb,Rca∗=Rcb∗Rba∗.(4) R_{ca}=R_{ba}R_{cb},\qquad R_{ca}^{*}=R_{cb}^{*}R_{ba}^{*}. \tag{4} Rca​=Rba​Rcb​,Rca∗​=Rcb∗​Rba∗​.(4)

This is an exact consistency requirement for direct and staged spatial reduction. Polarization maps obey the same composition only if they intertwine compatibly; projecting out an intermediate component can discard a coupling that a direct map retains.

Equation (4) does not multiply two interaction constants together. Reducing a source through an intermediate sector is not the same physical process as propagating through an intermediate dynamical mode. The latter includes that mode's propagator and allowed vertices.

Normalization and dimensional dilution

For a normalized nonnegative spatial density,

∫QaRbaρb dμa=∫Qbρb dμb=1. \int_{Q_a}R_{ba}\rho_b\,d\mu_a=\int_{Q_b}\rho_b\,d\mu_b=1. ∫Qa​​Rba​ρb​dμa​=∫Qb​​ρb​dμb​=1.

In a product chart, ρb(x,y)=p(x)f(y)\rho_b(x,y)=p(x)f(y)ρb​(x,y)=p(x)f(y) with ∫f dν=1\int f\,d\nu=1∫fdν=1 gives Rbaρb=pR_{ba}\rho_b=pRba​ρb​=p. Any containing depth admitting this preparation therefore gives the same local contact g∫ρap dμag\int \rho_a p\,d\mu_ag∫ρa​pdμa​. Equal contact here follows from the same marginal and vertex, not from an assumed probability of hitting a thin sheet.

The normalization of a cylindrical field is a separate issue. A probability-normalized extension through a uniform box of volume VFV_FVF​ has density ρa/VF\rho_a/V_Fρa​/VF​, and its overlap carries 1/VF1/V_F1/VF​. One cannot cancel this factor without specifying the source, flux, or coupling convention in the action. Changes to the marginal itself can also change the overlap even while its integral stays one.

Weighted discrete verification

For positive quadrature matrices Wa,WbW_a,W_bWa​,Wb​ and sampled marginal RRR, the dual acting on sampled test fields is

R∗=Wb−1RTWa. R^*=W_b^{-1}R^{\mathsf T}W_a. R∗=Wb−1​RTWa​.

This formula preserves (1), including unequal cell volumes and position-dependent fibre weights. It also makes (3) testable by independent finite differences of the scalar energy. Omitting the weights can falsely break reciprocity.
