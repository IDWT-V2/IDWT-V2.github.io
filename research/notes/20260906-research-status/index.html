Research status

Updated September 8, 2026

exploratory not a module

Physical premise (IDWT): There is one infinite-dimensional physical manifold. All sectors form one nested chain of submanifolds within it. Particle depth counts ordinary, literal macroscopic spatial dimensions. “Observed” and “unobserved” describe an observer's access to those dimensions, not different kinds of physical dimension. See spatial meaning and mathematical scope.

Summary: Current proof boundaries and correction routes for reading the research archive.

No research claim is promoted by this reorganization or import.

Current boundaries

September 8 spatial-meaning correction: all sectors are nested submanifolds of the one infinite-dimensional physical manifold. The electron occupies six literal macroscopic spatial dimensions; CP3 is the proposed geometry of that physical submanifold. Three dimensions are shared with us; this makes none of the six different in kind. The electron's six are also six of the tau's ten, making the electron lower-dimensional relative to the tau without dividing the tau into different kinds of space. Earlier wording such as “intrinsic sector” or “internal projective orbit” must not be read as an alternative ontology.

The attribution of three-dimensional experience to nuclear 3+3 locking is withdrawn. The general premise concerns the three-dimensional nuclear sector. A 3+3 grouping in a particular electron calculation is relative to the chosen observer; it is not an inherent split in the electron. Existing frame-lock calculations remain conditional models, with their physical application and dynamics to be justified.

The geometry premise audit now separates spatial submanifolds from coordinate and field representations. It also records why the old CP2/S5 candidate pair cannot supply the required smooth nesting. Candidate geometries and reduced models must be corrected to meet the fixed nesting premise. A CP3 family of polarization minima, a coefficient orbit, or a quotient map alone does not establish the physical spatial construction.

The follow-up lower-dimensional test gives a qualified positive result: six is the minimum vector-carrier dimension in the orthogonal-complex family for nontrivial static precession induced by an otherwise isotropic nuclear three-plane. In four dimensions the orientation invariant is fixed, the plane stabilizer acts transitively, and both the harmonic and finite contact-source equilibrium energies are constant.

This is not an "only 6D electrons" result. Unlike the six-dimensional low-rate branch, those nonzero harmonic branches track the response resonance and have no regular bounded-response static limit. That contrast is a conditional discrimination target, not yet an atomic measurement proposal with a derived current, clock, or detector coupling.

This does not make that circle a petalled spatial orbit; it shows that an unrestricted readout cannot by itself identify particle depth. Auxiliary field-model results, not an orbit derivation

The scalar ground-state no-lock result now has a precise excited-state complement. Pure circulating oscillator states carry mixed angular momentum even when their position moments are isotropic. In the isolated scalar model, they attain an exact fixed-circulation energy bound and have no exponential linear instability. The bound controls covariance and opposite-circulation occupation, not the distance to a unique wavefunction.

More importantly, a coupled 3-in-6 stationary family now includes the nuclear response. Unequal observed/unobserved widths compensate the nuclear contribution and restore the oscillator degeneracy supporting circulation. The complete 99-dimensional moment linearization has an exactly oscillatory nonzero spectrum along the equal-coupling family for every integer degree N >= 2. This is a stationary solution of the imported scalar model. No map from it to the demo's actual orbit, generator, or clock has been established; its linear spectrum is not the stability spectrum of those electron orbits. Nonlinear coupled stability, unequal-coupling spectra, radiative selection, and physical band assignments remain open.

The local Dirac vector-potential reduction supplies a conditional spin-orbit vertex. A frozen simple bivector leaves a twofold spin degeneracy; orbital projection can remove it. An exactly solved supplied orbital-gap model quantifies the resulting entanglement and the large-gap approximation. Neither that gap nor the vector-potential identification has been derived from V2. No numerical particle mass, gauge coupling, or CKM fit is claimed.

For a fixed nuclear three-plane and invariant binding energy V(s), the cross-plane block precesses while s is conserved. Thus a nonzero binding slope can supply precession away from exact alignment, but a positive energy slope alone is not dynamical capture into the lock. This result does not yet include a separate relative-orbit clock or derive the physical petal readout; it leaves the action scale and kernel slope explicit.

An inverse nuclear orientation kernel now realizes that binding slope: K(u,J)=gamma(u)||P(u)JP(u)||²/2. With a fixed common nuclear plane, its normalized marginal is mean(gamma) s. The same energy supplies both electron and nuclear orientation variations. An inverse formula reconstructs V(s) from a specified precession-rate function, but a single orbit leaves infinitely many kernels compatible. The full fixed petal generator instead requires an oriented linear source; a bare nuclear plane does not select that source under its unbroken SO(3) times SO(3) symmetry. These are inverse compatibility results, not a first-principles nuclear current or binding law.

The electromagnetic first pass now covers the circle, two/four/six petals, and three smooth detuned alignments. A conserved current pushforward and a checked two-time weak-field response specify the next calculation. The source bridge and physical clock remain unclosed.

A subsequent action/current pass supplies a concrete conditional bridge: twisting the spatial spinor bundle by O(L+2) places the degree-L orbit sections in its degree-zero line. The free Dirac operator annihilates that holomorphic component, so the orbit still needs a separately supplied Killing drive. Gauging that drive yields the matching transport current. Exact section integrals then give a geometric probe candidate. For L = 2 and 3, an explicit probe has zero four-mode compression but a nonzero response through other section modes. The physical twist, drive, observer map, ring bridge, and photon/nuclear normalization remain to be derived.

The drive-origin follow-up gives an exact scalar-potential compression to the Killing generator and an exact nonzero leakage norm. A positive-gap surrogate shows how omitted-mode corrections can spoil integer locking; its assumed gap is not the physical Dirac spectrum. Passive frame changes are distinguished from active motion by transforming the probe as well as the state. The ring's three-state generator now embeds in every degree k ≥ 2 section space, but its physical family/degree identification is open.

Nuclear electric polarization is now a dynamical variable in a reciprocal mean-field extension. Checked circle and two/four/six-petal preparations induce a nuclear dipole whose return field changes the electron state, with convergent energy balance. Feedback shifts and dipole-radiation interference are distinct; the illustrative runs do not establish radiative stabilization. Recoil alone does not cancel the neutral pair's relative dipole. A versioned ordinary proton benchmark gives only about 7.6×10⁻¹⁸ induced-to-electron dipole ratio at a fixed Bohr radius; this is not a bound on unspecified V2 source maps or on all atomic nuclear corrections. The microscopic response, local-field matrices, magnetic channels, and coherent radiation reaction remain open. The user-proposed induced magnetic feedback has not been calculated; these electric-polarization results must not be reported as answering that question.
