Holomorphic binding and its responsive-frame test

Updated September 14, 2026

conditional calculation

There is a useful exact alternative to the polarization-current binding law. Its complete fixed-frame linearization is a complex-linear self-adjoint Dirac operator, so it preserves the spatial norm of every linear disturbance. Allowing the defining frame to respond changes that conclusion: the simplest positive frame kinetic extension has genuine resolved growing radial controls.

This comparison identifies which part of the construction supplied the apparently stable response. It does not derive the simplex mass weights or a stable physical mass operator. Mass remains the selected frequency, and the physical directions remain ordinary and uncompactified.

Full spatial fixed-frame linearization

At χ0=0\chi_0=0χ0​=0, the rotating-frame Hessian is

(H+ω)2=diag⁡(L†L+ω2,LL†+ω2).(6) (\mathcal H+\omega)^2= \operatorname{diag}(L^\dagger L+\omega^2, LL^\dagger+\omega^2). \tag{6} (H+ω)2=diag(L†L+ω2,LL†+ω2).(6)

This is an analytic fixed-frame result, stronger than a finite local spectrum scan. It is not yet a statement about the complete moving particle.

Removing off-branch poles

The holomorphic quotient has poles away from the displayed core. Put ρ=(∥ϕ∥F2+∥χ∥F2)/s\rho=(\|\phi\|_F^2+\|\chi\|_F^2)/sρ=(∥ϕ∥F2​+∥χ∥F2​)/s, and multiply only the binding ratio term in (3) by the common cutoff η(∣b∣2/ρ2)\eta(|b|^2/\rho^2)η(∣b∣2/ρ2). Use the same twice continuously differentiable cutoff that is zero below 1/161/161/16 and one above 1/41/41/4, described in Regular polarization binding.

At the core its argument equals one. The profile, source, and entire linearization (5) are therefore unchanged. Away from the core the regularized potential is no longer holomorphic, but it is regular on the unit field sphere and homogeneous of degree two. Its source extends as a globally Lipschitz map at the total-field origin. All density and cutoff derivatives are retained in the source.

The fixed-frame nonlinear Dirac equation consequently has global canonical L2L^2L2 mild evolution, conserved norm and finite propagation. These facts do not imply nonlinear orbital stability of the core. In particular, pointwise linearization around an exponentially decaying profile does not by itself prove uniform Frechet differentiability of the flow in the unweighted spatial L2L^2L2 topology.

Covariance requires the frame to be included

The traces in (1) identify spinor and partner components in a specified frame. They are not by themselves a Lorentz-covariant interaction. A conditional completion evaluates them on Ψu=B(u)−1Ψ\Psi_u=B(u)^{-1}\PsiΨu​=B(u)−1Ψ, where uuu is a retained future unit timelike orientation of the same proposed field. Under a Lorentz transformation,

B(u′)−1SΛB(u)=diag⁡(R,R),Ψ′=SΛΨR†. B(u')^{-1}S_\Lambda B(u)=\operatorname{diag}(R,R), \qquad \Psi'=S_\Lambda\Psi R^\dagger. B(u′)−1SΛ​B(u)=diag(R,R),Ψ′=SΛ​ΨR†.

The rest-frame matrices transform by unitary conjugation, under which (1)–(3) are invariant. The induced partner connection must also be included in the kinetic term. This orientation and its realization in the master field are inverse premises; no additional interacting particle is being introduced.

Collective drift is a different requirement

A free localized particle must also admit its translation–boost pair. If T=∂aΦT=\partial_a\PhiT=∂a​Φ is a translation zero mode and bbb is the boost tangent, the time generator has Ab=−T\mathcal A b=-TAb=−T, up to the chosen translation sign. A complex-linear self-adjoint H\mathcal HH with A=−iH\mathcal A=-i\mathcal HA=−iH could not have both this generalized mode and HT=0\mathcal H T=0HT=0: its range is orthogonal to its kernel, whereas the required Hb\mathcal H bHb is a nonzero multiple of iTiTiT.

Thus fixed-frame unitarity cannot simply be promoted to uniformly bounded motion of the complete free particle. Legitimate drift has to be treated through the responding support or the full real Hamiltonian structure, and stability assessed modulo collective motion. This observation does not explain away the radial growing modes above: those are actual shape/frame disturbances in a separate invariant spatial channel.

Reproducible calculations

holomorphic_core_binding.py and regular_holomorphic_binding.py contain the complete analytic and regularized sources. The 1,110-check report verifies stationary Cartesian equations, full variations, adjoints, regularization, and frame covariance across depths 3, 4, 5, 6 and 10. The 38-check spatial frame report retains the refined growing eigenfunctions and all reciprocal frame terms. The independent nonlinear Cartesian control uses holomorphic_spatial_evolution.py; its finite periodic boxes are numerical approximations, not compactified physical dimensions or a proof of nonlinear particle stability. The 25-check nonlinear refinement report compares the complete disturbance fields across grids and time steps. Relative differences are about 0.172% for the depth-three grid comparison, 0.120% for the depth-four grid comparison, and 0.0314% for the depth-four time-step comparison. Canonical norm drift remains below six parts in a billion in the tested trajectories. These controls use a common illustrative frequency of one; they are not separate fitted particle assignments.
