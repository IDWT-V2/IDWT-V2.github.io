Research / Mass and normalization

A simpler current action with the same mass-core response

Updated September 14, 2026

conditional calculation

The regular polarization model admits a genuine simplification. Its two cutoff applications can be replaced by ordinary rational functions of the same field's total density. No cutoff thresholds remain. The maintained coherent mass-frequency branches, common spatial profiles and complete linearized equations on those branches are unchanged.

The replacement is a different nonlinear interaction away from the core. The statement proved here is equality of the potential, its first variation and its full second variation on the core, together with global regularity of the fixed-frame source. It is not an assertion that all nonlinear encounters are identical. The spatial carrier of the simplex weights and the physical selection of occupied branches remain open.

All physical coordinates are ordinary and uncompactified. Mass remains the selected frequency, with mc2=ℏωmc^2=\hbar\omegamc2=ℏω.

Equality of the complete core derivatives

These are identities for arbitrary complex spinor and partner variations, including variations outside the scalar/vector shape of the core.

The transverse-mode reduction further separates the complete finite hierarchy into one occupied coefficient block and complex-linear spatial blocks. The complete spatial linearized differential operator is consequently the same as before, with the same canonical kinetic term. Its existing local Clifford decomposition, spatial angular equations and outgoing matching controls transfer directly. This does not turn those limited stability results into a complete stability theorem.

Retaining the whole finite current hierarchy

The simplification is not confined to an occupied coefficient ray. For a finite set of grades, retain the canonical fields and exact symmetric-tensor splitting maps of the graded current construction. At retained order kkk, let RkR_kRk​ be the sum of the scalar-insertion currents from grades N≥kN\ge kN≥k, and define

ρk=∑N≥k∥ΨN∥F2,hk,d=(k+d−1d−1). \rho_k=\sum_{N\ge k}\|\Psi_N\|_F^2,\qquad h_{k,d}=\binom{k+d-1}{d-1}. ρk​=N≥k∑​∥ΨN​∥F2​,hk,d​=(d−1k+d−1​).

The mass action becomes

U~mass=ω0,d2∑khk,d[ρk+qsTr⁡Rk2+(1−q)(Tr⁡Rk)2ρk].(6) \boxed{ \widetilde U_{\rm mass} =\frac{\omega_{0,d}}2\sum_k h_{k,d} \left[\rho_k+ \frac{qs\operatorname{Tr}R_k^2+(1-q)(\operatorname{Tr}R_k)^2} {\rho_k}\right].} \tag{6} Umass​=2ω0,d​​k∑​hk,d​[ρk​+ρk​qsTrRk2​+(1−q)(TrRk​)2​].​(6)

An empty active channel contributes zero. The numerator uses the partner rank sss, not the generally larger size of the retained current matrix. The binding currents instead trace the oscillator indices and sum all grades; their denominator uses the total canonical density.

At an isolated coherent upper core, Rk=cIs⊗Pz,kR_k=cI_s\otimes P_{z,k}Rk​=cIs​⊗Pz,k​ and ρk=sc\rho_k=scρk​=sc. An initially empty channel above the highest occupied grade has a Lipschitz source but need not have a linear derivative at its zero field. No infinite-grade differentiability or uniform infinite-grade evolution estimate is asserted here.

The polarization-sensitive interaction survives

For two coherent upper cores of grades N≥MN\ge MN≥M, let ρ,σ\rho,\sigmaρ,σ be their total local canonical densities and let τ=∣z†w∣2\tau=|z^\dagger w|^2τ=∣z†w∣2 be the overlap of their coefficient rays. Subtracting the isolated mass energies in (6) gives exactly

Vmass=−ω0,dqρσρ+σ∑k=1Mhk,d(1−τk).(8) \boxed{ V_{\rm mass} =-\omega_{0,d}q\frac{\rho\sigma}{\rho+\sigma} \sum_{k=1}^M h_{k,d}(1-\tau^k).} \tag{8} Vmass​=−ω0,d​qρ+σρσ​k=1∑M​hk,d​(1−τk).​(8)

This is a nonzero polarization-sensitive interaction under the same stated conditions as the earlier current comparison. It remains independent of the larger grade. The complete initial source seen by the lower-grade probe has the same common-target identity, because every channel accessible to that probe contains the same target current and density.

Its finite-overlap numerical value differs from the original square-root interaction. In the weak-probe limit the original and new laws agree at leading order, as also follows from the complete Hessian equality. Neither expression is an observed gauge vertex or an encounter rate, and subsequent particle deformations need not be identical.

Setting q=0q=0q=0 would remove (8) entirely. On upper-only fields, (6) would then collapse to a sum of prescribed quadratic grade masses. A mass-only comparison would miss this loss of the polarization-sensitive self-interaction. Retaining the positive contraction term is therefore part of preserving the joint mass-and-coupling construction.

Regularity, the frame and positive inertia

For any fixed finite tower, the new potential is smooth on each nonzero active field sphere and homogeneous of degree two. Every denominator is bounded away from zero on that sphere. Its source is homogeneous of degree one with bounded angular derivative, and extends by zero as a globally Lipschitz map. A finite sum of these active-channel sources has the same property. The fixed-frame nonlinear Dirac system therefore retains global canonical L2L^2L2 mild evolution and finite propagation. This is not a nonlinear orbital-stability theorem.

Evaluate the normalized quantities in the retained future timelike frame, transforming the partner representation with its induced spatial rotation. Composition with this frame map preserves the core derivative identities. Thus the replacements add no new mixed frame-wave or frame-frame quadratic term. The existing responding-frame kinetic term and its vacuum-domain question remain.

The complete boosted branch and its canonical translation–boost pairing are unchanged:

Mab=ℏωQc2δab>0. M_{ab}=\frac{\hbar\omega Q}{c^2}\delta_{ab}>0. Mab​=c2ℏωQ​δab​>0.

The common Q=1Q=1Q=1 premise still fixes canonical normalization. No amplitude has been identified as mass.
