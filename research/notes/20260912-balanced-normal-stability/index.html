Stability of reciprocal normal polarization

Updated September 12, 2026

Summary: The complete finite-gap normal density and reciprocal coherent target have an exact quartic orientation dispersion, with bounded and growing nonlinear controls at equal canonical norm and stationary scalar source.

Test whether the response preparation persists

The balanced normal response has a common leading geometric coefficient across retained grades under specified canonical populations. A stationary preparation with that response need not persist when disturbed. This calculation retains the entire normal density, both normal Dirac signs and the reciprocal target ray to test the finite mode dynamics.

The normal kinetic gap and occupied spectator modes remain conditional reductions of the same field. The target has zero spatial kinetic operator in this control. Its coherent tensor ray is an invariant mode manifold of the degree-one interaction, not an established localized particle. No new physical dimensions, compactification or interaction particle is introduced.

Consequence for physical selection

The stationary scalar source and a canonical norm do not uniquely select the polarization preparation, even within this one action. Reciprocal finite-gap dynamics distinguishes some preparations that those stationary quantities cannot distinguish. It supplies a concrete stability constraint on the proposed common geometric response.

It does not yet select a unique population, identify the observed coupling currents or produce a spatial particle. The mass-source calculation retains the separate occupation, band matching and total-energy questions. Physical spatial geometry and full Dirac residuals must be supplied before these finite controls can establish particle stability or dimensionality.
