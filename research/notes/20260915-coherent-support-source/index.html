Research / Mass and normalization

Coherent phase and polarization tests of the supporting multiplet

Updated September 15, 2026

conditional calculation

The curvature-moment carrier has a positive complete averaged shape Hessian. A coherent field occupying those bands is a stricter test than its averaged density. Its different frequencies produce oscillating sources, and changing their phases cannot generally remove them.

Within one fixed surface harmonic, a determinant identity tests every polarization of the positive squared-carrier doublet. For resolved distinct beat frequencies and nonzero occupation of all bands, no such polarization choice makes even the two endpoint sources vanish at every time when at least three bands are occupied. The result does not exclude responsive geometry or additional surface-harmonic content. Continuous supporting motion remains a possible part of the model.

Any two-color assignment of ddd bands leaves at least ⌊(d−1)2/4⌋\lfloor(d-1)^2/4\rfloor⌊(d−1)2/4⌋ same-color pairs. This gives a simpler positive bound by replacing every ℓjr\ell_{jr}ℓjr​ with their minimum. The combinatorics concerns compatibility of the source polarizations; it is not an additional factor inserted into the mass formula.

The bound is strictly positive for three or more occupied bands. It is zero at depth two, where an opposite-block assignment attains zero source. For higher depths the bound can be very small. It excludes exact static cancellation in this restricted coherent state, but does not by itself exclude weak forcing or small sustained geometric motion. A displacement or stability claim needs the actual kinetic response to (3).

Constructive polarization choices can suppress the source

The same calculation can find useful coherent comparison states. Assign each occupied band to either pure chiral block, keeping its frequency and canonical population unchanged. Cross-block pair sources vanish exactly. Within-block pair sources remain. Exhaustively testing the 2d2^d2d assignments gives the smallest force variance in this discrete family.

Here the variance includes every centered real and imaginary deformation, with each covector component normalized by its actual positive geometric metric. Relative to equal chiral mixing, representative source RMS ratios are approximately:

Control Best pure-block assignment / equal mixing
Depth-two W, Z and Higgs 0
Depth-three down 0.000882
Depth-four up 0.001847
Depth-five first neutrino 0.2141
Depth-six electron 0.3127
Depth-ten tau 0.3386

These are constructive polarization controls of the same operator, not mass fits. The search covers all pure assignments, but not the full continuous polarization space; its minimum is an upper bound on that larger optimization problem. The field's dynamics has not been shown to select minimum variance. Nor are these internal supporting-source ratios particle encounter rates or a derivation of neutrino interaction suppression.

The comparison does show why retaining polarization matters: a positive averaged shape Hessian and the same frequency spectrum can coexist with very different oscillating sources. The next dynamical calculation must retain that source and its reciprocal geometry, rather than replacing it by the zero time average.

Verification

coherent_support_checks.py verifies (5), the complete finite binary bound in (8), arbitrary complex polarization controls and resolved beat gaps across all maintained sectors. Its 704-check report records the exact source hashes and refinement settings. The bound is computed with stable positive Gram eigenvalues, retaining small endpoint components at high precision.

polarized_support_checks.py uses every centered source matrix, compares them with direct differentiation of the full squared operator, and checks the selected assignments by an independent full polarization projection. Low- and high-depth spatial refinements test the force suppression. These are conditional field-source calculations; no complete particle inertia is inferred. The 82-check report preserves the full discrete searches and their independent controls.

The generalization to more polarization channels shows why the channel count must be established physically before this threshold can be interpreted as a law about spatial depth or inertia.
