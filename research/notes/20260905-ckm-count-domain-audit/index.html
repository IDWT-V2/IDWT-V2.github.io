CKM count-domain audit

Updated September 10, 2026

exploratory not a module

Physical premise (IDWT): There is one infinite-dimensional physical manifold. All sectors form one nested chain of submanifolds within it. Particle depth counts ordinary, literal macroscopic spatial dimensions. “Observed” and “unobserved” describe an observer's access to those dimensions, not different kinds of physical dimension. See spatial meaning and mathematical scope.

Summary: Corrected rank, degree-loss, charged-current normalization, and CP tests constrain CKM candidates.

NOT A PHYSICS MODULE. This note replaces the earlier count-domain negative-results document. It preserves three candidate constructions, corrects their mathematical treatment, and follows the corrected premises through to their consequences. Exact results below apply to the stated models; they are not a general impossibility theorem for V2 mixing.

1. Scope, source inputs, and conventions

The earlier document tested a separable power-ratio kernel, a binomial degree-loss table, and a one-step creation operator. It described their unitary factors as trivial and concluded that the cross-sector remainder must supply mixing. Those claims require replacement, not just a change of wording.

The parent CKM proposal, Sections 2 and 8, specifies up labels (3,20,72)(3,20,72)(3,20,72) with homogeneous degrees (2,19,71)(2,19,71)(2,19,71), down labels (1,4)(1,4)(1,4) with degrees (0,3)(0,3)(0,3), and an exceptional bottom state involving labels (16,17)(16,17)(16,17), hence degrees (15,16)(15,16)(15,16). Its physical bottom vector is not fixed by selecting one of those degrees.

For reproducibility, the binomial calculation below retains the earlier single-band approximation Nb=16N_b=16Nb​=16, meaning the upper label nb=17n_b=17nb​=17. It is not silently promoted to the actual two-band bottom state. The power-ratio rank proof needs only a positive bottom count and is independent of its numerical choice.

Throughout, matrix rows are (d,s,b)(d,s,b)(d,s,b) and columns (u,c,t)(u,c,t)(u,c,t). The down-row charged-current convention is the adjoint of the usual up-row CKM convention; in particular, the magnitude of its sususu entry corresponds to the usual ∣Vus∣|V_{us}|∣Vus​∣. The benchmark is a unitary three-generation charged-current matrix with nontrivial mixing and a nonzero rephasing-invariant CP phase. The 2025 PDG review supplies this benchmark and the definition of the Jarlskog invariant [E1]. No central-value scan or statistical exclusion is claimed here.

These are transition-operator tests using specified mode counts. No independently measured field amplitude is fitted or reconstructed. Eigenfrequency data and mode counts do not by themselves determine the relative orientation or phases of the two flavor bases, as the parent source already explains in Section 5.

2. First establish what normalization is allowed

2.1 Polar factorization is not automatically canonical normalization

For a square matrix KKK, the right polar decomposition is K=QHK=QHK=QH, with H=(K†K)1/2H=(K^\dagger K)^{1/2}H=(K†K)1/2. If KKK is invertible, Q=KH−1Q=KH^{-1}Q=KH−1 is a unique unitary matrix. If KKK is singular, the canonical polar factor is a partial isometry; its full unitary extensions are not generally unique [E2]. Discarding HHH is a new physical prescription unless its removal follows from the action.

To make that issue precise, suppose the independently derived left-handed kinetic metrics are positive definite matrices Zu,ZdZ_u,Z_dZu​,Zd​. Removing them gives

Kc=Zd−1/2KZu−1/2. K_c=Z_d^{-1/2}KZ_u^{-1/2}. Kc​=Zd−1/2​KZu−1/2​.

If Lu,LdL_u,L_dLu​,Ld​ subsequently put the canonically normalized mass operators into their physical bases, the charged-current matrix is

V↓=Ld†KcLu. V_\downarrow=L_d^\dagger K_cL_u. V↓​=Ld†​Kc​Lu​.

Consequently, after factoring out the common weak coupling, unitarity of this square matrix is equivalent to

K†Zd−1K=Zu. \boxed{K^\dagger Z_d^{-1}K=Z_u.} K†Zd−1​K=Zu​.​

This is a test of the derived vertex and kinetic metrics, not permission to choose metrics that force the answer. Choosing Zd=IZ_d=IZd​=I and Zu=K†KZ_u=K^\dagger KZu​=K†K reproduces the ordinary polar factor for invertible KKK, but the theory would have to derive precisely those metrics. For singular KKK, that choice is not positive definite.

An exact two-dimensional example, embeddable with a spectator third generation, shows the distinction. Let

Sd=diag⁡(2,1),Su=(2112),Zd=Sd2,Zu=Su2, S_d=\operatorname{diag}(2,1),\qquad S_u=\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\1&2\end{pmatrix},\qquad Z_d=S_d^2,\quad Z_u=S_u^2, Sd​=diag(2,1),Su​=(21​12​),Zd​=Sd2​,Zu​=Su2​,

and

K=SdSu=(4212). K=S_dS_u=\begin{pmatrix}4&2\\1&2\end{pmatrix}. K=Sd​Su​=(41​22​).

The actual canonical matrix is Sd−1KSu−1=IS_d^{-1}KS_u^{-1}=ISd−1​KSu−1​=I. Yet the Euclidean polar factor of KKK is

Q=137(61−16)≠I. Q=\frac{1}{\sqrt{37}}\begin{pmatrix}6&1\\-1&6\end{pmatrix}\ne I. Q=37​1​(6−1​16​)=I.

Thus extracting a rotation by polar factorization can invent a mixing angle that is absent after the specified physical normalization. Conversely, unknown kinetic or mass-basis data cannot be ignored when interpreting a raw kernel.

2.2 Rank survives every invertible normalization

Invertible left and right transformations preserve rank, so

rank⁡V↓=rank⁡K. \operatorname{rank}V_\downarrow=\operatorname{rank}K. rankV↓​=rankK.

A rank-one matrix cannot become a complete rank-three unitary charged-current vertex merely through legitimate nonsingular normalization. This conclusion is stronger than a poor numerical fit, but it does not forbid a rank-one term as one contribution to a larger vertex.

3. Test 1: the separable power-ratio family

Write the positive counts as AiA_iAi​ and BjB_jBj​, and take any finite real exponent qqq:

Kij=(Ai/Bj)q=xiyj,xi=Aiq,yj=Bj−q. K_{ij}=(A_i/B_j)^q=x_i y_j,\qquad x_i=A_i^q,\quad y_j=B_j^{-q}. Kij​=(Ai​/Bj​)q=xi​yj​,xi​=Aiq​,yj​=Bj−q​.

This is exactly an outer product. Every two-by-two minor vanishes. The singular values are

∥x∥∥y∥,0,0. \boxed{\|x\|\|y\|,\quad0,\quad0.} ∥x∥∥y∥,0,0.​

The earlier description in terms of extreme singular values obscured two exact zeros. No exponent scan is needed. Positive sector units, a different positive scalar bottom count, or separate row and column phases do not remove this separability.

With normalized vectors x^,y^\widehat x,\widehat yx,y​, the canonical partial isometry is Q0=x^y^†Q_0=\widehat x\widehat y^\daggerQ0​=xy​†. Every full unitary extension has the form

A further consequence matters for the CP claim. At q=0q=0q=0, KKK is the all-ones matrix, with positive polar factor H=KH=KH=K. Set

This is not a proposed physical CP phase. It proves that a blanket claim of zero J for every arbitrary unitary completion of the real rank-one kernel is false. A real completion has zero J; a chosen complex completion can introduce nonzero J, but that phase was not predicted by the original kernel.

3.2 Logical conclusion and size of the missing structure

As a complete vertex in canonical three-generation units, this family fails the rank test for every real qqq. As an input to an unspecified completion, it underdetermines mixing rather than predicting a unique wrong matrix.

If K+ΔK+\DeltaK+Δ is to have rank three, then

3≤1+rank⁡Δ,rank⁡Δ≥2. 3\le1+\operatorname{rank}\Delta, \qquad\boxed{\operatorname{rank}\Delta\ge2.} 3≤1+rankΔ,rankΔ≥2.​

If the completed matrix is unitary, a unit vector v∈ker⁡Kv\in\ker Kv∈kerK satisfies ∥Δv∥=1\|\Delta v\|=1∥Δv∥=1, so ∥Δ∥2≥1\|\Delta\|_2\ge1∥Δ∥2​≥1. The missing action on two directions cannot be supplied by a norm-small correction in these fixed canonical units. This bound is not a statement about raw unnormalized coefficients.

In particular, adding the rank-one one-step kernel of Test 3 to this rank-one family still gives rank at most two. At least three rank-one terms would be necessary in a purely separable sum, and even that would not guarantee unitarity, the correct hierarchy, or CP violation.

4.3 Three interpretations must remain separate

A raw coherent vertex: the displayed real BBB is not unitary. In fact,

(BTB)12=(1−p)21>0. (B^TB)_{12}=(1-p)^{21}>0. (BTB)12​=(1−p)21>0.

Its first two columns are not orthogonal. Diagonal nonzero state normalizations and row/column rephasings cannot cancel this single shared-row overlap or create the absent u→su\to su→s matrix element. More general kinetic and mass-basis maps require independent derivation and the test in Section 2; they cannot be assumed to be the polar prescription.

A defined polar-factor toy model: if one explicitly postulates V↓=polar⁡(B)V_\downarrow=\operatorname{polar}(B)V↓​=polar(B) with Euclidean metrics, its interior polar factor is uniquely real orthogonal. Therefore every CP quartet has zero imaginary part. No ppp in the interior supplies a nonzero J. Diagonal row and column phases cancel from those quartets and do not repair this. Nontrivial real mixing is possible; physical three-generation CP violation is not supplied by this prescription. This does not prove that no ppp approximates selected magnitudes, and that stronger unverified scan claim is not retained.

A traced-out loss channel: the parent construction is naturally a map on density operators, not a single coherent matrix. A number-state realization of the same binomial weights has Kraus operators

4.4 The genuine support conclusion

Every bare-loss Kraus operator has Nout≤NinN_{\rm out}\le N_{\rm in}Nout​≤Nin​. Therefore none creates the specified Nu=2→Ns=3N_u=2\to N_s=3Nu​=2→Ns​=3 transition. This remains true when the two bottom degrees are retained, because neither changes the u,su,su,s degree labels.

The conclusion is conditional on identifying those named physical bands with the input and output projectors of the loss model. Unknown non-diagonal basis maps are extra structure, not consequences of bare loss. A derived coherent transition operator must provide the missing degree-raising element or justify a different physical identification. Polarizing a probability table does neither.

5. Test 3: isolated one-step creation

For the stipulated support Nout=Nin+1N_{\rm out}=N_{\rm in}+1Nout​=Nin​+1, the selected matrix has only one possible nonzero cell:

K=α∣s⟩⟨u∣. K=\alpha|s\rangle\langle u|. K=α∣s⟩⟨u∣.

For α≠0\alpha\ne0α=0, it has rank one. Including both bottom degrees 15 and 16 adds no allowed cell, since the candidate outputs of degrees (2,19,71)(2,19,71)(2,19,71) are (3,20,72)(3,20,72)(3,20,72). At α=0\alpha=0α=0, there is no transition and no selected polar mapping at all.

5.1 A stronger conclusion than the old permutation claim

Write α=∣α∣eiϕ\alpha=|\alpha|e^{i\phi}α=∣α∣eiϕ. Every unitary polar extension must satisfy

Q∣u⟩=eiϕ∣s⟩. Q|u\rangle=e^{i\phi}|s\rangle. Q∣u⟩=eiϕ∣s⟩.

The other two columns are an arbitrary unitary map between the complementary two-dimensional spaces. They need not form a permutation. Nevertheless, every extension has ∣Qsu∣=1|Q_{su}|=1∣Qsu​∣=1, not a small Cabibbo transition. This failure is independent of the chosen completion.

There is a second completion-independent consequence: the fixed unit column decouples one generation, leaving only a two-by-two unitary block. All three-generation CP quartets then have zero imaginary part. The remaining two-by-two quartet is real by orthogonality, and every quartet involving the fixed row or column vanishes. Thus J is zero for every completion, including complex ones. This is stronger than the corresponding conclusion for Test 1, where a complex arbitrary completion can have nonzero J.

5.2 The coefficient was not determined by the stated operator alone

For a specified common bosonic mode space, normalized coherent states, and

the matrix element is

⟨v;N+1∣T∣u;N⟩=N+1 (v†t)(v†u)N. \boxed{\langle v;N+1|T|u;N\rangle =\sqrt{N+1}\,(v^\dagger t)(v^\dagger u)^N.} ⟨v;N+1∣T∣u;N⟩=N+1​(v†t)(v†u)N.​

A magnitude p(N+1)\sqrt{p(N+1)}p(N+1)​ follows only after specifying the relevant overlaps; for example, aligned unit coherent vectors and p=∣u†t∣2p=|u^\dagger t|^2p=∣u†t∣2. For the unnormalized sum with every tm=1t_m=1tm​=1, ∥t∥2\|t\|^2∥t∥2 is the number of summed modes, so that ppp is not automatically a probability bounded by one. The original definition omitted these premises.

The equation above is a comparison calculation in a defined common mode space. It does not supply the missing cross-depth intertwiner between different V2 carriers. The reliable negative result is the sparse support under the stipulated one-step rule, not an undocumented coefficient formula.

6. The bottom state cannot be supplied by its mass alone

The parent proposal explicitly distinguishes its bottom two-band singular-value construction from an ordinary band. Even in a simplified orthonormal two-band representation,

∣b⟩=c15∣15⟩+c16∣16⟩ |b\rangle=c_{15}|15\rangle+c_{16}|16\rangle ∣b⟩=c15​∣15⟩+c16​∣16⟩

would give a row

Kbj=c15∗K15,j+c16∗K16,j. K_{bj}=c_{15}^*K_{15,j}+c_{16}^*K_{16,j}. Kbj​=c15∗​K15,j​+c16∗​K16,j​.

The coefficients and their phases would have to come from the actual bottom operator. Its proposed mass or a geometric mean of two counts does not determine this row. The simple representation is illustrative, not a derivation of the physical bottom spinor structure.

The structural rank proof for a separable scalar-count family and the absence of the u→su\to su→s cell in bare loss survive their stated assumptions. Detailed singular values, mixing angles, and realness of a fully completed two-band model cannot be inferred from one arbitrarily chosen bottom level. A new complex bottom construction would be a new model and would need a fresh CP audit.

7. Corrected conclusions and their limits

Construction and premise Exact consequence What does not follow
Separable power-ratio kernel as the complete vertex Rank one for every finite real exponent; invertible normalization cannot restore unitarity A unique polar completion, unique mixing angles, or a universal J for arbitrary completions
Real binomial matrix, fixed single bottom degree, Euclidean polar prescription Rank three for interior p; nontrivial real orthogonal polar factor; J=0 Identity/permutation mixing, an exhaustive magnitude-fit exclusion, or a physical derivation of polar normalization
Binomial weights interpreted as traced bare loss A nonunitary reduced channel; no increase in degree; no specified u-to-s transition A coherent CKM matrix reconstructed uniquely from probabilities
Isolated nonzero one-step creation kernel Rank one; every unitary extension maps u entirely to s and has J=0 A unique permutation or the claimed coefficient without state and operator normalization

The full CKM problem is not solved by any of these prescriptions as stated. The reasons are now explicit and different: missing rank, missing predicted directions, absent coherent phase information, a forbidden physical transition, or an exactly unit-sized transition in the wrong place. None of those facts proves that all count-informed cross-sector operators fail.

For CP, the relevant condition is a nonzero rephasing-invariant quartet of the complete canonically normalized mass-basis vertex. Complex phases that are only row or column rephasings do not help. A numerical complex completion in an undetermined null space is not a predicted CP mechanism. Conversely, realness of one raw term does not establish a theorem about a larger model with independently derived complex kinetic, mass-basis, or interaction structure.

The missing dynamics is not proved to reside only in R34\mathcal R_{34}R34​. The parent source also leaves the off-diagonal coherent-contact operator unevaluated. The leading contact, the remainder, their combination, or a derived relation between physical flavor frames could contribute. What is required is a complete coherent construction, including the actual bottom state and kinetic metrics, not the naming of a particular remainder as the only possible source.

The parent source's leading identity result retains its stated assumption of no generation mixing; the scalar mass counts alone do not prove that assumption. Its bare-loss support result applies to bare loss. Its proposed identification of canonical normalization with an unspecified unitary part needs the metric and operator conditions in Section 2. This note records those qualifications without silently rewriting the parent module.
