Complete spatial polarization data

Updated September 10, 2026

Summary: Complete spatial Clifford tensors retain the amplitudes and phases of a polarized field, exposing information that a circulation tensor captures at six dimensions but can omit at ten.

Begin with the full oscillating field

For a monochromatic vector with complex amplitude a+iba+iba+ib, where a,ba,ba,b are real spatial vectors,

E(t)=Re⁡[(a+ib)e−iωt]=acos⁡ωt+bsin⁡ωt.(1) E(t)=\operatorname{Re}[(a+ib)e^{-i\omega t}] =a\cos\omega t+b\sin\omega t. \tag{1} E(t)=Re[(a+ib)e−iωt]=acosωt+bsinωt.(1)

Its trajectory lies in span⁡R{a,b}\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}\{a,b\}spanR​{a,b}, even when the containing physical space has many dimensions. The squared ellipse axes are the eigenvalues of

(∣a∣2a⋅ba⋅b∣b∣2).(2) \begin{pmatrix}|a|^2&a\cdot b\\a\cdot b&|b|^2\end{pmatrix}. \tag{2} (∣a∣2a⋅b​a⋅b∣b∣2​).(2)

Parallel a,ba,ba,b give linear polarization; orthogonal equal-length a,ba,ba,b give circular polarization. More spatial dimensions permit more plane orientations. A field with several modes, spatially varying amplitudes or different frequencies has further structure which (1) does not describe. The Dirac polarization waves give an exact multimode example with a shared scalar source and changing spatial currents.

For a particle field, the allowed components must come from its actual spatial equation. In a fixed degenerate spatial multiplet, write

Ψz(x,t)=e−iMtW(x)z,∫W†W dx=IN,z†z=1.(3) \Psi_z(x,t)=e^{-iMt}W(x)z,\qquad \int W^\dagger W\,dx=I_N,\qquad z^\dagger z=1. \tag{3} Ψz​(x,t)=e−iMtW(x)z,∫W†Wdx=IN​,z†z=1.(3)

The spatial Dirac construction supplies examples where W(x)W(x)W(x) includes both radial components and all bound coordinates. The coefficients zzz describe the polarization of this field. Their number is not the number of physical spatial dimensions. All xxx coordinates are ordinary macroscopic directions in the one nested manifold.

Equation (3) is a fixed-multiplet description. It does not assert that every physical particle has that profile, or that its other spatial modes can be discarded.

Retain amplitudes and relative phases without selecting one tensor

The matrix

ϱ=zz†,ϱ†=ϱ,ϱ2=ϱ,tr⁡ϱ=1(4) \varrho=zz^\dagger,\qquad \varrho^\dagger=\varrho,\quad \varrho^2=\varrho,\quad \operatorname{tr}\varrho=1 \tag{4} ϱ=zz†,ϱ†=ϱ,ϱ2=ϱ,trϱ=1(4)

retains every relative amplitude and phase in zzz. It removes only the common constant phase. For any operator OOO on the multiplet,

⟨O⟩=tr⁡(ϱO).(5) \langle O\rangle=\operatorname{tr}(\varrho O). \tag{5} ⟨O⟩=tr(ϱO).(5)

A common phase relative to another coherent field must still be retained in the combined field and its transition terms. Likewise, replacing zzz by a position-dependent phase cannot be justified by (4); its derivatives enter the spatial equation, as shown in Spatial polarization textures.

To express (4) as spatial tensors, choose Hermitian Euclidean Clifford matrices γi\gamma_iγi​, satisfying {γi,γj}=2δijI\{\gamma_i,\gamma_j\}=2\delta_{ij}I{γi​,γj​}=2δij​I. They describe how the field transforms under rotations of its actual spatial coordinates. For even depth use one specified chiral representation; for odd depth use the stated irreducible spin representation.

Define the Hermitian spatial operators

Bij=−iγiγj,Cijkl=γiγjγkγl,i<j<k<l.(6) B_{ij}=-i\gamma_i\gamma_j,\qquad C_{ijkl}=\gamma_i\gamma_j\gamma_k\gamma_l, \qquad i<j<k<l. \tag{6} Bij​=−iγi​γj​,Cijkl​=γi​γj​γk​γl​,i<j<k<l.(6)

Even a two-form interaction can read the additional information

Let a specified complex spatial rotation pattern act through Of=∑AfABAO_f=\sum_A f_A B_AOf​=∑A​fA​BA​, where AAA labels coordinate planes. For a pure polarization, its component orthogonal to the initial mode has squared norm

∥(I−ϱ)Ofz∥2=f†Vf,VAB=z†BABBz−JAJB.(15) \boxed{\|(I-\varrho)O_fz\|^2=f^\dagger\mathcal V f,\qquad \mathcal V_{AB}=z^\dagger B_AB_Bz-J_AJ_B.} \tag{15} ∥(I−ϱ)Of​z∥2=f†Vf,VAB​=z†BA​BB​z−JA​JB​.​(15)

This is a positive response matrix. It retains relative phases in the incoming pattern fff as well as the occupied field zzz.

For disjoint ordered planes, BijBkl=−CijklB_{ij}B_{kl}=-C_{ijkl}Bij​Bkl​=−Cijkl​. Shared indices instead reduce to a two-form, and identical planes give the identity. Consequently the response to a two-form probe can depend on four-form polarization data even though its mean source depends only on JJJ. No additional four-form interaction term is required for this distinction.

Equal-current spatial polarizations constructs the full bound fields and a reciprocal six-inside-ten example. The periodic polarization test supplies the corresponding approach when complete field resolvents and sidebands are needed. Neither an instantaneous response norm nor this operator classification is a physical scattering rate.
