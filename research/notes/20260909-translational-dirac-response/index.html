Translational Dirac response

Updated September 9, 2026

Summary: Varying the scalar center restores the exact translation zero mode and yields a calculable inertial response of the full self-bound spatial Dirac field.

A self-bound field must be able to move

The centered scalar interaction determines its own spatial profile and center. Holding that center fixed while varying the field would replace part of its dynamics by an external constraint. This note retains the center and derives its response from the same energy as the scalar Dirac moment modes.

All coordinates are ordinary spatial coordinates in a sector of the one nested physical manifold. Translation moves the field within those coordinates. Response coefficients and angular functions add no physical dimensions or interaction particles.

At finite numerical resolution the translation zero and its conjugate momentum direction can produce small spurious growth rates. Fixing total momentum and quotienting the translation direction removes that pair. This operation preserves the remaining fluctuation frequencies; it does not remove a nonzero physical instability.

Relation to a force between two patterns

This calculation supplies a field's response when a reciprocal pair interaction exerts a force. The force itself must come from differentiating that pair energy with respect to its two positions. A moment pairing which depends only on separately centered shapes and a fixed identification matrix has no dependence on their relative translation. Its orientation and shape responses remain meaningful, while a separation-dependent force requires the corresponding spatial contact or propagation kernel.
