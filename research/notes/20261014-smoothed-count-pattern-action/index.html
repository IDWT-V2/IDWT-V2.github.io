A Smoothed Count Action with Global Finite-Energy Histories

Updated October 14, 2026

A positive spatial kinetic term and a Gaussian-smoothed density energy can coexist with exact localized patterns at the supplied simplex frequencies. The construction below proves a continuous source on every finite-norm field, a real continuously differentiable density energy, and at least one global finite-energy weak and mild history for every finite-energy initial field. It also gives the complete nonnegative Gaussian shape Hessian, with an all-degree proof. These properties belong to one explicitly stated action.

This is an inverse construction: the count function, positive sector units, width sequence, smoothing length and restoring penalties are constitutive inputs. The local count action and the frequency-following mobility action are distinct constructions. The present completion regularizes density nodes and adds kinetic coercivity, but its translational inertia is N/(2α)N/(2\alpha)N/(2α), independent of the rest frequency at fixed norm.

All coordinates are ordinary physical coordinates in shared R10\mathbb R^{10}R10. Depth labels a pattern's broad directions, rather than an additional physical space. The field is classical; squared amplitude and normalized integration measures are not position probabilities. A pattern's internal profile, its center, and an electron's literal six-dimensional orbit and precession remain distinct. This scalar candidate does not derive that trajectory, spin or charge. It introduces no compact spatial dimension, mediator particle or particle-exchange mechanism.

and pointwise differentiation gives

partial_t [u Z(C)] =u[a Z-(1/2) Z_C:D²a].             (7)

Every term in (7) is bounded in L1(dx), uniformly on finite time intervals, by C sqrt(N)||grad psi||. Local time differentiation is justified by the smoothed continuity equation. Fubini and the integral fundamental theorem, using (6), therefore make J absolutely continuous with derivative the integral in (7).

Spatial integration by parts twice gives

J_t=integral W u_t.

This is a genuine integration by parts: compact cutoffs first give the identity; all cutoff-error terms are integrable by (6), including derivatives of Z_C, and their tails vanish. The remaining convolution calculation is also absolutely integrable by (6):

integral W(x) u_t(x) dx
  =-sum_j integral W(x) partial_j G(x-y) j_j(y) dx dy
  =integral grad H(y) . j(y) dy.                    (8)

The last sign follows from differentiating G(x-y) with respect to y. Equations (4) and (8) cancel exactly:

E(psi(t))=E(psi(s)) for all s,t in I.                (9)

Equation (4) makes the H1 seminorm continuous; weak H1 continuity plus continuity of its norm proves psi in C(I;H1). For the global constructed solutions, the coercive energy estimate now uses equality rather than only lower semicontinuity.

4. Concrete Gaussian-envelope uniqueness class

A useful sufficient condition can be stated without any lower bound on the physical density itself. Let q_t(x) be a positive Gaussian density on R10. Its covariance eigenvalues stay between positive finite bounds on each finite time interval. Suppose two solutions psi and phi obey, with uniform finite M and positive m,

|psi|², |phi|² <= M q_t,
G*|psi|², G*|phi|² >= m (G*q_t)                     (10)

pointwise on that interval. The same q_t is used for both solutions. Its center can move. Then the two solutions with the same initial data agree throughout the interval.

Here is the bounded-response estimate underlying that assertion. Interpolate densities rho_s=(1-s)|psi|²+s|phi|², so (10) persists. This density interpolation avoids zeros from interference along a field interpolation. Write U=G*q_t. All normalized scores of rho_s obey polynomial bounds in the centered spatial coordinate, because

|D^beta(G*rho_s)| <= M (|D^beta G|*q_t), G*rho_s>=m U.

Linearizing the explicit score-polynomial formula for W therefore gives

|delta W(z)| <= P(z) sum_(|beta|<=4)
                        |D^beta G*delta rho(z)|/U(z),           (11)

where P is a fixed polynomial bound; its constants are uniform in s and time. Also

|delta rho(y)| <= (|psi(y)|+|phi(y)|)|psi(y)-phi(y)|
                <=2 sqrt(M q_t(y)) |psi(y)-phi(y)|.

After multiplying G*delta W by phi, (11) is dominated by integral operators whose kernels are a polynomial times

K(x,y)=sqrt(q_t(x)q_t(y))
          integral G(z-x) G(z-y)/U(z) dz.            (12)

Derivatives of G add only polynomials. Each such kernel is Hilbert–Schmidt. To see this directly, diagonalize the Gaussian covariance. In one eigen-direction with variance tau², write s=sigma² and a=tau². The exponent in (12), after integrating z, is

-A(x²+y²)+Bxy,
A=(2a²+2as+s²)/[4as(2a+s)],
B=(a+s)/[s(2a+s)].

Its quadratic form is strictly positive because

2A-B=s/[2a(2a+s)]>0,
2A+B=(2a+s)/(2as)>0.

For clarity, the z integral contributes (a+s)(x+y)^2/[2s(2a+s)] to the exponent, which gives these coefficients directly.

Thus polynomial multiples remain square integrable. The response bound is

||phi (H_phi-H_psi)||_2 <= C ||phi-psi||_2.           (13)

For d=psi-phi, write F(psi)-F(phi)=H_psi d+phi(H_psi-H_phi). The real H_psi term cancels in the L2 difference identity. Equation (13) and Gronwall give uniqueness. The individual H_psi phi products are integrable under (10) and the polynomial score bounds, so this cancellation is justified.

Condition (10) is a sufficient comparison-class assumption, not a proved invariant neighborhood for arbitrary H1 data. It includes each exact target Gaussian history and permits physical nodes whenever the convolved density still satisfies its lower envelope. General H1 uniqueness, or propagation of (10), remains open. An unbounded L2 Frechet derivative of F at a Gaussian is compatible with this result: the unbounded real multiplication part cancels in the difference norm.

The bounded-current completion retains the count branches while adding frequency-dependent inertia and proving its own energy balance. Separated-pattern and orientation results establish energy and source clustering and delimit the energetic meaning of the positive Gaussian Hessian.
