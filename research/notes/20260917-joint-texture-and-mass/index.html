Research / Mass and normalization

Testing spatial patterns, compatible geometry and the mass law together

Updated September 17, 2026

conditional calculation

A spatial field can nearly solve its standing equation while having the wrong mass ratios or an unbalanced supporting variation. All three must be kept visible. This continuation retains the same finite-current candidate, the same compatible nested conformal-sphere geometry and all six maintained depths. Mass is the selected positive frequency.

The geometry remains one containing metric and its restrictions. Particle patterns vary independently inside their sectors; they are not restrictions of other particles. Global spherical topology is a conditional geometric choice, not compactification.

Numerical results

The alternating search completed three cycles of shared metric adjustment and spatial field relaxation. Its final largest relative field defect was 0.4613 and its largest normalized metric-path force was 30.063. Doubling the quadrature changed its field defects by at most 2.14e-8. Neither condition is close to a solution, and the required mass units also remain inconsistent.

The simultaneous search varied all sixteen spatial patterns and the nine compatible metric parameters in one objective. It stopped after 49 iterations at its specified computation limit. Its endpoint is not converged, and six controls failed the subsequent integration-refinement check. The original reports retain those failures.

The problem is especially clear where the optimized local current reverses sign. Its numerator is a polynomial of degree at most twice the retained axial cutoff after removing the common harmonic density factor. Locating all its interior roots allows adaptive integration to split around the finite-current transition widths. For the depth-five index-22 control, the relative field defect rises from the optimizer-grid value 8.5414 to 9.8044. For depth-ten index 23 it rises from 19.0004 to 21.7907. These are large failed field equations, not small corrections to particle masses. The apparent objective reduction must not be interpreted as a physical improvement. The full positive-wave derivative also fails at these endpoints; its projected residual already suffices to exclude them.

This search retains a finite axial basis with a fixed spatial heading, including both spectral signs and complex component coefficients. It is not unrestricted angular or orientation variation. Further basis refinement cannot be replaced by quadrature refinement. No global minimum or exhaustive exclusion of the conformal family is claimed.

Eight identity and implementation reports pass 1,505 checks. A separate 32-check endpoint integration audit contains six failures, which motivated the adaptive investigation rather than being discarded. Four completed numerical screens record the alternating search, simultaneous search, scalar-curvature controls and adaptive audit. These categories are kept distinct from a successful physical solution.

The clearest positive result is the larger full-wave family and the action-level propagation identity. The clearest restriction is that the tested scalar-curvature support constructions do not simultaneously produce supported compatible geometry and the maintained mass units. Developing the supporting term from the same field remains the central open step; none of these calculations completes the mass operator.
