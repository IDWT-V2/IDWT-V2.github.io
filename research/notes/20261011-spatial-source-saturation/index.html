The Same Spatial Carrier and Its Source

The spatial projector construction provides actual positive Dirac bands on a round ordinary spatial sphere S^d_R. This is a specified comparison geometry, with undetermined physical radius, rather than compactification or a replacement for the proposed physical CP3 electron geometry. Its classical spinor field describes field structure; its squared norm is not an electron-position probability law. Literal deterministic electron trajectories remain a separate physical requirement.

The theorem concerns a static source operator. A point source following a deterministic path retains the instantaneous point-contact strength at each position; its time-ordered field response need not equal the response to a static source distributed along that path. The moving spatial contact calculation makes that distinction explicit, retaining bare spatial frequencies and reciprocal source energy. Neither the static theorem nor an orbit average alone establishes a moving electron's mass or coupling.

Let H_N contain the positive Dirac shells 0 through N, let V be the physical sphere volume, and put

gl=(l+d−1d−1),SN=∑l=0Ngl=(N+dd),rd=2⌊d/2⌋. g_l=\binom{l+d-1}{d-1},\qquad S_N=\sum_{l=0}^Ng_l=\binom{N+d}{d},\qquad r_d=2^{\lfloor d/2\rfloor}. gl​=(d−1l+d−1​),SN​=l=0∑N​gl​=(dN+d​),rd​=2⌊d/2⌋.

The shell frequencies are (l+d/2)/R, and their multiplicities are r_d g_l. The spectral setup is given by Camporesi and Higuchi, Sections 3–5. The positive shell kernel and complete-source comparisons below are calculated explicitly on that setup.

Physical evaluation E_N,x obeys E_N,x E_N,x†=(S_N/V)I. For a nonnegative Borel measure ρ with total mass one, define the reference-normalized source operator by

⟨ψ,Tρ,Nψ⟩=V∫∣ψ(x)∣2 dρ(x). \langle\psi,T_{\rho,N}\psi\rangle =V\int |\psi(x)|^2\,d\rho(x). ⟨ψ,Tρ,N​ψ⟩=V∫∣ψ(x)∣2dρ(x).

The existing evaluation bound gives 0≤T_ρ,N≤S_N I. A single point source attains S_N. The question here is precisely when a spread source can attain that same bound, followed by explicit tests of finite spread and self-density.

An Explicit Off-Diagonal Spinor Kernel

Use the global trivialization supplied by the positive ground Killing spinors, and temporarily use probability-normalized sphere volume. Fix a north pole x. Write a second point y as

Independent projection onto actual polynomial Dirac kernels checks the matrix formula at degrees zero, one and two for every occupied depth.

This is the overlap of normalized evaluation images, not a new interaction field. Its role in a physical response still depends on the common action.

Exact Saturation Theorem

For N≥1, the source operator attains its maximal eigenvalue S_N if and only if ρ is a single point measure. At N=0 every probability source attains the bound.

The existing equality condition says that some unit field must saturate the evaluation bound at ρ-almost every point. At a saturation point x, that field has the form

ψ=V/SN EN,x†u,∣u∣=1. \psi=\sqrt{V/S_N}\,E_{N,x}^\dagger u, \qquad |u|=1. ψ=V/SN​​EN,x†​u,∣u∣=1.

Suppose the same field saturates at a second point y, with polarization v. Comparison of the ground-shell components, which are constant in the chosen Killing frame, forces u=v. The first positive shell has kernel

K1(y,x)=dcos⁡θ I−isin⁡θ γ(n). K_1(y,x)=d\cos\theta\,I-i\sin\theta\,\gamma(n). K1​(y,x)=dcosθI−isinθγ(n).

Its two evaluation vectors with this common unit polarization obey, in probability-normalized volume,

∥E1,x†u−E1,y†u∥2=2d−2Re⁡(u†K1(y,x)u)=2d(1−cos⁡θ). \|E_{1,x}^\dagger u-E_{1,y}^\dagger u\|^2 =2d-2\operatorname{Re}(u^\dagger K_1(y,x)u) =2d(1-\cos\theta). ∥E1,x†​u−E1,y†​u∥2=2d−2Re(u†K1​(y,x)u)=2d(1−cosθ).

The imaginary Clifford term has zero real expectation. The difference can vanish only when y=x. Thus a saturating excited-band field has exactly one saturation point. The support condition for ρ forces a point measure; the converse follows from the evaluation identity.

At N=0 the ground evaluation map is square and unitary after multiplication by √V. Every unit ground field has constant density 1/V, so T_ρ,0=I for every probability source. This boundary case is essential.

An independent overlap version uses the first cumulative band:

s12=(1+dt)2+1−t2(d+1)2, s_1^2=\frac{(1+d t)^2+1-t^2}{(d+1)^2}, s12​=(d+1)2(1+dt)2+1−t2​,

Splitting a larger cumulative evaluation image into this band and its remaining shells gives

sN(θ)≤1−d+1SN[1−s1(θ)]<1(θ≠0). s_N(\theta)\le 1-\frac{d+1}{S_N}[1-s_1(\theta)]<1\quad(\theta\ne0). sN​(θ)≤1−SN​d+1​[1−s1​(θ)]<1(θ=0).

The theorem concerns this source class and sphere carrier. Signed scalar currents, matrix-valued sources, other spatial geometries and different nonlinear actions require separate arguments. It is not a general theorem that IDWT particles must contain a physical delta source.

A Finite-Spread Contact and Its Geometric Correction

For two equal point sources, T=(T_x+T_y)/2. The overlap formula gives its nonzero eigenvalues

λ±=SN2(1±sN(θ)), \boxed{\lambda_\pm=\frac{S_N}{2}(1\pm s_N(\theta)),} λ±​=2SN​​(1±sN​(θ)),​

each repeated r_d times when the two evaluation images are distinct. The ground or coincident case is degenerate and the minus values vanish. A small 2r_d-dimensional Gram matrix verifies these eigenvalues independently from the polynomial spatial modes.

The existing spatial geometric tensor supplies

GN=(d−1+1d+2)N(N+d+1)(d+1)2. G_N=\left(d-1+\frac1{d+2}\right) \frac{N(N+d+1)}{(d+1)^2}. GN​=(d−1+d+21​)(d+1)2N(N+d+1)​.

Thus the contact's finite-spread correction is fixed by the same mode geometry already governing transport and deformation. Physical separation is Rθ.
