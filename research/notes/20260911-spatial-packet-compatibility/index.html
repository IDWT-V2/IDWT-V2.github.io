Changing compatibility of localized spatial packets

Updated September 11, 2026

Summary: A prescribed free-encounter functional separates reciprocal spatial deformation from free motion; the same local scalar dynamics can increase or decrease it for fully localized complex preparations, with its first change beginning beyond the leading scattering variation.

Compare both patterns using the same spatial rule

Use the local scalar Dirac equation

i∂tΨ=DΨ+gσβΨ,D=−iα⋅∇,σ=Ψ†βΨ,(1) i\partial_t\Psi=D\Psi+g\sigma\beta\Psi, \quad D=-i\alpha\cdot\nabla, \quad \sigma=\Psi^\dagger\beta\Psi, \tag{1} i∂t​Ψ=DΨ+gσβΨ,D=−iα⋅∇,σ=Ψ†βΨ,(1)

with its full complex source and reciprocal variation as in Dirac source linearization. The incoming field is a sum of two opposite complete kinetic chiralities, Ψ=Ψu+Ψv\Psi=\Psi_u+\Psi_vΨ=Ψu​+Ψv​. Their individual scalar self-currents vanish, while their cross pattern supplies the entire scalar. The positive-frequency vertices retain its polarization, phase and spatial interference.

For a fixed comparison duration T>0T>0T>0, let U(t)=e−iDtU(t)=e^{-iDt}U(t)=e−iDt and define

CT[ψ]=∫0Tdt∫dDx [(U(t)ψ)†βU(t)ψ]2.(2) \boxed{\mathcal C_T[\psi]= \int_0^Tdt\int d^Dx\, \left[(U(t)\psi)^\dagger\beta U(t)\psi\right]^2.} \tag{2} CT​[ψ]=∫0T​dt∫dDx[(U(t)ψ)†βU(t)ψ]2.​(2)

Both complete fields enter (2). A fixed target, a count of components, or an isotropic density replacement would define a different functional. It is nonnegative and uses exactly the scalar entering (1). With dimensionless positive norm and ℏ=c=1\hbar=c=1ℏ=c=1, [CT]=length1−D[\mathcal C_T]=\mathrm{length}^{1-D}[CT​]=length1−D, so gCTg\mathcal C_TgCT​ is dimensionless. It is not a measured interaction probability or a particle reaction rate.

The free propagation in (2) specifies a controlled encounter for the chosen preparation and duration. It permits reproducible comparisons of spatial patterns without declaring every overlap measure physically interchangeable. It is the spatially localized counterpart of the traveling-pulse compatibility comparison.

Remove the known free evolution before comparing outgoing patterns

Let Ψg(t)\Psi_g(t)Ψg​(t) solve the full nonlinear equation from ψin\psi_{\rm in}ψin​. At time TTT define

ψoutI=U(−T)Ψg(T).(3) \boxed{\psi_{\rm out}^{I}=U(-T)\Psi_g(T).} \tag{3} ψoutI​=U(−T)Ψg​(T).​(3)

The same unitary inverse removes the free evolution of both chiral patterns. Compare

ΔCT=CT[ψoutI]−CT[ψin].(4) \Delta\mathcal C_T= \mathcal C_T[\psi_{\rm out}^{I}]-\mathcal C_T[\psi_{\rm in}]. \tag{4} ΔCT​=CT​[ψoutI​]−CT​[ψin​].(4)

If g=0g=0g=0, (3) returns the incoming field exactly and (4) vanishes. The control retains every generated component; it does not project the outgoing field back into the incoming positive-frequency band or remove its spatial deformation. Individual chiral norms are reported separately.

Equation (3) is a mathematical comparison protocol. It does not assert that freely departing packets spontaneously refocus or encounter one another again. The sign of (4) says how the complete outgoing patterns perform in the specified subsequent free-encounter test. It is not a universal, protocol-independent definition of compatibility.

The first scattering variation preserves this functional

Use the positive spatial inner product and the complex gradient convention

δCT=2Re⁡⟨∇∗CT,δψ⟩.(5) \delta\mathcal C_T=2\operatorname{Re} \langle\nabla_*\mathcal C_T,\delta\psi\rangle. \tag{5} δCT​=2Re⟨∇∗​CT​,δψ⟩.(5)

Vary the local scalar before integrating. Its exact gradient is

∇∗CT[ψ]=2∫0Tdt U(−t)[σ(U(t)ψ) βU(t)ψ].(6) \boxed{ \nabla_*\mathcal C_T[\psi] =2\int_0^Tdt\,U(-t) \left[\sigma(U(t)\psi)\,\beta U(t)\psi\right].} \tag{6} ∇∗​CT​[ψ]=2∫0T​dtU(−t)[σ(U(t)ψ)βU(t)ψ].​(6)

The finite spatial regulator uses the orthogonal Galerkin projection of the displayed source and the same kinetic norm; no additional interaction coefficient appears.

Duhamel's formula for (1), at fixed TTT and in a regime where the small-ggg expansion is valid, gives

ψoutI=ψin−ig2∇∗CT[ψin]+O(g2).(7) \boxed{\psi_{\rm out}^{I} =\psi_{\rm in}-\frac{ig}{2} \nabla_*\mathcal C_T[\psi_{\rm in}]+O(g^2).} \tag{7} ψoutI​=ψin​−2ig​∇∗​CT​[ψin​]+O(g2).​(7)

Consequently the first derivative of (4) at g=0g=0g=0 is

2Re⁡⟨∇∗CT,−i2∇∗CT⟩=0,ΔCT=O(g2).(8) 2\operatorname{Re}\left\langle \nabla_*\mathcal C_T,-\frac{i}{2}\nabla_*\mathcal C_T \right\rangle=0, \qquad \boxed{\Delta\mathcal C_T=O(g^2).} \tag{8} 2Re⟨∇∗​CT​,−2i​∇∗​CT​⟩=0,ΔCT​=O(g2).​(8)

The leading field deformation can therefore be nonzero while the leading change in this particular compatibility measure vanishes. Its later change depends on the evolving kinetic and source response. Equation (8) does not fix that change's sign or impose alignment.

Separate pattern change from redistribution of norm

For nonzero chiral norms Qu,QvQ_u,Q_vQu​,Qv​, define the same encounter with each complete pattern rescaled by its positive real norm:

ST[ψ]=CT[ψuQu+ψvQv]=CT[ψ]QuQv.(11) \boxed{\mathcal S_T[\psi] =\mathcal C_T\left[\frac{\psi_u}{\sqrt{Q_u}} +\frac{\psi_v}{\sqrt{Q_v}}\right] =\frac{\mathcal C_T[\psi]}{Q_uQ_v}.} \tag{11} ST​[ψ]=CT​[Qu​​ψu​​+Qv​​ψv​​]=Qu​Qv​CT​[ψ]​.​(11)

The equality follows because each individual chiral scalar vanishes, free evolution preserves both chiral norms, and the cross scalar scales by 1/QuQv1/\sqrt{Q_uQ_v}1/Qu​Qv​​. Every relative phase and every spatial component is retained. This removes an amplitude product from the comparison; it does not replace the actual nonlinear evolution by renormalized dynamics.

Verification and scope

axial_packet_benchmarks.py implements the exact preparation (10), complete nonlinear evolution and (2)–(4), with timestamped reports and optional full coefficient fields. For example:

OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 MKL_NUM_THREADS=1 \
python experiments/axial_packet_benchmarks.py \
  --dimension 5 --radial-size 40 --axial-size 192 \
  --coupling 50 --rtol 2e-12 --texture .6 \
  --polarization-angle .7 --phase 0 --contact-order 128

Changing --phase to .7 gives the second preparation. --output-dir selects a directory for the timestamped numerical reports and optional coefficient fields.

The two signs survive radial cutoffs 16, 24, 32 and 40, axial refinement from 128 to 192 points, stricter adaptive integration and time quadrature from 64 to 128 nodes. The last quadrature change alters the reported relative changes by less than 10−1410^{-14}10−14. Increasing the axial interval from [−16,16][-16,16][−16,16] to [−20,20][-20,20][−20,20] changes the two relative differences by less than 5×10−115\times10^{-11}5×10−11. The finite interval is a numerical regulator of localized ordinary spatial fields, not a physical compactification.

packet_compatibility_checks.py runs 26 checks. It independently integrates the free kinetic equation with an accumulated local scalar square, verifies the full complex derivative (6), and checks the first-order field map and quadratic compatibility change against complete nonlinear evolution. It also verifies canonical normalization and positive-frequency preparation of both textured incoming fields.

These calculations establish a conditional spatial response of the local scalar term. They do not supply bound-particle solutions, identify electric or weak channels, determine physical widths or derive observed coupling rates. The comparison complements those missing calculations; it does not replace them.

axial_packet_study.py repeats the specified preparation over a phase interval while keeping both norms, polarization vectors, widths, encounter duration and coupling fixed. Each case reports its own incoming and outgoing-control contact and conserved-quantity errors.

Time ordering of spatial compatibility derives the first nonzero contact coefficient from the Poisson bracket of the full source Hamiltonians at different encounter stages. Its complete complex variation distinguishes ordered feedback from the leading integrated overlap.
