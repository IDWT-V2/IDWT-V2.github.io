Nonlinear mass reduction with spatial response

Updated September 11, 2026

Summary: Eliminating complementary spatial Dirac modes gives a nonlinear effective action, its exact induced norm, and a positive source-leakage criterion for departures from a common linear contact scale.

The interaction coefficient and the occupied field

The occupied-graph action retains the simplex count as a nonlinear coefficient. Determining its physical consequence requires solving the spatial field and its normalization. The calculation below eliminates complementary modes of that same field while retaining their contribution to both quantities. It does not derive the graph action from the master interaction.

Let the spatial Hilbert space have a fixed physical measure and canonical time-kinetic norm. Its self-adjoint spatial Dirac operator and chirality obey

D†=D,β†=β,β2=I,{D,β}=0.(1) \mathscr D^\dagger=\mathscr D,\qquad \beta^\dagger=\beta,\qquad \beta^2=I,\qquad \{\mathscr D,\beta\}=0. \tag{1} D†=D,β†=β,β2=I,{D,β}=0.(1)

These are operators on actual spatial fields, not a replacement of spatial coordinates by coefficient rays. The calculation applies at any spatial depth for which the following spectral and regularity hypotheses hold; six dimensions alone do not establish them.

Let PPP project onto the entire zero space in the problem being reduced, assume βP=P\beta P=PβP=P, and write Q=I−PQ=I-PQ=I−P. Require an invertible complementary operator DQ=QDQ\mathscr D_Q=Q\mathscr DQDQ​=QDQ. If a restricted spatial symmetry class is used, it must be invariant under the full equation, and every zero mode in that class must be retained. A negative-chirality zero mode cannot silently be inverted or omitted.

For finite Galerkin systems these are finite matrix assumptions. On a smooth closed spatial manifold, an elliptic Dirac operator with a spectral gap on QQQ supplies the complementary inverse between the appropriate Sobolev spaces. One sufficient framework uses HsH^sHs, s>d/2s>d/2s>d/2, so products are controlled, and a scalar kernel bounded on that space. The local kernel is the identity. The resulting real-analytic implicit-function argument is local near zero coupling and zero frequency. It is not a claim about arbitrary coupling or continuous spectra without a gap.

The stationary second variation has an equally exact expression. In real coordinates retaining both quadratures, let HHH be the full Hessian of (4). Where HQQH_{QQ}HQQ​ is invertible,

The band-consistency calculation explains why eliminating modes and bare compression obey different consistency rules. Neither (7) nor a positive curvature in a restricted subspace establishes dynamical stability of the full Dirac field.

A normalized branch and a criterion for an exact linear scale

An independent six-dimensional spatial comparison

The specified spatial CP3 branch provides an independent differential-equation test of the reduction. CP3 here is the candidate actual six-dimensional spatial topology with an undetermined metric radius, not compactified auxiliary dimensions. Its section degree LLL is not the cumulative mass-band label NNN.

The known local-contact instability at positive LLL remains present. Correctly deriving its effective frequency does not turn that branch into a viable electron.

The spatial source-preservation theorem classifies the vanishing condition (16) for the specified holomorphic CP3 zero modes. It requires a constant scalar potential; a covariant kernel satisfying it also loses shape selection within that band. The theorem includes a full nonlinear stability result for the mean kernel and bounds on a finite screened-response correction.

The scalar gap comparison examines a different mechanism: generating a scalar coefficient by summing occupied kinetic modes of the same field. It derives the complete collective response and tests whether the cumulative count survives at a fixed canonical norm. Its global kernel is specified independently of the occupied-graph prescription used in (17).
