76. Gap-origin audit: primitive winding and orientation reversal do not derive the two-unit branch

Section 75 showed that the desired orbit generator follows uniquely from two relative axial gaps,

f(−1)−f(0)=1,f(1)−f(0)=2, f(-1)-f(0)=1, \qquad f(1)-f(0)=2, f(−1)−f(0)=1,f(1)−f(0)=2,

for a quadratic polynomial

f(Jz)=a+bJz+cJz2. f(J_z)=a+bJ_z+cJ_z^2. f(Jz​)=a+bJz​+cJz2​.

The next question is whether the second equation follows from the first together with orientation reversal or another elementary symmetry of the locked beam geometry.

It does not.

76.1 Primitive winding fixes only one equation

The primitive clock condition supplies

f(−1)−f(0)=1. \boxed{f(-1)-f(0)=1.} f(−1)−f(0)=1.​

For

f(m)=a+bm+cm2, f(m)=a+bm+cm^2, f(m)=a+bm+cm2,

this gives

−b+c=1, -b+c=1, −b+c=1,

or

b=c−1. \boxed{b=c-1.} b=c−1.​

Thus a one-parameter family remains. Primitive winding alone does not determine the orbit generator.

76.2 Orientation reversal does not supply the missing target equation

There are two obvious ways to impose a reversal condition on the axial spectrum.

If reversal is taken to make the two extremal gaps antisymmetric about the m=0m=0m=0 level,

f(1)−f(0)=−[f(−1)−f(0)], f(1)-f(0) =-[f(-1)-f(0)], f(1)−f(0)=−[f(−1)−f(0)],

then together with the unit gap one obtains

b=−1,c=0. \boxed{b=-1,\qquad c=0.} b=−1,c=0.​

This is a purely linear axial generator, not the required one.

If reversal is instead taken to make the spectrum even,

f(1)=f(−1), f(1)=f(-1), f(1)=f(−1),

then one obtains

b=0,c=1. \boxed{b=0,\qquad c=1.} b=0,c=1.​

which is also not the required generator.

Therefore neither elementary reversal prescription produces

b=12,c=32. \boxed{b=\frac12,\qquad c=\frac32.} b=21​,c=23​.​

76.3 The two-unit branch is genuinely additional information

The target generator requires

f(1)−f(0)=2. f(1)-f(0)=2. f(1)−f(0)=2.

In the currently written orbit construction, that two-unit separation comes from the extremal amplitude weights

+Land−L +L \qquad\text{and}\qquad -L +Land−L

relative to the chosen clock/reference sector. It is therefore already encoded in the amplitude-plane premise of the earlier uniqueness theorem.

The locked-spin reformulation does not eliminate that premise; it translates it into a spectral condition on JzJ_zJz​.

76.4 Consequence

The derivation chain is now accurately separated into two levels:

primitive clock winding⟹one unit gap, \boxed{ \text{primitive clock winding} \Longrightarrow \text{one unit gap}, } primitive clock winding⟹one unit gap,​

but

two-unit opposite gap⇍primitive winding + reversal symmetry alone. \boxed{ \text{two-unit opposite gap} \not\Leftarrow \text{primitive winding + reversal symmetry alone}. } two-unit opposite gap⇐primitive winding + reversal symmetry alone.​

So the factor-three orbit spectrum is not yet derived from the beam lock alone.

The remaining physical input is precisely the origin of the extremal amplitude splitting. A genuine V2 closure must show why the stationary projected electron operator has two axial extremal states separated by two primitive clock quanta.

76.5 Sharpened next target

The most promising place to look is the magnetic orbital coupling already written as

Hmag=−q2m∑A<BFABLAB. H_{\rm mag} =-\frac{q}{2m}\sum_{A<B}F_{AB}L^{AB}. Hmag​=−2mq​A<B∑​FAB​LAB.

A single rotation generator naturally has opposite extremal eigenvalues. If the primitive projective clock fixes their unit normalization, an actual projected curvature tensor might supply the required +1+1+1 and −1-1−1 amplitude weights directly, making their separation two rather than postulating it.

The next test should therefore determine whether a beam-compatible full-dimensional curvature with the V2 photon/electron geometry can produce a normalized axial orbital generator with eigenvalues +1+1+1 and −1-1−1 while simultaneously retaining the primitive clock gap.
