Physical scale accounting for the normal carrier

Updated September 14, 2026

conditional calculation

The inverse carrier supplies a concrete geometric conversion between its surface radius and its simplex frequency unit. That conversion depends on the normal hopping strength. Equal frequency units therefore do not imply equal radii without an additional relation among the physical coefficients.

These are conditional scale constraints on the prescribed operator. The calculation does not determine a physical radius, introduce compactified dimensions, or fit a new particle assignment.

The frequency unit includes the spatial matrix factor

Let R_n be the ordinary radius of the surface feature, c the common wave speed, and gamma_n the dimensionless hopping coefficient of the gapped normal carrier. Its leading simplex frequency unit is

ω0,n=cRnKn(γn),Kn(γ)=n!γn−1∏l=0n−2hl.(1) \omega_{0,n}=\frac{c}{R_n}K_n(\gamma_n),\qquad K_n(\gamma)=\frac{n!}{\gamma^{n-1}\prod_{l=0}^{n-2}h_l}. \tag{1} ω0,n​=Rn​c​Kn​(γn​),Kn​(γ)=γn−1∏l=0n−2​hl​n!​.(1)

The h_l are fixed spatial integrals. Every occupied degree at one depth uses the same R_n, gamma_n and h_l. Finite-gamma corrections still depend on the degree, and must be checked before using the leading simplex polynomial as an exact spectrum.

If G_n denotes the dimensional coefficient multiplying the bounded hopping term, then

Gn=cRnγn.(2) G_n=\frac{c}{R_n}\gamma_n. \tag{2} Gn​=Rn​c​γn​.(2)

G_n is a coefficient of this inverse spatial operator. Its relation to the same-field interaction remains unproved. Holding gamma fixed gives omega_0 proportional to R^(-1). Holding dimensional G fixed instead gives, at leading order,

ω0,n=n!cnGnn−1Rnn∏lhl.(3) \omega_{0,n}= \frac{n!c^n}{G_n^{n-1}R_n^n\prod_l h_l}. \tag{3} ω0,n​=Gnn−1​Rnn​∏l​hl​n!cn​.(3)

The source law must therefore say which coefficients remain fixed when the cavity scale varies. The conversion from a frequency unit to a radius cannot silently switch between these choices.

An all-strength bound for equal radius and dimensional hopping
