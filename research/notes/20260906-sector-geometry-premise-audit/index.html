Sector geometry premise audit

Updated September 8, 2026

Summary: The sphere and projective sector assignments occur in V1 and remain conditional candidates in V2; spatial depth, topology, phase rules, and interaction dynamics require separate justification.

Provenance found, physical derivation not established

The user cautioned that the depth geometries may be incorrect and may have been copied from a mathematically underjustified V1. The local V1 forces document, section 0.8b, already lists CP1, S3, CP2, S5, CP3, and CP5 at depths 2,3,4,5,6,10. This establishes their presence in V1, not a complete edit-by-edit copying history and not the correctness of the accompanying force claims.

Section 12 of the V2 polarization module gives an inverse reconstruction conditional on:

  1. a smallest complex amplitude carrier;
  2. fixed normalization;
  3. a chosen physical or redundant status of common phase;
  4. transitive unitary action; and
  5. an already prescribed real configuration dimension.

Within that ansatz, retaining phase gives S(2r−1), and quotienting it gives CP(r−1). The dimension arithmetic is valid. It does not show that a particle's actual configuration is this amplitude space, that its norm is constrained in this way, or that its common phase has the assumed physical status. The written reconstruction does not derive these premises from the master interaction or establish that alternative geometries are impossible.

Three questions must remain separate

Question What would support it What does not settle it
Spatial depth d Independent kinetic derivative directions in a derived sector operator Number of display controls or complex coefficients
Geometry of Q_d A derived metric, constraints, identifications, and global structure Real dimension alone or a convenient homogeneous-space construction
Interaction and observed orbit Derived source, dynamics, physical projection/current and perturbation response A chosen generator with a trajectory-shaped readout

The particle-depth module already identifies depth with the number of local spatial derivative directions. Even if that count were established, it would not select a unique global topology or metric. Its projected-symbol theorem itself requires a sector embedding, projector, and compatibility assumptions; writing a d-direction operator after assuming the sector is not a measurement or a derivation selecting d.

The local symmetric-mode count is likewise conditional on independent quadratic directions and the specified mode construction. It is not a unique signature of CP3, S5, or another global manifold.

Consequences for the current orbit investigation

  • CP3 precession and the finite response model are valid conditional constructions on a chosen six-real-dimensional geometry. They do not establish that geometry for the electron.
  • The earlier SO(4)/U(2) comparison varied carrier dimension, not V2's proposed particle-base dimension. Its minimum-carrier theorem cannot establish a minimum-six particle depth.
  • The exact three-amplitude restriction on candidate CP2 and S5 reproduces the prepared ideal demo with the restricted original readout. It is a counterexample to uniqueness inside these candidate constructions, not evidence that CP2 or S5 is the correct lower-depth geometry.
  • A response-control rank calculated in the assumed CP3 model is not yet an empirical dimension witness. Source state, readout, phase sensitivity, and memory must be accounted for before interpreting it as spatial rank.

The five-depth reminder is therefore not resolved by merely substituting S5 into every calculation. Depth five stays in scope even if the S5 assignment is eventually rejected. The same applies to CP3 at depth six.

Revised next task

Following the user's clarification, take the mass-formula depth assignments as fixed inputs to this investigation. Do not compare d=4,5,6 as alternative electron assignments: the electron is depth six. Start with unspecified geometry at that fixed depth and derive the local kinetic symbol, source coupling, and physical readout from the proposed master equations. Use explicitly labeled geometric models to obtain concrete examples, while keeping claims dependent on those choices separate from dimension-only facts.

A first target is an interaction that selects the CP3 complex-structure constraint from unconstrained six-dimensional variables, with controlled fluctuations and a justified kinetic reduction. This concerns the geometry at the assigned depth, not a new test of that depth. No new geometry is selected in this audit itself.
