Research / Mass and normalization

A common geometric and mass-carrier differential operator

Updated September 15, 2026

conditional calculation

The differential operator obtained from the linked geometric Hessian can also serve as a candidate normal carrier for the mass construction. It has exactly the required number of localized modes, equally spaced projected polarization, positive adjacent hopping integrals, and a controlled normal complement. This comparison is made at depths 3, 4, 5, 6 and 10 with the maintained particle indices.

There is also a simplification that applies to both the old and new carriers: a local anticommutator proves that their entire first projected polarization variation vanishes at the radial configuration. The full first shape source comes from one hopping matrix.

The geometric carrier changes the spatial integrals. It is a candidate consolidation of two differential expressions, rather than an assertion that all previous numerical predictions are unchanged. Its geometric fluctuation module and an ordinary spinor module have different full-turn rotation laws. Their physical identification, the occupied same-field geometry and its total inertia remain to be constructed. Mass remains frequency, in equivalent units.

Later calculations replace the independent hopping profile by the curvature moment, include depth two, and derive the leading complementary kinetic correction. The bounded angular completion specifies a full extension that preserves the leading simplex factors after radial dressing. Its finite-scale bound controls the remaining spectral error. These developments do not identify the physical rotation modules or solve the occupied supporting geometry.
