Complete Sector Assignments Under the Fixed Mass Prescription

Updated October 4, 2026

Result

Only the maintained complete assignment matches or improves every tested particle's present mass accuracy under the stated prescription. The comparison lets entire particle blocks change depths, requires distinct occupied depths, retains the original sector scales and measured electron reference, and covers every positive integer mode index. It examines all 120 permutations before applying the reference and scale equations.

The surviving assignment is

(dWZH,ddsb,duct,deμ,dτ)=(2,3,4,6,10). (d_{WZH},d_{dsb},d_{uct},d_{e\mu},d_\tau)=(2,3,4,6,10). (dWZH​,ddsb​,duct​,deμ​,dτ​)=(2,3,4,6,10).

The closest admissible different assignment has approximately 8.88588 times the RMS logarithmic mass error. The result gives the rigidity argument in the sector-support paper a complete-assignment test: improving an isolated family match is insufficient if the other families can no longer occupy distinct sectors under the same equations.

Two explicit uniqueness theorems below give a direct elimination proof for the depths and an exact proof for all twelve integer indices. The uniqueness is conditional on this numerical prescription and benchmark. The calculation leaves the origin of the scale coefficients, physical mode selection and bottom's mass unresolved. It does not assign zero probability to coincidence. The exact proof and the separate finite statistical controls are given below, with complete executable results.

What Has Been Proved

Theorem 1 establishes a unique depth arrangement without imposing tight accuracy on the other nine non-reference masses. Theorem 2 establishes the exact complete-assignment statement at the original individual accuracies, with unrestricted positive indices. Neither result requires a probability interpretation, a numerical upper cutoff or assuming that a finite search happened to find every alternative.

The hypotheses include the five scale-defined depths, block grouping, distinct occupancy, numerical sector factors and shared lepton unit. The electron factor already contains its reference count. These theorems prove uniqueness within those hypotheses; deriving the hypotheses physically, resolving bottom and selecting particle states dynamically remain separate problems. The aggregate RMS comparison later in this article is an additional reported calculation, not a consequence of componentwise uniqueness alone.

All finite arithmetic in the proofs is independently reproducible with the theorem certificate checker and exact rational certificates, using the same public sector-inputs.json. Each strict comparison records its exact positive rational difference; no rounded displayed percentage is used to decide the theorem.

8. Meaning for the Sector Proposal

The result adds a simultaneous restriction to the familiar adjacent-rung rigidity. The current arrangement preserves close matches across the defined blocks under one numerical scale chain. Every rearrangement either fails the reference and shared-scale equations or sacrifices at least one of those existing mass accuracies, even when arbitrarily large mode indices are permitted.

That is useful support for investigating this particular sector prescription. Its restrictive behavior survives a complete rearrangement test; it is more than a collection of isolated nearby integer values. It also makes the research target sharper: explain why one spatial law should produce these scales and occupied counts while retaining the observed common properties of the charged leptons.

The calculation does not derive the numerical coefficients, dynamically forbid the unoccupied rungs, remove the measured residuals, explain bottom or identify the physical mass and coupling operator. The additional spin, orbit, helicity and charge constructions in the support paper were not used as extra numerical scores here. Their shared geometric assumptions should be assessed together, without multiplying their consequences as independent probabilities.
