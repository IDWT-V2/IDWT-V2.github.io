Dimensional precession test

Updated September 8, 2026

Summary: Six dimensions are minimal for the stated static plane-induced precession mechanism, but a reciprocal 4D model also precesses and an unconstrained nonlinear readout cannot identify particle depth.

Question and fair comparison

Particle-depth correction: the D=4 carrier used below has sector CP1, not V2's Q4=CP2. V2 also has Q5=S5, which is not excluded by the absence of an odd-dimensional orthogonal complex matrix J. The result below is only about a restricted vector-carrier family. It must not be promoted to a minimum-six V2 particle-depth theorem. The subsequent five-depth comparison tests the actual CP2 and S5 sectors and reproduces the prepared demo trajectory exactly.

The user requested a lower-dimensional reproduction attempt as a possible route to evidence for "only 6D electrons." A failed numerical search would not establish that. This note gives an analytic obstruction within a stated model family, then an explicit lower-dimensional counterexample to the broader claim. These are actual geometric trajectories, not stationary orbital densities. No experimental electron trajectory is fitted here.

Retain the same nuclear three-plane O, orthogonal complex structure J on the electron's real vector carrier, invariant Hamiltonian rule, isotropic cross-plane response cost, and no additional fixed direction inside O. The response channel is still an effective-model premise in every dimension.

Here D is the real dimension of the vector carrier on which J acts. The sector manifold SO(D)/U(D/2) has its own dimension: it is a two-real-dimensional sphere for D=4 and six-real-dimensional CP3 for D=6. Neither the number of complex coefficients nor the response mode count is automatically D. Only the even-D orthogonal-complex family is being compared. A real J with J squared equal to minus the identity cannot exist in odd D, since det(J) squared would equal minus one. A three-plane cannot fit in D=2. This does not rule out alternative 3D or 5D dynamical theories using different state variables.

Exact obstruction in four dimensions

With a three-plus-one split, write

J=(Au−uT0),A=[a]×. J=\begin{pmatrix}A&u\\-u^T&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad A=[a]_\times. J=(A−uT​u0​),A=[a]×​.

The constraint gives u dot u=1, Au=0, and A squared minus uu^T=-I3. Since A squared=aa^T−|a|² I3, taking traces gives |a|²=1, and substitution gives aa^T=uu^T. Thus a=plus or minus u. The two signs label the two components; select u=a in the explicit checks. The same conclusions hold in the other component.

Consequently s=−tr(A squared)/2=1 and ||Q||²=||u||²=1 everywhere. SO(3) rotations inside O act transitively on this sphere. Every smooth scalar energy depending only on J and the unoriented three-plane, invariant under those rotations, is constant on each component. This is stronger than testing a few candidate potentials.

In particular, eliminating the same positive-cost response gives

Eeff(4)=−χg22,Eeff(6)=−χg22(3−2s). E_{\rm eff}^{(4)}=-\frac{\chi g^2}{2},\qquad E_{\rm eff}^{(6)}=-\frac{\chi g^2}{2}(3-2s). Eeff(4)​=−2χg2​,Eeff(6)​=−2χg2​(3−2s).

The finite singlet-plus-excitation source gives the same contrast:

E−(4)=Δ−Δ2+4g22,E−(6)=Δ−Δ2+4g2(3−2s)2. E_-^{(4)}=\frac{\Delta-\sqrt{\Delta^2+4g^2}}2,\qquad E_-^{(6)}=\frac{\Delta-\sqrt{\Delta^2+4g^2(3-2s)}}2. E−(4)​=2Δ−Δ2+4g2​​,E−(6)​=2Δ−Δ2+4g2(3−2s)​​.

The 4D source has three excited components rather than nine. Its induced response is not zero, but its equilibrium energy cannot distinguish the electron orientations. The static precession flow is zero. In 6D the interior 0<s<1 supports a nonzero flow when the kernel slope is nonzero. The endpoints remain stationary as established in the earlier note.

Therefore six is the minimum vector-carrier dimension for this particular static, isotropic, three-plane-induced precession mechanism. It is not a theorem that only six-dimensional particles can precess, nor that D>6 is excluded.

Reciprocal four-dimensional precession nevertheless exists

Keep the response dynamical instead of eliminating it. Let x and y be three-component canonical response variables, with chi,M,I_e positive:

E=∣y∣22M+∣x∣22χ−gx⋅a,∣a∣=1, E=\frac{|y|^2}{2M}+\frac{|x|^2}{2\chi}-g x\cdot a,\qquad |a|=1, E=2M∣y∣2​+2χ∣x∣2​−gx⋅a,∣a∣=1,

a˙=gIea×x,x˙=y/M,y˙=−x/χ+ga. \dot a=\frac{g}{\mathcal I_e}a\times x,\qquad \dot x=y/M,\qquad \dot y=-x/\chi+ga. a˙=Ie​g​a×x,x˙=y/M,y˙​=−x/χ+ga.

The first equation is precisely the same commutator convention restricted to the four-dimensional J, not an independently assigned orbit drive. The equations conserve E, |a|, and the vector charge I_e a+x cross y. The axis below is selected by preparation/conserved charge, not inserted into the Hamiltonian.

For q=sqrt(1−c²), 0<|c|<1, write

a(t)=(qcos⁡ωt,qsin⁡ωt,c),x(t)=(bcos⁡ωt,bsin⁡ωt,z),y=Mx˙. a(t)=(q\cos\omega t,q\sin\omega t,c),\quad x(t)=(b\cos\omega t,b\sin\omega t,z),\quad y=M\dot x. a(t)=(qcosωt,qsinωt,c),x(t)=(bcosωt,bsinωt,z),y=Mx˙.

With Omega²=1/(M chi) and kappa=chi g², the oscillator equations require

b=χgq1−ω2/Ω2,z=χgc. b=\frac{\chi gq}{1-\omega^2/\Omega^2},\qquad z=\chi gc. b=1−ω2/Ω2χgq​,z=χgc.

The electron equation is omega q=(g/I_e)(cb−qz). In addition to the static omega=0 branch, nonzero solutions satisfy

ω2+κcIeω−Ω2=0. \omega^2+\frac{\kappa c}{\mathcal I_e}\omega-\Omega^2=0. ω2+Ie​κc​ω−Ω2=0.

For c=.6, chi=1.2, g=.7, I_e=1.3, Omega=2, the computed rates are 1.86890551 and −2.14029012. Direct integration of all three vectors agrees with the exact rotations to about 2.5e-11 through t=8. Norm, energy, and rotation charge are conserved to the stated numerical tolerances. These are nontrivial, autonomous, reciprocal lower-dimensional solutions. Their stability, physical source, and atomic relevance have not been established. This harmonic-source result must not be transferred without checking to the finite, bounded contact-state model.

A sharper conditional discriminator: the slow-response-ratio branch

For fixed chi,g,I_e and nonzero c, the nonzero 4D rates approach plus or minus Omega as Omega increases, not a finite nonzero static rate. Their transverse response amplitude grows with Omega; they do not have a regular bounded-response adiabatic limit. The 4D static family remains stationary.

By contrast, the 6D low-rate branch satisfies

ω(1−ω2/Ω2)=2κsIe \omega(1-\omega^2/\Omega^2)=\frac{2\kappa\sqrt{s}}{\mathcal I_e} ω(1−ω2/Ω2)=Ie​2κs​​

and approaches the finite nonzero right-hand side at an interior s. This suggests testing precession well below an independently established source-response resonance, not merely testing whether precession occurs. It distinguishes these two specified effective models. Extra anisotropy, position-dependent interactions, or other source channels could change the 4D result and would need separate tests. A physical clock, source resonance, and readout still have to be derived before this becomes an experimental prediction for electrons.

A second conditional discriminator is mixed-block rank. Q maps D−3 hidden directions into O, so rank(Q)<=D−3. The 6D interior has singular values (1,sqrt(1−s),sqrt(1−s)), hence rank three. A physically justified measurement of three independent geometric mixed directions would imply D>=6 within this interpretation. Nine fitted response coefficients alone would not: internal source modes could supply coefficients without extra spatial depth. Neither this rank criterion nor the static argument isolates exactly D=6.

Why the displayed petal family is not itself a dimension witness

The existing CP3-to-demo bridge permits a chosen nonlinear readout. Allowing the same freedom in a smaller state space makes the displayed trace non-identifying. On the equatorial circle a=(cos theta,sin theta,0) of the 4D sector sphere, the autonomous Hamiltonian H=I_e omega a_z generates theta=omega t. This Hamiltonian does introduce an axis; it is a separate readout counterexample, not the isotropic static-source model above.

Let T_L be the Chebyshev polynomial, so T_L(cos theta)=cos(L theta). Choose

w(a)=R∣TL(ax)∣(ax+iay),h(a)=ηR1−TL(ax)2(4ax3−3ax). w(a)=R|T_L(a_x)|(a_x+i a_y),\qquad h(a)=\eta R\sqrt{1-T_L(a_x)^2}(4a_x^3-3a_x). w(a)=R∣TL​(ax​)∣(ax​+iay​),h(a)=ηR1−TL​(ax​)2​(4ax3​−3ax​).

This gives exactly w=R|cos(L theta)| exp(i theta) and h=eta R|sin(L theta)|cos(3 theta), including the demo's 2L planar petals. Checks include L=1,2,3,4,7,10. The absolute-value readout has the same non-smooth zeros as the target formula.

This is deliberately a detector/readout counterexample: the underlying geometric trajectory is a circle, not a petalled position trajectory. It is not a linear spatial projection or a derived physical apparatus law. It shows why the freely chosen nonlinear readout in the previous CP3 bridge cannot, by itself, certify depth. A physical restriction on that readout could restore discriminatory power and must be applied equally to both dimensional hypotheses.

Verification

lower_dimension_response_checks.py checks the exact polynomial complex-structure identity, random-frame static obstruction, harmonic and finite-source energies, 6D rank and static flow, vector-to-matrix dynamics, differential conservation, both integrated 4D rotations, the contrasting fast-response limits, and the readout identity. All verdicts use explicit runtime conditions, including under Python -O. Normal and optimized runs each complete 577 evaluations across 23 runtime conditions. An in-memory mutation audit reverses the electron velocity and, separately, rescales the readout by 1.1. Both errors are rejected in normal and optimized execution by the independent matrix-dynamics and readout conditions respectively; no mutated source is saved. The complete 41-script diagnostic suite and source checks also completed successfully after this addition; the knowledge base contains 142 notes. Numerical success is model consistency, not evidence of an observed particle.
