Research / Mass and normalization

Spatial compatibility and reciprocal adjustment under the mass current

Updated September 16, 2026

conditional calculation

The homogeneous current law gives more than isolated frequencies. Applying that same local action to two spatial patterns produces a reciprocal response that depends on their relative phase and physical polarization. Holding both original patterns fixed can greatly overstate the cost of their overlap: a small adjustment of the faster field components reduces that cost substantially.

The later stability refinement is essential to the result: the present depth-five moving trial retains a downhill fast direction. Lowering the overlap energy has not yet produced a stable coupling reduction.

The calculations here are conditional tests on the stated geometry. They evaluate two-mode fields on one round reference geometry. Constructing two spatially separated particle structures and following their encounter requires an extension of this calculation. They do not yet give a scattering trajectory, a cross-depth vertex, observed force strengths, or neutrino neutrality. The frequencies remain the mass variables throughout; amplitudes enter through canonical phase normalization and the coupled field equations.

Use the same action for one pattern and for a pair

Ψ0=ψa2ωa+eiθψb2ωb,Π0=−iωa2ψa−ieiθωb2ψb.(1) \Psi_0=\frac{\psi_a}{\sqrt{2\omega_a}} +e^{i\theta}\frac{\psi_b}{\sqrt{2\omega_b}},\qquad \Pi_0=-i\sqrt{\frac{\omega_a}{2}}\psi_a -i e^{i\theta}\sqrt{\frac{\omega_b}{2}}\psi_b. \tag{1} Ψ0​=2ωa​​ψa​​+eiθ2ωb​​ψb​​,Π0​=−i2ωa​​​ψa​−ieiθ2ωb​​​ψb​.(1)

The complete initial energy and its excess are

Epair=∥Π0∥2+∥F[Ψ0]∥2,ΔE=Epair−(ωa+ωb).(2) E_{\rm pair}=\|\Pi_0\|^2+\|F[\Psi_0]\|^2,\qquad \Delta E=E_{\rm pair}-(\omega_a+\omega_b). \tag{2} Epair​=∥Π0​∥2+∥F[Ψ0​]∥2,ΔE=Epair​−(ωa​+ωb​).(2)

Equation (2) evaluates the full nonlinear source in physical space. It does not insert an independent pair potential or assume that a superposition remains a solution. A common rotation of the two fields and their geometry preserves this action.

The model is reciprocal because both patterns belong to the same varied field functional. This establishes a common source law; a dynamical encounter still requires their coupled evolution.

A fixed superposition can be a poor encounter state

For coaxial patterns with the same spinor polarization, direct evaluation of (2) gives a large positive overlap cost at the higher depths. At gR/c=100gR/c=100gR/c=100, the down/strange example costs roughly 4.3×1044.3\times10^44.3×104 times the isolated pair energy. In the tested fixed-ratio regularizer family, the unadjusted cost grows approximately as g2d−2g^{2d-2}g2d−2 in the large-gap regime.

This is an initial-state calculation. It does not establish that an actual pair must retain its original faster components throughout an encounter. A small change in those components can cancel a large source that was exposed by the overlap.

To test that possibility, retain a spatial trial space invariant under H0H_0H0​, while evaluating FFF in the full physical space. Hold the slow component coefficients fixed and vary the faster component directions:

min⁡δΨf ∥F[Ψ0+δΨf]∥2.(11) \boxed{\min_{\delta\Psi_f}\ \|F[\Psi_0+\delta\Psi_f]\|^2.} \tag{11} δΨf​min​ ∥F[Ψ0​+δΨf​]∥2.​(11)

The fast directions are orthogonal to the two occupied rays, so the initial phase charges in (1) remain fixed in this static adjustment test. The norm in (11) includes the part of the nonlinear source outside the trial space. Projecting that source away before minimization would artificially reduce the cost.

The actual spinor basis includes negative azimuths and its full moving spinor frame. Depth two instead uses all four differential-form types. No extra spatial dimensions or independent interaction field are added.

In the first common trial space, the down/strange fast field changes by only about 9.5×10−59.5\times10^{-5}9.5×10−5 in relative norm, while its excess energy falls to about 6.52 times the isolated pair energy. Enlarging the spatial basis reduces that ratio to 5.61 and then 3.65. The up/charm control falls from about 1.46 to 0.864 when the spatial basis is enlarged. Thus the large reduction is established, but the remaining interaction has not reached a demonstrated infinite-space limit.

The larger trial keeps axial radial cutoff three and two sidebands on each side of the occupied pair. Its refined excess-energy ratios are 3.65, 0.864, 0.688, 0.504 and 0.209 at depths three, four, five, six and ten respectively. Independent integration uses 256 radial nodes and 384 phase nodes. The depth-five optimizer reached its iteration limit, and all these values remain dependent on the spatial trial space.

These ratios describe energy costs. They are not physical coupling constants obtained by dividing an interaction by mass, and they do not yet establish the observed grouping of responses by depth.

Include the actual motion of the adjusted pattern

For coaxial indices n,mn,mn,m, nonlinear spatial response generates azimuths n+k(m−n)n+k(m-n)n+k(m−n). Phase covariance and spatial rotation force the corresponding frequency to be

ωk=ωa+k(ωb−ωa).(12) \boxed{\omega_k=\omega_a+k(\omega_b-\omega_a).} \tag{12} ωk​=ωa​+k(ωb​−ωa​).​(12)

Ja=2∑k(1−k)ωk∥ck∥2,Jb=2∑kkωk∥ck∥2.(14) \boxed{ J_a=2\sum_k(1-k)\omega_k\|c_k\|^2,\qquad J_b=2\sum_k k\omega_k\|c_k\|^2. } \tag{14} Ja​=2k∑​(1−k)ωk​∥ck​∥2,Jb​=2k∑​kωk​∥ck​∥2.​(14)

In the smaller common trial space, the extra kinetic energy is about 1.3×10−71.3\times10^{-7}1.3×10−7 of the isolated pair energy for down/strange, 1.1×10−51.1\times10^{-5}1.1×10−5 for up/charm, and 1.3×10−51.3\times10^{-5}1.3×10−5 for electron/muon. The moving and static adjustments remain close in those tests. The electron/muon moving optimization reached its iteration limit, so its reported state is a variational trial with a nonzero optimization residual. The full low-mode Euler equations and an encounter trajectory remain to be solved.

What is established and what remains

One explicit local law now supplies isolated spatial frequencies, an orientation-sensitive current channel, and a reciprocal fast adjustment of overlapping patterns. The adjustment can be small in field norm and large in its effect on the overlap energy. Its forced motion and phase normalization are calculable from the same action.

The residual interaction still depends on the retained spatial space. The supporting geometry, the low-mode encounter dynamics, cross-depth maps, physical sector scales, observed vertices and complete translational inertia remain unresolved. In particular, a same-depth neutrino-pair test does not derive its interaction with ordinary matter.
