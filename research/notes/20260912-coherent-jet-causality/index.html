Research / Mass and normalization

Derivative constraints and coherent mass propagation

Updated September 12, 2026

Summary: Restricting the homogeneous coherent action to normalized derivatives of one spinor preserves its massive plane waves but gives an exactly nonlocal linear response, so this specified lift does not preserve finite propagation.

The carrier must have a physical realization

The homogeneous coherent action has exact simplex mass branches and finite propagation when its components are independent canonical fields in the specified spatial mode bundle. A different proposal identifies those components with derivatives of one smaller spinor. That proposal must retain both their compatibility conditions and their induced kinetic norm.

Here this identification is tested in a flat constant-coefficient control on ordinary physical spatial coordinates. The calculation does not choose a new geometry for the electron or compactify any directions. It excludes this particular derivative realization, not all reductions of the master field on the curved physical sector.

Why canonical independent jets impose a strong restriction

There is a general local explanation. Consider a first-derivative field (f,g)(f,g)(f,g) with g=ℓ∂xfg=\ell\partial_xfg=ℓ∂x​f, in a fixed flat canonical metric. Suppose an algebraic source (F0,F1)(F_0,F_1)(F0​,F1​) both comes from a real local potential and preserves this constraint for every smooth fff. Then

F1=ℓ∂xF0=(DfF0)g+ℓ(DgF0)∂xg.(10) F_1=\ell\partial_xF_0 =(D_fF_0)g+\ell(D_gF_0)\partial_xg. \tag{10} F1​=ℓ∂x​F0​=(Df​F0​)g+ℓ(Dg​F0​)∂x​g.(10)

Work in real and imaginary components. A real potential has a symmetric real Hessian. Its upper off-diagonal block is zero, so the lower one must also vanish:

DfF1=(D2F0)(f)[ ⋅ ,g]=0for every g.(11) D_fF_1=(D^2F_0)(f)[\,\cdot\,,g]=0 \quad\hbox{for every }g. \tag{11} Df​F1​=(D2F0​)(f)[⋅,g]=0for every g.(11)

Thus F0F_0F0​ is affine. With phase covariance its constant term and conjugate-linear part vanish; it is one fixed Hermitian complex-linear map, repeated on the derivative components. A nonlinear local gradient on independent canonical jets generally fails this compatibility test.

This argument has stated limits: it assumes an algebraic source, a fixed flat canonical fibre metric and exact first-derivative compatibility. A dynamical complementary field, a different induced kinetic structure, or a frequency-dependent elimination is not covered by it. Such ingredients must be derived from modes of the same master field; they are not mediator particles or unspecified extra physical dimensions.

Consequence for the mass investigation

The homogeneous action remains a valid conditional construction on its independent kinetic bundle. Its interpretation as the particular normalized derivative lift (1) is excluded as an exact finite-speed law by the full response, despite the plane-wave success (4).

A viable master reduction must therefore specify how the carrier is realized, retain its actual norm and complementary response, and pass a propagation test on perturbations. Equality of carrier dimensions or an exact rest-frequency sequence does not supply those missing steps.
