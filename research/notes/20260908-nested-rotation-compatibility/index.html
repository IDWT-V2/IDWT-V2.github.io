Rotations of nested spatial sectors

Updated September 10, 2026

Summary: A rotation preserving every fixed member of the nested spatial chain has a restricted block structure, while rotating the whole chain requires the moving embeddings in the projected dynamics.

The same spatial chain must support the interaction

Local geometric coupling operators have to fit together on the one nested manifold. Consider a common point with a nested chain of tangent spaces

Vd1⊂Vd2⊂⋯⊂VdN,d1<⋯<dN=D.(1) V_{d_1}\subset V_{d_2}\subset\cdots\subset V_{d_N}, \qquad d_1<\cdots<d_N=D. \tag{1} Vd1​​⊂Vd2​​⊂⋯⊂VdN​​,d1​<⋯<dN​=D.(1)

All directions are ordinary macroscopic spatial directions. The orthogonal differences between these spaces record their relation to other sectors; they give no particle an inherent division into different kinds of space. The following theorem concerns rotations of this finite tangent flag. It does not classify all interactions, differential operators, curved global embeddings, or dynamical sector projectors of the master field.

Consequence for a rotation-neutral construction

Rotation-neutral spatial modes uses two equal rotation planes at depth five and three at depth six. In an adapted six-dimensional frame these are generated by

K5=J12+J34,K6=J12+J34+J56,(6) K_5=J_{12}+J_{34},\qquad K_6=J_{12}+J_{34}+J_{56}, \tag{6} K5​=J12​+J34​,K6​=J12​+J34​+J56​,(6)

∥[K,Pr]∥F2={0,r even,2,r odd,(7) \|[K,P_r]\|_F^2= \begin{cases}0,&r\text{ even},\\2,&r\text{ odd},\end{cases} \tag{7} ∥[K,Pr​]∥F2​={0,2,​r even,r odd,​(7)

for the coordinate flag with the last incomplete plane crossing its boundary. The crossing term cannot be discarded while calling (6) the restriction of a rotation that fixes every member of the chain.

Indeed, under the fixed-flag assumption a nonzero five-dimensional spin generator has only one plane: Q5=κH12/2Q_5=\kappa H_{12}/2Q5​=κH12​/2. Including the ordinary single-valued orbital functions does not produce a neutral mode of this one-plane rotation: the orbital weights are integers and the spin weights are half integers. Twists or further field structure change that premise.

This locates a definite requirement for the two-plane cancellation: it must come from dynamics that move the nested embeddings, a derived effective operator, or other specified geometric structure beyond (3).

Use in generalized coupling

The fixed-chain result provides a precise check on proposed common rotation generators. The moving-chain result supplies a constructive alternative when the higher spatial pattern rotates the nested geometry. Equation (10) then identifies the additional terms that a downward source calculation must include.

Neither result derives a preferred embedding motion, electromagnetic charge, or the occupied five-dimensional neutral states. They keep the proposed cancellation compatible with the single nested chain of spatial submanifolds while making its next dynamical calculation explicit.
