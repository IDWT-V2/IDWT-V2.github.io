Bounded mass operator and its joint dynamics

Updated September 14, 2026

conditional calculation

The inverse spatial mass operator can now use smooth bounded coefficients. Its complete normal spectrum has a proven positive gap, and its actual localized modes give the equally spaced factors needed by the simplex frequency construction. There is also a derived common geometric response within each depth. The construction is still conditional: its occupied wave drives a deformation of the proposed supporting geometry, so their joint stationary dynamics has not yet been obtained.

Mass is the selected frequency, mc^2=hbar omega. These calculations retain the assigned particle indices and read frequency from the operator. Field magnitude supplies canonical normalization where required; it is not substituted for mass. All spatial coordinates remain ordinary and uncompactified. The normal matrices describe field components, and the sectors remain nested rather than the particles.

The localized action supplies a different piece

A separate polarization-ratio current action has an explicit localized profile with the same shape for all grades at one depth. Its radial ratio is

The common magnitude is absent from this equation. Under its declared current weights and canonical norm, the full action gives E=hbar omega Q and P=gamma hbar omega Q v/c^2. Thus it realizes positive total inertia on the displayed oscillating branch.

Its stability test is limiting. Exact full-multiplet algebra and a translated-packet argument show that the formal linearized spatial evolution is not uniformly bounded. This action therefore supplies an existence and inertia example, not a stable physical mass operator. Its binomial current weights also remain premises. It has not been identified with the bounded normal carrier above.

The phase calculation also establishes when a signed partial momentum response is opposite in phase to an imposed oscillating velocity. Such a response must not be called negative physical particle inertia. Computing the complete particle still requires its sustaining motion, momentum and stress.

The next useful model is a joint same-field action whose occupied wave and supporting geometry solve the same stationary and dynamical equations. The endpoint source and the full linear response now provide concrete tests for it. A specified common physical premise is acceptable; its predicted frequencies, geometry and stability must then be checked together.
