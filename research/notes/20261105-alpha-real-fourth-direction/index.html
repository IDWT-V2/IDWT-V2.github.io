Alpha Decay Through a Real Fourth Spatial Direction

Updated November 5, 2026

physical proposal with conditional mathematics

The maintained IDWT assignments place up quarks at depth four and down quarks at depth three. An alpha particle contains two protons and two neutrons, hence six up and six down valence quarks. The observed composition is summarized by Brookhaven's alpha-decay guide and CERN's quark bookkeeping.

In the proposed aligned nuclear configuration, the up patterns have a bound fourth coordinate. The down patterns extend along it as one of their free directions. Alpha-type escape can therefore be investigated as separation of the composite through a real fourth spatial direction. The whole alpha leaves; the difference concerns the participating field structures. A barrier in the observer's three-dimensional slice need not block every path in the full geometry. Whether a usable path actually exists depends on the field and contact configuration.

All directions are ordinary spatial directions in one infinite-dimensional space, with time separate. The displayed four-space model describes a candidate part of that field geometry. The proposed mechanism uses no particle-exchange force and retains deterministic electron trajectories.
