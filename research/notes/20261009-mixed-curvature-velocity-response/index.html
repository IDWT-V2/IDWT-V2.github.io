Mixed Spatial-Mode Curvature, Induced Inertia and Spectral Response

Updated October 9, 2026

A changing complex spatial-mode frame can generate a reciprocal velocity interaction. The same eliminated modes also generate inertia; keeping both gives a different high-frequency response from retaining a constant velocity vertex alone. This is a conditional construction from a stated positive mode action. Its physical mode functions, occupation and gap still need derivation from the common spatial field law.

5. Paired Responses and a Related Spectral Method

The paired-mode continuation checks two equal positive fast bands with opposite pattern maps. Their gyro cross-response cancels at every frequency, while their induced inertias and direct source responses add. Unequal gaps or occupations leave residuals. The same continuation shows why zero Berry curvature does not establish a color singlet.

A related general adiabatic method derives both Berry forces and an inverse-gap-weighted inertia tensor; see D’Alessio and Polkovnikov, equations 15–27. The independently checked finite-mode completion here is a conditional application of that kind of geometry, with its exact complement retained. Its existence at every sufficiently rich spatial depth means that dimensional selection must enter through actual mode functions, their sector embeddings, occupation and source maps.
