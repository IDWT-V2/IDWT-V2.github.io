Spatial source complementarity

Updated September 8, 2026

Summary: Projecting a normalized odd-dimensional rotation tensor and its null-axis vector onto a lower spatial subspace gives an exact complementarity identity and conditional source-strength bound.

A source determined by the higher geometry

This calculation starts with a higher-dimensional spatial source and asks which of its components survive on a chosen lower subspace. All spaces are ordinary macroscopic spatial directions in one nested manifold. The split is relative to that lower subspace; it is not an inherent division of the higher particle.

Use the normalized skew tensor and unit null vector from the dimensional magnetic kernel:

JT=−J,J2=−I+nnT,∣n∣=1(1) J^T=-J,\qquad J^2=-I+nn^T,\qquad |n|=1 \tag{1} JT=−J,J2=−I+nnT,∣n∣=1(1)

in dimension d=2m+1d=2m+1d=2m+1. These imply Jn=0Jn=0Jn=0 and unit rotational singular values on n⊥n^\perpn⊥. At depth five, the specified pure spatial spinor model supplies both JJJ and nnn. For other odd dimensions, (1) defines a conditional tensor family rather than a claim about all spinors or physical particles.

Let WWW be an rrr-dimensional lower spatial subspace and let Z=W⊥Z=W^\perpZ=W⊥, of dimension s=d−rs=d-rs=d−r. In adapted orthonormal frames,

J=(AQ−QTC),n=(nWnZ).(2) J=\begin{pmatrix}A&Q\\-Q^T&C\end{pmatrix}, \qquad n=\begin{pmatrix}n_W\\n_Z\end{pmatrix}. \tag{2} J=(A−QT​QC​),n=(nW​nZ​​).(2)

AAA is the restriction of the rotational two-form to WWW, while nWn_WnW​ is the projected vector. Use ∥A∥2=−tr⁡(A2)/2\|A\|^2=-\operatorname{tr}(A^2)/2∥A∥2=−tr(A2)/2 for skew matrices and the ordinary Euclidean norm for vectors.

Exact projection identity

The diagonal blocks of (1) are

A2−QQT=−Ir+nWnWT,C2−QTQ=−Is+nZnZT. A^2-QQ^T=-I_r+n_Wn_W^T, \qquad C^2-Q^TQ=-I_s+n_Zn_Z^T. A2−QQT=−Ir​+nW​nWT​,C2−QTQ=−Is​+nZ​nZT​.

Writing t=∣nW∣2t=|n_W|^2t=∣nW​∣2 and using ∣nZ∣2=1−t|n_Z|^2=1-t∣nZ​∣2=1−t, their traces give

2∥A∥2+∥Q∥F2=r−t,2∥C∥2+∥Q∥F2=s−1+t. 2\|A\|^2+\|Q\|_F^2=r-t, \qquad 2\|C\|^2+\|Q\|_F^2=s-1+t. 2∥A∥2+∥Q∥F2​=r−t,2∥C∥2+∥Q∥F2​=s−1+t.

Subtracting eliminates every mixed component and yields

∥A∥2+∣nW∣2−∥C∥2=r−m.(3) \boxed{\|A\|^2+|n_W|^2-\|C\|^2=r-m.} \tag{3} ∥A∥2+∣nW​∣2−∥C∥2=r−m.​(3)

Consequently

∥A∥2+∣nW∣2≥r−m.(4) \|A\|^2+|n_W|^2\geq r-m. \tag{4} ∥A∥2+∣nW​∣2≥r−m.(4)

The lower bound is useful when r>mr>mr>m. It is a statement about projected source descriptors, before identifying an interaction channel or rate.

Five-dimensional source viewed in three dimensions

For d=5d=5d=5 and r=3r=3r=3, equation (3) reads

∥A∥2+∣nW∣2=1+∥C∥2≥1.(5) \boxed{\|A\|^2+|n_W|^2=1+\|C\|^2\geq1.} \tag{5} ∥A∥2+∣nW​∣2=1+∥C∥2≥1.​(5)

Every real three-dimensional skew matrix has one rotational singular value a≥0a\geq0a≥0, with ∥A∥2=a2\|A\|^2=a^2∥A∥2=a2. Since AAA is a compression of a norm-one operator, a≤1a\leq1a≤1. These bounds sharpen two endpoint cases:

  • If nW=0n_W=0nW​=0, then ∥A∥2=1\|A\|^2=1∥A∥2=1 and C=0C=0C=0.
  • If A=0A=0A=0, then ∣nW∣=1|n_W|=1∣nW​∣=1 and C=0C=0C=0.

Both cases are attainable. In an orthonormal basis of R5\mathbb R^5R5, take J=e1∧e2+e3∧e4J=e_1\wedge e_2+e_3\wedge e_4J=e1​∧e2​+e3​∧e4​ and n=e5n=e_5n=e5​. The first case is W=span⁡(e1,e2,e3)W=\operatorname{span}(e_1,e_2,e_3)W=span(e1​,e2​,e3​); the second is W=span⁡(e1,e3,e5)W=\operatorname{span}(e_1,e_3,e_5)W=span(e1​,e3​,e5​).

Thus orientation alone cannot make both projected descriptors small in this normalized family. If a response reads these as orthogonal, distinguishable channels with positive weights gB,gVg_B,g_VgB​,gV​, then

gB∥A∥2+gV∣nW∣2≥min⁡(gB,gV).(6) g_B\|A\|^2+g_V|n_W|^2\geq\min(g_B,g_V). \tag{6} gB​∥A∥2+gV​∣nW​∣2≥min(gB​,gV​).(6)

This bound requires both channels to be present with the stated positive metric. A physical map might omit one channel, assign different derived normalizations, or coherently combine contributions. In those cases the actual map must be evaluated; (6) does not replace it. In particular, the five-dimensional vector supplied by the scalar-exchange benchmark has not been identified as an electromagnetic source by this identity.

Relation to photon observations in a shared three-plane

Let k∈Wk\in Wk∈W be a unit propagation direction and take the observer's photon plane E=W∩k⊥E=W\cap k^\perpE=W∩k⊥. Because kTJk=0k^TJk=0kTJk=0,

C(E,k;J)=∣b∣2−(b⋅k)2.(8) C(E,k;J)=|b|^2-(b\cdot k)^2. \tag{8} C(E,k;J)=∣b∣2−(b⋅k)2.(8)

For a uniform propagation direction within this fixed WWW,

Ek∈S(W)C=23∥A∥2.(9) \mathbb E_{k\in S(W)}C=\frac23\|A\|^2. \tag{9} Ek∈S(W)​C=32​∥A∥2.(9)

Even-dimensional comparison

For a normalized even-dimensional tensor J2=−IJ^2=-IJ2=−I in d=2md=2md=2m, the same trace calculation gives

∥A∥2−∥C∥2=r−m.(10) \boxed{\|A\|^2-\|C\|^2=r-m.} \tag{10} ∥A∥2−∥C∥2=r−m.​(10)

At d=6,r=3d=6,r=3d=6,r=3, the two restricted norms are equal and both may vanish. For example J=e1∧e2+e3∧e4+e5∧e6J=e_1\wedge e_2+e_3\wedge e_4+e_5\wedge e_6J=e1​∧e2​+e3​∧e4​+e5​∧e6​ and W=span⁡(e1,e3,e5)W=\operatorname{span}(e_1,e_3,e_5)W=span(e1​,e3​,e5​) give A=C=0A=C=0A=C=0, while the mixed block carries the complete rotation. This is a relation to a chosen observer three-plane; the electron's six spatial dimensions remain equivalent and have no inherent split.

The odd/even identities provide exact tests of a proposed descending source map. They do not determine a particle's global spatial topology, prepare the required orientations, or assign empirical coupling strengths.
