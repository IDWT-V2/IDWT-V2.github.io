Spatial mode tensors carried by the field's rest frame

Updated September 14, 2026

Summary: A same-current rest-frame connection gives a covariant spatial tensor action and preserves zero-rotation-moment branches, while polarized principal modes expose a high-wave-number limitation of this completion.

A precise transformation law for spatial coefficients

The localized frequency and inertia action has an explicit spatial solution. Its initial boost calculation treats coefficient labels as spectators. If the oscillator coefficients instead represent ordinary spatial tensors, their transformations must compose consistently with the spinor boosts.

One local inverse completion uses the field's own current to define its rest frame. It introduces no independent frame field, additional particle or additional spatial dimension. It gives an exactly covariant action on a stated domain. It also produces a new kinetic response for complex polarized mode rays. That response has a high-wave-number limitation, derived below, so covariance alone does not complete the physical carrier.

A finite probe identity with the new kinetic term

For a target whose spatial rotation moment is zero, its contribution to (8) vanishes for every connection: the trace of each tensor rotation generator is zero on that ray. A fixed finite probe can change the common current uuu and its derivatives, but their values remain the same for different larger target grades at the same target profile.

Thus the cross contribution of (8), its full probe variation and its target translation variation remain independent of target grade under the same distinct-grade conditions as the earlier coupling identity. The result uses the complete new action, rather than keeping its frame fixed during the probe variation.

The diagnostic uses a unit-norm Gaussian probe with a spatially varying matrix polarization, retaining constant and linear spatial coefficient terms, their phases and a moving center. The added cross term is nonzero. Targets of grades two and three give equal values and equal arbitrary first-derivative probe variations at three points. This is a conditional functional comparison, not a solved encounter or an observed vertex.

The remaining carrier requirement

This construction supplies an actual transformation law, its local action, its reciprocal kinetic response and a precise propagation restriction. A more complete kinetic reduction must address the polarized defect while retaining the mass-frequency branch and its normalization. Additional terms or constraints would need their own same-field origin and variations.

It also remains necessary to identify the coefficient grades with physical spatial modes and explain their selection. Rest-frame covariance is not that identification. The derivative-carrier calculation already shows why simply declaring the coefficients to be normalized derivatives can introduce a nonlocal response. Neither a representation dimension nor a boost composition law bypasses that dynamical test.
