Spatial Curvature, Persistent Patterns and Observable Projections

Updated November 5, 2026

conditional research

One useful way to connect particle properties is to ask which features of a field pattern can change continuously and which remain fixed during that change. Mass, radius and magnetic response may depend on the detailed shape, while another quantity can depend only on how its orientation closes at the boundary. A shared charge-like label would then be compatible with different masses without assigning each particle an unrelated law.

This note constructs such a distinction for an existing IDWT spatial profile. The main result is an explicit phase connection whose curvature is built from the same spinor polarization that already determines the profile's circulating norm current. In a pure-polarization branch, a normalized integral of the highest curvature product equals one at depths four, six and ten. The value is independent of the profile radius and overall amplitude. At depths six and ten it reverses sign when the connection reverses. These are conditional mathematical results, author-checked and awaiting independent review. Electric charge, count selection and physical stability are further identifications to test.

All coordinates below belong to a ten-direction matter reduction within infinitely many ordinary physical spatial dimensions plus time. Restriction to a depth means dependence on a subset of those spatial directions. Spinor coefficients and trajectory covariance matrices are descriptions of this field and its motion, not additional physical manifolds. No mediator or compact spatial dimension is introduced.

3. One Curvature Gives Several Closed Forms

Where the normalized field is smooth,

dF=0,d(F∧k)=0.(5) \mathrm d\mathcal F=0, \qquad \mathrm d(\mathcal F^{\wedge k})=0. \tag{5} dF=0,d(F∧k)=0.(5)

The first equality follows from taking an exterior derivative twice; the second follows from the product rule. These are kinematic identities. They do not yet say that a particular integral is a physical Noether charge or that a detector measures it.

On a spatial d-dimensional restriction, a two-form has at most floor(d/2) independent plane blocks. Its kth exterior power must vanish when 2k>d. For maximal-rank preparations:

10 F through F to the fifth power

These are products of oriented area forms, not additional oscillators. A particle may retain one common temporal fundamental while carrying several such spatial quantities. Actual rank can be lower than the dimensional maximum, and derivatives in containing directions require a full ambient extension.

The absence of a top-degree even product in odd spatial dimension does not establish neutrality. Odd-depth fields still have lower-degree closed forms and can carry boundary data. The maintained depth-five charge construction must remain a separate source until an explicit map connects it to these quantities.

4. A Size-Independent Integral at Six and Ten

Take d=2m and choose the pure-polarization branch with J consisting of m equally normalized oriented two-plane blocks. In coordinates aligned with them, each block is

(01−10),J2=−I. \begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad J^2=-I. (0−1​10​),J2=−I.

This branch is compatible with the maintained six-dimensional chiral polarization construction. At ten dimensions purity is an additional restriction on the seed, not automatic for every chiral seed.

Rotational symmetry preserving J lets us place x in the first two-plane. Equation (4) then has first block coefficient 2a2/D22a^2/D^22a2/D2 and all remaining block coefficients 2/D2/D2/D. Therefore

Pf⁡(F)=2ma2(a2+r2)m+1,F∧m=m!2ma2(a2+r2)m+1d2mx.(6) \operatorname{Pf}(\mathcal F)=\frac{2^m a^2}{(a^2+r^2)^{m+1}}, \qquad \mathcal F^{\wedge m}=m!\frac{2^m a^2}{(a^2+r^2)^{m+1}}\mathrm d^{2m}x. \tag{6} Pf(F)=(a2+r2)m+12ma2​,F∧m=m!(a2+r2)m+12ma2​d2mx.(6)

The Pfaffian is the signed product of the independent plane coefficients; its square is the determinant. Using the sphere area 2πm/Γ(m)2\pi^m/\Gamma(m)2πm/Γ(m) and

∫0∞r2m−1(a2+r2)m+1dr=12ma2, \int_0^\infty\frac{r^{2m-1}}{(a^2+r^2)^{m+1}}\mathrm dr =\frac{1}{2ma^2}, ∫0∞​(a2+r2)m+1r2m−1​dr=2ma21​,

we obtain the dimensionless quantity

Qm=1(2π)m∫R2mF∧m=1.(7) \mathcal Q_m=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^m}\int_{\mathbb R^{2m}}\mathcal F^{\wedge m}=1. \tag{7} Qm​=(2π)m1​∫R2m​F∧m=1.(7)

This normalization is stated explicitly. It is not a measurement of elementary electric charge and is not the factorial convention sometimes used for a Chern character.

Inside a ball of radius R the same calculation gives

Qm(R)=(R2a2+R2)m.(8) \mathcal Q_m(R)=\left(\frac{R^2}{a^2+R^2}\right)^m. \tag{8} Qm​(R)=(a2+R2R2​)m.(8)

Thus the spatial distribution changes with a and depth while the full integral remains one. A cancels before differentiation. The mathematical result also holds at d=2, where the original profile's norm is not finite; that endpoint is an algebraic control, not a localized particle claim.

Under A↦−A\mathcal A\mapsto-\mathcal AA↦−A, the integral becomes (−1)mQm(-1)^m\mathcal Q_m(−1)mQm​. Complex conjugation of the normalized coefficient field produces this reversal of its phase connection. For six and ten dimensions, m is odd and the sign reverses. At four dimensions it does not. Whether that coefficient conjugation is the physical antiparticle operation requires the actual common dynamical law; the sign calculation alone does not establish it.

This provides a concrete candidate reason for different charged-lepton shapes to retain an equal signed geometric label. It neither forces the pure ten-dimensional branch nor incorporates the occupied simplex counts. Its value can coexist with varying mass because it is insensitive to a continuous size change and an overall amplitude change. Agreement with electric charge would require a common physical source law and the existing charge-generator normalization, not a relabeling of (7).

5. Boundary Conditions Are Part of the Mechanism

There is no contradiction between F=dA\mathcal F=\mathrm d\mathcal AF=dA globally on ordinary space and the nonzero integral in (7). Stokes' theorem gives

∫BRF∧m=∫∂BRA∧F∧(m−1).(9) \int_{B_R}\mathcal F^{\wedge m} =\int_{\partial B_R}\mathcal A\wedge\mathcal F^{\wedge(m-1)}. \tag{9} ∫BR​​F∧m=∫∂BR​​A∧F∧(m−1).(9)

The normalized orientation has nontrivial angular dependence at large radius, even though the unnormalized field decays. The boundary term survives. No compactification is being used.

For a smooth variation of the connection,

δQm=m(2π)m∫∂VδA∧F∧(m−1).(10) \delta\mathcal Q_m=\frac{m}{(2\pi)^m} \int_{\partial V}\delta\mathcal A\wedge\mathcal F^{\wedge(m-1)}. \tag{10} δQm​=(2π)mm​∫∂V​δA∧F∧(m−1).(10)

Consequently, compactly supported smooth variations preserve the full integral. Unrestricted boundary changes, zeros at which normalization fails, or changes of seed class need separate treatment. The result is a boundary-controlled invariant for this class, not automatic integer quantization for all fields.

The normalized orientation also cannot be assigned an independent Maxwell energy without checking the tail. For m greater than one, the tangential curvature falls as r to the minus two, so the unweighted spatial integral of its squared magnitude diverges at d at least four. A constructive finite diagnostic is instead the density-weighted integral of rho times the squared curvature: since rho falls as r to the power 2 minus 2d, its radial tail falls as r to the power minus d minus three and converges. Such weighting would be a new constitutive choice if added to the action. At fixed total norm its value scales as a to the minus four, so this term alone does not select a finite size. Moreover, the original field becomes small at infinity: changing normalized boundary orientation need not have a finite lower energy cost. A conserved boundary label is not by itself energetic protection against decay.

A smooth phase change z↦eiθ(x,t)zz\mapsto e^{i\theta(x,t)}zz↦eiθ(x,t)z gives A↦A+dθ\mathcal A\mapsto\mathcal A+\mathrm d\thetaA↦A+dθ, so curvature is unchanged. This establishes insensitivity to an overall temporal phase and to a common smooth local phase factor. It does not establish insensitivity to arbitrary changes in relative phases of the constituent portions: those can change z itself, introduce zeros or change boundary orientation. Nor does invariance of this diagnostic make arbitrary local phase changes symmetries of the original field equation.

6. Join the Invariant to the Persistent-Configuration Idea

The constructive adaptation of the geometric-electron paper is to search for distinguished actual field patterns at fixed conserved data. A possible variational question is

inf⁡E[Ψ]subject to the same physical charges, prescribed boundary class, and permitted spatial support.(11) \inf E[\Psi]\quad\text{subject to the same physical charges, prescribed boundary class, and permitted spatial support.} \tag{11} infE[Ψ]subject to the same physical charges, prescribed boundary class, and permitted spatial support.(11)

The energy and constraints must come from one action. Equation (7) supplies a concrete candidate geometric constraint to investigate. It does not supply E, nor does it establish that Q equals electric charge.

Three separate checks then become explicit. First, does a minimizer exist rather than spreading out or collapsing? Second, is its shape unique after allowing translations, rotations and the common temporal phase? Third, are there higher persistent branches with the same physical charge but allowed lower-energy final configurations and outgoing field flux?

A constrained minimum can support persistence; uniqueness can support identical intrinsic properties. These are different conclusions and must be checked separately. In particular, a topological label alone does not choose a size: (7) is identical for every a. The existing critical profile's scale and clock issues therefore remain active. Our new invariant gives a sharper constrained problem rather than silently resolving those issues.

The analogy with a descending functional must also retain total energy conservation. In a closed finite Hamiltonian system, a generic volume of initial states cannot simply contract into an attracting minimum. A localized core can instead lose energy to other regions of the same field. Then a reduced core description can relax while the full field conserves energy. A local minimum without an accessible escape process does not yield a finite decay rate merely because another lower minimum exists. One must derive outgoing channels, an instability or an allowed transfer mechanism; no noise or tunneling law is inserted here.

For a periodic field, a constrained functional such as E minus omega times a genuine conserved phase charge can locate relative equilibria if that phase symmetry exists. The charge in that multiplier is not automatically (7), the simplex count, or electric charge. Their identification would need a derivation. Likewise, counting critical points of a chosen auxiliary geometry does not establish three physical generations. The IDWT question is the number of persistent solutions of its actual common equations.

7. Use Beam Mathematics to Separate Motion from Registration

The related electron-beam paper reconstructs covariance from position and momentum measurements. That is useful even though its phase-space dimensional count is different from IDWT's physical spatial count. The applicable ingredient is a diagnostic for literal trajectories.

Let q(t), p(t) be deterministic physical trajectories and momenta. A finite observation record or ensemble of specified initial conditions supplies a covariance matrix Sigma of their coordinate list. These averages introduce no Born law. For a linear transfer M and detector projection P,

Σout=MΣinMT,Σdetector=PΣoutPT.(12) \Sigma_{\rm out}=M\Sigma_{\rm in}M^T, \qquad \Sigma_{\rm detector}=P\Sigma_{\rm out}P^T. \tag{12} Σout​=MΣin​MT,Σdetector​=PΣout​PT.(12)

Here is an exact control. In coordinate order (q1,p1,q2,p2)(q_1,p_1,q_2,p_2)(q1​,p1​,q2​,p2​), take unit input covariance and the canonical shear

q1′=q1,p1′=p1−tp2,q2′=q2+tq1,p2′=p2.(13) q'_1=q_1,\quad p'_1=p_1-tp_2, \quad q'_2=q_2+tq_1,\quad p'_2=p_2. \tag{13} q1′​=q1​,p1′​=p1​−tp2​,q2′​=q2​+tq1​,p2′​=p2​.(13)

The map preserves the symplectic form and the determinant of the full covariance, which remains one. But the first observed pair has covariance diag⁡(1,1+t2)\operatorname{diag}(1,1+t^2)diag(1,1+t2), with determinant 1+t21+t^21+t2. An apparently broader registered distribution can therefore result from deterministic coupling to unobserved directions without an increase in the complete covariance volume. The t here is a dimensionless transfer parameter, not a fitted IDWT evolution time.

For the electron's six spatial coordinates, the complete canonical list would have twelve entries if all six have independent conjugate momenta. For a ten-spatial-dimensional model it would have twenty before any constraints. These lists are bookkeeping for trajectories in the single physical space. They are not additional spatial arenas.

This gives a concrete analysis to apply to the maintained orbit-plus-precession trajectory: compare the complete trajectory covariance, the detector's projection and polarization-dependent changes of that projection. It can distinguish a change of observed shape from a change of the underlying field structure. Equation (13) is the completed diagnostic example, not an assertion that this transfer already describes the electron or explains diffraction.

8. Combined Consequences and Next Calculation

The three useful ingredients fit a common sequence: extract spatial orientation from the field; build its curvature and boundary data; constrain persistent configurations by the quantities the common dynamics actually conserves; compute how those configurations appear in a specified detector projection.

The strongest immediate calculation is to put the existing charge generator and the new connection on the same spatial profile. Test whether one physical source law relates the signed integral to electric charge while retaining the known fractional weights and neutral sector. The four-dimensional sign parity and the ten-dimensional purity condition are informative controls, not details to hide. Then vary the count-dependent shape while testing whether charge stays fixed and magnetic response changes consistently with the supplied mass.

There is also a direct coupling lead: for two spatial patterns, compare their connections, curvature ranks and boundary compatibility under the same action. Arbitrarily multiplying the separate invariants would not derive an interaction. A cross term must follow from the combined field, and it must account for both particles' responses and their actual relative phases and frequencies.

The current calculation establishes a concrete geometric quantity built from an existing IDWT field, not merely a resemblance to another theory. It also shows exactly why that quantity can stay the same while other geometric features vary. Its physical interpretation and compatibility with complete dynamics on the infinite-dimensional physical spacetime remain the next derivation.
