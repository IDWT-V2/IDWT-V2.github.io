56. Orthonormalized scattering-frame candidate and exact Schur compatibility

The strongest candidate from section 55 can be made exact by separating directional information from metric distortion before constructing the frame link.

From the observed and hidden scattering data form

Os=[pO  bO  pO×bO],Hs=[pH  bH  pH×bH]. O_s=[p_O\;b_O\;p_O\times b_O], \qquad H_s=[p_H\;b_H\;p_H\times b_H]. Os​=[pO​bO​pO​×bO​],Hs​=[pH​bH​pH​×bH​].

For nondegenerate impact geometry these matrices are invertible. Take their orientation-preserving polar factors

O^=polar⁡SO(3)(Os),H^=polar⁡SO(3)(Hs). \widehat O=\operatorname{polar}_{SO(3)}(O_s), \qquad \widehat H=\operatorname{polar}_{SO(3)}(H_s). O=polarSO(3)​(Os​),H=polarSO(3)​(Hs​).

Define

Qscat=O^H^T. \boxed{ Q_{\rm scat}=\widehat O\widehat H^T. } Qscat​=OHT.​

Then automatically

QscatTQscat=I,det⁡Qscat=1, Q_{\rm scat}^TQ_{\rm scat}=I, \qquad \det Q_{\rm scat}=1, QscatT​Qscat​=I,detQscat​=1,

Qscat↦ROQscatRHT. \boxed{ Q_{\rm scat}\mapsto R_OQ_{\rm scat}R_H^T. } Qscat​↦RO​Qscat​RHT​.​

56.2 What this does and does not derive

This construction removes the arbitrariness in the orientation of QQQ once a nondegenerate scattering state supplies observed and hidden momentum/impact directions.

It does not yet derive:

  • why the V2 master operator should use the polar-frame map QscatQ_{\rm scat}Qscat​;
  • the positive coefficient κ\kappaκ;
  • how the hidden scattering vectors pH,bHp_H,b_HpH​,bH​ are determined from directly prepared beam data;
  • or whether the corresponding mixed/extrinsic block is generated by the current master connection.

Thus the candidate is stronger than a hand-chosen QQQ but remains a proposed completion rather than a theorem of the current V2 action.

56.3 Candidate status

Among the tested constructions, the current ranking is now:

  1. Orthonormalized scattering-frame map QscatQ_{\rm scat}Qscat​: full rank, exactly orthogonal, covariant, statically Schur-compatible; a sufficient candidate.
  2. Raw scattering triad EtriE_{\rm tri}Etri​: full rank and covariant, but metric-weighted before polar normalization.
  3. Two generic bivectors: capable of full rank, but no preferred V2 combination.
  4. Single orbital bivector: generically rank two; already sufficient for unique SO(3)SO(3)SO(3) orientation and more economical than a full triad.
  5. Pair commutator [J1,J2][J_1,J_2][J1​,J2​]: the tested mixed block is rank one and vanishes at coincidence; this is not a universal rank classification for arbitrary laboratory placements.

The remaining task is to derive a source selecting QscatQ_{\rm scat}Qscat​ or an equivalent orientation from the master operator, and determine the restoring spectrum. A generic rank-two source can already select an SO(3)SO(3)SO(3) orientation (Section 99).
