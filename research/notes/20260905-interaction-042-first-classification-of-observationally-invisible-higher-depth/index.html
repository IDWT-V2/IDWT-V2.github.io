42. First classification of observationally invisible higher-depth operators

Use a local product chart

Q6≃Q2×F6/2, Q_6\simeq Q_2\times F_{6/2}, Q6​≃Q2​×F6/2​,

with coordinates

x∈Q2,y∈F6/2. x\in Q_2, \qquad y\in F_{6/2}. x∈Q2​,y∈F6/2​.

A higher-depth operator correction can be split schematically into

N=Nxx+Nxy+Nyy+N0, \mathcal N = \mathcal N_{xx} +\mathcal N_{xy} +\mathcal N_{yy} +\mathcal N_0, N=Nxx​+Nxy​+Nyy​+N0​,

where the pieces contain tangential, mixed, vertical, and algebraic/projector terms.

The direct projection condition does not require every one of these pieces to vanish separately. It requires only their pushforward sandwich to vanish:

MNL=0. M\mathcal NL=0. MNL=0.

42.1 Vertical derivatives

For a cylindrical lifted test field

Lϕ(x,y)=ϕ(x), L\phi(x,y)=\phi(x), Lϕ(x,y)=ϕ(x),

one has

∂yiLϕ=0. \partial_{y^i}L\phi=0. ∂yi​Lϕ=0.

Therefore any operator whose rightmost action begins with a pure vertical derivative satisfies

NyL=0 \mathcal N_yL=0 Ny​L=0

and hence

MNyL=0. M\mathcal N_yL=0. MNy​L=0.

Such a term is completely invisible when tested only on cylindrically lifted lower-depth fields, yet it can act on a genuinely depth-six electron state with nontrivial yyy dependence.

This gives an explicit nonempty class of observationally invisible higher-dimensional dynamics.

42.2 Fibre-divergence terms

Suppose

NΨ=∇y⋅Jy[Ψ] \mathcal N\Psi=\nabla_y\cdot\mathcal J_y[\Psi] NΨ=∇y​⋅Jy​[Ψ]

and the fibre current has vanishing boundary flux or adequate decay. Then

MNΨ=∫F∇y⋅Jy dμF=0. M\mathcal N\Psi = \int_F\nabla_y\cdot\mathcal J_y\,d\mu_F =0. MNΨ=∫F​∇y​⋅Jy​dμF​=0.

Thus total fibre divergences can change the local hidden distribution while leaving the complete marginal unchanged.

This is particularly relevant to the collision question: hidden coordinates can rearrange while their depth-two marginal remains exactly fixed.

42.3 Zero-mean fibre potentials

An algebraic fibre term

V⊥(x,y) V_\perp(x,y) V⊥​(x,y)

can be directly invisible on a cylindrical test state if

∫FV⊥(x,y)dμF(y)=0. \int_F V_\perp(x,y)d\mu_F(y)=0. ∫F​V⊥​(x,y)dμF​(y)=0.

It may nevertheless produce forces within the fibre through

−∇yV⊥. -\nabla_yV_\perp. −∇y​V⊥​.

Whether such a term is allowed by covariance, self-adjointness, and the one-field action is a separate constraint.

42.4 Mixed connection terms

A mixed covariant derivative can alter yyy-dependence while integrating to zero under the lower projection. These terms are natural candidates for the previously identified normal-connection slot,

A⊥, A^\perp, A⊥,

but their source law remains to be derived.

The important conclusion is now constructive rather than merely permissive:

ker⁡P is nontrivial, and it contains explicit operator classes capable of acting on hidden coordinates while leaving the direct observed projection unchanged. \boxed{ \ker\mathfrak P\text{ is nontrivial, and it contains explicit operator classes capable of acting on hidden coordinates while leaving the direct observed projection unchanged.} } kerP is nontrivial, and it contains explicit operator classes capable of acting on hidden coordinates while leaving the direct observed projection unchanged.​
