The source and inertia requirements

The local source test does not reproduce the assumed normal texture under the simplest tested matrix-current closure. Its dominant angular component instead opens a direct link between the ends of the projected chain. Electron and muon have nearly equal absolute responses in that deformation direction, despite their different frequencies.

The general shape calculation shows that genuine deformations change independent lower-degree terms of the frequency polynomial. Winding and the number of normal modes therefore do not protect the simplex coefficients on their own. A common restoring energy plus an independently normalized wave cannot support both branches at the same reference shape in the tested separable completion: their two geometric source gradients are not parallel. A joint same-field action can change those source equations; it has not yet been derived.

Keep the inverse constructions distinct

The earlier localized current action has a conditional frequency and inertia solution, but its mode weights and physical spatial carrier remain inputs. The new dynamical spatial frame repairs a particular polarized principal defect of that action. It has not been combined with the gapped normal carrier into a single self-supported solution.

The current task is to derive a joint sustaining oscillation and spatial geometry, preserving the assigned frequencies while accounting for their complete energy and momentum. A physical source, its common normalization, its depth dependence, and stability remain open. The calculations below make those requirements explicit and provide independently checked mathematics for testing a proposed completion.
