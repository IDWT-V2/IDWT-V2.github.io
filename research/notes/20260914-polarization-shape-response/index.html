The spatial response of a shape-preserving mass core

Updated September 14, 2026

conditional calculation

A positive shape term can change the disturbance response of the polarization core without changing its mass-frequency, spatial profile or canonical inertia. The useful distinction is between the background matrix form fI+ihαx^fI+i h\alpha_{\hat x}fI+ihαx^​ and the additional matrix components available to a disturbance. Those components remain physical field variations; none is removed from the equations.

Preserving motion and positive inertia

Translations and spatial rotations of the core stay in the scalar/vector span. In a retained component frame, an infinitesimal boost of an upper-only core enters the lower block. Consequently the linear map δ(P⊥ϕu)\delta(P_\perp\phi_u)δ(P⊥​ϕu​) has no frame-only contribution on this branch. The mixed frame-wave and pure frame second variations of (1) are zero.

This matters because a binding term that merely preserves the stationary profile can still produce a new reciprocal frame force. The analytic comparison in Holomorphic binding and its responsive-frame test does exactly that. The shape penalty has no such mixed quadratic source. The cutoff terms also have zero added branch variations, since their cutoffs are constant near the core.

A positive retained frame kinetic action therefore adds its own linear frame equation without this new wave-frame mixing. For a polarized grade there can also be the existing antisymmetric rotation term; its contribution to the quadratic frame energy derivative is zero. This does not remove the separate nonlinear frame-vacuum problem or derive the physical carrier.

The uniformly boosted core still solves the complete branch equations. The canonical translation–boost pairing remains

in natural units. With Q=1Q=1Q=1, inertia has the mass-frequency value. Neither a signed shape response nor a complex disturbance frequency is being interpreted as a negative particle inertia.

These are local spectral controls. A frozen center is a finite spatial region of the actual particle, so local amplification there is not by itself a theorem of unbounded whole-particle evolution. Independent Cartesian finite differences verify (5), and arbitrary complex variations at other physical directions verify the complete current and shape source. These checks include every constant-Clifford form degree at depths 3, 4, 5, 6 and 10, and both representation sectors at odd depth. They do not include all higher spherical-harmonic families.

Regular Laguerre radial bases give finite Hamiltonian matrices whose Hermitian structure and physical radial norms are independently checked. Some raw complex eigenvalue pairs occur near the radial basis cutoff. Their appreciable highest-basis weights and changing frequencies make them insufficient evidence of localized unstable modes. Separate central and staggered radial difference controls also show outer-domain and grid sensitivity in that high-frequency region.

Testing the outgoing condition

For a complex temporal growth value λ\lambdaλ, the radial equation can be written in canonically weighted variables as

The local action now avoids its original current-zero divisions and its specific tested tail defect, while a complete stable physical mass operator remains unestablished.
