Polarized photon transition strength

Updated September 8, 2026

Summary: Summing all spinor response directions gives an exact two-polarization transition matrix for a simple spatial Pauli probe, including helicity-sensitive zeros and a nonzero total-strength bound at every tested depth.

Include the target's allowed polarization changes

The photon-plane calculation compares a fixed target source with a specified photon. An incident wave also perturbs the target. This note computes that response for a particular spatial Pauli term, keeping all polarization variations in the adopted spin module.

Work in a finite Euclidean spatial chart of the one nested macroscopic manifold. Let k,e1,e2k,e_1,e_2k,e1​,e2​ be orthonormal ordinary spatial vectors, with e1,e2e_1,e_2e1​,e2​ the photon plane and kkk a propagation direction perpendicular to it. At higher depth these are three selected directions within the containing space, not a separate three-dimensional compartment.

Let zzz be a normalized target spinor. In even dimensions use a selected half-spin representation; the following bivector operators preserve it. Their components describe the spatial field's polarization and do not add physical dimensions. This is a frozen-envelope Pauli benchmark, not the complete electromagnetic current or a derived neutrino coupling.

The two spatial probe operators

With γ(v)=vaγa\gamma(v)=v^a\gamma_aγ(v)=vaγa​ and Euclidean Clifford relations, define

Ta=−iγ(ea)γ(k),a=1,2,T0=−iγ(e1)γ(e2).(1) T_a=-i\gamma(e_a)\gamma(k),\quad a=1,2, \qquad T_0=-i\gamma(e_1)\gamma(e_2). \tag{1} Ta​=−iγ(ea​)γ(k),a=1,2,T0​=−iγ(e1​)γ(e2​).(1)

T1T2=iT0,T2T1=−iT0.(2) T_1T_2=iT_0,\qquad T_2T_1=-iT_0. \tag{2} T1​T2​=iT0​,T2​T1​=−iT0​.(2)

G=(1−h12−h1h2+is−h1h2−is1−h22).(5) \boxed{G= \begin{pmatrix} 1-h_1^2&-h_1h_2+is\\ -h_1h_2-is&1-h_2^2 \end{pmatrix}.} \tag{5} G=(1−h12​−h1​h2​−is​−h1​h2​+is1−h22​​).​(5)

A full response cannot be dark to both polarizations

Since C≤1C\leq1C≤1, equation (7) gives the dimension-independent bound

tr⁡G≥1.(9) \boxed{\operatorname{tr}G\geq1.} \tag{9} trG≥1.​(9)

Consequently this nonzero simple Pauli coupling cannot annihilate the complete two-polarization response on any normalized pure target spinor. One circular or linear polarization may be blocked; both cannot be blocked simultaneously in the complete module. If h1=h2=0h_1=h_2=0h1​=h2​=0, the unperturbed source vanishes but the total transition strength is instead two.

For a real polarization operator TTT with T2=IT^2=IT2=I, a finite pulse gives an independent exact check:

This measures departure from the original ray. A nonzero source expectation can still produce an elastic phase response, so (9)–(10) are statements about the orthogonal transition component, not a partition of every possible scattering effect.

Five- and six-dimensional isotropic comparison

Spin(5)(5)(5) and either Spin(6)(6)(6) half-spin module have four complex components. On both, the subgroup generated by the three operators in (1) consists of two equivalent spin-one-half copies. One can identify the module for this calculation with C2⊗C2\mathbb C^2\otimes\mathbb C^2C2⊗C2 so that these probes act on the first factor. This tensor notation is a representation decomposition, not a new physical spatial factorization.

Uniform spatial rotations of a fixed pure ray give the same invariant probability distribution on CP3\mathbb{CP}^3CP3 in both cases. Spin(5)(5)(5) is transitive there, and its invariant distribution agrees with the Fubini–Study distribution, which is also invariant under Spin(6)(6)(6). The prior isotropic comparison provides the underlying spatial ensemble and its assumptions.

Therefore the entire joint distribution of (h1,h2,s)(h_1,h_2,s)(h1​,h2​,s), and consequently of GGG, is identical under this specified ensemble. In particular

for any fixed normalized complex photon polarization. What can still distinguish physical couplings

Equation (6) sums unweighted polarization transitions in a fixed profile. It does not impose the energy of the incident photon, include all spatial profile variations, or normalize the incident flux. The action can supply different spatial form factors, kinetic coefficients and accessible frequency spectra. The polarized response spectrum retains those frequency and exposure restrictions.

A coupling generated by a higher spatial operation need not reduce to the simple bivectors in (1). For example, rotation-neutral modes cancel a sum of two spatial rotation generators as an operator; (9) does not apply to that sum. Identifying the actual higher-sector source is therefore substantive. The photon having two bound spatial dimensions does not alone select (1) as the complete interaction law.
