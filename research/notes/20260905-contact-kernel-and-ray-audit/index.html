Contact-kernel audit

Updated September 8, 2026

exploratory not a module

Summary: Point-contact and smooth-density spectra are compared without identifying their operators.

NOT A PHYSICS MODULE. These are exact finite-band comparison calculations and an action-level audit. The graph-supported point-contact construction remains a live hypothesis; no alternative tested here is silently substituted for it.

1. The source distinction that motivated the test

The self-contact source, Section 6.2, explicitly lifts the scalar current onto the graph of a chosen projective ray:

Jd(q,[u])=Jd(q)δFS([u],[zΨd(q)]),Jd=ΨˉdΨd. \mathcal J_d(q,[u])=J_d(q)\delta_{\rm FS}([u],[z_{\Psi_d}(q)]), \qquad J_d=\bar\Psi_d\Psi_d. Jd​(q,[u])=Jd​(q)δFS​([u],[zΨd​​(q)]),Jd​=Ψˉd​Ψd​.

It then writes the scalar interaction Lsc=−(κd/2)Jd2\mathcal L_{\rm sc}=-(\kappa_d/2)J_d^2Lsc​=−(κd​/2)Jd2​, whose scalar Euler--Lagrange term is −κdJdΨd-\kappa_dJ_d\Psi_d−κd​Jd​Ψd​. Its diagonal kernel is local multiplication. Later in the same subsection, the self-field insertion is identified with a delta concentrated at the selected ray. Section 6.3 proves the finite-band projection theorem for that point insertion, and Section 6.4 applies it to the proposed nonlinear Dirac term.

The distinction is important: a kernel f(u)δ(u,u′)f(u)\delta(u,u')f(u)δ(u,u′) specifies multiplication by fff, whereas a coefficient f(u)=δ(u,z)f(u)=\delta(u,z)f(u)=δ(u,z) specifies a point-supported multiplier. Locality of the diagonal kernel alone does not choose that coefficient. The graph lift is explicitly an additional part of the written construction; it must neither be ignored nor mistaken for an already completed derivation of the action's projected dynamics.

Merely integrating J(q)2J(q)^2J(q)2 against the normalized graph delta returns J(q)2J(q)^2J(q)2. It does not automatically insert a new finite-band pairing kernel into the action. Establishing that pairing is the remaining dynamical step identified by this audit, not a request for an amplitude measurement.

2. The exact point-contact theorem survives

For

HN=H0(CPd,O(N)),DN=(N+dd), \mathcal H_N=H^0(\mathbb{CP}^d,\mathcal O(N)), \qquad D_N=\binom{N+d}{d}, HN​=H0(CPd,O(N)),DN​=(dN+d​),

It is not the claim that every algebraic scalar interaction has this spectrum. This demonstrates the importance of the operator choice. It does not disprove the graph-supported point construction by imposing the smooth construction in its place. No experimental precision or significance claim is made in this note.

6. Single contact evaluation versus double smearing

The finite-band coherent pairing kernel is

BN(z,w)=⟨z;N∣Tδw∣z;N⟩=DN∣⟨z,w⟩∣2N. B_N(z,w)=\langle z;N|T_{\delta_w}|z;N\rangle =D_N|\langle z,w\rangle|^{2N}. BN​(z,w)=⟨z;N∣Tδw​​∣z;N⟩=DN​∣⟨z,w⟩∣2N.

It satisfies

∫BN(z,w)dν(w)=1,BN(z,z)=DN. \int B_N(z,w)d\nu(w)=1, \qquad B_N(z,z)=D_N. ∫BN​(z,w)dν(w)=1,BN​(z,z)=DN​.

A point source evaluated at its own ray uses the coincident value of this one kernel. Replacing the source by its smooth coherent density and averaging the test coherent density against it instead gives

∫BN(z,w)2dν(w)=DN2D2N. \int B_N(z,w)^2d\nu(w)=\frac{D_N^2}{D_{2N}}. ∫BN​(z,w)2dν(w)=D2N​DN2​​.

This is the previously undocumented explanation of the two eigenvalues. A graph-source interaction with one such finite-band pairing can retain the linear simplex count; two smoothings yield a different quantity. Neither operation may be silently substituted for the other.

What remains open is the action-level origin and ordering of the pairing: the proposed graph current, the nonlinear variation, and the finite-band projection have to be joined in one specified construction. The test establishes a consistent mathematical distinction, not that the full master action has already supplied this joining.

7. Moving self-consistent ray: the limited cancellation that is exact

Differentiate P2=PP^2=PP2=P for a differentiable orthogonal projector:

(δP)P+P(δP)=δP. (\delta P)P+P(\delta P)=\delta P. (δP)P+P(δP)=δP.

Multiplying by PPP on both sides gives

P(δP)P=0. \boxed{P(\delta P)P=0.} P(δP)P=0.​

For a normalized uuu on the occupied line,

⟨u,δP u⟩=0. \langle u,\delta P\,u\rangle=0. ⟨u,δPu⟩=0.

This was checked with a complex ray u=(3/5,4i/5)Tu=(3/5,4i/5)^Tu=(3/5,4i/5)T and a Hermitian generator of projector motion. It removes the automatic objection that every change of the self-consistent ray must shift the occupied eigenvalue at first order.

It does not imply (δP)u=0(\delta P)u=0(δP)u=0, erase off-diagonal variations, or prove the full nonlinear Euler--Lagrange equation. In particular, derivative dependence of the ray, variation of the contact coefficient, kinetic contributions, and stability of the coherent branch remain separate questions. No claim of complete variational closure follows from the diagonal projector identity.

8. Findings to carry forward

Checked: the point-contact identity; the constant, point, and smooth comparison spectra; the exact smooth-symbol family ratio; normalization and coincidence of the single pairing kernel; its squared-kernel integral; and the diagonal moving-projector cancellation.

Not established: that ordinary diagonal locality alone forces the graph symbol; that the graph lift by itself supplies the required action pairing; that double smearing is the correct V2 prescription; that the chosen coherent branch solves and is selected by the full projected dynamics; or that these calculations determine a baryon frequency.

No current magnitude, field amplitude, or profile parameter was fitted. The unknowns are operator identification and the derivation or cancellation of their physical coefficients. The regression script reproduces the finite tests; the source passages are self-contact Sections 6.1--6.4 and 9.
