A Hodge-Gated Gauss Constraint Preserves Complete Spatial Singlet Branches

Updated October 10, 2026

A vanishing quartic contact does not remove a color source already present in a covariant kinetic term. A different common action can address that gap: put the geometric Hodge factor in the connection's actual electric-energy coefficient. Its exact zero then imposes a Gauss constraint on the source. There are nontrivial color-singlet branches satisfying that constraint without losing any active spatial direction.

This is a conditional action construction in the selected ten-dimensional parent. It is not a derivation of that coefficient from the master field, a complete strong-force law or an exclusion of all larger containing carriers.

Physical Scope

The common coefficient and the actual normal Clifford commutant now supply a coherent mechanism for excluding an independent color source in the other maintained sectors while preserving their spatial kinematics. Higher-dimensional form counts alone do not generate higher color forces in this action. The zero source follows from a declared dynamical coefficient and a constraint, rather than from the component count alone.

Several parts remain conditional. The symmetric Hodge choice is a premise; complementary contacts and an ordinary positive floor are not forbidden by the existing symmetry argument. Zero dielectric degenerates the connection's vacuum kinetic term, so a complete Cauchy formulation remains to establish. Positive gauge energy does not by itself prove positivity of an unspecified complete matter action. Active three/four has an ordinary second-order static Gauss response when the positive dielectric is frozen; its nonlinear dynamics, attraction, flux support, confinement and empirical strength must be calculated. The earlier fourth-order confinement window is a different response law and has not been transferred to this action.
