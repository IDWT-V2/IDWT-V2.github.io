Summary: The local scalar Dirac term gives exact polarized pulse equations, a complete spatial mismatch criterion and reciprocal weak-scattering maps with both ordinary and phase-conjugate contributions.

A direct local same-field calculation

i∂tΨ=−iαj∂jΨ+g(Ψ†βΨ)βΨ.(1) i\partial_t\Psi=-i\alpha_j\partial_j\Psi +g(\Psi^\dagger\beta\Psi)\beta\Psi. \tag{1} i∂t​Ψ=−iαj​∂j​Ψ+g(Ψ†βΨ)βΨ.(1)

The Hermitian matrices satisfy {αi,αj}=2δijI\{\alpha_i,\alpha_j\}=2\delta_{ij}I{αi​,αj​}=2δij​I, {αi,β}=0\{\alpha_i,\beta\}=0{αi​,β}=0, β2=I\beta^2=Iβ2=I. The coefficient ggg is the one scalar coefficient in this equation; no separate one-way or pair coefficient is inserted. Other contributions to a completed master action are not supplied by (1).

Choose a physical unit direction eee and let x=e⋅xx=e\cdot\mathbf xx=e⋅x. Fields homogeneous in all transverse ordinary directions form an exact class of (1). This is a plane pulse control with finite norm per unit transverse volume, not a transversely bound particle. It introduces no compactification and does not fix the global geometry of any IDWT sector. The bound-profile polarization waves have different profile and mass assumptions.

They are two parts of the same field, not mediator particles or extra spatial dimensions. Their positive kinetic norm is additive because the two frames are orthogonal. Their scalar current is the cross term

Positive free frequencies sharpen the selection rule

The exact zero (8) also strengthens. The two-variable Fourier transform of A†BA^\dagger BA†B has support in the negative-negative frequency quadrant; its conjugate has support in the positive-positive quadrant. These supports are disjoint. Their sum can vanish only if

Scope and verification

local_polarized_collision.py implements the complete local equations, both moments and both weak maps. local_polarized_collision_checks.py verifies the full Clifford reconstruction and physical rotation covariance at several spatial dimensions, exact nonlinear source zeros, independent spacetime source integrals, complete complex action derivatives and quadratic convergence to the weak map.

The same checks test continuous Fourier integrals of positive-frequency packets and full nonlinear collisions. Their changing spatial compatibility is measured after both patterns have responded, including spatial textures in all four traveling polarizations of an eight-component module.

The result establishes local polarization-sensitive coupling in this specified term and spatial class. It does not identify photons, neutrinos or bound leptons, select different particle depths, supply transverse localization, or derive a common coefficient after distinct-depth reduction. Every tested depth admits nonzero scalar coupling as well as permitted mismatches; no special depth-five zero follows from component counting.
