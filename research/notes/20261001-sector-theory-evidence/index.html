The Case for Investigating IDWT's Sector Structure

Updated October 4, 2026

Abstract

Can the dimensionality of a particle's spatial wave structure organize both its mass and its other properties? Infinite Dimensional Wave Theory (IDWT) proposes a nested sector structure and a mass-frequency law proportional to a simplex count. This paper makes the case for investigating that structure through the constraints it already satisfies. With the maintained assignments, a common sector scale gives an electron–muon mass ratio about 17.3 parts per million below the benchmark, while neighboring muon indices change the ratio by 15–17%. Fixed-index family comparisons favor the assigned depths. A complete rearrangement test with the original absolute scales, fixed measured electron reference, shared charged-lepton unit and exclusive occupied depths now finds only the maintained arrangement at every existing individual mass accuracy, covering all positive integer indices. At those same depths, explicit geometric constructions address orbital patterns, spinorial motion, handedness and neutral channels. A recent common-connection construction realizes the target six- and ten-dimensional lepton counts as polynomials in spatial Dirac eigenvalues, on families that also contain observer half-spin states. This is an inverse construction with explicit geometric constraints. The explanatory opportunity lies in reusing structure across these questions. The paper displays what is fixed, what follows and what is additionally chosen, and proposes concrete tests of state selection, complete mass-frequency dynamics and reciprocal coupling. The results constitute a reproducible case for investigation; the occupied particle states and a common physical interaction law remain to be derived.

1. The Scientific Question

A useful organizing principle earns attention when it continues to constrain new questions. If a proposed spatial structure helps organize masses, the next test is whether the same structure can also accommodate the particles' rotational and interaction properties without acquiring a separate adjustable rule for every observation.

That is the question pursued here. IDWT associates particle families with depths in one nested spatial manifold. The empirical starting point is a combinatorial mass pattern. The further motivation is that the same candidate depths support explicit mathematics relevant to other properties. The research opportunity is to determine whether these pieces follow from one physical law.

The case for investigation is the combination of numerical restriction and reusable geometry. Neither requires claiming that the full theory is complete. Both can be examined now, with explicit equations and reproducible comparisons.

Three results give a short route into the argument:

  • A rigid mass relationship and a complete assignment test: one exact count ratio approximates the electron–muon ratio closely despite large jumps to neighboring counts. Under the full numerical scale prescription, only the current complete arrangement matches every existing individual mass accuracy, even when all positive integer indices are allowed.
  • A concrete six-dimensional orbit construction: one geometric generator produces a sequence of two-, four- and six-lobed projections under a stated preparation and observer map.
  • A common six-/ten-dimensional construction: the two target counts can be reconstructed as polynomials on actual spatial spectral families under one specified connection, and those families contain half-spin representations under the same type of observer action.

The distinction between these results matters. The first compares with measurements. The second establishes a geometric mechanism for a visual pattern. The third establishes simultaneous spectral and representation compatibility. Their value together is the possibility of a common explanation, which the remaining calculations must test.

What a Sector Means

The depth ddd counts ordinary spatial dimensions of the same kind as the familiar three. Sectors form one nested chain in an infinite-dimensional physical manifold. The proposed electron sector has six such dimensions; the tau sector has ten. Sector inclusion does not make an electron a constituent of a tau. Particle patterns remain distinct, and their orientations need not define a universal fixed grid.

Mass is expressed in equivalent frequency units,

ωm=mc2ℏ. \omega_m=\frac{mc^2}{\hbar}. ωm​=ℏmc2​.

The model seeks the dynamics selecting that frequency. Physical field amplitude is not identified with mass; it remains unspecified where it cancels and must be retained where nonlinear dynamics makes it relevant.
