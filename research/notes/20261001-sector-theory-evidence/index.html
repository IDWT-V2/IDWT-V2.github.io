The Case for Investigating IDWT's Sector Structure

Updated October 17, 2026

Infinite Dimensional Wave Theory proposes that particles are patterns of one field, Ψ∞\Psi_\inftyΨ∞​, confined to layers of different dimensionality within one shared higher-dimensional physical space, with at least ten spatial dimensions. These are ordinary spatial directions in the sense of being the same kind as the familiar three; the ambient space is not three-dimensional. Its maintained assignments use depths two, three, four, five, six and ten. The case for investigating them has three parts: a constrained numerical mass structure, mathematical criteria that distinguish some of those depths, and explicit field constructions showing that higher-depth patterns can retain properties shared by observed particles.

Those parts answer different questions. A criterion that singles out six is potentially a selector. A construction that works at six and ten can be just as relevant to whether an electron and a tau can share charge and spin under one law, although it does not select either depth. A fitted mass or mixing relation is an empirical comparison. A reconstructed action accommodating supplied frequencies is a compatibility test. Keeping those roles visible makes the case stronger and testable.

The central result is therefore a research opportunity, not a completed alternative particle theory. Fourteen massive-state assignments have one combinatorial organization; several further properties can be calculated on related geometries; recent flat ten-dimensional actions support the supplied count clocks with complete positive inertia. Deriving the physical field, sector units and observed occupied indices from a common law remains unfinished.

Higher-Dimensional Space, Dimensional Layers and What Is Being Modeled

The dimensions are literal spatial directions of the same kind as the familiar three. The current containing-space hypothesis is ordinary flat R10\mathbb R^{10}R10; the total number of dimensions could be ten, larger or infinite. Particles may be described as confined to nested layers or submanifolds of different dimensionalities in this same space. The layer description does not specify the field dynamics that confine and sustain particles; those dynamics still need derivation. A layer need not be a separate substance, a compactified direction or an additional field.

The proposed electron involves six spatial dimensions and the tau ten. An electron is not a constituent of a tau. A pattern's internal bound directions also need not be the only directions in which its center can move. Its physical position, internal classical field, mode coefficients and detector registration are distinct. In particular, electron binding must preserve literal deterministic motion of the six-dimensional electron itself around the nucleus, with the maintained orbit-plus-precession trajectory as a baseline. No calculation below replaces those trajectories with a probability cloud.

The real-space clarification matters. An auxiliary amplitude sphere, a complex notation for paired real field components, or a representation space does not add spatial dimensions. Older projective and round-sphere constructions retain useful conditional mathematics. Their curvature, compact global geometry, spectra and finite-volume normalizations cannot be imported into flat physical space by changing names. No compactified dimensions, Kaluza–Klein towers, gravitons, gluons or particle-exchange forces are assumed here. Interactions must ultimately be changes of the same field.
