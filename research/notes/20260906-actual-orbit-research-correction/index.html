Actual orbit research correction

Updated September 8, 2026

Summary: Restores the actual-orbit target and Coulomb-plus-precession history, separating the existing mechanism from auxiliary models and its unresolved physical source.

Correction to the preceding research pass

The target in V2 is an actual electron orbit, represented by Hamiltonian evolution and its observer projection. It is not a conventional stationary electron orbital. The earlier assistant discussion substituted the latter as the target and incorrectly suggested that only circulation and second moments had been worked out. The existing 6D orbit demo already specifies the complete projected curve and its time-occupation measure.

The recent scalar Hartree and oscillator-state calculations remain correct within their stated auxiliary models. They do not establish the actual trajectory, the clock, the selected four-state orbit representation, or its observer map. Calling them progress toward the observed shapes without establishing this connection was unjustified. A stationary field carrying an angular expectation is not a derivation of a particle trajectory. The coupled scalar branch is an auxiliary field solution, not an established source for the demo's generator or proof of stability of its orbits.

Some preserved note IDs and filenames contain “orbital”; they name the auxiliary calculation that was actually performed. Replacing that word everywhere with “orbit” would falsely relabel the mathematics. Those notes now carry this correction explicitly. Standard terms such as spin-orbit coupling and orbital angular momentum do not imply a conventional-orbital ontology and are retained where mathematically appropriate.

Existing orbit construction

Provenance correction: Coulomb binding plus precession

The user's stated starting point was a Coulomb-bound 6D orbit, with precession subsequently added to produce petals. The assistant's later framing as a search for forces to create an otherwise unexplained shape lost that history. Inspecting only the earliest trigonometric demo was insufficient; subsequent revisions contain the relevant construction.

The history of visualizations/6d-orbit-slice.html records:

  • ce71f75: computeShell rotates the four-component direction in six coordinate planes, with rates abs(I[i]-I[j])*W1, where I[i]=exp[i]+1. It explicitly sets the precession lock κ = ω₁, takes component moduli, and combines them with commensurate phase motion to generate the path. The paths are computed from this motion, not spherical-harmonic surfaces. The assigned rates, phases, and small radial breathing term are not a numerical integration of an electron–nucleus force law. Its prose's closure and stability claims are not interchangeable.
  • f2ad825: explicitly clarifies that the direction of the trajectory in CP3 precesses, correcting the earlier wording “its amplitude precesses.”
  • 1f8a54a: replaces the legacy rotation recipe with a specified norm-preserving Hamiltonian flow, an L-dependent exchange, and a relative-phase clock. This is a mathematical replacement, not evidence that the legacy ordered rotations obeyed that same Hamiltonian.
  • c6d6bcf and 4701c3a: construct the exchange and clock as parts of one degree-L representation of a higher-dimensional symmetry action. Their frequency ratio follows within the selected construction; identifying its source with physical electromagnetism and fixing its rate remain conditional. Later corrections distinguish sector points from section coefficients and constrain the observer map.

These revisions can be inspected with git show <commit>:visualizations/6d-orbit-slice.html from this repository. The earlier Coulomb-plus-confinement Hamiltonian in the page's prose (for example c50ef57) does not by itself derive the later precession term. The historical intent and the implemented dynamics must both be recorded without conflating them.

The research baseline is therefore the existing orbit-plus-precession construction, not an arbitrary petal curve awaiting a mechanism. The next physical task is to derive or test that precession and its lock from the coupled electron–nucleus dynamics. Nuclear feedback is a candidate contribution or correction, not an already demonstrated prerequisite for petals. The recent nuclear-polarization diagnostic treats an electric dipole response; it has not calculated the induced magnetic feedback the user proposed.

Current mathematical construction

The demo distinguishes the sector coordinate [Z] in CP3, the orbit-state coefficient ray [c] in P(V_L), and the intrinsic Dirac field at [Z]. The four-state subspace V_L is selected inside the degree-L section space. For the specified Hamiltonian, [c(t)] = [exp(-iM_L t)c(0)]. With θ = ωt, the amplitude exchange and relative-phase clock give the aligned orbit

ΓLxy(θ)=R∣cos⁡(Lθ)∣eiθ. \Gamma_L^{xy}(\theta)=R|\cos(L\theta)|e^{i\theta}. ΓLxy​(θ)=R∣cos(Lθ)∣eiθ.

This has 2L radial maxima and intervening zeros for integer L ≥ 1. The L = 0 ring has a separately supplied clock completion, since the degree-zero section space alone is one-dimensional. The observer's finite registration density is the response-broadened time measure of this actual orbit:

ρobs(x)=1T∫0TGσ(x−ΓL(t)) dt. \rho_{\rm obs}(x)=\frac1T\int_0^T G_\sigma(x-\Gamma_L(t))\,dt. ρobs​(x)=T1​∫0T​Gσ​(x−ΓL​(t))dt.

The shape construction and its internal frequency ratio are therefore present. Deriving why the physical bound system selects that representation and generator, how its clock locks to the electron–nucleus motion, and its electromagnetic curvature identification, physical rate, and observer response from the action remains open. This does not erase the conditional representation-theoretic lock already obtained. A successful derivation must connect to this construction rather than replace it with stationary oscillator densities.

Scope of the geometric corrections

The characteristic-class obstructions concern full-rank global bundle identifications, not the existence of the coefficient trajectory in P(V_L). They do not invalidate the demo's orbit formula. The relation between the section-coefficient state, the sector coordinate, and the intrinsic field must retain the distinctions already made in the demo.
