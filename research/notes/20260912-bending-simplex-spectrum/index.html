The simplex spectrum from surface bending

Updated September 12, 2026

Summary: A conformal bending energy gives the exact simplex frequency sequence under a declared geometric time law, while ordinary tension changes its ratios and the round-surface translation response requires a different physical completion.

The frequency target and the spatial assumption

Mass is already expressible as frequency:

mn,d=ℏωn,dc2,ωn,d=ω0,dS(n,d),S(n,d)=(n+d−1d).(1) m_{n,d}=\frac{\hbar\omega_{n,d}}{c^2},\qquad \omega_{n,d}=\omega_{0,d}S(n,d),\qquad S(n,d)=\binom{n+d-1}{d}. \tag{1} mn,d​=c2ℏωn,d​​,ωn,d​=ω0,d​S(n,d),S(n,d)=(dn+d−1​).(1)

The problem is to obtain that frequency from the spatial dynamics and its corresponding inertial response. An arbitrary normalization of an eigenfunction is not a variable to fit in (1). A nonlinear fixed-point equation may additionally determine a field's physical magnitude and shape.

Consider an explicit geometric comparison: a round surface Σ=SRm⊂Rd\Sigma=S_R^m\subset\mathbb R^dΣ=SRm​⊂Rd, with m=d−2m=d-2m=d−2 even. The containing space has ddd ordinary spatial dimensions. The surface has two normal directions, represented by radial displacement uuu and axial displacement vvv. It would have to arise as a feature of the same physical field; the surface is not the whole sector, an extra particle, or a prescribed hard wall. No compactification is used.

This differs from the scalar conformal operator on SdS^dSd. Here the bending energy of an embedded surface supplies the operator. Neither comparison determines the physical geometry of an IDWT particle.

Which time law makes this a frequency?

An oriented surface with two normal directions admits a geometric symplectic form. Declare its physical coefficient to be Γ>0\Gamma>0Γ>0:

ΩΣ(U,V)=Γ∫Σ(UuVv−UvVu)dA.(8) \Omega_\Sigma(U,V)=\Gamma\int_\Sigma (U_uV_v-U_vV_u)dA. \tag{8} ΩΣ​(U,V)=Γ∫Σ​(Uu​Vv​−Uv​Vu​)dA.(8)

This is the codimension-two Marsden–Weinstein form; see Song and Sun, Section 2.1. It pairs two actual normal displacements. Its occurrence in the master field is an additional dynamical hypothesis.

The canonical field projection gives a conditional origin for its coefficient as a normal boundary-current integral. A common surrounding field can make it independent of core shape; a fully localized normal field has an outer contribution that cancels it.

Physical scope and verification

This construction supplies a natural curvature origin for the complete simplex polynomial and a concrete frequency equation. It does not supply a derived master-field surface, a spinor realization, mode selection, a selected radius or the required complete inertia.

The unrestricted first-order evolution in (10) also has a spatial operator of order ddd. Its commutator with a smooth nonconstant coordinate has order d−1d-1d−1 and is unbounded. The finite-propagation criterion therefore excludes using it unchanged as a fundamental causal equation for every spatial disturbance. A controlled effective bending response would need its same-field causal parent and frequency dependence.

bending_surface_checks.py checks the exact product by independently applying the differential operator to Gegenbauer polynomials, including the assigned degrees. It also differentiates the full six-dimensional curvature integral (2) numerically, in both normal directions and their mixed spatial variations. The largest relative error in the reported curvature Hessians is below 2×10−72\times10^{-7}2×10−7; radius and quadrature refinement are retained.

Independent variations of the actual area and momentum verify (14). The full two-coordinate trajectories check (15), while spatial spherical quadrature and velocity differentiation verify the complete tensor (19). These are tests of the stated geometric action, not experimental evidence that particles have the assumed surface structure.
