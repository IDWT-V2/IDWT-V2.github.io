Competing Ranges from the Shape Energy of a Geometric Guide

Updated October 13, 2026

A geometric guide can produce long-range attraction and a shorter-range outward response without postulating two independent force laws. In the existing neutral-frame action, guide tension supplies the first contribution; the cost of matching different endpoint shapes supplies the second. The calculation is conditional on a supported commuting guide and prescribed small endpoint deformations. It also reveals why a radial force balance alone does not establish a stable composite: freely moving endpoint shapes can rotate toward agreement.

This uses the independently varied connection version of the mixed neutral-frame action. Its reduction from the single field remains a physical premise. The later canonical J-only connection and determinant orientation actions have different variations and cannot inherit this guide by identification of notation.

No isolated transverse mode below the gap exists, because the profile potential is positive. The following calculation retains the complete transverse operator and its continuum.

2. Exact Finite-Segment Energy

Let x run along an ordinary spatial segment of length L. Write z=(v,w), with endpoint shapes g_1(y),g_2(y). These are slow shape coordinates, which can be constructed from phase-invariant field bilinears. Static shape data do not impose equal rapid carrier frequencies on distinct particles. The quasistatic boundary approximation and endpoint-source work remain explicit.

4. The Same Energy Permits Alignment

Take equal endpoint norms and let theta be the angle between q_1 and q_2. With |q_i|=q, the angular derivatives are

Uθ=q2sin⁡θ ⟨f,Kcsch⁡(KL)f⟩, U_\theta=q^2\sin\theta\,\langle f,K\operatorname{csch}(KL)f\rangle, Uθ​=q2sinθ⟨f,Kcsch(KL)f⟩, Uθθ∣θ=π=−q2⟨f,Kcsch⁡(KL)f⟩<0.(12) U_{\theta\theta}|_{\theta=\pi} =-q^2\langle f,K\operatorname{csch}(KL)f\rangle<0. \tag{12} Uθθ​∣θ=π​=−q2⟨f,Kcsch(KL)f⟩<0.(12)

Opposite shapes are an angular maximum even when their separation sits at a radial energy minimum. An orthogonal pair generally has nonzero alignment torque. Allowing unconstrained orientations therefore removes the inference of a stable composite from the radial balance alone. Actual cores, conserved data or continuing rotation could change the encounter, but they must be derived rather than used as an implicit angular clamp.

The polarization angle here describes the traceless symmetric frame deformation in (1). Under ordinary simultaneous rotation of its physical two-plane and mode basis, (v,w) rotates by twice the spatial angle. Opposite shape coordinates need not mean opposite spatial vectors. They do not identify a particle with an antiparticle, electric charge or a color label.

The L->0 radial divergence is a property of the quadratic boundary problem. Large gradients or overlapping end cores can leave its valid regime. A formal finite radial minimum need not lie in the small-deformation domain.

There is a quantitative control on that last issue. Suppose opposite endpoint deformations obey ||g||^2<=delta^2 M_0, where M_0=int u_0^2 and delta is a small relative shape amplitude. At a formal force balance, equation (11) gives L_*^2<=2 delta^2 M_0/T. The actual mixed-frame guide moments give, with n=d-1 and t=kappa_2 E_0^2/Omega^2,

Thus small endpoint distortions cannot balance tension at separations much larger than the guide width. Their formal radial balance lies where a long uniform guide with independent end caps is not controlled. The increasing force correction at larger separations remains derived; a controlled hadron-sized equilibrium is a further problem, not an outcome of this linearization.

The dynamic endpoint continuation retains each drive frequency, source work and the actual longitudinal stress. Distinct count-harmonic cross terms average exactly to zero over a fundamental period, while individual response remains. Direct high-frequency forcing can open radiation channels; phase-invariant slow shapes can remain static without locking unequal mass clocks. These alternatives must not be interchanged.
