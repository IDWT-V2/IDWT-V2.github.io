Uniform Extension and Geometric Precession of the Electron Orbit

Updated October 13, 2026

One Spatial Manifold, Two Distinct Geometric Requirements

The electron follows a literal deterministic orbit in six ordinary spatial directions. In the maintained bound-within/free-without premise, its pattern extends uniformly through additional directions. It is not a particle trapped at one value of each outer coordinate. A six-dimensional sector embedding and a uniform extension of its field are separate parts of the construction.

The ten-dimensional containing sector is itself part of the infinite spatial manifold. CP5 with a Fubini–Study metric is a candidate for this finite part, not a consequence of dimension alone. The following calculations test that candidate against the maintained uniform extension and orbit. They do not introduce probability clouds, compactified dimensions or mediator particles.

Translating the other pattern also leaves this overlap unchanged when the whole fibre and measure are translation invariant and there are no boundary contributions. A higher-depth perturbation with zero fibre marginal has zero contribution to this particular interaction. This is an actual selection statement within the declared action, rather than an absence of all higher-dimensional contact.

Variation with respect to the six-coordinate electron component gives g times the fibre marginal, and variation with respect to the higher-depth component gives the y-independent electron source. The reduced action thus keeps the electron uniform by construction. A visible force can still act in x. Its orbit q(t) and its projected breathing need not change just because the containing manifold has more dimensions.

Infinite fibres retain the existing cylindrical-field normalization caveat: finite overlap with an integrable partner is not a proof of finite total ten-dimensional field energy. No normalized constant state on an infinite fibre is assumed. The present conclusion concerns the force and allowed variations of the specified reduced action.

Preservation by the Full Equation

This is the condition to derive from the master action; writing a reduced action directly already restricts the allowed variations.

A two-sample diagnostic makes the distinction exact. The constant vector is (1,1)/sqrt(2). A matrix with equal diagonal entries a and equal off-diagonal entries b preserves it. There is no globally continuous extension of a nonconstant scalar pullback f([Z]) across that CP1. Fix a nonzero eta and approach [0:eta] along [epsilon Z:eta]. Every choice of [Z] reaches the same limit, but the pullback has limit f([Z]). Continuity would force f constant. What This Establishes About the Orbit

Uniform extension can remove an outer translation force while curvature rotates a transported orientation within the electron's active directions. Those two mechanisms are compatible. A precessing orientation needs neither an outer kick nor localization in an unused direction.

The explicit holonomy supplies a candidate geometric contribution to the existing orbit-plus-precession construction. It does not establish that the electron orientation is parallel transported, that the actual orbit plane precesses by these angles, or that its force law selects the required lock. The radius-to-curvature ratio remains physical input. For a composite field pattern, torques and its transport law must be derived from the same action. In particular, the earlier representation-theoretic 1:1:3 weights are not automatically these tangent-transport frequencies.

The useful next mathematical target is one uniform-sector-preserving kinetic and interaction law whose deterministic bound trajectory has the required orientation transport. Product geometry supplies a simple uniformity control; the tested Fubini–Study CP5 needs a different extension or operator to satisfy that condition. Neither model is selected by these calculations.
