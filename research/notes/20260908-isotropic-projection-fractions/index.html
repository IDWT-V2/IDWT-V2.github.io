Geometric preparation

Let vvv be a uniformly distributed unit vector in a DDD-dimensional Euclidean spatial space, and PPP the orthogonal projector onto a fixed qqq-dimensional subspace, 0<q<D0<q<D0<q<D. The squared projected fraction is

F=∥Pv∥2∈[0,1]. F=\|Pv\|^2\in[0,1]. F=∥Pv∥2∈[0,1].

Uniformity is a preparation assumption. In a scattering application the interaction, incoming state, and detector acceptance determine whether that assumption is justified. The subspaces here are ordinary spatial directions within the single nested physical manifold.

Beam-relative example in six ordinary spatial dimensions

Suppose a beam direction lies within the observer's three-dimensional spatial subspace of an electron's six dimensions. Its orthogonal spatial complement has D=5D=5D=5 dimensions. Two of them lie in the observer's transverse plane and three are perpendicular to the observer's subspace. If the transverse direction is isotropic in that five-dimensional space, the fraction perpendicular to the observer's subspace has

F∼Beta⁡(3/2,1),E[F]=35,Var⁡(F)=12175,Pr⁡(F≤t)=t3/2. F\sim\operatorname{Beta}(3/2,1),\qquad \mathbb E[F]=\frac35,\qquad \operatorname{Var}(F)=\frac{12}{175},\qquad \Pr(F\leq t)=t^{3/2}. F∼Beta(3/2,1),E[F]=53​,Var(F)=17512​,Pr(F≤t)=t3/2.

The electron has six equivalent ordinary spatial dimensions. The grouping above is relative to the beam and observer, with no inherent electron split. The 3/53/53/5 result concerns squared transverse momentum under the specified preparation. It supplies neither a scattering rate nor an energy threshold. Opposing momentum components can balance within a multi-particle event even when each has a nonzero perpendicular component.

Projected mass shell

If a particle satisfies the relativistic mass shell in the containing spatial sector, decompose its momentum relative to the observer as p=p∥+p⊥p=p_{\parallel}+p_{\perp}p=p∥​+p⊥​. Orthogonality gives

E2=c2(∣p∥∣2+∣p⊥∣2)+m2c4. E^2=c^2(|p_{\parallel}|^2+|p_{\perp}|^2)+m^2c^4. E2=c2(∣p∥​∣2+∣p⊥​∣2)+m2c4.

An observer reconstruction using the full particle energy EEE and only p∥p_{\parallel}p∥​ would therefore assign

Mapp2=E2c4−∣p∥∣2c2=m2+∣p⊥∣2c2. M_{\rm app}^2=\frac{E^2}{c^4}-\frac{|p_{\parallel}|^2}{c^2} =m^2+\frac{|p_{\perp}|^2}{c^2}. Mapp2​=c4E2​−c2∣p∥​∣2​=m2+c2∣p⊥​∣2​.

The qualification that EEE is the full particle energy matters: a partial energy deposit has its own detector model. This equation is kinematic; whether a process populates p⊥p_{\perp}p⊥​ requires its interaction.
