Nuclear stability test

Updated September 8, 2026

Summary: Coherent radiation and passivity impose a precise dark-mode condition for persistent dipole motion, while the ordinary proton benchmark falls far short of cancellation.

Question and present verdict

This is a conditional radiation/polarization test, not a test of whether precession can produce petals: the visualization already contains that construction. It also does not evaluate induced nuclear magnetic feedback. Its dipole cancellation conditions must not be promoted to necessary conditions for every physical realization of the 6D precessing orbit.

Can the induced nuclear response stabilize V2's actual electron orbits? The preceding simulations demonstrated feedback but did not establish this. This note adds coherent electric-dipole radiation to the harmonic response equations and tests necessary conditions for persistent passive motion. It is not a fully nonlinear Maxwell–Dirac–nucleus solution.

The ordinary, low-frequency, fixed-Bohr-radius proton-polarization mechanism is too weak to cancel that benchmark electron dipole. A nonradiating coupled mode is mathematically possible in the ideal two-dipole model, but requires specific matching and absence of loss. Existing V2 equations have not selected those conditions or supplied the physical local-field matrix and nuclear susceptibility. Thus this is a conditional exclusion plus an exact target for the remaining calculation, not a general no-go for 6D orbits.

Periodic motion must pass an energy test

For a passive isolated system with no incident power, Poynting balance gives

ΔErecoverable=−∫0T(Poutward+Pinternal loss) dt. \Delta E_{\rm recoverable} =-\int_0^T(P_{\rm outward}+P_{\rm internal\ loss})\,dt. ΔErecoverable​=−∫0T​(Poutward​+Pinternal loss​)dt.

Take a sphere surrounding the source in the radiation zone and a complete period, so periodically stored near-field energy returns to its original value. A genuinely periodic state with the same stored energy after T requires zero average outgoing radiation and zero irreversible internal loss. Nuclear feedback can exchange energy within the system, but cannot replace energy escaping to infinity indefinitely. A metastable orbit with slow decay is a different target; it needs a calculated lifetime.

This argument does not forbid a moving multidimensional state whose physical electromagnetic source is nonradiating. It also does not apply classical moving-charge radiation to a stationary quantum atomic source. The physical current, rather than the visual outline, is what matters.

Coherent radiative response

Use one spatial dipole channel with electron dipole d and nuclear dipole p, and time dependence Re[(d,p)e^{-iωt}]. For a passive two-oscillator diagnostic, let s_e,s_N > 0, bare frequencies Ω_e,Ω_N, real reciprocal coupling κ, and internal damping γ_e,γ_N ≥ 0. Neither susceptibility here already includes radiative damping. To leading electric-dipole order, both sources radiate through d+p. Define r_rad = 1/(6πε₀c³). The inverse response is

The last term is the common dipole radiation field, not two independent radiative damping terms. It retains electron–nucleus interference. This frequency-domain test avoids integrating an unregularized third-derivative radiation-reaction equation with runaway solutions. Finite spatial phase, higher multipoles, and the full causal high-frequency response are outside this leading-order model.

For v = (d,p), direct multiplication gives

Multiplication by ω/2 yields the time-averaged radiated and absorbed power. The radiation normalization agrees with the dipole-power derivation in UT Austin's radiation notes. Every term is nonnegative for positive ω. Therefore a real-frequency source-free mode Mv = 0 must have d+p = 0 and no internal loss in either occupied component. If γ_N > 0, a nonzero nuclear cancellation dipole cannot give an exactly persistent passive mode in this model.

This positivity calculation is for passive positive-response electron and nuclear oscillators. Some prepared section-space electron responses have population-dependent signs; those require their full dynamical treatment. The isolated-system energy requirement remains applicable without assuming an equilibrium susceptibility.

An exact dark-mode condition

Set γ_e = γ_N = 0 and p = −d ≠ 0. The remaining real equations require

ωd2=Ωe2+κse=ΩN2+κsN>0. \omega_d^2=\Omega_e^2+\kappa s_e =\Omega_N^2+\kappa s_N>0. ωd2​=Ωe2​+κse​=ΩN2​+κsN​>0.

Equivalently, Ω_e²−Ω_N²+κ(s_e−s_N) = 0. A positive-definite conservative energy and this matching condition admit a dipole-dark normal mode. The diagnostic constructs one explicitly, then shows that changing a frequency or adding nuclear absorption destroys that exact dark solution.

This proves mathematical possibility, not physical selection or attraction. Even a perfect dipole-dark mode can radiate through omitted multipoles. It is not automatically the prepared petalled trajectory. Every actual nonzero source harmonic and radiation channel must pass the corresponding test; petal count alone does not tell us which electromagnetic harmonics are present. For constant κ and a single lossless Lorentz nuclear response, the driven cancellation equation 1+κ α_N(ω) = 0 has at most one positive squared frequency. Arbitrary multi-harmonic cancellation is not automatic.

How far the ordinary proton benchmark is from cancellation

For the previously derived fixed-radius electrostatic geometry, κ = −1/(4πε₀R³), and α_N(0) = 4πε₀α_V. Cancellation requires an induced dipole comparable to the electron dipole, hence |κ α_N(ω)| approximately 1 with the correct phase. The versioned benchmark has η = α_V/a₀³ = 7.56×10⁻¹⁸. Relative to its static response, this requires an enhancement of approximately 1.32×10¹⁷. The inputs are the PDG 2024 proton polarizability and CODATA 2022 Bohr radius, as documented in the companion benchmark. This is not a bound on all nuclear corrections to spectroscopy or on an unspecified V2 projection.

In the one-pole nuclear model, normalized cancellation amplitude is

d+pd=1−η1−ω2/ΩN2−iγNω/ΩN2 \frac{d+p}{d}=1-\frac{\eta} {1-\omega^2/\Omega_N^2-i\gamma_N\omega/\Omega_N^2} dd+p​=1−1−ω2/ΩN2​−iγN​ω/ΩN2​η​

when electron backreaction is temporarily excluded solely to test the required nuclear amplitude and phase. For zero damping it vanishes at 1−ω²/Ω_N² = η, or relative detuning (Ω_N−ω)/Ω_N ≈ 3.78×10⁻¹⁸. For small residual amplitude, γ_Nω/Ω_N² must be much smaller than η as well. At the same real detuning with imaginary part equal to η, the residual amplitude is 1/√2, not near zero. The exact source-free mode must additionally satisfy the coupled matching condition above; a zero of the driven amplitude alone is insufficient.

These numbers do not propose an actual ultra-narrow nuclear resonance. They quantify what would be required to rescue this particular benchmark using the assumed one-pole response. Ordinary proton Compton measurements constrain its electric and magnetic polarizabilities; an independently measured example is the MAMI result. No matching atomic-frequency nuclear response has been derived here. Replacing the ordinary response with a resonant or multidimensional one without deriving its spectrum would merely introduce the desired answer as a parameter.

What is now determined, and what is not

Determined: mere induction does not establish stability; coherent radiation and internal absorption give explicit loss conditions; the two-dipole model has a sharply specified exceptional dark mode; the ordinary static proton benchmark misses the required cancellation amplitude by about 17 orders of magnitude. The prior conservative feedback simulations cannot be cited as evidence for radiative stabilization.

Not determined: whether V2's actual projected source, nuclear spectrum, magnetic response, and full coupled field dynamics produce a nonradiating or sufficiently long-lived bound orbit. The current project leaves those physical quantities unspecified. This cannot be decided uniquely by further tuning the illustrative oscillator. The next substantive task is to derive the local-field/current map and nuclear response from the proposed action, or identify a measured response with the same physical coupling.

Reproduction and related

nuclear_stability_checks.py verifies the coherent loss identity on complex amplitudes, the tuned dark mode and its failure under absorption/mismatch, and the single-frequency cancellation condition. High-precision arithmetic resolves the small benchmark detuning. It does not claim a complete radiative time evolution or a V2 stability proof.

After the user questioned the hardcoded success message, this diagnostic was changed to explicit runtime checks rather than disableable Python assertions. It reports 88 executed numerical evaluations across ten named conditions, plus the measured loss-identity residual, not a bare PASS label. An in-memory mutation reversing the response matrix's dissipative sign failed at the loss-identity check with exit status 1 under both normal and optimized Python. The correct script also runs its checks under optimization. This verifies that the checks can reject that error, not that they exhaust all possible errors. Constructed matching examples and algebraic identities remain internal model-consistency tests, not independent physical evidence.
