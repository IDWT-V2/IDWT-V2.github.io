Independent verification (Opus agent, 10 November 2026)

Reproduced independently (closed forms, adaptive quadrature, finite differences): the convergence thresholds (6D total finite iff p > 1/3, marginal iff p > 1/6) and the table values at r0 = 0.5; p_eff; the 6D total-variation distances 0.0000, 0.0019, 0.0163, 0.0885, 0.4192, 0.7763, 0.9687, 0.9995, 1.0000 and the 3D marginal values 0.0123, 0.0651, 0.3014; the angular factor 8 pi^2/3 (numerical test with W = exp(-r^2)); sigma and r_eff; K5 = 0.2562 and 0.4222 (insensitive to the interpolation, about 85% from d < 3), tail exponents -13.467 and -13.409, and L/a = 3.126e4 and 3.693e4 (24.1 and 28.5 nm for a = 7.7232e-11 cm); all loop-field ratios.

Corrections accepted:

  1. Crossover medians. The median column was computed with an unweighted cumulative sum on a non-uniform grid cut at r = 40. Exact medians: Born 0.687; u_c = 1: 0.696; 10: 0.737; 1e3: 0.975; 1e6: 1.537; 1e12: 3.433 (page 2.68, wrong); 1e48: 343.9 (page 33.689, truncation artefact, the rho^(1/9) regime reaching r near 460). The total-variation values are unaffected. The Case uses only the total-variation values.
  2. The rate rho^(2/9) is finite over the three unresolved directions but its six-direction total diverges (as R^2/2). The energy-density weight rho^(10/9) is an assumption.
  3. 'Born-linear' means a weight proportional to density; the factor 1/9 drops out on normalisation. The weight assumes the detector registers the increase of the self-term when densities (not amplitudes) add on a uniform ambient density. u_c is the core value.
  4. Contact: the normalised pair-energy cross term is taken as the contact measure, W(0) = 1, not derived from a scattering amplitude; offsets uniform in a three-ball and independent of the steered offsets; exactly three hidden directions; a = 2 reduced Compton wavelengths chosen; the tail exponent is fitted from the last two data points; L is not derived. The 1e-17 cm target is the high-energy compositeness bound; reading it as a geometric contact radius is the mapping. The same suppression would apply to other short-range contacts, which the page does not address. The Case states the result as a condition on L.
  5. Loop field: the law kernel agrees with the four-direction Laplace kernel because for a << R it reduces to it (1/(1 + R^2/eps^2) is eps^2/R^2 up to O(eps^2/R^2)); the agreement with the six-direction Laplace kernel at h = 0.3 is a numerical coincidence (they differ by 24% at h = 0.1 and by a factor of 3 at h = 1). A_w = 0 follows for a planar loop. F_xw is the off-plane component of a potential from a postulated linear field (the field law contains no gauge field; the kernel is the pattern's self-potential profile reused as a propagator); the three-direction analogue with z as the displaced coordinate gives -0.126 at 0.3. A four-direction Laplace kernel gives a 1/R^2 potential in the resolved directions, which is not observed three-direction magnetostatics unless the hidden extent is bounded. The Case now states the postulate.
