Physical polarization and the complete spatial current variation

Updated September 16, 2026

conditional calculation

The homogeneous current action preserves an entire spatial eigenspace. Its stability still requires perturbations outside that eigenspace. The calculation below uses actual spatial fields and retains the contribution of omitted angular modes to the positive square.

At depth two, a differential-form parent now produces the same component carrier as the spinor comparison. Its tangent polarization is an actual spatial vector, with scalar and top-form companions retained. This does not establish the complete observed particle spin assignments or a photon.

What the numerical stability tests establish

The depth-two form calculation tests the maintained indices 76, 81 and 95, two complex polarization choices, azimuth shifts one and three, and three angular cutoffs. At gR/c=1000gR/c=1000gR/c=1000, all 36 cases have growth rates indistinguishable from numerical zero in the tested frequency pencil. Independent finer quadrature agrees with the complete squared operator. All four form types are retained.

The spinor calculation uses the same current law at depths two, three, four, five, six and ten, with the maintained indices and three axial cutoffs. Its illustration uses gR/c=100gR/c=100gR/c=100 to resolve the small frequencies more reliably. No resolved growing mode appears in those channels. A conservative squared-matrix resolution estimate reaches roughly one percent of the selected frequency for the depth-ten control; that numerical limitation is reported separately.

These are finite spatial perturbation tests, including their exact positive angular-square contribution. They do not prove stability in all azimuthal channels, nonlinear stability, supporting geometry, or complete particle inertia. Increasing the spatial basis is also required when a two-mode encounter drives new angular components.
