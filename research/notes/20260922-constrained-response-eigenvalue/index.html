Fixed-norm response as an eigenvalue contribution

Updated September 28, 2026

conditional calculation

A fixed canonical norm is a natural way to prevent the support calculation from turning particle masses into adjustable amplitudes. It changes the question, however. The response no longer has to be transparent in the radial field direction, because that direction is excluded from the constrained tangent space. Its radial derivative instead enters the Lagrange multiplier, which is the standing frequency. This note determines what that means for the current positive response model.

What the constraint does and does not solve

The canonical constraint is still useful. It removes the radial direction from perturbations at a specified conserved QiQ_iQi​ and turns the support problem into an honest nonlinear eigenvalue problem whose output is a frequency. It does not require equal peak amplitudes, equal profiles or even equal conserved norms unless the common action supplies that rule.

The present equal-norm source controls are not sufficient as that eigenvalue problem. Preserving the established simplex law requires the complete amplitude-dependent Rayleigh contribution to follow one common spectral rule. A compensating mode-dependent bare term would merely move the fit into the bare operator.

The useful next target is therefore a constrained same-field action in which the spatial operator, source, response and amplitude are derived together. At each depth, the particle harmonics must follow one common field law and shared fundamental parameters. That law may generate different sizes, effective stiffnesses, conserved norms, amplitudes and profiles. Equal size is a testable subclass; independent per-particle retuning does not derive a spectrum. The resulting phase eigenvalue remains a spectral diagnostic until its relation to complete rest energy and inertia is established.
