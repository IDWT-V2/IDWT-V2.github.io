Research / Mass and normalization

Regular polarization binding with unchanged mass-frequency cores

Updated September 14, 2026

conditional calculation

A later density normalization removes the cutoff function and its thresholds while preserving the complete coherent-core Hessian. The formulas below retain the original construction and its independently reviewed evidence.

The localized polarization model can be extended through its zero-current sets without changing its particle-frequency branches. A separate positive shape term can then change disturbances outside the core's scalar/vector matrix form. The two changes have different jobs: the regularization makes the fixed-frame field equation well defined for arbitrary finite fields; the shape term changes its stability problem.

The resulting action remains an inverse model. The simplex current weights, the physical realization of their spatial carrier, and the common canonical normalization remain premises. Mass is the selected frequency, mc2=ℏωmc^2=\hbar\omegamc2=ℏω; no field magnitude is being substituted for it. All spatial coordinates are ordinary and uncompactified.

Evolution and its precise scope

On the unit sphere of finite matrix fields, (1) is twice continuously differentiable. The cutoffs remove every current denominator before it vanishes. The potential is homogeneous of degree two, so its source is homogeneous of degree one and its derivative is bounded on that sphere. Extending the source by zero gives a globally Lipschitz map: integrate its bounded derivative along line segments, splitting a segment at the origin if necessary.

With the component frame held fixed, the Dirac kinetic operator is self-adjoint. The Duhamel integral equation with this globally Lipschitz local source consequently has a unique global mild solution on the canonical spatial L2L^2L2 space. This follows by contraction on a short time interval and iteration using the finite-time Lipschitz estimate. This statement fixes the depth, representation rank and occupied-grade frequency. It does not give a bound uniform over the infinite graded hierarchy or supply its joint nonlinear evolution domain. Phase invariance gives the pointwise continuity equation

∂tTr⁡Ψ†Ψ+∂aTr⁡Ψ†AaΨ=0. \partial_t\operatorname{Tr}\Psi^\dagger\Psi+ \partial_a\operatorname{Tr}\Psi^\dagger A_a\Psi=0. ∂t​TrΨ†Ψ+∂a​TrΨ†Aa​Ψ=0.

The Clifford bound on its flux gives speed at most one. The same local estimate for the difference of two solutions on a shrinking spatial cone establishes finite propagation. These statements do not prove that a localized particle returns to its original shape after a disturbance.

For covariance, evaluate the frame-dependent quantities in a retained future timelike frame uuu, carrying the partner representation by its induced spatial rotation. The original current terms are invariant, and the added expressions are invariant under the simultaneous spinor and partner rotation in that frame. A positive finite-speed frame kinetic term can be retained as a same-field collective premise. Its coefficient vanishes with the particle density, so global evolution through the frame's vacuum degeneracy is a separate problem. The fixed-frame global existence statement is not being extended to that coupled vacuum problem.

On the core, an infinitesimal frame boost changes only the lower block. It has zero linear image under the upper shape remainder. The added shape term therefore has zero mixed frame-wave and pure frame quadratic coefficients there. The cutoffs also contribute no such coefficients, because they are constant near the branch. This property distinguishes (1) from the analytic binding replacement's nonzero mixed source.

For the complete unboosted branch, the energy is E0=ωQE_0=\omega QE0​=ωQ, where Q=∫Tr⁡Ψ†ΨQ=\int\operatorname{Tr}\Psi^\dagger\PsiQ=∫TrΨ†Ψ. The added terms and their first variations vanish on both the unboosted and uniformly boosted solutions when the retained frame is transformed with the field.

With the common premise Q=1Q=1Q=1, it has the mass-frequency value. Its positivity does not by itself prove stability, and an arbitrary eigenfunction amplitude is not a mass.
