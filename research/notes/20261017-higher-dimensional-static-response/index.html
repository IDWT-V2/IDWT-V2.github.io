Three-Dimensional Static Response from Ordinary Higher-Dimensional Space

Updated October 17, 2026

Ordinary higher-dimensional space need not force an observer's static response to have the bare higher-dimensional point-source falloff. Two explicit constructions make this precise. They use field response, without compactification or exchanged particles. Neither by itself derives the physical source or its universal coupling strength.

An exact integrated Green identity

The integrand is integrable in the extra coordinates even though the observer's uniform weight is not a normalized finite-volume weight. The integral has different units from the unintegrated kernel. Calling it a normalized average, or claiming universal physical coupling from its coefficient alone, would therefore be incorrect. A normalized weight over all of Rk\mathbb R^kRk cannot be constant and nonzero. Finite windows and nonconstant detector weights generally change the response.

The physical hypothesis is that a lower-depth pattern's uniform extension through directions unused by it realizes this integrated static registration. That hypothesis must be derived from the complete source, detector and reciprocal field equations with their action normalization. The mathematical coefficient does not independently identify gravity or electromagnetism. Time-dependent response and timing need their own calculation. The formula also does not apply to depth two, since k=−1k=-1k=−1 is not an integration dimension.

This alternative requires a transverse operator and gap; the integrated-kernel construction requires a different detector/source weighting premise and has no crossover scale. Their predictions must not be blended. In particular, confinement of an internal response does not automatically bind a particle center. A localized pattern in translation-invariant flat space can still have a continuous center-translation family. Its hidden active directions and its unused uniform directions are different. Neither static identity settles the project's decay kinematics or permits deleting a center momentum by assertion.

What this adds to the dimensional case

The exact identity establishes a constructive possibility: higher-dimensional field equations can yield the observed three-dimensional static falloff under a definite registration rule. The confined operator establishes another possibility with a different short-distance signature. These answer a concrete compatibility question that naive dimension substitution does not address. Selecting the rule, normalizing complete sources and deriving reciprocal dynamics remain physical research tasks. No graviton, gluon, compact direction or Kaluza–Klein tower is assumed.
