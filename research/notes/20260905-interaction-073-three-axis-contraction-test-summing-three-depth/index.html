73. Three-axis contraction test: summing three depth-six channels does not yield the required axial bilinear

Section 72 found the corrected beam-axis decomposition

G~=14σz⊗I+14I⊗σz+34σz⊗σz. \widetilde G =\frac14\sigma_z\otimes I +\frac14 I\otimes\sigma_z +\frac34\sigma_z\otimes\sigma_z. G=41​σz​⊗I+41​I⊗σz​+43​σz​⊗σz​.

Because the adopted depth-six self-coupling is

g66=14, g_{66}=\frac14, g66​=41​,

the bilinear coefficient obeys the numerical relation

34=3g66. \frac34=3g_{66}. 43​=3g66​.

The simplest possible explanation would be that the factor three comes from summing the same depth-six coupling over the three locked spatial directions. That hypothesis can be tested directly.

73.1 Isotropic three-axis contraction

For two spin-1/21/21/2 frame factors, the canonical rotationally invariant bilinear obtained by summing three equal directional channels is

Hiso=g66∑a=13σa⊗σa. \boxed{ H_{\rm iso} =g_{66}\sum_{a=1}^3\sigma_a\otimes\sigma_a. } Hiso​=g66​a=1∑3​σa​⊗σa​.​

This is the ordinary isotropic exchange operator. With

g66=14, g_{66}=\frac14, g66​=41​,

its eigenvalues are the familiar singlet/triplet values

−34,14,14,14 \boxed{-\frac34,\quad\frac14,\quad\frac14,\quad\frac14} −43​,41​,41​,41​​

up to ordering.

This operator is not equal to

3g66 σz⊗σz. 3g_{66}\,\sigma_z\otimes\sigma_z. 3g66​σz​⊗σz​.

The latter has eigenvalues

34,−34,−34,34, \frac34,-\frac34,-\frac34,\frac34, 43​,−43​,−43​,43​,

and explicitly singles out the beam axis.

Therefore merely saying “there are three spatial directions, so sum three copies of g66g_{66}g66​” does not derive the required orbit-generator bilinear.

73.2 What the factor three would actually require

A scalar isotropic contraction gives

∑ag66σa⊗σa, \sum_a g_{66}\sigma_a\otimes\sigma_a, a∑​g66​σa​⊗σa​,

whereas section 72 requires, after the beam axis is selected,

3g66σz⊗σz. \boxed{3g_{66}\sigma_z\otimes\sigma_z.} 3g66​σz​⊗σz​.​

To obtain the latter from three directional channels, the dynamics would have to project or coherently transfer all three channel weights onto the selected axial component rather than retain the rotationally invariant sum.

That is an additional dynamical statement. It is not implied by dimension counting, by ns=4n_s=4ns​=4, by the existence of three spatial axes, or by the value g66=1/4g_{66}=1/4g66​=1/4.

73.4 Important distinction from the isotropic exchange spectrum

There is nevertheless an interesting representation coincidence. The isotropic operator

14∑aσa⊗σa \frac14\sum_a\sigma_a\otimes\sigma_a 41​a∑​σa​⊗σa​

has one eigenvalue −3/4-3/4−3/4 and a triply degenerate eigenvalue +1/4+1/4+1/4, precisely the characteristic singlet/triplet splitting of

(2,2)↓Spin(3)diag=1⊕3. (\mathbf2,\mathbf2)\downarrow_{Spin(3)_{\rm diag}}=\mathbf1\oplus\mathbf3. (2,2)↓Spin(3)diag​​=1⊕3.

This is geometrically natural for the locked frame decomposition, but it is not the same spectrum as the orbit generator

G~:(54,−34,−34,14). \widetilde G:\qquad \left(\frac54,-\frac34,-\frac34,\frac14\right). G:(45​,−43​,−43​,41​).

Thus the depth-six value 1/41/41/4 fits naturally as an isotropic two-frame exchange normalization, while the orbit generator additionally requires one-body axial terms and an axial enhancement.

73.5 Verdict

The proposed shortcut

3g66 comes automatically from three spatial directions \boxed{3g_{66}\text{ comes automatically from three spatial directions}} 3g66​ comes automatically from three spatial directions​

fails.

The surviving stronger possibility is instead:

  1. an isotropic locked-frame interaction supplies a natural 1⊕31\oplus31⊕3 exchange operator;
  2. the scattering/beam state breaks the diagonal rotational symmetry to an axial subgroup;
  3. the stationary projected dynamics then reshuffles or splits that exchange operator together with one-body axial terms into the exact orbit generator.

That third step is still missing and is now the precise place where the 3/43/43/4 coefficient must be derived.
