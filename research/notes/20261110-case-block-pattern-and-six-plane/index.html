Four documents in order: the six-plane question; the charge integrals for every depth; the uniform background; the block table with mixing, the Planck-norm check and the neutral-value conjugation.

Normal six-plane versus the electron's bound six-plane

9 November 2026. User question on the case document, section 13: "The charge construction's normal six-plane differs from the electron's bound six-plane." Work it out or decide whether it is an actual problem.

Verdict

Not a contradiction. The two planes have to differ, and the way they differ is consistent with the charge table, the electron's neutrality under color and the four-direction orbit. One real condition comes out of it, and one interaction question stays open.

Setup (as in the case, section 9)

  • Ten matter directions e1..e10. Observer three-plane inside the base four B4 = e1..e4.
  • Charge construction: normal six N6 = e5..e10, paired (5,6), (7,8), (9,10). Color is SU(3) on those three pairs; charge is the common rotation of all three pairs (with the base part).
  • Electron: bound six Q6 = e1..e6, free four e7..e10.

So Q6 = B4 + L with L = (5,6), and N6 = L + the electron's free four. They share exactly one paired plane.

Why they must differ

N6 is perpendicular to the base four, which holds the three observed directions. An electron bound only in N6 would be uniform over ordinary space. Q6 must contain the observed directions; N6 must not (color has to commute with observer spin). Identifying them is not an option.

What the mismatch costs, checked in check.py (14 of 14 pass)

  1. Color rotations that keep Q6 in place: 4 of the 8 (u(2)). Keeping Q5 as well: 3 (su(2)), and that su(2) acts only on e7..e10. This reproduces the case's statement.
  2. The charge rotation maps Q6 to itself. The electron's shape is unchanged by it, so the charge label is not touched by the mismatch.
  3. This holds because L is one of the paired planes. A generic real two-plane in N6 is not kept by the charge rotation. Condition: the electron's two extra bound directions form a paired plane. Currently this is a premise (case 9.10, "assume a pure seed's plane structure is compatible with its bound/free split").
  4. The electron's component value (normal degree 0) is unchanged by all eight color rotations. Quark values (degree 2) form a triplet, Casimir 4/3.
  5. The four color rotations that do move Q6 tilt L into the free four. On the electron field F s + i G gamma(xhat) s their spin expectation is zero (F part and G part); the orbital expectation is zero because the profile is even in the bound directions and uniform in the free ones. All eight color charges of the electron are zero.

So the electron is an oriented object that is not rotating: its shape is not invariant under the four tilts, but it carries no color charge, and mass, charge and spin are the same for every tilt. A tilted electron is the same electron.

What the 4 + 2 split of the electron lines up with

  • Orbit. Quarks (depth 3 and 4) are uniform across all of N6, so a nucleus exerts no force on the electron along e5, e6. Only four of the electron's six position coordinates can change in an atom: e1..e4. That is the four-direction orbit already used, with the up quarks (depth 4) acting on four-direction distance and the down quark (depth 3) on three-direction distance. Consistency with the existing model, not a new derivation.
  • Charge. The remaining two bound directions are the plane the charge rotation turns.
  • Color. The electron's free four are the other two paired planes; the su(2) of item 1 acts there, matching the depth-6 row of the case's bound/free table (free factor su(2)).
  • Unsteered directions. A three-direction apparatus leaves w, x5, x6 unsteered: three, the same power used in the electron-electron contact fraction.

Open

  • A. Why the two extra bound directions form a paired plane (item 3). Needs the field law, not algebra.
  • B. Interaction. An electron near a quark is non-uniform along e5, e6 where the quark is uniform, so it singles out one of the three color planes locally. The density term is blind to the quark's color value, and a three-quark color-neutral combination treats the planes equally at first order. Any residual effect comes through the derivative terms along e5, e6. Not computed.
  • C. Depth 5. The charge rotation turns e5 into e6, which is outside Q5. The neutrino's bound five is the only assigned depth with half a paired plane. Geometric fact only; whether it connects to neutrality is untested.

Move the item out of "principal incompatibilities". Say instead: the two planes share one paired plane; charge and color neutrality of the electron hold for a fixed bound plane; remaining tasks are A and B.

The charge integral equals the table value exactly when the bound set is the whole base four (or none of it) plus whole normal pairs. Otherwise it is off by a multiple of w.

A. Why the electron's extra two directions must be a pair

  • The field law does not choose. The radial equations are the same for every seed and every bound plane (rho = F^2 + G^2 holds for random seeds and planes, error 2e-16). Energy and norm are identical for a paired and an unpaired plane.
  • The charge integral does choose. Tilting the bound plane by theta out of the pair (5,6) gives q = -1 + (w/9) sin^2(theta). Quadrature at theta = 0, 0.3, pi/4, pi/2 matches.
  • So the paired plane is the only orientation where the electron's charge integral is exactly the table value. At the case's supplied parameters (m0 = g = 1, omega = 0.99, table 12.15), w = 2.14e-3 at depth 6, so the coefficient w/9 is 2.4e-4.

Supports, with this limit: the selection comes from the charge, not from the energy. Nothing in the isolated static law pulls a tilted electron back.

B. Do the three colors stay equivalent next to an electron?

Yes, exactly, for an electron (or several sharing the same bound six) and quarks uniform across the normal six.

  • Everything is uniform in e7..e10, so only gamma_1..gamma_6 carry derivatives. The occupancies of the pairs (7,8) and (9,10) are never changed by the kinetic operator or by beta.
  • The electron value and the three color values sit in four different such blocks. Every cross density between them vanishes identically. The total density is the plain sum of the separate densities, so the density term cannot tell the colors apart at any order.
  • The color whose odd pair is (5,6) maps to another color by T = a4^dagger gamma_6. T leaves gamma_1..gamma_5 and beta alone and reverses gamma_6: a mirror of x6 only. The electron's density is even in x6 and the quark is uniform there, so the two colors solve the same problem with the same frequency and density. The other two colors exchange by a rotation inside the electron's free four.

Addendum 2: a uniform polarised background

9 November 2026. User question: could the field have a general polarity that aligns particles? Test asked for: put the electron-type solution on a uniform polarised background and see whether its bound plane is pulled onto the pair. Script: background_alignment.py (7 of 7 checks pass).

Setup

Law of case eq. 12.24. Background: constant value s0 with beta s0 = s0, an exact solution with frequency m0 - g A^(2/9). Particle: depth-6 radial field with seed s, bound in a six-plane Q. kappa = |s0^dagger s| is the overlap of the two values.

Result 1 (exact): the background does not act on the bound plane

Fields of the form [F1 + i G1 gamma(xhat)] s + [F0 + i G0 gamma(xhat)] s0, with complex radial F and G, are mapped into the same form by the full right-hand side of the law. The radial coefficients are the same for every orientation of Q. Only kappa enters.

Reason: s0^dagger gamma(xhat) s = 0 because the two sides have opposite beta, and (gamma(xhat) s0)^dagger gamma(xhat) s = s0^dagger s. The density therefore never sees how Q sits relative to either value.

Checked with a finite-difference D_10 in all ten directions at random points: closure residual 1.3e-10; coefficients for a straight, a tilted (0.6 rad) and a random six-plane differ by 1.4e-10 (the finite-difference error); changing kappa changes them by 4.5e-2 (control).

So a uniform background, however strong, exerts no pull on the bound plane within this family. The open condition of addendum 1, item A (the extra two bound directions must be a pair) is not supplied by a uniform polarity.

Result 2 (estimate): the background does act on the value

Depth-6 profile by shooting at m0 = g = 1, omega = 0.99. It reproduces the case's E/(m0 N) = 0.991429513 and the balance value w = 2.140974e-3.

Reading

  • A uniform polarity can make particle values line up with it, and so share one pairing of directions. That is the reference pairing the charge construction currently assumes.
  • It cannot orient bound planes. Anything that does must be non-uniform or act through something other than the density.
  • Quark values are orthogonal to a degree-0 background value by construction, which is the top of this energy. Conserved charge and color would have to be what holds them there.
  • Not addressed: the odd-depth charge defect (addendum 1, item C).

1. Geometry and subgroup bookkeeping

Within the ten-direction matter domain set B4=span(e1,...,e4), N6=span(e5,...,e10), Q6=span(e1,...,e6), and L=span(e5,e6). Then

Q6 = B4 direct-sum L,
N6 = L direct-sum span(e7,...,e10),
Q6 intersection N6 = L.

This is consistent linear geometry. With the given observer plane inside B4, identifying Q6 with N6 would discard localization along the observed directions. The distinction is therefore required by this particular arrangement. It does not independently derive either physical plane, their orientation, or all possible representations of observer spin.

Let J be the reference complex structure on N6 with pairs56,78,9,10. Its orthogonal centralizer is U(3). The color SU(3) subgroup preserving the complex line L setwise is S(U(1)×U(2)), of dimension4, locally u(2). Fixing the real e5 direction, as required by the connected stabilizer of Q5=B4+Re5, also fixes Je5=e6; the surviving group is SU(2), dimension3, acting on e7,...,e10. This distinguishes the setwise Q6 stabilizer from the pointwise kinetic commutant. The latter is dimension3 at both depths5 and6, consistent with the integration note's9.29. Neither table derives a weak group.

The pure-seed/full-split SU(5) stabilizer table and this fixed-reference SU(3) subgroup calculation answer different questions. Matching their free SU(2) factors does not make them the same complete construction.

2. Charge integral: independent derivation

Use exactly the stated conventions

L_ab = -i(x_a partial_b-x_b partial_a),
S_ab = -(i/2)gamma_a gamma_b,
Gamma4 = gamma1 gamma2 gamma3 gamma4,
K4 = Gamma4[3/2+2 sum_(a<b<=4) S_ab L_ab],
N6 = sum_(ab=56,78,9,10)(L_ab+S_ab),
Qang = (K4+N6)/3.

Let s be a unit simultaneous eigenvector of beta, Gamma4 and the three normal pair spins, with values eta, g4 and sigma_k=±1/2. Let F,G be real radial functions on a d-plane, and

Psi = F(r)s+iG(r)gamma(n)s,       n=x_bound/r,
N = integral_bound(F²+G²)>0,
w = integral_bound G²/N.

Beta anticommutes with each gamma, so the two components are pointwise orthogonal. The field is uniform in the complementary directions; averages below use centered symmetric free-volume regularization, not a finite ten-dimensional norm. Require convergent bound integrals, sufficient smoothness and vanishing boundary terms for conserved/operator claims. These trial integrals alone do not establish a self-adjoint generator domain or absence of free-boundary flux.

For a coordinate-aligned bound subset with nb base and dn=d-nb normal axes, sphere averaging gives <n_i n_j>=delta_ij/d on retained axes. Cross terms odd in n vanish. On the upper component K4 has value(3/2)g4. On the lower component its spin part contributes g4[(3/2)(dn-nb)/d], and its base orbital part contributes g4 nb(nb-1)/d. Thus

kappa = (3/2)dn/d+(nb-5/2)nb/d
      = 3/2-nb(4-nb)/d.

A normal pair fully bound or fully free contributes the same sigma_k to the normalized total rotation on both components. If exactly one axis is bound, its orbital free-coordinate term averages to zero and its spin value reverses on that one lower direction; the lower mean is sigma_k(1-2/d). Therefore, writing h_k=1 for a straddled pair,

q_table = [(3/2)g4+sum sigma_k]/3,
q = [(1-w)(3/2)g4+w g4 kappa
     +sum sigma_k(1-2w h_k/d)]/3,

or, more transparently,

q-q_table = -w [g4 nb(4-nb)+2 sum sigma_k h_k]/(3d).        (A)

This independently verifies Claude's coordinate-subset formula, including its signs and normalization. The formula is kinematic and valid for any such radial trial profiles; it does not require Gaussian F,G or a numerical radial branch.
