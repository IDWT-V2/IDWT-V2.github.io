61. Projector-curvature candidate: nonzero geometry beyond the pure-gauge frame map

Section 60 showed that differentiating the scattering-selected frame itself,

A=dQscatQscat−1, A=dQ_{\rm scat}Q_{\rm scat}^{-1}, A=dQscat​Qscat−1​,

produces a Maurer--Cartan connection with zero curvature. A different V2-native object is available: the sector projector.

Let

P2=P=PT P^2=P=P^T P2=P=PT

be a smooth rank-three projector selecting the observed three-plane inside a six-dimensional space. Differentiating P2=PP^2=PP2=P gives

P(dP)P=0. \boxed{P(dP)P=0.} P(dP)P=0.​

Thus dPdPdP is intrinsically off-diagonal between the selected and complementary subspaces. Nevertheless, two different projector derivatives need not commute. The natural curvature of the induced projector connection is

FP=P(dP∧dP)P. \boxed{F_P=P(dP\wedge dP)P.} FP​=P(dP∧dP)P.​

Unlike dQ Q−1dQ\,Q^{-1}dQQ−1, this can be nonzero.

61.1 Exact base-point test

Take

P0=(I3000) P_0=\begin{pmatrix}I_3&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix} P0​=(I3​0​00​)

and two pure observed--hidden generators X,YX,YX,Y. At the base point,

Px=[X,P0],Py=[Y,P0]. P_x=[X,P_0],\qquad P_y=[Y,P_0]. Px​=[X,P0​],Py​=[Y,P0​].

Choosing XXX to mix observed direction 111 with hidden direction 111 and YYY to mix observed direction 222 with the same hidden direction gives

P0PxP0=P0PyP0=0, P_0P_xP_0=P_0P_yP_0=0, P0​Px​P0​=P0​Py​P0​=0,

while

Fxy=P0[Px,Py]P0 F_{xy}=P_0[P_x,P_y]P_0 Fxy​=P0​[Px​,Py​]P0​

has observed block, up to orientation convention,

Fxy(O)=±(0−10100000). F_{xy}^{(O)}=\pm \begin{pmatrix} 0&-1&0\\ 1&0&0\\ 0&0&0 \end{pmatrix}. Fxy(O)​=±​010​−100​000​​.

The sign depends only on the ordering convention for the two tangent directions. The invariant result is

−12tr⁡[(Fxy(O))2]=1. \boxed{-\frac12\operatorname{tr}[(F_{xy}^{(O)})^2]=1.} −21​tr[(Fxy(O)​)2]=1.​

Thus projector geometry supplies genuine nonzero curvature with fixed dimensionless algebraic normalization for unit tangent motions.

61.2 Full unit-tangent basis

The tangent space of the rank-three Grassmannian at P0P_0P0​ has nine elementary mixed directions labelled by

(a,i),a=1,2,3,i=1,2,3, (a,i),\qquad a=1,2,3,\quad i=1,2,3, (a,i),a=1,2,3,i=1,2,3,

where aaa labels the observed direction and iii the hidden direction. With the normalization

12tr⁡(dPai2)=1, \frac12\operatorname{tr}(dP_{ai}^2)=1, 21​tr(dPai2​)=1,

the observed curvature between two basis tangents is nonzero exactly when they share the same hidden index and have different observed indices:

i=j, a≠b. \boxed{i=j,\ a\ne b.} i=j, a=b.​

Every such nonzero curvature has the same invariant norm

−12tr⁡(Fai,bi2)=1. \boxed{-\frac12\operatorname{tr}(F_{ai,bi}^2)=1.} −21​tr(Fai,bi2​)=1.​

So the unit projector geometry contains a universal curvature quantum at the algebraic level.

61.3 Hidden-channel multiplicity

For a fixed observed rotation plane (ab)(ab)(ab) there are three hidden channels i=1,2,3i=1,2,3i=1,2,3. If their curvature matrices enter with equal phase and are added before taking the norm, their amplitudes add to three and

∥Fabcoh∥2=9. \boxed{\|F_{ab}^{\rm coh}\|^2=9.} ∥Fabcoh​∥2=9.​

If instead the three hidden channels are orthogonal/incoherent in the physical quadratic form, their norms add to

∑i∥Fab,i∥2=3. \boxed{\sum_i\|F_{ab,i}\|^2=3.} i∑​∥Fab,i​∥2=3.​

This is an important new fork. The projector algebra fixes the individual unit curvature to one and fixes the multiplicity to three, but does not by itself decide whether the physical interaction contracts those channels coherently or orthogonally.

61.4 Why this evades the Maurer--Cartan no-go

The full moving frame may have a flat Maurer--Cartan connection while the induced connection on a moving subbundle has nonzero curvature. The off-diagonal pieces of dPdPdP encode how the selected subspace bends inside the ambient space, and their commutator returns a tangential rotation:

off-diagonal dP×off-diagonal dP⟶tangential curvature. \boxed{\text{off-diagonal }dP\times\text{off-diagonal }dP\longrightarrow\text{tangential curvature}.} off-diagonal dP×off-diagonal dP⟶tangential curvature.​

Therefore the pure-gauge result of section 60 does not rule out curvature generated by sector projector geometry.

61.5 Remaining normalization issue

If projector derivatives are rescaled,

Px↦aPx,Py↦bPy, P_x\mapsto aP_x,\qquad P_y\mapsto bP_y, Px​↦aPx​,Py​↦bPy​,

then

Fxy↦abFxy, F_{xy}\mapsto abF_{xy}, Fxy​↦abFxy​,

and

∥Fxy∥2↦a2b2∥Fxy∥2. \boxed{\|F_{xy}\|^2\mapsto a^2b^2\|F_{xy}\|^2.} ∥Fxy​∥2↦a2b2∥Fxy​∥2.​

Thus the Grassmann algebra fixes the curvature generated by unit normalized projector motions, but not the physical magnitude of those motions.

61.6 Strongest current closure candidate

The most V2-native chain is now

scattering data→Qscat→Pscat→dPscat→P(dP∧dP)P→lock curvature. \boxed{ \text{scattering data} \to Q_{\rm scat} \to P_{\rm scat} \to dP_{\rm scat} \to P(dP\wedge dP)P \to \text{lock curvature}. } scattering data→Qscat​→Pscat​→dPscat​→P(dP∧dP)P→lock curvature.​

This candidate survives the present tests. The unresolved master equation must fix both

∥∇P∥ \boxed{\|\nabla P\|} ∥∇P∥​

and the physical contraction law among the three hidden curvature channels. If V2 derives those two pieces, the remaining algebraic normalization is no longer arbitrary.
