Spatial polarization textures

Updated September 10, 2026

Summary: Allowing polarization to vary across a bound particle can preserve its scalar density while creating a full spatial kinetic residual, whose omitted components must be solved before the texture is a physical mode.

Allow the polarization to vary over the actual bound coordinates

A constant vector of complex amplitudes describes one part of a particle's polarization; Complete spatial polarization data identifies its full tensor description. Its phase and orientation can also vary across its spatial profile. That possibility must be tested in the field equation rather than accepted because it preserves a selected density.

Use the complete stationary radial frame of Equal-current spatial polarizations:

HbW=MW,W†W=ρ(x)I,W†βW=σ(x)I.(1) H_bW=MW,\qquad W^\dagger W=\rho(x)I,\qquad W^\dagger\beta W=\sigma(x)I. \tag{1} Hb​W=MW,W†W=ρ(x)I,W†βW=σ(x)I.(1)

The bound coordinates xxx are ordinary macroscopic directions in the particle's submanifold. There is only the one infinite nested physical manifold. The frame's column labels describe field components over it.

Consider

Ψ(x,t)=e−iMtW(x)z(x,t),z†z=1.(2) \boxed{\Psi(x,t)=e^{-iMt}W(x)z(x,t),\qquad z^\dagger z=1.} \tag{2} Ψ(x,t)=e−iMtW(x)z(x,t),z†z=1.​(2)

At every point, the number and scalar densities remain ρ\rhoρ and σ\sigmaσ. For a scalar self-binding source depending only on the latter density's centered moments, its coefficient is unchanged. This statement holds regardless of how rapidly zzz varies. It establishes the source value, not the full spatial equation.

The spatial derivative supplies an additional response

The original scalar Dirac equation has a global phase symmetry. A local phase with its connection held fixed changes the field dynamics. If a gauge connection is part of a different specified action, transform that connection too before declaring two configurations gauge-equivalent.

What a complete polarization calculation must add

The constant polarization waves supply exact solutions with their own normal partner and unchanged scalar source. The scalar envelope derives a separate remainder when the bound profile changes with its amplitude. Equations (3)–(14) add the bound-coordinate texture response.

A proposed richer particle pattern must therefore supply its spatial amplitudes and phases, satisfy the complete kinetic equation, and retain the profile components and source changes generated by that equation. The neutral-doublet transport provides another example where a projected derivative omits a necessary Dirac complement. Neither a density plot nor the number of polarization parameters is sufficient to establish a physical mode.
