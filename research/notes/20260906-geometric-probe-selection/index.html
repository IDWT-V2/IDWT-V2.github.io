Geometric probe selection

Updated September 8, 2026

Summary: Exact CP3 section integrals give a conditional low-order probe coupling and show that four orbit modes can miss a nonzero weak-field response.

Candidate and assumptions

Continue the selected spinor-line lift and its explicitly added Killing drive. Test observer coordinate symbols built from the smooth projective functions f_ij = conjugate(Z_i) Z_j / (Z†Z). A real coordinate is a Hermitian combination of these functions times a length ℓ. This is a geometric candidate for an electromagnetic readout, not the nonlinear petal detector map and not a derivation of physical spatial coordinates. The demo and its actual coefficient trajectories are unchanged.

With density q|Φ|², its dipole operator is q times the section-space compression of multiplication by the chosen coordinate. There are now explicit matrix elements, rather than arbitrary four-by-four probe matrices. All results below are conditional on this source assignment and on restricting to the holomorphic section sector. Other intrinsic spinor modes remain outside this calculation and may contribute to a fully physical probe response.

Exact matrix elements

Normalize the FS measure to unit volume and use |Z| = 1 representatives. For an occupation multi-index n with Σ_i n_i = L, normalized monomials are

sn(Z)=(L+3)!6∏ini!∏iZini,∫∏i∣Zi∣2nidμ=6∏ini!(L+3)!. s_n(Z)=\sqrt{\frac{(L+3)!}{6\prod_i n_i!}}\prod_i Z_i^{n_i}, \qquad \int\prod_i|Z_i|^{2n_i}d\mu=\frac{6\prod_i n_i!}{(L+3)!}. sn​(Z)=6∏i​ni​!(L+3)!​​i∏​Zini​​,∫i∏​∣Zi​∣2ni​dμ=(L+3)!6∏i​ni​!​.

Integrating an additional f_ij factor gives

Tij=ΠLfijΠL=aj†ai+δijIL+4 T_{ij}=\Pi_L f_{ij}\Pi_L =\frac{a_j^\dagger a_i+\delta_{ij}I}{L+4} Tij​=ΠL​fij​ΠL​=L+4aj†​ai​+δij​I​

on the degree-L symmetric representation. The a symbols implement monomial occupation changes; no physical flat oscillator is introduced. In particular, Σ_i T_ii = I, ensuring that normalized total charge does not grow with L. For i ≠ j, the only nonzero matrix element moves one unit of occupation from i to j and equals √(n_i(n_j+1))/(L+4).

These identities follow by the displayed monomial integral; the diagnostic compares every matrix entry against that integral for L = 1, 2, 3.

What this candidate sees on the actual orbit

In the monomial eigenbasis, the four occupied states are

u=(L,0,0,0),v=(0,L,0,0),w=(0,0,1,L−1),r=(0,0,0,L), u=(L,0,0,0),\quad v=(0,L,0,0),\quad w=(0,0,1,L-1),\quad r=(0,0,0,L), u=(L,0,0,0),v=(0,L,0,0),w=(0,0,1,L−1),r=(0,0,0,L),

with initial coefficients (A,A,B,B)/√2, A²+B² = 1. Their frequencies are ν(L,−L,−1,0), so this is the same orbit as the amplitude/clock construction. For L ≥ 2, a one-occupation transfer cannot connect either u or v to any other selected state. Only the w↔r clock pair remains off-diagonal:

⟨r∣T34∣w⟩=LL+4. \langle r|T_{34}|w\rangle=\frac{\sqrt L}{L+4}. ⟨r∣T34​∣w⟩=L+4L​​.

Thus low-order geometric dipoles need not reveal all the frequencies permitted by an arbitrary four-state matrix:

two petals, L = 1 4 ν, 2ν
four petals, L = 2 10 ν
six petals, L = 3 20 ν

For the complex dipole choice d_x+i d_y = qℓ⟨T_34⟩, the signal is qℓ B²√L e^{iνt}/[2(L+4)]. It can trace a circle even when the detector shows petals. This is not a statement that the actual multidimensional orbit has become a circle; the two probes retain different information. Nor does a hidden petal frequency prove no radiation: the clock signal is still time dependent under this particular charge readout.

A symbol polynomial of bidegree (k,k) can transfer at most k occupations. Connecting either extremal state to another selected state requires k ≥ L. That is a projective-coordinate degree threshold, not automatically an ordinary electromagnetic multipole order or a number of photons.

A response that four modes miss entirely

Choose the real coordinate X = ℓ Re(conjugate(Z_1)Z_3)/(Z†Z), giving

D=C(a1†a3+a3†a1),C=qℓ2(L+4). D=C(a_1^\dagger a_3+a_3^\dagger a_1),\qquad C=\frac{q\ell}{2(L+4)}. D=C(a1†​a3​+a3†​a1​),C=2(L+4)qℓ​.

Let P retain just {u,v,w,r}. For L ≥ 2, PDP = 0, but (1−P)DP ≠ 0. A four-mode calculation would predict no dipole or linear response for this probe. The full section-space generator is h = ℏν(N₁−N₂−N₃). Using [a₁†a₃,a₃†a₁] = N₁−N₃ gives the exact retarded response to −E(t)D:

χ(t,s)=iℏ⟨[D(t),D(s)]⟩=−C2ℏ(A2L−B2)sin⁡(2ν(t−s)),t>s. \chi(t,s)=\frac{i}{\hbar}\langle[D(t),D(s)]\rangle =-\frac{C^2}{\hbar}(A^2L-B^2)\sin(2\nu(t-s)),\qquad t>s. χ(t,s)=ℏi​⟨[D(t),D(s)]⟩=−ℏC2​(A2L−B2)sin(2ν(t−s)),t>s.

Here ⟨N₁−N₃⟩ = (A²L−B²)/2. For the demo's A = 0.6 and B = 0.8 this response is nonzero at L = 2 and 3 despite PDP = 0. The intermediate states are real members of the 10- or 20-dimensional section space, not additional fitted energy levels. An exact small impulse independently reproduces this kernel in the diagnostic. Its sign depends on the prepared populations; it is not a fitted absorption rate or a passivity claim.

This particular commutator depends only on conserved occupations and t−s, even though the prepared orbit is not stationary. That simplification is special to this probe and generator, not a license to replace every orbit response by an equilibrium susceptibility.

Consequence for the previous frequency test

The earlier finite-generator frequency bounds remain correct for an unforced dipole within that specified four-state model. They do not alone bound a physical absorption spectrum when the probe can excite omitted modes. Exact preservation of the unforced orbit is insufficient; probe closure or a controlled elimination of omitted modes is also needed.

The conditional line lift, density, current, and these matrix elements form a calculable candidate chain. V2 still has to select the twist, Killing term, observer symbols and scale, photon normalization, and nuclear state. The separate ring sector is not derived by setting L = 0 here. No atomic spectrum or independent evidence for extra spatial dimensions is claimed.
