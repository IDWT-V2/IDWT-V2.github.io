CP5 tangent splitting obstruction

Updated September 10, 2026

proved topological obstruction

Physical premise (IDWT): There is one infinite-dimensional physical manifold. All sectors form one nested chain of submanifolds within it. Particle depth counts ordinary, literal macroscopic spatial dimensions. “Observed” and “unobserved” describe an observer's access to those dimensions, not different kinds of physical dimension. See spatial meaning and mathematical scope.

Summary: The complex tangent bundle of CP5 admits no global rank-three plus rank-two splitting, even without imposing determinant one.

Why this matters

Gauge selection §15 already excludes determinant-one tangent holonomy because c1(T CP5)=6H. The obstruction is stronger: a global complex 3+2 tangent splitting itself is impossible. Keeping the determinant connection does not cure that failure.

1. Exhaust the possible Chern factors

Let E=T^(1,0) CP5. The Euler sequence gives

c(E)=1+6H+15H2+20H3+15H4+6H5,H∗(CP5;Z)=Z[H]/(H6). c(E)=1+6H+15H^2+20H^3+15H^4+6H^5, \qquad H^*(\mathbb{CP}^5;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z[H]/(H^6). c(E)=1+6H+15H2+20H3+15H4+6H5,H∗(CP5;Z)=Z[H]/(H6).

Suppose E=A_3 direct-sum B_2. The Whitney product formula says c(E)=c(A)c(B). Since both factors have degree at most their ranks, their product has degree at most five. Equality modulo H^6 is therefore an exact polynomial equality. The top coefficient is nonzero, so the degrees must be exactly three and two.

Over the rationals the polynomial factors as

c(E)=(1+2H)(1+H+H2)(1+3H+3H2). c(E)=(1+2H)(1+H+H^2)(1+3H+3H^2). c(E)=(1+2H)(1+H+H2)(1+3H+3H2).

The two quadratics are irreducible (discriminant -3), and the linear factor occurs only once. Normalizing constant terms to one, the only possible rank-two Chern factors are

c(B)=1+H+H2orc(B)=1+3H+3H2. c(B)=1+H+H^2\quad\hbox{or}\quad c(B)=1+3H+3H^2. c(B)=1+H+H2orc(B)=1+3H+3H2.

2. Both factors fail a necessary bundle condition

Restrict B to a linear CP3 inside CP5. A complex rank-two bundle on CP3 with c1=aH and c2=bH^2 must satisfy ab=0 modulo 2. This is the classical Atiyah–Rees/Schwarzenberger condition; see Opie, Theorem 1.2 and Remark 1.3. The two candidates have (a,b)=(1,1) and (3,3), hence ab odd in both cases. Neither is the Chern class of such a bundle. The supposed splitting cannot exist.

An independent check uses K(CP3)=Z[t]/(t^4), t=[O(1)]-1, and ch(t)=exp(H)-1. The rank-two candidate characters are

ch⁡(B)=2+aH+a2−2b2H2+a3−3ab6H3. \operatorname{ch}(B)=2+aH+\frac{a^2-2b}{2}H^2 +\frac{a^3-3ab}{6}H^3. ch(B)=2+aH+2a2−2b​H2+6a3−3ab​H3.

Their coefficients in the integral K-theory basis (1,t,t^2,t^3) would be (2,1,-1,1/2) and (2,3,0,-1/2), respectively. Half-integral final coefficients are impossible. The K-theory basis is established in Hatcher, Vector Bundles and K-Theory, §2.2.

3. Precise consequence for V2

No unitary connection on T^(1,0) CP5 can have its full holonomy contained in a conjugate U(3) times U(2): such a reduction would produce the excluded rank-three and rank-two subbundles. This conclusion does not assume the connection is Levi–Civita, holomorphic, or determinant one.

Tensoring the tangent bundle by a line bundle cannot help with the splitting: a split of E tensor L would untwist to a split of E. Separately its first Chern class would be (6+5k)H for L=O(k), never zero for integer k.

The pointwise exterior-algebra branching, charge normalization, and local stabilizer calculations remain valid. What fails is promoting that local split to the literal global tangent bundle. An auxiliary gauge bundle, a different effective carrier, or a properly specified non-global construction is required. This theorem does not forbid the Standard Model group on CP5 or every possible V2 gauge completion.

The normal-spinor calculation provides a compatible distinction: the tangent/normal splitting along an embedded CP3 exists and has an explicit induced connection. It is not a rank-three plus rank-two splitting over the entire CP5 base.
