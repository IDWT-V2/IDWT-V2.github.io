One spatial kernel on a projective chain

Updated September 11, 2026

Summary: A single bounded spatial moment kernel gives reciprocal full-spinor Dirac equations on specified nested projective sectors, with conserved norms, global evolution and no independent interaction coefficient for each pair.

A declared common spatial action

Consider any finite nested family of linear projective spatial submanifolds inside one specified containing projective space. Their metric, embeddings and spin or Spinc^cc connections are fixed. These are ordinary physical spatial directions within the same manifold; no dimension is compactified. The projective geometry and its physical scale remain conditional inputs.

This finite family is a partial chain of even-dimensional submanifolds. The intermediate-depth calculation shows why it cannot automatically be filled in at every ordinary spatial depth: for example, the standard CP3-to-CP5 inclusion admits no seven- or nine-dimensional intermediate submanifold. The pair action below remains conditional on its specified partial family; it does not complete the geometry of the full hierarchy.

For each family ddd, let ψd\psi_dψd​ be its complete spatial spinor and DdD_dDd​ its self-adjoint Dirac operator, with chirality involution βd\beta_dβd​. Use canonical positive kinetic norms. This specifies an orthogonal decomposition of retained mode families of the one field and the additive scalar source below. If another decomposition has cross kinetic or cross-current terms, those terms must be retained rather than inferred away from spatial nesting; see canonical normalization.

Let idi_did​ embed the spatial sector in the common projective space and put PX=XX†/(X†X)P_X=XX^\dagger/(X^\dagger X)PX​=XX†/(X†X). Specify the same kernel everywhere:

K(X,Y)=Tr⁡(PXPY)=∣X†Y∣2∣X∣2∣Y∣2.(1) \boxed{K(X,Y)=\operatorname{Tr}(P_XP_Y) =\frac{|X^\dagger Y|^2}{|X|^2|Y|^2}.} \tag{1} K(X,Y)=Tr(PX​PY​)=∣X∣2∣Y∣2∣X†Y∣2​.​(1)

There is no dimension-dependent multiplier in (1). Its unit-FS integral on CPmmm is 1/(m+1)1/(m+1)1/(m+1), which follows from the geometry. Normalizing it separately to one at every depth would change the common kernel.

This is a specified reduction to test, not a derivation of (1)–(3) from the master action. Scalar bilinears make the interaction globally defined without a spinor-to-spinor bundle identification between different sectors. It describes scalar-current interaction, not a particle-changing vertex. The normal spinor factors remain relevant when such a vertex or a nonscalar current is proposed.

The interaction in (3) is spatially nonlocal and instantaneous: every potential uses the other sources at the same time. Its mathematical well-posedness below does not establish relativistic causal propagation. A physical use as an effective interaction requires a controlled reduction of local master-field dynamics, with the omitted propagation times and response frequencies specified.

The local-current causality test now gives smooth, norm-preserving separated initial data for which this instantaneous equation changes the target's current derivative immediately. The spatial harmonic comparison identifies the kernel as a specific low-harmonic truncation, and shows why the direct term and signed induced response of local Dirac dynamics do not yet derive it.

Both complete spatial responses

Varying (3), including the dependence of every moment on its field, gives

For two families, the cross energy is κTr⁡(MaMb)\kappa\operatorname{Tr}(M_aM_b)κTr(Ma​Mb​). The response of either family uses the other one's same moment matrix. In particular, the lower response and the higher response contain the weighted marginal and its adjoint; the higher-dimensional potential is a smooth function on its full space.

Every spinor component and relative phase within each field remains in (4). This particular additive scalar-current action has an independent overall phase symmetry for each family. Consequently

The entire construction is covariant under a common projective isometry of the fields and all embeddings: P↦UPU†P\mapsto UPU^\daggerP↦UPU† and M↦UMU†M\mapsto UMU^\daggerM↦UMU†. Rotating one source while holding its physical embedding fixed is a different configuration, not this covariance test.

The norm identity can first be proved for smooth approximations and then passed to their L2L^2L2 limit.

Smooth initial fields remain smooth. This argument concerns the complete spinor equations, independently of a finite-mode approximation or a profile symmetry.

Global existence is not stability of a particular particle. The spatial Dirac energy has both signs; (5) does not supply an orbital stability theorem or a preferred occupied mode.

Spatial phase information changes subsequent interaction

For a fixed scalar test function fff, the scalar moment is mf=⟨ψ,fβψ⟩m_f=\langle\psi,f\beta\psi\ranglemf​=⟨ψ,fβψ⟩. On a regular solution,

m˙f=i⟨ψ,[D,fβ]ψ⟩.(7) \boxed{\dot m_f=i\langle\psi,[D,f\beta]\psi\rangle.} \tag{7} m˙f​=i⟨ψ,[D,fβ]ψ⟩.​(7)

The nonlinear term cancels from this first derivative because two scalar multiplication operators times β\betaβ commute. It changes the field and therefore changes later values of (7). A holomorphic or other finite projection can alter this commutator, so its moment flow must not be substituted for the uncompressed spatial equation.

The spatially varying relative phase between even and odd form components is consequential. Multiplying all odd components by eiθe^{i\theta}eiθ leaves the instantaneous scalar density unchanged, but generally changes (7). Opposite phase preparations can therefore have identical initial scalar interaction overlap and different subsequent responses. The full spatial pair calculation supplies an explicit example and checks its coordinate residuals.
