Self-source from complementary Dirac modes

Updated September 12, 2026

Summary: An explicit canonical normal-mode reduction produces a spectral square-root source and its full response; the projected count, total norm and complete scalar square determine what remains missing from a physical mass law.

Derive a source before interpreting it as mass

The current hierarchy reconstructs an action with a simplex self-source. A complementary calculation starts from an explicit canonical Dirac pair and derives the interaction obtained by following its occupied modes. The reduction explains a square-root law, but also fixes its matrix meaning, its occupation dependence and its frequency response. Those details cannot be chosen independently afterward.

All retained and complementary variables below are conditional modes of the same field. Their normal kinetic matrix must ultimately follow from the physical spatial Dirac operator and its geometry. No extra interaction particle is introduced. The matrix rank counts coefficient modes; it adds no physical spatial dimensions and imposes no compactification.

Let a Hermitian retained current be RRR on an rrr-dimensional coefficient space. Consider the complementary Hamiltonian

H(R)=ϵτx⊗I+gτz⊗R,ϵ>0,Ei=ϵ2+g2ri2.(1) H(R)=\epsilon\tau_x\otimes I+g\tau_z\otimes R, \qquad \epsilon>0, \qquad E_i=\sqrt{\epsilon^2+g^2r_i^2}. \tag{1} H(R)=ϵτx​⊗I+gτz​⊗R,ϵ>0,Ei​=ϵ2+g2ri2​​.(1)

Here rir_iri​ are the eigenvalues of RRR, τx,τz\tau_x,\tau_zτx​,τz​ are Pauli matrices, and ϵ\epsilonϵ is a specified normal kinetic frequency. It is not an inserted physical particle mass. The two signs of the Dirac spectrum are retained without assigning them a particle–antiparticle interpretation.

Canonical occupation and the spectral root

Represent the occupied complementary field by a matrix YYY whose columns are explicitly orthogonal spectator modes of the same field. With one common canonical norm qqq per positive eigenmode, its density is

Y†Y=qIr,YY†=qP+(R),Qf=Tr⁡Y†Y=qr.(2) Y^\dagger Y=qI_r,\qquad YY^\dagger=qP_+(R),\qquad Q_f=\operatorname{Tr}Y^\dagger Y=qr. \tag{2} Y†Y=qIr​,YY†=qP+​(R),Qf​=TrY†Y=qr.(2)

A single pure coefficient vector does not automatically give the complete projector in (2). The physical spectator modes and their occupation need an origin in the master field. A quantum filled sea is not assumed.

The positive spectral space maximizes Tr⁡(Y†HY)\operatorname{Tr}(Y^\dagger H Y)Tr(Y†HY) at this fixed Gram matrix: diagonalizing HHH bounds the occupied trace by the sum of its rrr largest eigenvalues. This is an energy maximum, rather than a passive ground state. Its dynamical viability must be checked in the coupled system and in its actual spatial domain.

For a scalar current this is exactly the smooth saturating law, with μ=q∣g∣\mu=q|g|μ=q∣g∣ and σ=ϵ/∣g∣\sigma=\epsilon/|g|σ=ϵ/∣g∣. For a matrix current it is a trace of spectral roots. It is not μTr⁡R2+σ2\mu\sqrt{\operatorname{Tr}R^2+\sigma^2}μTrR2+σ2​. Those two functions agree on an isolated rank-one current but give different finite interactions.

For example, let R=APz+BPwR=AP_z+BP_wR=APz​+BPw​, A,B>0A,B>0A,B>0, and τ=∣z†w∣2\tau=|z^\dagger w|^2τ=∣z†w∣2. Its two nonzero eigenvalues are

At positive ϵ\epsilonϵ, (3) gives a nonnegative cross energy which increases with τ\tauτ and vanishes for orthogonal rays. At zero gap, Tr⁡∣R∣=Tr⁡R=A+B\operatorname{Tr}|R|=\operatorname{Tr}R=A+BTr∣R∣=TrR=A+B: the positive-current cross energy vanishes at every orientation. The Hilbert–Schmidt root instead gives A2+B2+2ABτ−A−B\sqrt{A^2+B^2+2AB\tau}-A-BA2+B2+2ABτ​−A−B, which is generally negative.

The zero-gap and zero-probe limits also fail to commute at a current with a null eigenspace. At every positive gap an infinitesimal probe orthogonal to a rank-one target has zero first derivative, whereas a finite positive probe has a nonzero slope after taking the root limit. An isolated source coefficient therefore does not determine the pair law.

The joint mass and coupling question

Equation (3) is a derived effective source for the specified canonical normal-mode preparation. Equations (10)–(12) identify the missing band matching, occupation and physical mass interpretation. The spatial normal spectrum itself must also come from the same physical manifold. The localization conditions remain relevant when searching for actual finite-norm spatial particles.

A further balanced-population calculation retains paired positive and negative normal occupations. Their stationary scalar contributions cancel while their moving polarization gives a nonzero geometric response. That result supplies a way for a shared response to coexist with count-dependent stationary sources under the same scalar interaction. It does not select those populations or derive the physical mass operator.
