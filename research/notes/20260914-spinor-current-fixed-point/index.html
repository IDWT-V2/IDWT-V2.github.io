Nonlinear spinor frequencies under one common surface action

Updated September 14, 2026

conditional calculation

The curvature parent now has nonlinear stationary spinor branches near the assigned depth-six modes. The radius, intermediate gap, interaction coefficient and canonical norm are common to both branches. Their frequencies are read directly from the stationary equation. The interaction deforms the spatial pattern and changes the frequency ratio; it does not preserve the simplex ratio automatically.

This is a conditional inverse action on a specified round field feature. The existence argument below concerns its complete surface equation. The numerical calculation resolves its low parent modes and explicitly measures forcing outside that space. A physical construction of the feature, its two ordinary normal profiles, gap and complete inertia is still required.

A local existence statement for the complete surface equation

This statement uses the entire first-order chain on the compact surface, not a finite harmonic truncation. Compactness here describes the specified round field feature. It is not compactification of ordinary spatial dimensions.

Within the fixed rotation family, the base monogenic mode has a one-dimensional positive eigenspace. At all larger compatible degrees the smallest positive chain frequency is strictly larger. To see the latter, the absolute entries of the triangular inverse are products of positive factors G/(beta+shift). They decrease strictly as beta increases above the largest negative shift. Its largest singular value therefore decreases, by positivity of the corresponding Gram matrix. The smallest chain frequency, its reciprocal, increases. Every other positive singular frequency at a larger degree is at least as large. At the base degree, the other chain frequencies exceed its simple smallest one. Negative frequencies cannot equal this positive value.

The full parent is self-adjoint and elliptic: its principal symbol squares to c^2|p|^2 on every chain component. The base frequency is therefore an isolated simple eigenvalue in the stated rotation family. Fix its overall phase and Q=1. The derivative of (2), including the norm equation and frequency unknown, is invertible on the orthogonal complement of this mode. For Sobolev index s>2 on the four-sphere, the pointwise cubic is smooth from H^s to H^s and hence to H^(s-1). The complementary elliptic inverse maps H^(s-1) back to H^s. The implicit function theorem then gives a unique nearby normalized branch, up to overall phase, for sufficiently small real g.

This establishes conditional local existence. It supplies neither an explicit allowed interval for g nor stability against other rotation families. It also does not turn the externally specified surface or gap into a self-supported six-dimensional particle.

Frequency, energy and the omitted response

Because the interaction is quartic, every complete stationary solution obeys

E=ωQ−gU.(8) \mathcal E=\omega Q-gU. \tag{8} E=ωQ−gU.(8)

Equation (8) is checked in the numerical solutions. It is not the complete particle inertia: the sustaining geometry and its energy and stress are not present in this surface action. Their contribution cannot be discarded when testing the proposed inertial mechanism.

The calculation also reconstructs the complete spatial source. Its component outside the retained scalar-spinor harmonics is measured as the norm of the actual difference of fields. The high chain source is measured by subtracting the full canonical low-mode projection from the injected endpoint source. These direct differences avoid subtracting nearly equal squared norms.

Within a retained degree band, the first high-chain correction is bounded by |g| times this source norm divided by its spectral distance from omega. The simple bound G-max_j|L_j|-|omega| is positive for the tested bands. This is a first-response estimate; the local existence argument concerns the complete nonlinear equation separately.
