Polarized Dirac shape response

Updated September 9, 2026

Summary: Full spatial Dirac quadrupole sources split into two calculable angular families whose polarization weights, radial spectra and reciprocal source feedback determine the allowed shape response.

Calculate the shapes the field can actually change into

The transition-density interaction compares both objects' changing spatial patterns. A complete calculation also needs the motions those patterns can undergo. Here those motions are calculated from the full first-order Dirac operator and the same scalar moment source which determines the stationary profile.

This is a conditional model on ordinary spatial coordinates x∈Rdx\in\mathbb R^dx∈Rd within the one nested physical manifold. Field components, angular basis functions and shape coefficients are descriptions of this field, not further physical dimensions. The radial profile is the stated scalar model's solution; it does not replace the actual electron orbit.

Dynamical opening of spatial shape channels uses this complete response to calculate how a higher shape with zero static restriction can drive a lower polarized field at nonzero frequency.

Incoming spatial shape modes compares these active modes at depths five and six using one common interaction coefficient, checks their moment restrictions and distinguishes their incident amplitudes from forces which first create a higher-sector response.

Verification

angular_dirac_response_checks.py constructs full Clifford fields, differentiates their spatial polynomials, and independently integrates their angular Gram matrices with a positive spherical rule. It tests both radial operators against differentiated physical radial functions, source completeness, low-frequency convergence, and the full matrix resolvent against (16). The centered full-field energy is separately varied by finite differences. Conditions remain active with Python optimization enabled.
