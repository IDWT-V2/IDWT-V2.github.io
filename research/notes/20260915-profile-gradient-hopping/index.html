Research / Mass and normalization

Obtain the hopping coefficient from the linked density

Updated September 15, 2026

conditional calculation

The common normal operator previously used a separately specified bounded function to couple adjacent normal modes. That function can instead be obtained from the linked geometry itself:

hgeom=(∂1+i∂2)∣W∣2.(1) \boxed{h_{\rm geom}=(\partial_1+i\partial_2)|W|^2.} \tag{1} hgeom​=(∂1​+i∂2​)∣W∣2.​(1)

The same rule works at depths 3, 4, 5, 6 and 10. It removes one independent spatial function. The simplex numerator and grouping of responses within a depth survive; the spatial integrals and response between depths change. Equation (1) is an explicit candidate choice, not a uniquely derived master interaction. Its dimensional coefficient remains to be fixed.

All coordinates here are the same dimensionless ordinary normal coordinates used for the linked profile. Restoring physical length gives a derivative an inverse-length factor. That factor must enter the common operator normalization, rather than becoming a separate particle fit.

The later second-variation test

The preserved spectral numerator and first response are insufficient for the supporting-state calculation. The complete nonlinear spatial Hessian has negative energy curvature in the endpoint direction at every tested depth for this gradient rule. It therefore fails that averaged local-minimum test. These energy curvatures are not negative particle inertia. The curvature-moment rule removes the same independent spatial function and passes the complete centered mixed-Hessian test under its stated retained-action assumptions.
