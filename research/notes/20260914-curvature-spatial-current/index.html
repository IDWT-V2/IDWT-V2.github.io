The kinetic current and the chosen endpoint interaction

Updated September 14, 2026

conditional calculation

The first-order parent fixes a conserved norm and its spatial flux. That flux is different from the endpoint bilinear used in the quartic control. The distinction can be calculated exactly. The nearly equal endpoint coefficients therefore do not, on their own, derive physical coupling strengths from the kinetic equation.

This comparison preserves the preceding endpoint-current calculation as a declared interaction model. An interaction need not be built from a conserved current, but choosing another bilinear requires its own origin in the master action. Neither current is identified here with electric charge or an observed force.

Exact parity of the endpoint overlap

The signed Hodge branches have equal positive low frequencies. For the positive beta branch choose real endpoint coefficients

eℓ+=(Aℓ,(−1)d−1Bℓ),Aℓ,Bℓ>0.(7) e_\ell^+=(A_\ell,(-1)^{d-1}B_\ell),\qquad A_\ell,B_\ell>0. \tag{7} eℓ+​=(Aℓ​,(−1)d−1Bℓ​),Aℓ​,Bℓ​>0.(7)

Reversal of the full bidiagonal chain gives, on negative beta at the same positive parent frequency,

eℓ−=(Bℓ,−Aℓ).(8) e_\ell^-=(B_\ell,-A_\ell). \tag{8} eℓ−​=(Bℓ​,−Aℓ​).(8)

This follows by reversing the x and y equations and their alternating signs; it is also checked with the complete chain eigenvectors. Hence

(eℓ+)†eℓ−={0,d odd,2AℓBℓ,d even.(9) (e_\ell^+)^\dagger e_\ell^-= \begin{cases}0,&d\text{ odd},\\2A_\ell B_\ell,&d\text{ even}. \end{cases} \tag{9} (eℓ+​)†eℓ−​={0,2Aℓ​Bℓ​,​d odd,d even.​(9)

For an operator acting equally on both endpoints, the odd-depth off-diagonal matrix element vanishes throughout that fixed-degree polarization pair. This is an operator selection within the specified subspace, rather than merely a vanishing expectation for one vector. It does not remove the diagonal channels.

At unequal degrees, the odd-depth overlap instead obeys

(ea+)†eb−=(d−1)(a−b)2η2+O(η−4),η=GR/c.(10) (e_a^+)^\dagger e_b^-= \frac{(d-1)(a-b)}{2\eta^2}+O(\eta^{-4}), \qquad \eta=GR/c. \tag{10} (ea+​)†eb−​=2η2(d−1)(a−b)​+O(η−4),η=GR/c.(10)

The expansion uses A_ell=2^(-1/2)[1-(ell-1)^2/(2 eta^2)+...] and B_ell=2^(-1/2)[1-(ell+m)^2/(2 eta^2)+...]. The effect holds at other odd depths as well as five. An endpoint operator with opposite weights would have a different rule. Equal endpoint weighting remains part of this trial action, not a derived universal force law.

The kinetic current provides a useful control: equation (4) makes its matrix element between opposite b at the same omega vanish at every depth. Thus the even/odd rule in (9) belongs to the selected endpoint interaction and cannot be attributed to every current of the geometry.

Resolve the small components before computing flux

The earlier scaled-inverse singular value calculation accurately resolves the low frequency, canonical norm and endpoint weights. A standard floating-point singular vector can nevertheless lose relative accuracy in intermediate entries many orders smaller than its norm. The kinetic flux in (2) depends on those small entries.

The scaled inverse has separable row and column signs. Taking its entrywise absolute value gives a positive matrix P. Alternating the normalized products u=P^T v and v=P u restores relative precision in the small components without cancellation; the signs are then restored. Eight iterations suffice in the tested large-gap cases. This is a numerical method for those cases, not a uniform iteration bound for arbitrary gap.

Independent 100-digit singular decomposition checks every component. The old unrefined vectors would give kinetic-flux errors of about 17% and 11% in the depth-ten degree-two and degree-24 controls. Those were not previously adopted flux results. The newly resolved vectors agree with the high-precision calculation and the derivative identity. The earlier frequencies, endpoint interaction and norm results are unchanged at their stated accuracy.
