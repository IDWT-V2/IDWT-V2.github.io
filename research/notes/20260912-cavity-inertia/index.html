Inertia of a moving cavity

Updated September 12, 2026

Summary: A moving-well sum rule gives count-additive inertia for quadratic kinetics, while an exact Dirac cavity derives inertia from confined field motion and exposes the confinement stress needed for a closed particle.

The inertial quantity to calculate

The mass-frequency relationship is already available: ωm=mc2/ℏ\omega_m=mc^2/\hbarωm​=mc2/ℏ. The proposed spectrum is

m(n,d)=μdDN,DN=(N+dd),N=n−1.(1) \begin{aligned} m(n,d)&=\mu_dD_N,\\ D_N&=\binom{N+d}{d},\qquad N=n-1. \end{aligned} \tag{1} m(n,d)DN​​=μd​DN​,=(dN+d​),N=n−1.​(1)

A route to its dynamics is to calculate the momentum response of an oscillating spatial pattern when its overall velocity changes. Continuous internal oscillation is compatible with zero total momentum. In a translation-invariant setting, the target coefficient is

∂Pi∂vj∣v=0=μdDNδij.(2) \left.\frac{\partial P_i}{\partial v_j}\right|_{v=0} =\mu_dD_N\delta_{ij}. \tag{2} ∂vj​∂Pi​​​v=0​=μd​DN​δij​.(2)

The following two controls isolate the required calculation. They are declared spatial wave equations, rather than derived IDWT particles. The quadratic comparison contains a kinetic mass parameter. The Dirac comparison admits zero bare mass and has explicit spatial confinement. Its confining background is prescribed, so its momentum must not be silently identified with that of a closed particle plus its well.

All coordinates used below are ordinary spatial coordinates. The controls do not change the particle depth assignments, impose compactification, or make one particle a constituent of another.

The confinement stress belongs in the particle calculation

For a stationary localized stress-energy distribution, a small boost gives a translational momentum coefficient

∂Pi∂vj=1c2(Eδij+∫Tij ddx).(17) \frac{\partial P_i}{\partial v_j} =\frac1{c^2}\left(E\delta_{ij} +\int T^{ij}\,d^dx\right). \tag{17} ∂vj​∂Pi​​=c21​(Eδij​+∫Tijddx).(17)

For a complete conserved tensor in a flat translation-invariant domain, the integrated stationary stresses vanish under the required decay conditions. Conservation and a vanishing boundary term give ∫Tij=0\int T^{ij}=0∫Tij=0 for a stationary tensor. A periodic localized tensor gives the corresponding cycle-averaged identity. The covariant total momentum of a closed system then gives M=E/c2M=E/c^2M=E/c2. The geometric hypotheses and background-field qualifications are developed by Giulini.

The positive stress in (15) is therefore a specific term that a self-contained confinement calculation must balance. Within IDWT, the balancing contribution must come from the same master field and its spatial geometry. An uncounted external wall is not that derivation. On a curved sector, the available translation or Killing symmetries and the appropriate momentum definition must be stated separately.

Consequence for the inverse mass problem

The count in (1) can enter a collective inertial trace without being the eigenvalue of a rank-one point-contact operator. Equation (7) establishes that possibility for a concrete kinetic law. Equations (10)–(17) identify the extra calculation required when inertia comes from the actual Dirac field motion: its full spatial spectrum and confinement stress must be included.

The route remains conditional on deriving the common per-mode inertia, the occupied mode content, the physical cavity and its same-field confinement. A count of available states alone supplies none of these dynamical inputs. The complementary Dirac calculation provides a related normal-mode energy and response construction; the homogeneous inverse action instead supplies prescribed simplex frequencies with a different mass mechanism.

The exact oscillator level moments are relevant when computing the energy of an occupied cavity. Summing oscillator energies and counting equally weighted modes are distinct operations. Finally, the spatial localization conditions remain necessary when promoting a finite mode or external-well control to an actual localized same-field particle.
