Canonical coupling normalization

Updated September 8, 2026

Summary: Kinetic Gram matrices fix normalized transition vertices and conserved generators, separating charge universality from profile-dependent interaction strength.

Modes and the kinetic metric

Use finite sets of linearly independent, admissible mode sections in arbitrary nested physical sectors. Their coefficient labels are not additional spatial dimensions. Let SdS_dSd​ map coefficients cdc_dcd​ to the spatial field, and let the positive time-kinetic form induce

Zd=Sd†Sd>0,Lkin=∑dicd†Zdc˙d. Z_d=S_d^\dagger S_d>0,\qquad L_{\rm kin}=\sum_d i c_d^\dagger Z_d\dot c_d. Zd​=Sd†​Sd​>0,Lkin​=d∑​icd†​Zd​c˙d​.

The dagger includes the specified spatial measure and bundle inner product. The block form assumes kinetic orthogonality between the retained families. Spatial nesting alone does not imply it. If cross terms occur, use the full positive Gram matrix for the independent combined basis and normalize that matrix. Redundant sections must first be removed; a singular Gram matrix cannot be normalized with an inverse.

Generators, charge, and selection

Let a self-adjoint generator Q\mathsf QQ preserve each retained mode subspace, so

QSd=Sdqd,qd†Zd=Zdqd. \mathsf Q S_d=S_d q_d,\qquad q_d^\dagger Z_d=Z_dq_d. QSd​=Sd​qd​,qd†​Zd​=Zd​qd​.

The normalized generator is Hermitian:

q^d=Zd1/2qdZd−1/2. \widehat q_d=Z_d^{1/2}q_dZ_d^{-1/2}. q​d​=Zd1/2​qd​Zd−1/2​.

If the underlying generator is the same scalar qqq on two families, QSa=qSa\mathsf Q S_a=qS_aQSa​=qSa​ and QSb=qSb\mathsf Q S_b=qS_bQSb​=qSb​, then their unnormalized charge matrices are qZa,qZbqZ_a,qZ_bqZa​,qZb​. After normalization both become qIqIqI, regardless of spatial depth, profile width, or the number of retained modes. This proves preservation of a common charge under projection and normalization. It does not derive which master generator is electric charge, its eigenvalues, or why both physical sectors carry the same one.

A neutral field perturbation preserves this charge when

q^aK^ab=K^abq^b.(4) \widehat q_a\widehat K_{ab}=\widehat K_{ab}\widehat q_b. \tag{4} q​a​Kab​=Kab​q​b​.(4)

For charge eigenvectors with eigenvalues qi,qjq_i,q_jqi​,qj​, (4) reads (qi−qj)K^ij=0(q_i-q_j)\widehat K_{ij}=0(qi​−qj​)Kij​=0. A charged complex field amplitude transforming as A↦eiqAθAA\mapsto e^{i q_A\theta}AA↦eiqA​θA, with matter transforming as zd↦eiq^dθzdz_d\mapsto e^{i\widehat q_d\theta}z_dzd​↦eiq​d​θzd​, changes the criterion to

q^aK^ab−K^abq^b=qAK^ab.(5) \widehat q_a\widehat K_{ab}-\widehat K_{ab}\widehat q_b =q_A\widehat K_{ab}. \tag{5} q​a​Kab​−Kab​q​b​=qA​Kab​.(5)

This is the infinitesimal invariance of Aza†K^abzbA z_a^\dagger\widehat K_{ab}z_bAza†​Kab​zb​. It is a selection rule, not a numerical prediction of an allowed entry. For non-Abelian generators the perturbation index transforms too, giving an invariant tensor rather than a single charge-difference equation.

Universal charge and resolved spatial structure

For a normalized positive density ρd\rho_dρd​ carrying the same charge qqq, the projected spatial form factor is

Fd(k)=q∫Qdeik⋅r(x)ρd(x) dμd(x),Fd(0)=q. F_d(k)=q\int_{Q_d}e^{ik\cdot r(x)}\rho_d(x)\,d\mu_d(x), \qquad F_d(0)=q. Fd​(k)=q∫Qd​​eik⋅r(x)ρd​(x)dμd​(x),Fd​(0)=q.

Here rrr is the specified readout onto shared spatial coordinates. The zero-momentum identity uses the complete conserved marginal. It does not require all the Fd(k)F_d(k)Fd​(k) to agree at nonzero momentum. For a centered Gaussian shared marginal with covariance CdC_dCd​,

Fd(k)=q e−kTCdk/2. F_d(k)=q\,e^{-k^{\mathsf T}C_dk/2}. Fd​(k)=qe−kTCd​k/2.

Thus equal charge is consistent with unequal spatial response and contact integrals. A mode count or spatial width need not multiply a normalized particle's charge. This statement concerns an assumed conserved charge; neutral composite distributions or other electromagnetic operators need their own form factors.

The quantum-field benchmark is charge universality in the Thomson limit, described in PDG, Electroweak Model, §10.3.1. The calculation above is an action-level projection identity, not an all-orders proof of that benchmark for V2. A common gauge-field kinetic normalization and a gauge-consistent elimination of omitted modes remain necessary. The projected current note gives the exact local conservation defect when a truncation drops such modes.
