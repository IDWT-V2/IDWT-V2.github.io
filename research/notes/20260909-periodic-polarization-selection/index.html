Polarization selection of a periodic spatial source

Updated September 9, 2026

Summary: A positive matrix calculated from full spatial source operators identifies exactly which occupied polarizations cancel a selected frequency sideband, while retaining the incoming object's spatial pattern and common-rotation covariance.

Turn a proposed polarization barrier into a complete calculation

The reciprocal periodic field equation retains the source of both deformed objects. Its bare spatial response also permits an exact question: for a given occupied positive-frequency profile, which second polarizations cancel a specified source sideband?

The answer depends on the spatial source operators, the complete bound Dirac resolvents, normal propagation and the incoming pattern. It can be calculated without selecting a special zero for one particle depth. All fields live over ordinary macroscopic spatial directions in the one nested manifold. Polarization coefficients label field components over those directions; they are not additional physical dimensions.

The dagger in (1) includes integration over the full bound spatial field. The frame contains both upper and lower Dirac components, not merely their rest spinors. In the tested multiplets every unit zzz preserves the same self-consistent binding moments. This property is checked before their polarizations are varied.

Thus the nullspace criterion does not require adding dimensional quantities with an assumed physical metric. In a solved interaction, the appropriate force is the effective force after both objects' response, including self-feedback. A zero in (7) for the initially applied pattern does not prove that the readjusted force stays in that kernel. The full pair equation supplies that additional calculation.

Verify a rest cancellation at every pole and derive its opening

A few sampled frequencies cannot prove a frequency-independent barrier. Let PEP_EPE​ be each complete spectral projector of the finite bound operator, and put Uf=∑BfBUBU_f=\sum_Bf_BU_BUf​=∑B​fB​UB​ and Vfˉ=∑BfˉBVBV_{\bar f}=\sum_B\bar f_BV_BVfˉ​​=∑B​fˉ​B​VB​. This is a derived momentum barrier for a stated spatial source. It does not equate the normal wavenumber with the particle's bound clock, and it does not remove the partner's feedback or the normalization needed for an interaction rate.

The result is independent of a coordinate or polarization-frame choice

Under an occupied-frame change W↦WVW\mapsto WVW↦WV, with VVV unitary,

FAB↦V†FABV,zj↦V†zj,G↦V†GV.(8) F_{AB}\mapsto V^\dagger F_{AB}V,\qquad z_j\mapsto V^\dagger z_j,\qquad G\mapsto V^\dagger GV. \tag{8} FAB​↦V†FAB​V,zj​↦V†zj​,G↦V†GV.(8)

A common physical rotation of the spatial field, incoming shape and inclusion acts on the source channels through an orthogonal matrix DDD: the symmetric tensors rotate as tensors and the dipoles as vectors. The complete spatial field rotation includes its orbital and spin parts. Its response satisfies

FAB′=∑C,EDACDBEFCE,f′=Df.(9) \boxed{F'_{AB}=\sum_{C,E}D_{AC}D_{BE}F_{CE}, \qquad f'=Df.} \tag{9} FAB′​=C,E∑​DAC​DBE​FCE​,f′=Df.​(9)

Consequently the complete GGG is unchanged in the correspondingly rotated occupied frame, and Gf′′=GfG_{f'}'=G_fGf′′​=Gf​. A common rotation cannot create or remove the polarization barrier. A relative rotation of the objects can change fff and their compatibility, which is the geometric question being tested.

Verification

periodic_polarization_checks.py constructs an isometric frame from the full solved spatial eigenspace and checks its binding moments for independent occupied polarizations. Independent ordinary/conjugate resolvents verify every entry of (4), while extremal Gram eigenvectors reproduce the full measured sideband norm.

Both Cartesian degrees, both normal momenta, strictly positive channel weights and the explicitly embedded lower tensor are checked. Every complete spectral residue verifies the rest cancellation; independent small-momentum resolvents verify its linear amplitude and quadratic Gram. Actual orbital-plus-spin field rotations verify (9), including rotation of the incoming pattern. Reports use timestamped filenames and checks remain active under Python optimization. The physical photon map, the occupied neutrino geometry and normalized rates still require their own derivation.
