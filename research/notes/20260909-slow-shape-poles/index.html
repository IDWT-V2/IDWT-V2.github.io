Slow shape poles and their signed response

Updated September 9, 2026

Summary: The two-frequency scalar shape response has poles whose frequency and residue decrease together, but their canonical sign and finite total response prevent identifying them from softness alone as stable weakly coupled particles.

Solve for the collective frequency instead of supplying it

The complete periodic response distinguishes the bound field's carrier from its collective source poles. In the radial rest examples, scalar quadrupole sidebands cancel and the remaining frequency equation can be solved directly. This gives a concrete test of whether a slow source also has a weak response.

An unstable full background has no generic settled periodic response. For it, (1) is a meromorphic source resolvent, and its individual real-pole weight does not establish stable absorption. The radial dynamics already transfer existing radial instabilities to these two-frequency fields. Stability and selection of the occupied field must therefore precede any particle interpretation.

Verification

slow_shape_pole_checks.py compares full four-block field resolvents with (1), locates the slow poles independently, and checks their residues using both source derivatives and the complete canonical eigenvectors. Increasing radial resolution verifies the quoted frequencies and signed residues. Both source components, both angular radial families and both signs of the Dirac spectrum are retained.

Direct real-time work verifies the convention in (7), and the complete canonical pair checks (9) and energy conservation during growth. Reports use timestamped filenames and verification remains active under Python optimization. The results establish the stated source spectrum and its limits, without a stable weak-particle identification.
