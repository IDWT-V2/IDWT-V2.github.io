4. A Competing-Range Interaction Hypothesis

A long-range attractive response partly cancelled by a shorter-range repulsive response can yield net attraction increasing with separation. Both could be contributions from the same physical field. A simple mathematical comparator is

U(r)=σr+Aae−r/a,Fr=−dUdr=−σ+Ae−r/a,0<A<σ.(7) U(r)=\sigma r+A a e^{-r/a},\qquad F_r=-\frac{dU}{dr}=-\sigma+A e^{-r/a},\quad 0<A<\sigma. \tag{7} U(r)=σr+Aae−r/a,Fr​=−drdU​=−σ+Ae−r/a,0<A<σ.(7)

The inward force magnitude sigma-A exp(-r/a) increases monotonically toward sigma. This proves the cancellation mechanism is mathematically possible; it does not derive either term from the count action. With two finite-range exponentials, increasing attraction can instead hold over a finite interval before both responses decay.

The force and separation energy are distinct tests. The conventional heavy-quark Cornell comparison U=sigma r-kappa/r has inward force sigma+kappa/r squared, tending toward a constant even though the energy increases with separation. See the explicit potential and phenomenological comparison in Mateu et al., equation (1). This is an external comparison, without importing its mediator interpretation into IDWT. Increasing separation energy does not require increasing force or uniquely imply two forces.

Testing the competing-range hypothesis requires a physical separation coordinate, field profiles extending through the containing manifold and the derivative of their complete same-field energy. Neither circle alignment nor the positive prepared contact supplies that calculation. In particular, the zero same-branch contact does not explain color binding. A viable common mechanism must also explain why the analogous higher-depth channels do not generate their own strong interactions.
