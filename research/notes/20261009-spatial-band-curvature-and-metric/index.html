The Spatial Mode Count Fixes Curvature and a Quantum Geometric Tensor

Updated October 9, 2026

The spatial band used for the simplex contact also determines its changing evaluation image. On a round-sphere control, this gives an explicit connection, curvature and positive quantum geometric tensor. The same binomial weights fix all of them. The construction therefore makes the count, transport and deformation response jointly calculable rather than prescribing them independently.

This is a result for actual spinor fields on an ordinary spatial manifold. The sphere is a compatible nested control family, not an adopted particle geometry or compactification. The physical sector radius and occupied field action remain unresolved. A tensor eigenvalue below is not a measured particle property or a new mass-frequency law.

6. Physical Scope and Reproducibility

One actual spatial band now supplies its count, contact, kinetic moments, induced connection, curvature and positive quantum tensor. These are linked consequences under a common spatial operator. They are not independent experimental evidence for the sector assignments. Applying them to particle properties requires the physical spatial geometry, the occupied field action, source maps and the spectral gaps that weight an eliminated-mode response.

The single checker passes 4,456 conditions across 51 cases, preserved in its report. It explicitly constructs normalized polynomial eigenspaces at depths two through ten, including neighboring controls, and checks cumulative moments through depth twelve. The explicit matrix cutoffs range from N=6N=6N=6 at depth two to N=2N=2N=2 at depths seven through ten; the much larger assigned bands are covered by the derived moment identities, rather than represented as enormous matrices. No particle simulation or amplitude fit is used.
