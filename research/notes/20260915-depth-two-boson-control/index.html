Physical polarization is a further spatial test

For a complex spatial vector ε=a+ib\varepsilon=a+ibε=a+ib, the real harmonic motion is

Re⁡(εe−iωt)=acos⁡ωt+bsin⁡ωt.(6) \operatorname{Re}(\varepsilon e^{-i\omega t}) =a\cos\omega t+b\sin\omega t. \tag{6} Re(εe−iωt)=acosωt+bsinωt.(6)

Its trajectory lies in the real plane spanned by a,ba,ba,b. Collinear vectors give linear motion; orthogonal equal lengths give circular motion; the general case is elliptical. This trajectory can have a component along the propagation direction unless transversality is imposed or derived. The ellipse's rank does not determine the spatial support dimension of the complete particle field.

The physical photon plane in photon transversality is perpendicular to propagation. Its two circular components have spatial rotation weights ±1\pm1±1. The existing two-form coupling gives the analyzer overlap ∣e†ε∣2|e^\dagger\varepsilon|^2∣e†ε∣2: a cosine-squared law for linear preparations and one half for a circular preparation with a linear analyzer. Direct real-space contractions verify these comparisons.

Massive bosons need not inherit that transverse-plane restriction. Longitudinal W/Z polarization is part of the measured comparison, including joint longitudinal states in the ATLAS WZ measurement. The Higgs mass assignment also does not prescribe a vector polarization: the ATLAS spin study found evidence for spin zero against its tested alternatives. The depth assignment is an IDWT premise, while these measurements constrain the required mode content.

Within each nonzero singular-frequency doublet, the current retained shape-source matrix is proportional to the identity. Its self-shape response therefore does not itself supply a polarization analyzer. The spatial plane identification, vector versus scalar state content and the massive longitudinal channel remain to be obtained from the full field. Higher-depth patterns can be compared with massive-boson polarization without assuming that the photon is either universal or exceptional.
