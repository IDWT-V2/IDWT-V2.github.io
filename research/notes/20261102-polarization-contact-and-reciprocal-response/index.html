Polarization Contact and Reciprocal Spatial Response

Updated November 3, 2026

conditional research

The hypothesis that coupling is higher-dimensional polarization becomes testable when a stated field energy supplies both objects' response. This note takes one simple local term and calculates its consequences. It gives a conditional mechanism with explicit limitations, not the completed IDWT interaction law.

The calculation retains the full field coefficients, including their relative phases. A two-form describing average circulation is not always enough: the existing complete polarization calculation and equal-current example show that ten-dimensional patterns can share their current tensor while differing in higher-order polarization and response. Those older results retain their own field equations; the contact below is a new, separately specified benchmark.

All fields live on the single physical ten-space plus time. The two contributions below are a trial decomposition of Psi_infinity, not two mediator fields. Their coefficients are classical field data and introduce no position-probability law. Amplitudes remain symbolic and uncalibrated.

Both trial contributions use a common component carrier so their overlap is defined. Applying this benchmark to two actual particle depths requires their compatible embeddings in the same field; assigning different support dimensions alone does not derive those embeddings.

7. Consequences for the Sector Programme

This contact gives a concrete conditional route from full polarization to reciprocal spatial derivatives. It preserves the separation between the count-scaled mass label and the directional response, but its scalar baseline prevents identifying all coupling with orientation matching. More selective interactions must follow from an actual current, derivative structure or dynamics of the same field.

The nested-support result is useful independently of that limitation: in the aligned uniform-extension control, the force contribution lies in shared bound directions. It does not grow new directional components merely because the deeper object has more dimensions. The role of tilted subspaces and finite support is a subsequent calculation, not already covered by the nested Gaussian result.
