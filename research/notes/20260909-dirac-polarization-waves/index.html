Spatial Dirac polarization waves

Updated September 10, 2026

Summary: The full spatial Dirac field supports exact linear polarization waves and nonlinear polarized plane superpositions whose scalar binding source stays fixed while their spatial currents change.

Restore the polarizations omitted by a single-amplitude envelope

The scalar envelope fixes a rest polarization while following changes of the bound profile. The scalar spatial source itself leaves a family of rest polarizations degenerate. Their normal propagation can be derived from the full spatial Dirac equation, without assuming that those motions are frozen or gapped.

All fields below occupy the same ordinary bound spatial coordinates x∈Rdx\in\mathbb R^dx∈Rd and an existing normal coordinate yyy in a containing sector. The polarization amplitudes specify spatial field patterns on that manifold. Their component labels do not add physical dimensions.

Every column belongs to the containing spatial Clifford representation. A further selected chirality can be imposed when it defines the intended radial family, but scalar binding alone does not select that chirality or the occupied polarization.

This gives a precise coupling limit: scalar self-binding permits and transports different polarizations, but does not choose the occupied one. A physical neutrality mechanism must supply that selection and the current sampled by an incoming field; it cannot infer them from the unchanged scalar source alone.

Reflection symmetry of scalar Dirac sources shows why this degeneracy can persist with unequal scalar confinement axes. It also checks the nonlinear moment source before treating a frozen eigenspace as a degenerate family of self-consistent fields.

Verification

dirac_polarization_wave_checks.py constructs the full containing Clifford fields, checks (4) at spatial points for arbitrary complex amplitudes, and integrates the canonical norm over the bound coordinates. Independent source integrals and finite spatial derivatives verify the linear polarization modes.

For the finite two-wave solutions it recomputes the scalar moment and checks the complete nonlinear equation using independent finite derivatives in time, the normal coordinate and every bound coordinate. It also checks both normalization factors, the normal current and the changing bound current. Conditions remain active under Python optimization.
