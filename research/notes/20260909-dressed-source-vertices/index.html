Dressed spatial source vertices

Updated September 9, 2026

Summary: Eliminating spatial modes dresses both the interaction vertex and its kinetic normalization, with exact finite-frequency conservation identities and nonzero responses that a bare projection can miss.

Carry the source through the same reduction as the dynamics

The projected Dirac current shows why a current can lose required terms under direct mode compression. This note calculates those terms by eliminating the omitted modes of the same field. It supplies the physical normalization and the complete source matrix for a specified linear Hermitian problem.

All modes describe Ψ∞\Psi_\inftyΨ∞​ on the one nested manifold of ordinary macroscopic spatial dimensions. Retained and omitted components are a mathematical decomposition of that field. They are not extra dimensions, independent interaction fields or mediator particles.

Let a fixed orthogonal mode projector define a block decomposition

H=(ABB†C),H=H†.(1) H=\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\B^\dagger&C\end{pmatrix}, \qquad H=H^\dagger. \tag{1} H=(AB†​BC​),H=H†.(1)

The formulas apply to a finite regulator or to operators with compatible domains and the stated inverses. Work at a real spectral parameter EEE outside the spectrum of CCC. A continuum or a resonance requires the appropriate boundary-value resolvent and flux normalization; the positive norm identities below concern this Hermitian, real-energy setting. The full real-linear Dirac fluctuation problem may also require its ordinary and conjugate blocks before this reduction is applicable.

Charge normalization and a finite-frequency identity

If the adopted action already supplies one conserved generator qIqIqI on the full mode family, its normalized equal-energy density vertex is exactly

Z−1/2Ω†(qI)ΩZ−1/2=qI.(10) Z^{-1/2}\Omega^\dagger(qI)\Omega Z^{-1/2}=qI. \tag{10} Z−1/2Ω†(qI)ΩZ−1/2=qI.(10)

A small retained fraction cannot turn this supplied charge into a neutral particle. This preserves a given charge; it does not assign qqq to any sector or identify the physical electromagnetic symmetry.

The full spatial current retains continuity

For a nonlinear spatial symbol, the finite-momentum current must instead be obtained by varying its actual coupling to the probe. Replacing that vertex by a derivative at one momentum need not satisfy the finite-difference identity. A transverse photon would contract the correctly derived spatial vertex with its physical polarization; continuity fixes the longitudinal relation but does not supply the transverse interaction by itself.

Application to the neutral spatial modes

Spatial transport of a neutral doublet shows that a fixed spinor projection can remove the first-order Dirac action of perpendicular spatial momenta. Equations (2)–(14) provide a way to retain that contribution in both the dynamics and the source before identifying a physical coupling. They also prevent a missing projected current from being mistaken for derived electromagnetic neutrality.

The required next inputs are the full spatial particle solution, the actual photon inclusion or collective map, and the action which defines VVV. The descending photon source specifies the propagation-preserving output projection; its complete current must be carried through the same reduction.
