Free Pattern Orientation: Derived Inertia and Angular Response

Updated October 14, 2026

The neutral orientation directions in the free count action have a derived response and inertia. Solving the complete wave and material equations gives the conjugate wave correction and the counter-motion required by zero total rigid momentum. The result is not obtained by assigning a rotor inertia or making a visualization drift.

The spectral certificate uses chain M=100000, b=40 and kappa=1/10. M in the response equations instead denotes the material kinetic operator. Numbered equation references apply within their own derivation section.

For all thirteen nonconstant maintained preparations, the full signed spectrum supplies a common positive inertia bound at 0<r=s/c<=1/100000. The constant preparation has no profile orientation. A supplied rotating perturbation-energy fraction gives an explicit upper bound on its linear angular coefficient per carrier oscillation. Zero angular initial data gives zero drift. No torque or disturbance level is selected by this calculation.

The angle describes an internal material-profile axis. The observer-space field requires reconstruction through the material map, and neither angle is the deterministic electron center trajectory. The linear response below is exact. Finite nonlinear rotation remains a separate solvability problem; the formal sphere equation alone does not prove it.

Exact Response of the Complete Free Action

Definitions and Actual Equations

All scalar products below are real, with the full weighted form metric and all chain components retained. The canonical parent H is self-adjoint on its polynomial-closure domain. Its selected real profile obeys H psi=omega psi, omega>0. The separate temporal structure J satisfies J²=−1, J*=−J and commutes with H. The word real means the first temporal quadrature; J maps it to the orthogonal second quadrature.

Use the audited normalized variables and operators

chi=sqrt(h) xi/c, h=omega²|psi|²,
B=−JA, ||A||<=1, M=1−r²A*A,
S=H²−omega², D0=H+omega.

Here 0<r<1/sqrt(2) is the common normalized symbol speed. E is the natural FREE material operator. Let T be an orthonormal inclusion of ker E into material L2. Its range consists of sqrt(h)/c times the physical affine rigid fields; it is not the unweighted affine space. Write chi=Ta+z, T*z=0, and define

I=T*MT, C=T*A*, L=C J D0,
p=I a_t+r C eta_t−r L eta+T*M z_t.                 (1)

The exact reduced action at p=0 is L_red=L_0−j^T I^−1j/2 with j the last three terms of (1). Its static wave operator is

Vw=S+r² L*I^−1L.                                 (2)

For reference the UNREDUCED linear equations, with the free E, are

eta_tt+rA chi_tt−2omega J eta_t+rD0 B chi_t+S eta=0,
rA*eta_tt+M chi_tt−rB*D0 eta_t+r^−2 E chi=0.       (3)

These equations, not a clamped action or an assumed rotor, determine the response below.

Let K=ker S, Q=K-perp. The selected signed eigenspaces, both temporal quadratures and all their angular multiplicities are retained. Define O={R_Omega psi}, the real orientation tangent space. R_Omega acts by the INDUCED rotation of spatial forms, so R_Omega(du)=d(R_Omega u). Holding one-form basis components fixed and differentiating only their coefficient functions is a different operation, wrong for the shell compression even at N=1.

For N>0 the audited angular calculation gives dim O=d−1, L_K has support precisely on JO, and its restriction

F=L|JO : JO -> rigid-charge space

is injective. In particular L u=0 for u in O. Since D0u=2omega u,

F Ju=L Ju=−2omega C u.                            (4)

The spatially bounded, finite-rank rows L are justified by the actual polynomial backgrounds, not just by finite rank.

On the off-shell space Q, S_Q has a bounded inverse but is generally indefinite. Set

W=I+r² L_Q S_Q^−1L_Q*,
D=F*W^−1F : JO -> JO.                            (5)

In the audited sufficiently small-r regime W>0, hence D>0. D here is a finite response matrix, not the spatial Dirac operator. No inverse of S on its kernel is used.

2. Solve the entire conjugate response

Take a real tangent u in O and seek a solution of (3) of the form

eta(t)=t u+v, chi(t)=t T alpha+z.                 (6)

Because A maps real material displacements into the first temporal quadrature, while JD0u is in the second,

A*JD0u=0 pointwise.                              (7)

Consequently the static nonrigid material equation gives E z=0; taking z in the free nonrigid complement gives z=0 exactly. This is specific to the balanced real orientation tangent. It is not a general assertion that nonrigid material response vanishes for complex polarization or time-dependent acceleration.

The remaining equations and zero momentum are exactly

S v+rL*alpha=2omega Ju,
I alpha=rL v−rC u.                               (8)

Choose v orthogonal to the stationary kernel K0=K intersect (JO)-perp. Adding a stationary vector would merely change the representative. Then v=v_K+v_Q with v_K in JO. Projecting the first equation to K and Q gives

rF*alpha=2omega Ju,
v_Q=−r S_Q^−1L_Q*alpha.                          (9)

Substitute into the second equation:

W alpha=rF v_K−rC u.                             (10)

Equations (4), (5), (9), and (10) now solve the response without discarding any off-shell wave modes:

v_K=(2omega/r²)D^−1Ju−Ju/(2omega),
alpha=(2omega/r)W^−1F D^−1Ju,
v_Q=−2omega S_Q^−1L_Q*W^−1F D^−1Ju.              (11)

The negative directions in S_Q are retained in W. These formulas do not replace W by I or assume that its off-shell term has positive sign.

They also give the reduced generalized vector directly. With q0=(u,0) and q1=(v,0),

V_red q1=−G_red q0,
−(G_red q0)_wave=2omega Ju+r²L*I^−1C u,
(G_red q0)_nonrigid=0.                            (12)

The source is in wave L2. S_Q^−1 maps it into Dom(S_Q), and v_K is polynomial, so v is in Dom(S). Thus (q1,q0) belongs to the actual combined evolution-generator domain. Formula (6) is an exact solution of the QUADRATIC system for all t. It is not an approximation to that system, though its growth eventually leaves the small-perturbation neighborhood of the nonlinear background.

3. The effective kinetic tensor and its sign

For tangent vectors u_a=partial psi/partial theta^a define e_a=J u_a and v_a by (11). The kinetic tensor is the conserved quadratic energy of these generalized modes:

M_ab=<u_a,K_red u_b>+<v_a,V_red v_b>.              (13)

Here the reduced material entries of u_a and v_a are zero. The kinetic wave block is 1−r²C*I^−1C. Eliminating the conjugate response in the action, including the gyro terms and their integration by parts, gives

L_orientation^(2)=1/2 M_ab theta_t^a theta_t^b.   (14)

The same tensor follows from projection of the equations: M_ab=<u_a,K_red u_b+G_red v_b>. Since G is skew and Vv_b=−Gu_b, this equals (13). This derivation fixes the sign; simply restricting the bare kinetic term to the stationary sphere would miss the response energy.

There is a useful exact cancellation. Evaluate the unreduced energy on (6):

M(u,u)=||u||²+2r<C u,alpha>+<alpha,I alpha>+<v,Sv>.

Use (8) to obtain

M(u,u)=||u||²+r<C u,alpha>+2omega<v,Ju>.

Equations (4) and (9) give r<C u,alpha>=−||u||². Therefore

M(u,u')=(4omega²/r²)<Ju,D^−1Ju'>−<u,u'>.          (15)

The negative final term is real and must be retained. It is the finite correction to a positive leading response; it is not negative complete inertia.

For a direct sufficient sign bound, D<=||F||²/lambda_min(W). Hence

M(u,u)>=[4omega² lambda_min(W)/(r²||F||²)−1]||u||².

For each fixed nonconstant preparation, a sufficiently small positive r makes the bracket strictly positive. The finite maintained list therefore has a common positive sufficient range, without per-particle retuning. The full-spectrum calculation below evaluates a sufficient lower bound at the common r=1/100000.

There is a second sign proof using the audited energy structure. The generalized vector Z_u=(q1,q0) is energy-orthogonal to the invariant negative-frequency subspace W_neg. Indeed the generator sends Z_u to the stationary vector (q0,0), which is in the energy radical; invertibility of the generator on W_neg and energy skewness imply H(Z_u,W_neg)=0. The energy on the complementary stationary quotient is strictly positive. A nonzero u gives a nonzero velocity, so Z_u is not a stationary radical vector. Thus M(u,u)=H(Z_u,Z_u)>0 in the audited small-speed regime. This proof does not pretend that V_red itself is positive on every excited field direction.

For fixed amplitude and normalized angle, the leading scaling is

M=(4omega²/r²) (F*F)^−1 on JO + O(1),             (16)

interpreted through u_a, since W=1+O(r²). In particular the conjugate wave correction is O(r^−2) times angular speed, and alpha is O(r^−1) times angular speed. A small numerical angular speed alone is insufficient uniformly as r tends to zero. Perturbative use requires ||v(theta_t)|| small relative to the chosen profile scale and reconstructed physical velocity small relative to c. Amplitude scaling psi -> a psi multiplies the physical tangent tensor by a²; the normalized operators stay unchanged. No physical amplitude has been fitted to a mass.

4. Symmetry family, sphere geometry and reconstruction

A constant ordinary spatial rotation of the zonal axis gives another balanced standing profile psi_n, n in S^(d−1), with the same frequency. The induced form action and the radial material coefficients make this an exact stationary family of the specified action. The sphere parametrizes orientations of a spatial pattern; it is not an additional physical dimension or electron-position space.

For even N, psi_(-n)=psi_n, so the sphere is a covering parametrization of a projective orientation orbit. For odd N the two profiles differ by sign; they become identified only if the temporal phase pi is also quotiented. Locally both descriptions have the same d−1 tangents and inertia. These identifications neither remove the Jordan directions nor change their local kinetic tensor.

Rotational covariance and the zonal stabilizer imply that the symmetric tensor (15) is a constant multiple of the round metric on the axis sphere. In an orthonormal angular frame,

M_ab=I_orient delta_ab, I_orient>0.               (17)

For d=2 this is a one-dimensional statement. At d=3 the real symmetric tensor commuting with SO(2) is scalar; at the larger depths the standard stabilizer representation gives the same conclusion. I_orient depends on the selected profile, its physical amplitude and the supplied constitutive parameters; its exact matrix value is not evaluated here; a certified lower bound is given below.

Promoting this local kinetic tensor to a collective-coordinate ansatz gives the FORMAL leading action

L_eff=I_orient |n_t|²/2, |n|=1,
n_tt+|n_t|² n=0.                                 (18)

Its solutions are great circles with constant speed. Their tangent at zero speed is precisely the secular term t u. Thus linear drift is compatible with bounded finite change of the orientation parameter. Equation (18) is not, by itself, a proof that the actual field follows that curve.

Material reconstruction is essential. Formula (11) gives

a_t=alpha(theta_t),
xi_t(y)=c T alpha(theta_t)/sqrt(h(y)).             (19)

The latter is an actual affine rigid physical velocity field, possibly expressed in mixed rigid coordinates. The wave's first-order angular response and this material response have total p=0 by (8). Omitting it changes the physical solution and misses the cancellation in (15). At finite orientation one must transport T, h and the response tensors with the profile; integrating the fixed infinitesimal coordinates a as if they were global rotation angles is unjustified.

In the existing scalar-component convention the Eulerian field is obtained from U(y,t) by y=X^−1(x,t). Its first variation includes the transport contribution −xi dot grad psi in addition to eta (and the common temporal carrier). If a different physical tensor pushforward is prescribed, that pushforward must also be differentiated. The induced form representation defining psi_n must not be silently substituted for coefficientwise Eulerian transport. The computed theta therefore describes the material-profile orientation; the observer-space pattern requires reconstruction. Its internal field axis remains distinct from the literal deterministic electron center trajectory q(t) and its six-dimensional orbit-plus-precession dynamics.

5. Exact statements, formal modulation and available errors

Four levels must be kept separate.

  1. Constant-orientation standing profiles and constant material rotations/translations are exact symmetry relatives. This establishes static degeneracy.
  2. Equations (6) and (11) are exact solutions of the linearized free action, including its conjugate response and conserved zero rigid charges. There are exactly d−1 length-two chains for N>0 under the audited hypotheses.
  3. Equation (18) is the covariant leading slow collective-coordinate prediction from that quadratic response. It suggests bounded travel on the orientation orbit, while including the required O(speed) response. It is not a proved invariant nonlinear manifold.
  4. An exact finite uniformly rotating solution requires solving the rotating-frame field and material equations, including centrifugal strain, the quadratic charge corrections, and every other stationary-mode solvability condition. No such family has been established by this calculation.

A precise linear residual is available. At one fixed background let U map angle variations to u_a and R map them to q1_a. For an arbitrary smooth small theta(t), put q_app=U theta+R theta_t. Then the exact reduced linear residual is

Res=(K_red U+G_red R) theta_tt+K_red R theta_ttt.  (20)

The zeroth- and first-derivative terms cancel exactly. Projection with U* gives exactly the inertia tensor multiplying theta_tt: U* K_red R=0 by the orthogonal real/J temporal quadratures. The third-derivative term remains in the transverse residual. Constant theta_t gives zero residual. For theta(t)=Theta(epsilon t), with bounded derivatives, (20) is O(epsilon²) in the appropriate dual norm for fixed r; its constants can grow as r decreases because R=O(r^−2).

This is a controlled residual statement for the linear model, not a nonlinear error theorem on time O(1/epsilon). For such a theorem one needs nonlinear well-posedness in a norm controlling the material map and coefficients, uniform tame remainder estimates, compatibility of natural moving boundaries, and solvability of the O(epsilon²) transverse response. The stationary kernel is much larger than O. Quadratic orientation motion may source those other stationary directions; a gap on the nonzero oscillatory quotient alone does not remove that obstruction. They may have to be included as additional collective variables. The indefinite nonlinear energy and possible resonances also require their own control. Compactness of S^(d−1) establishes none of these properties.

Exact Spatial Angular Norm

Put u(t)=C_N^((d+1)/2)(t), t=y1, lambda=N(N+d+1), on the unit ball with w=sqrt(1-|y|^2). Integrating transverse coordinates gives marginal density proportional to (1-t^2)^(d/2). At fixed t the conditional second moment is

average(y2^2 | y1=t)=(1-t^2)/(d+2).

The rotation generator tilting the axis toward e2 has scalar action R12 u=plus/minus y2 u'(t). Integration by parts in the Gegenbauer equation therefore gives, for N>0,

||R12 u||^2 = lambda ||u||^2/(d+2).                (1)

Both boundary terms vanish under the displayed marginal weight. The rotation is the induced rotation of forms. It commutes with d and with the scalar spatial operator, so

||R12(du/sqrt(lambda))||^2 = ||R12 u||^2,
||du/sqrt(lambda)||^2 = ||u||^2.

Every scalar/exact-one-form chain eigenprofile is a fixed linear combination of these two components in its slots. Component orthogonality transfers (1) without changing its coefficient, regardless of the trace-one balancing choice. Thus the actual full profile satisfies

||partial_theta psi||^2/||psi||^2=lambda/(d+2).    (2)

This is a geometric angular sensitivity, not an angular frequency. For N=0 the profile is constant and this ratio vanishes; there is no orientation coordinate to invert. Exact rational moment integration verifies (1) at all fourteen maintained labels; the derivation applies to every degree.

Certified Bounds From the Complete Signed Spectrum

Exact Response Bound

Use the moving action's natural free material domain, real balanced zonal preparation, full weighted form metric and all retained chain slots. Let K=ker(H²−omega²), Q=1−P_K, S_Q=(H²−omega²)|Q, D0=H+omega, C=TA, and L=C J D0. Here T is an isometric inclusion of the weighted rigid material kernel, ||A||<=1, J*=−J, J²=−1 and [J,H]=0. Both complete signed eigenspaces H=+omega and H=−omega, including temporal and angular multiplicities, belong to K and are removed before any inverse is taken.

The exact finite rigid response matrix is

W=1−r²CC*+r² L_Q S_Q^−1 L_Q*.

Here 1 is the identity on rigid-charge space; the earlier rigid inertia I=TMT equals 1−r²CC. These are different operators.

Spectral calculus on Q gives the bounded self-adjoint operator

D0 Q S_Q^−1 Q D0 = f(H)Q,
f(nu)=(nu+omega)/(nu−omega),   nu != +/-omega.

The apparent cancellation at −omega is not permission to invert S there: Q removes that eigenspace first. At +omega the pole is likewise excluded. Compact resolvent and isolation of these eigenspaces make f bounded on Q. Composition with J and C consequently extends the expression to a bounded rigid matrix, consistently with its original weak form.

For nu>omega or nu<−omega, f(nu)>0. For −omega<nu<omega it is negative, including negative signed H branches. Set

beta=max(0, sup_{nu in spec(H|Q), |nu|<omega}
               (omega+nu)/(omega−nu)).

Thus L_Q S_Q^−1 L_Q* >= −beta CC*, because J commutes with f(H)Q and is unitary. Since CC*<=I,

W >= I−r²(1+beta)CC* >= [1−r²(1+beta)]I.       (1)

This retains the negative off-shell stiffness exactly as a lower bound. Negative H does not mean negative laboratory wave energy. Its frequencies in (−omega,0) contribute ratios between zero and one; they are checked separately in the script rather than deleted by convention. Frequencies below −omega contribute positive f and cannot worsen (1).
