Reflection symmetry of scalar Dirac sources

Updated September 9, 2026

Summary: Spatial reflection symmetries force degenerate Dirac polarization multiplets even for unequal scalar confinement axes, and identify when those families also preserve the nonlinear moment source.

Anisotropic scalar confinement can retain polarization degeneracy

The spatial polarization waves use the rest-polarization family of a radial Dirac profile. A stronger statement applies to scalar potentials which are even in each principal spatial coordinate: unequal confinement strengths do not by themselves remove the entire degeneracy.

Work in the stated flat-coordinate spatial model, with a self-adjoint operator and a domain preserved by the reflections below. The bound coordinates are ordinary macroscopic directions of the physical sector. A curved global sector or different boundary conditions require their own symmetry check; a coordinate expression alone does not establish these global symmetries there.

Let

H0=−i∑i=1dαi∂i+βM(x),M(x1,…,−xi,…,xd)=M(x).(1) H_0=-i\sum_{i=1}^d\alpha_i\partial_i+\beta M(x), \qquad M(x_1,\ldots,-x_i,\ldots,x_d)=M(x). \tag{1} H0​=−ii=1∑d​αi​∂i​+βM(x),M(x1​,…,−xi​,…,xd​)=M(x).(1)

The proof permits unequal strengths along every axis. It requires the reflection symmetries and operator domain, rather than spherical symmetry. These numbers count degenerate field modes, not additional spatial coordinates or a physical particle census.

For the central five-dimensional family, the balanced total spatial rotation has two neutral members within its four-mode family, as derived in Rotation-neutral Dirac fields. Scalar confinement with the symmetries above cannot create an isolated two-mode energy band by splitting a four-mode multiplet. Occupation may depend on formation and other source terms, which require their own derivation.

Check the nonlinear source before declaring a degenerate solution family

A degeneracy of a frozen operator alone is insufficient for a nonlinear field equation. Different eigenvectors could change the source which was used to construct that operator. For the centered quadratic moment kernel, the relevant source data can be checked explicitly.

Suppose a finite eigenspace consists of exactly one minimal multiplet. For any operator commuting with all the even reflection transformations, its compression onto that multiplet is scalar. This follows directly from the sign proof: commuting with the WjW_jWj​ makes it diagonal in the distinct sign states, and commuting with their flips makes all diagonal entries equal.

Every diagonal scalar moment operator βxi2\beta x_i^2βxi2​ commutes with the even transformations. Its expectation is therefore independent of the chosen normalized polarization in that eigenspace.

For a pure scalar quadratic source

M(x)=m+∑iaixi2,(7) M(x)=m+\sum_i a_ix_i^2, \tag{7} M(x)=m+i∑​ai​xi2​,(7)

assume the aia_iai​ are distinct. The full ordinary spatial rotation generator

Jij=−i(xi∂j−xj∂i)−i2αiαj(8) J_{ij}=-i(x_i\partial_j-x_j\partial_i) -\frac i2\alpha_i\alpha_j \tag{8} Jij​=−i(xi​∂j​−xj​∂i​)−2i​αi​αj​(8)

obeys

[Jij,H0]=2i(ai−aj)βxixj.(9) \boxed{[J_{ij},H_0] =2i(a_i-a_j)\beta x_ix_j.} \tag{9} [Jij​,H0​]=2i(ai​−aj​)βxi​xj​.​(9)

The orbital and spin commutators cancel in the kinetic term. Between any two vectors at the same energy, the left side has zero matrix element. Thus the mixed scalar moment operator has zero compression onto that whole eigenspace.

The parity operator Π=βP1⋯Pd\Pi=\beta\mathcal P_1\cdots\mathcal P_dΠ=βP1​⋯Pd​ also commutes with H0H_0H0​ and the even transformations. A minimal multiplet has one parity sign. Its scalar density is even under x↦−xx\mapsto-xx↦−x, so its scalar first moment vanishes. Provided the total scalar integral is nonzero, its scalar center is the origin.

Consequently every normalized member has the same centered scalar moment matrix. If one solves the nonlinear quadratic moment source equation, all members of that minimal multiplet solve the same source equation. This statement concerns a kernel determined by those moments; another nonlinearity depending on further local density data needs another check.

When some axis coefficients coincide, (9) alone does not determine the corresponding mixed moments. The explicit central and two-block fields in Scalar Dirac modes and Nested Dirac pair separately verify their polarization-independent scalar sources.

Implication for coupling calculations

The scalar model can bind spatial profiles, deform a nested pair and produce polarization-dependent dynamical shape response. Its persistent polarization families explain why those results do not yet select an occupied neutral mode. Increasing axis anisotropy while preserving the same scalar reflection symmetries does not supply that missing energy splitting.

The full shape response can still distinguish prepared polarizations through its angular spectrum and source matrix. The required physical calculation must retain that response and determine which polarization is occupied and which current the incoming field samples. A source symmetry and a transition selection rule are separate parts of that calculation.

Verification

dirac_reflection_symmetry_checks.py checks (4) by differentiating complete spatial fields at coordinate points, including an even nonquadratic scalar potential. An independent Cartesian Hermite regulator with unequal axis coefficients preserves the reflections and reproduces the predicted multiplet sizes at depths three, five, six and ten. These spectra verify multiplicity, not converged physical masses.

The diagnostic checks each sign-flipping operation, the complete orbital-plus-spin commutator, scalar moment compression and parity within one minimal eigenspace. It also verifies that the multiplicity requirement is conditional on the source symmetry. All conditions remain active under Python optimization.
