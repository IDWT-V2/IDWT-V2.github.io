Why the affine expression is globally geometric

A smooth section of O(L)\mathcal O(L)O(L) can be represented in homogeneous coordinates by a function FFF satisfying

F(λZ,λˉZˉ)=λLF(Z,Zˉ).(9) F(\lambda Z,\bar\lambda\bar Z)=\lambda^L F(Z,\bar Z). \tag{9} F(λZ,λˉZˉ)=λLF(Z,Zˉ).(9)

Its holomorphic Euler identity is ZA∂AF=LFZ^A\partial_AF=LFZA∂A​F=LF. Differentiating it LLL times shows that

ZA0∂A0∂A1⋯∂ALF=0.(10) Z^{A_0}\partial_{A_0}\partial_{A_1}\cdots\partial_{A_L}F=0. \tag{10} ZA0​∂A0​​∂A1​​⋯∂AL​​F=0.(10)

Thus ∂L+1F\partial^{L+1}F∂L+1F is horizontal in every tensor slot. Its homogeneity makes it descend precisely to the bundle in (4). This gives the coordinate-independent projective operator (8). The argument applies to smooth sections; it does not assume that the field itself is holomorphic.

On a holomorphic section, FFF is a homogeneous polynomial of degree LLL, so (8) vanishes. Conversely, the holomorphic sections supply every holomorphic jet through order LLL at each point. A nonzero purely holomorphic differential operator of smaller order cannot annihilate all those jets.

This construction belongs to the projective differential setting described by Eastwood and Gover. Equations (5)–(10) give the particular operator used here directly. The differential operator is projectively defined; its positive adjoint square additionally uses the specified FS metric and measure. No general conformal invariance of the spinor mass action is assumed.

What has and has not been supplied by the construction

The inverse dynamics calculation now gives explicit local actions whose complete paired frequencies equal the simplex values. Those actions modify both kinetic and scalar terms and compensate the field norm consistently. They are reconstructed candidates, with physical coupling and causal propagation still to be tested.

The calculation supplies an explicit global geometric differential operator, its physical adjoint, its complete zero-form spectrum and a local Hermitian extension to the associated Dirac pair. The relative polynomial coefficients and the all-degree simplex identity follow from those data.

The remaining physical step is to obtain the derivative energy from the one-field master action, together with its common normalization, connection and occupied spatial states. The natural projective jet lift now has an exact norm calculation. Using that full lift as the canonical parent changes the reduced derivative coefficient to P(X)/Z(X)P(X)/Z(X)P(X)/Z(X); the unmodified local scalar response stays bounded on any fixed smooth background. That concrete lift does not by itself supply the proposed mass interaction.

The written algebraic local scalar current does not already contain the third-derivative norm in (1). Treating (22) as an additional action is a concrete candidate, rather than a derivation of that action from the original current. The nonlinear reduction also explains why a field-dependent effective action must retain its complementary response and induced norm.

Neither these operator identities nor their numerical verification establish the physical projective geometry or experimental particle masses.
