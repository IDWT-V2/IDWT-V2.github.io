Collective precession branches

Updated September 8, 2026

Physical premise (IDWT): There is one infinite-dimensional physical manifold. All sectors form one nested chain of submanifolds within it. Particle depth counts ordinary, literal macroscopic spatial dimensions. “Observed” and “unobserved” describe an observer's access to those dimensions, not different kinds of physical dimension. See spatial meaning and mathematical scope.

Summary: The reciprocal tensor-response model admits uniformly precessing electron–response solutions whose rate obeys a cubic, with distinct branches and stability that must be checked rather than assumed.

Branch structure

Define omega_ad=2 kappa a/I_e > 0. For

ωad<2ΩX33 \omega_{\rm ad}<\frac{2\Omega_X}{3\sqrt3} ωad​<33​2ΩX​​

there are two positive sub-resonant roots and one negative root below −Omega_X. The two positive branches merge at Omega_X/sqrt(3). Beyond that threshold only the negative real branch remains. The negative branch has an out-of-phase planar response, not a negative susceptibility parameter. This root count follows from the extrema of the cubic.

For a fixed positive a and increasingly fast response, the low positive branch approaches omega_ad. This is the finite-response continuation of the static precession result. The relation determines the rate given (kappa,I_e,Omega_X,a); it does not determine those physical inputs or select an integer petal count.

Exact real-root counting after setting t=lambda² shows that every nonzero factor has strictly negative real t roots. Thus the nonzero linear eigenvalues are imaginary for this example, without a numerical tolerance being used as the proof. Zero has algebraic multiplicity four, nullity three, and squared-matrix nullity four: one size-two Jordan block remains. The result is absence of exponential linear growth, not bounded linear evolution or nonlinear orbital stability.

The high positive branch has no numerically resolved exponential mode in the same full tangent test. This is a numerical result only; it is not covered by the exact low-branch polynomial proof.

The negative branch has a positive growth rate approximately .665240. A small constraint-preserving perturbation grows in an independent nonlinear rotating-frame integration. At amplitudes 1e-5 and 5e-6, the differences from linear evolution at time 3 are approximately 1.31e-10 and 3.56e-11. Thus this branch supplies a genuine unstable counterexample to treating every self-consistent rotation as stable.

Verification and boundaries

collective_precession_checks.py checks the three branches at the same coupling, the full equilibrium equations, tangent-space closure, nonlinear perturbations, exact characteristic factors and root counts, and the neutral Jordan structure. Its normal run completes 32 evaluations across 13 runtime conditions.

No autonomous capture into one branch, integer L selection, Coulomb orbital clock, physical current normalization, or petal readout has been derived. What is new is an explicit reciprocal model with self-consistent precession and a nontrivial tested stability distinction between branches.
