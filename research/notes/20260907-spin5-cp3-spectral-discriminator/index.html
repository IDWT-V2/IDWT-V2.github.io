Five-dimensional CP3 selection and spectral discrimination

Updated September 8, 2026

Physical premise (IDWT): There is one infinite-dimensional physical manifold. All sectors form one nested chain of submanifolds within it. Particle depth counts literal macroscopic spatial dimensions. The electron's CP3 sector has six; nuclear 3+3 locking constrains ordinary matter and observers to an experienced three-dimensional sector. Unobserved spatial directions remain physical. See spatial meaning and mathematical scope.

Summary: A five-dimensional spinor-bivector model also selects a CP3 minimum family, but its exact spectral sum rule excludes the six-dimensional singlet-triplet gap within the linear-bivector operator class.

Why this comparison matters

The six-dimensional selection uses one pure normalized irreducible spinor, a real skew source, positive quadratic source energy, and linear spinor-bivector coupling. Here the same construction is performed for a five-dimensional Euclidean carrier. No inherited V1 spatial geometry is assigned to that carrier.

An irreducible Spin(5) spinor has four complex components, as does either irreducible half-spin representation of Spin(6). The general dimensions are 2n2^n2n for Spin(2n+1)(2n+1)(2n+1) and 2n−12^{n-1}2n−1 for each half-spin of Spin(2n)(2n)(2n). See Woit's representation construction. The explicit five-dimensional representation and its quaternionic structure are also discussed in Niedzialomski's five-dimensional spinor analysis.

The following energy and spectrum calculations are explicit local derivations, not claims taken from those references.

Conditional exact-six theorem

Assume all of the following are independently justified:

  1. The relevant polarization is one fundamental complex spatial spin module, or one irreducible half-spin module in even dimensions, of a Euclidean ddd-dimensional carrier. It has exactly four complex components. This means the module supplied by the spatial Clifford construction, not an arbitrary higher-spin representation, multiplicity space, or four-level internal system.
  2. Its source Hamiltonian is linear in spatial bivectors, apart from a scalar shift; no extra vector or higher-degree operator is present.
  3. A selected ray has one nondegenerate energy and three exactly equal higher energies separated by a nonzero gap.

The spin-representation dimension formula restricts ddd to five or six. The spectral sum rule excludes five. Within these premises, d=6d=6d=6. Unlike a theorem that assumes chirality from the start, this argument uses the spectrum to discriminate the two four-component irreducible spinors. It does not establish that an actual electron satisfies premises 1–3. In the existing archive the six-dimensional operator produces this spectrum, but deriving a physical requirement for it independently of that operator remains necessary to avoid circular identification.

Operator-class escape route: why the hypotheses matter

The same five-dimensional matrices obey

S(b)+∑anaγa=4zz†−I4.S(b)+\sum_a n_a\gamma_a=4zz^\dagger-I_4.S(b)+a∑​na​γa​=4zz†−I4​.

Allowing an additional vector source therefore reproduces the singlet-triplet operator exactly in five dimensions. The no-go theorem is a theorem about linear bivector coupling, not arbitrary five-dimensional interactions. Scalar nonlinearities in the bivector operator could also alter its spectrum. A microscopic derivation must justify the allowed operator class before this discriminator can decide spatial dimension.

None of these source-polarization energy gaps have yet been identified with measured atomic excitation energies. Their multiplicities should not be relabelled p/d/f shell counts.
