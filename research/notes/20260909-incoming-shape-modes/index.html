Incoming spatial shape modes

Updated September 9, 2026

Summary: A specified incoming spatial moment and an applied force have different response maps; individual shape helicities obey exact hyperplane restriction bounds which clarify the frequency-dependent cancellation mechanism.

Distinguish an incoming pattern from a force that creates it

Dynamical shape opening follows a fixed force applied to a higher spatial field. The higher response converts that force into a changing moment, which then drives the lower partner. A freely propagating particle must also satisfy its own field equation, so its actual moment cannot automatically be identified with that forcing vector.

The two objects occupy nested submanifolds of the same ordinary macroscopic spatial manifold. Both polarizations, the actual spatial inclusion and the response of each object remain in the following equations. Moment and helicity labels describe spatial patterns; they are not additional spatial dimensions or physical particle assignments.

Frequency opening describes cancellation between visible components

Decompose a specified force as f=∑λfλf=\sum_\lambda f_\lambdaf=∑λ​fλ​. Then

Rχ(z)f=∑λχλ(z)Rfλ.(10) \mathcal R\chi(z)f =\sum_\lambda\chi_\lambda(z)\mathcal Rf_\lambda. \tag{10} Rχ(z)f=λ∑​χλ​(z)Rfλ​.(10)

At z=0z=0z=0, the odd function BBB vanishes, so all χλ(0)\chi_\lambda(0)χλ​(0) are equal. A force with ∑λRfλ=0\sum_\lambda\mathcal Rf_\lambda=0∑λ​Rfλ​=0 is therefore statically hidden even though its individual components remain visible. As frequency changes, their different responses can spoil that cancellation. This is the mechanism behind linear or quadratic opening in Persistent spatial shape barriers.

An actual occupied particle could realize a coherent combination or a frequency-dependent spatial mode, but its field equation must establish that preparation. A low bound frequency alone does not choose the cancelling combination. Source frequencies also explains why the frequency driving that combination must be derived from its actual spatial source.

Verification
