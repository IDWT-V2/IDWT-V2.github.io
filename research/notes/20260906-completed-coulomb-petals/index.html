Completed Coulomb petals

Updated September 10, 2026

Physical premise (IDWT): There is one infinite-dimensional physical manifold. All sectors form one nested chain of submanifolds within it. Particle depth counts ordinary, literal macroscopic spatial dimensions. “Observed” and “unobserved” describe an observer's access to those dimensions, not different kinds of physical dimension. See spatial meaning and mathematical scope.

Summary: The quadratically completed Coulomb–angular-source Hamiltonian has exact autonomous signed-rose relative equilibria; the one-electron L=2 and L=4 petal branches have no resolved exponential mode, while every tested antipodal pair branch is unstable.

Correction to the first completion test

The preceding Coulomb-angular calculation added the quadratic term required by its uniform-connection interpretation to the initial conditions of the linear-coupling solution. Those data departed strongly from the old orbit. That test correctly rejected the old preparation, but it did not test whether the completed Hamiltonian has different petal solutions.

Solving the completed relative-equilibrium equations answers that question: it does. During exploration, the canonical momentum equation was once transcribed with the wrong column-vector sign. The production Hamiltonian was correct. Re-deriving the equation from its tested energy gradient and correcting the solver changed the stability verdict materially; only the corrected calculation is recorded below.

The computed residual is at most 1.52e-13. Independent integrations over a full clock turn agree with the exact group orbit to 8.62e-9, conserve energy to 4.52e-9, and return the full state to 8.62e-9.

Actual projected trajectories

The first electron's first two Cartesian coordinates are exactly

(x,y)=cos⁡(Lθ)(cos⁡θ,sin⁡θ),θ=βt, (x,y)=\cos(L\theta)(\cos\theta,\sin\theta), \qquad\theta=\beta t, (x,y)=cos(Lθ)(cosθ,sinθ),θ=βt,

while its full six-dimensional radius is one. This is an actual smooth six-coordinate orbit with a signed linear projection, not a stationary orbital and not a curve assigned after integration. Even L gives the same planar image as the visualization's modulus-folded construction: L=2 has four petals and L=4 has eight. Odd L remains a different image: L=1 is an offset circle and L=3 is a three-petal rose rather than the displayed two and six petals. The visualization's height readout is not derived here.

Stability result

Linearize the full Hamiltonian equations in the co-rotating frame. The Jacobian contains Cartesian orbit variables and all fifteen source components. An independent central difference checks random Jacobian directions to 7.38e-10. For branches with a positive real eigenvalue, repeating the Jacobian with a five-times smaller step gives the same growth rate within 2e-6; direct perturbation integrations reproduce exp(sigma t) within 1.98e-6.

There is no resolved exponential mode. This is linear spectral stability, not automatically nonlinear orbital stability: symmetry zero modes can produce drift.

All four antipodal two-electron branches have resolved exponential modes. The completed magnetic term therefore does not stabilize that particular classical pair configuration. This does not exclude non-antipodal correlated motion or the separately checked antisymmetric carrier model.

What is finished and what is not

The existence question for a consistent one-electron completion now has a positive answer: the quadratically completed autonomous Hamiltonian supports actual four- and eight-petal projected orbits, and those two tested branches have no resolved exponential linear instability. The earlier claim that quadratic completion simply destroys the petals was too broad; it destroys the old preparation but admits new relative equilibria.

The physical theory is not finished in three respects. First, C=0 remains C=0, so the model does not induce the required source magnitude. Second, the integer frequency ratio is encoded in conserved initial source data. Third, the tested two-electron pair is unstable. A source with canonical amplitude and momentum can in principle be driven from zero, but its action, spectrum, and stable relative equilibria must be derived and tested rather than appended to force the desired answer.
