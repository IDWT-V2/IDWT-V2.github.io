Compare the operators in the same variables

A direct calculation expresses the smooth coherent scalar interaction in those same variables. The comparison identifies exactly what a proposed master reduction must change.

What the shell action changes

For equal degrees, normalize the weights in (3) by their sum. The two distributions over current order are

qN,k=(Nk)2(2NN),sN,k=(k+d−1d−1)DN.(6) q_{N,k}=\frac{\binom Nk^2}{\binom{2N}{N}},\qquad s_{N,k}=\frac{\binom{k+d-1}{d-1}}{D_N}. \tag{6} qN,k​=(N2N​)(kN​)2​,sN,k​=DN​(d−1k+d−1​)​.(6)

Vandermonde and Hockey-Stick normalize them, respectively. Their exact mean orders are

∑kkqN,k=N2,∑kksN,k=dNd+1.(7) \sum_k kq_{N,k}=\frac N2,\qquad \sum_k ks_{N,k}=\frac{dN}{d+1}. \tag{7} k∑​kqN,k​=2N​,k∑​ksN,k​=d+1dN​.(7)

The first follows from the symmetry k↔N−kk\leftrightarrow N-kk↔N−k. For the second, use k(k+d−1d−1)=d(k+d−1d)k\binom{k+d-1}{d-1}=d\binom{k+d-1}{d}k(d−1k+d−1​)=d(dk+d−1​) and sum. Thus replacing the smooth action by the shell action changes both its overall self-coefficient and the relative weights of its responses.

Its currents at different orders are constrained contractions of the same fields; they cannot be varied as independent matrix coordinates while retaining that positivity.
