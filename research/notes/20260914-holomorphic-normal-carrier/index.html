A gapped finite normal kernel for the simplex parent

Updated September 14, 2026

conditional calculation

A prescribed spatial Dirac matrix texture gives a complete finite normal kernel, a positive gap to every other normal mode, and the equally spaced factors needed by the inverse simplex construction. The normal wavefunctions are explicit functions on an ordinary Euclidean plane. Their canonical normalization fixes the projected coefficients; no amplitude is adjusted to set a particle mass.

This is a mathematical carrier construction. The texture is a given coefficient of a spatial operator, not a new independently interacting scalar field. No Yukawa coupling or particle-exchange mechanism is being adopted. Its origin in the one IDWT field, its supporting energy, and the choice of its winding from the physical sector depth remain unresolved. The calculation therefore improves the inverse operator; it does not derive a physical master interaction.

The full ordinary normal Dirac operator

Let z=x+iy denote two ordinary spatial coordinates normal to the proposed round field feature. For a positive integer n, set W(z)=z^n in dimensionless normal units. Use the four Clifford matrices

α1=σ1⊗I,α2=σ2⊗I,β1=σ3⊗τ1,β2=σ3⊗τ2,C=σ3⊗τ3.(1) \alpha_1=\sigma_1\otimes I,\quad \alpha_2=\sigma_2\otimes I,\quad \beta_1=\sigma_3\otimes\tau_1,\quad \beta_2=\sigma_3\otimes\tau_2, \qquad C=\sigma_3\otimes\tau_3. \tag{1} α1​=σ1​⊗I,α2​=σ2​⊗I,β1​=σ3​⊗τ1​,β2​=σ3​⊗τ2​,C=σ3​⊗τ3​.(1)

They act on components of the same proposed field, not on additional spatial coordinates. Define the self-adjoint spatial operator

Dn=−iα1∂x−iα2∂y+β1Re⁡(zn)+β2Im⁡(zn).(2) D_n=-i\alpha_1\partial_x-i\alpha_2\partial_y +\beta_1\operatorname{Re}(z^n)+\beta_2\operatorname{Im}(z^n). \tag{2} Dn​=−iα1​∂x​−iα2​∂y​+β1​Re(zn)+β2​Im(zn).(2)

Its principal symbol is the ordinary two-dimensional Clifford symbol, and C anticommutes with D_n. The polynomial coefficient confines its normal spectrum. Its growth at infinity is an inverse background premise, not a demonstrated finite-energy IDWT field configuration.

The use of winding to count a planar Dirac kernel has a mathematical precedent in index calculations for first-order vortex operators. Here the required kernel and gap are obtained directly from (2); the physical field interactions in that reference are not assumptions of the present construction.

A complete kernel and a positive complementary gap

In the two chiral subspaces C=+1 and C=-1, the operator is off diagonal. With the component ordering in (1), its block from positive to negative chirality is

Q=(W−2i∂z−2i∂zˉ−W‾).(3) Q=\begin{pmatrix} W&-2i\partial_z\\ -2i\partial_{\bar z}&-\overline W \end{pmatrix}. \tag{3} Q=(W−2i∂zˉ​​−2i∂z​−W​).(3)

Holomorphy gives the exact full-space factorization

Q†Q=(−Δ+∣z∣2n)I2.(4) \boxed{Q^\dagger Q=(-\Delta+|z|^{2n})I_2.} \tag{4} Q†Q=(−Δ+∣z∣2n)I2​.​(4)

The derivative cross terms vanish because partial_bar_z W=0 and partial_z conjugate(W)=0. Thus Q has no square-integrable zero mode. Every nonzero singular frequency of Q is bounded below by the square root of the ground energy of -Delta+r^(2n).

A useful explicit lower bound follows without a numerical spectrum. For a normalized scalar field in two dimensions, the uncertainty inequality gives <p^2><r^2> >= 1. Jensen's inequality gives <r^(2n)> >= <r^2>^n. Minimizing 1/s+s^n therefore yields

gn=(n+1)n−n/(n+1)(5) \boxed{g_n=\sqrt{(n+1)n^{-n/(n+1)}}} \tag{5} gn​=(n+1)n−n/(n+1)​​(5)

as a lower bound on every nonzero frequency of D_n. It is sqrt(2) for n=1, about 1.22759646 for n=6, and 1.16453055 for n=10. The factorization includes the whole normal spatial domain; there is no imposed finite normal-mode cutoff.

Write q=n-1 and let l be any integer angular label. A negative-chirality zero mode has components

Φl(r,θ)=(0,eilθUl(r),iei(l−q)θVl(r),0)T.(6) \Phi_l(r,\theta)= (0,e^{il\theta}U_l(r),i e^{i(l-q)\theta}V_l(r),0)^T. \tag{6} Φl​(r,θ)=(0,eilθUl​(r),iei(l−q)θVl​(r),0)T.(6)

Its complete first-order equations are

Ul′=lrUl−rnVl,Vl′=q−lrVl−rnUl.(7) U_l'=\frac lrU_l-r^nV_l,\qquad V_l'=\frac{q-l}{r}V_l-r^nU_l. \tag{7} Ul′​=rl​Ul​−rnVl​,Vl′​=rq−l​Vl​−rnUl​.(7)

Set x=r^(n+1)/(n+1) and nu=(n-l)/(n+1). The decaying solution is

Ul(r)=clrnKν(x),Vl(r)=clrnK1−ν(x),cl2=sin⁡(πν)π2(n+1).(8) \begin{aligned} U_l(r)&=c_l r^n K_\nu(x),\\ V_l(r)&=c_l r^n K_{1-\nu}(x),\\ c_l^2&=\frac{\sin(\pi\nu)}{\pi^2(n+1)}. \end{aligned} \tag{8} Ul​(r)Vl​(r)cl2​​=cl​rnKν​(x),=cl​rnK1−ν​(x),=π2(n+1)sin(πν)​.​(8)

For 0 <= l <= q these functions are regular at the origin and decay as r^((n-1)/2) exp[-r^(n+1)/(n+1)] at infinity. The small-radius powers are r^l and r^(q-l). Outside those n angular labels one of the two components is not square integrable at the origin. The other radial solution grows at infinity. Angular decomposition, (4), and these endpoint conditions establish

dim⁡ker⁡Dn=n,ker⁡Dn⊂{C=−1}.(9) \dim\ker D_n=n,\qquad \ker D_n\subset\{C=-1\}. \tag{9} dimkerDn​=n,kerDn​⊂{C=−1}.(9)

All moments of the normal spatial position are finite. The count in (9) is the winding of the prescribed matrix texture. Setting n=d supplies a d-dimensional normal kernel, but no argument here forces that equality from spatial dimension alone.

Canonical polarization gives equally spaced diagonal factors

∥Ul∥22=n−ln+1,∥Vl∥22=l+1n+1.(10) \|U_l\|_2^2=\frac{n-l}{n+1},\qquad \|V_l\|_2^2=\frac{l+1}{n+1}. \tag{10} ∥Ul​∥22​=n+1n−l​,∥Vl​∥22​=n+1l+1​.(10)

The sum is one. Let P be the orthogonal projection onto the entire kernel (9), and write J_z=diag(l-q/2). For the constant matrix S= sigma_3 tensor I,

PSP=−2n+1Jz.(11) \boxed{P S P=-\frac{2}{n+1}J_z.} \tag{11} PSP=−n+12​Jz​.​(11)

The equally spaced values follow from the actual normal spinor polarization. They are not assigned as a diagonal matrix after cutting off a larger oscillator tower.

For an adjacent angular coupling, use the bounded ordinary spatial multiplier h(z)=z/sqrt(1+|z|^2). Its complete kernel projection has only the l to l+1 entries

hl=2π∫0∞r21+r2[Ul+1Ul+Vl+1Vl] dr,0<hl<1.(12) h_l=2\pi\int_0^\infty \frac{r^2}{\sqrt{1+r^2}} [U_{l+1}U_l+V_{l+1}V_l]\,dr, \qquad 0<h_l<1. \tag{12} hl​=2π∫0∞​1+r2​r2​[Ul+1​Ul​+Vl+1​Vl​]dr,0<hl​<1.(12)

These are fixed positive spatial integrals, independent of the surface degree L and frequency parameter beta. They need not be the particular square-root weights of a Schwinger representation. For n=6 they are approximately (0.64662506, 0.71097328, 0.72458655, 0.71097328, 0.64662506).

The projected frequency is the rising product

Introduce a two-component canonical pair, with Pauli matrices chi. These are retained components, not extra spatial dimensions. On the normal kernel, the proposed bounded polarization and adjacent-angle terms give the complete Hermitian pair

HP=(0T†T0),Tll=β+l−q/2,Tl+1,l=γhl.(13) H_P=\begin{pmatrix}0&T^\dagger\\T&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad T_{ll}=\beta+l-q/2,\quad T_{l+1,l}=\gamma h_l. \tag{13} HP​=(0T​T†0​),Tll​=β+l−q/2,Tl+1,l​=γhl​.(13)

All n normal modes and both components of the pair are retained. There is one small positive frequency when gamma is large relative to the diagonal factors. The exact triangular inverse gives

ωn(β)=∏l=0n−1(β+l−(n−1)/2)γn−1∏l=0n−2hl[1+O(γ−2)].(14) \omega_n(\beta)= \frac{\prod_{l=0}^{n-1}(\beta+l-(n-1)/2)} {\gamma^{n-1}\prod_{l=0}^{n-2}h_l} [1+O(\gamma^{-2})]. \tag{14} ωn​(β)=γn−1∏l=0n−2​hl​∏l=0n−1​(β+l−(n−1)/2)​[1+O(γ−2)].(14)

For n=d and beta=L+(d-3)/2, the numerator is

(L−1)L⋯(L+d−2)=d!(L+d−2d)=d!DL−2,d.(15) (L-1)L\cdots(L+d-2) =d!\binom{L+d-2}{d}=d!D_{L-2,d}. \tag{15} (L−1)L⋯(L+d−2)=d!(dL+d−2​)=d!DL−2,d​.(15)

Thus the complete normal-mode matrix has the required simplex frequency with one common coefficient per depth. The coefficient comes from the same normal hopping integrals for every L at that depth. The physical common scale across different depths is still unexplained, as are the physical source and selection of n=d.

At gamma=10000, the projected electron and muon frequencies in the common dimensionless surface unit are approximately 8.72769605e-13 and 1.80452918e-10. Their finite-gamma corrections to (14) are 5.90e-6 and 3.38e-5, respectively. The limiting ratio is the fixed simplex ratio; finite gamma does not preserve that ratio exactly. The n=10, beta=27.5 comparison has frequency 5.90247175e-21 in the same chosen surface unit. These numbers are operator tests, not predicted physical masses or a derived relation between sector units.

A first-order full normal completion

The full normal gap allows a controlled extension beyond P. On the product reference geometry, let B_K be the actual surface spinor operator and keep the entire normal Dirac field. A concrete trial is

H0=ΛDn−Cχ1BK,V=−n+12Sχ1−Cγ(χ−h+χ+h‾),H=H0+V.(16) \begin{aligned} \mathcal H_0&=\Lambda D_n-C\chi_1 B_K,\\ V&=-\frac{n+1}{2}S\chi_1 -C\gamma(\chi_-h+\chi_+\overline h),\\ \mathcal H&=\mathcal H_0+V. \end{aligned} \tag{16} H0​VH​=ΛDn​−Cχ1​BK​,=−2n+1​Sχ1​−Cγ(χ−​h+χ+​h),=H0​+V.​(16)

Normal, pair and surface tensor factors in (16) are implicit. On the kernel C=-1, (16) projects to (13). Both V terms are bounded and commute with C. The free normal operator anticommutes with C, so at a fixed surface eigenvalue beta,

H02=Λ2Dn2+β2.(17) \mathcal H_0^2=\Lambda^2D_n^2+\beta^2. \tag{17} H02​=Λ2Dn2​+β2.(17)

In particular, the complete normal complement has gap at least Lambda g_n. This is a genuine operator gap, unlike a finite-cutoff level spacing. Taking v=gamma+(n+1)/2 bounds ||V||.

The normal Clifford symbols anticommute with the surface symbols -C chi_1 gamma_a. With equal physical normal and tangential speeds, the principal symbol of the full reference equation squares to c^2 |p|^2 in every ordinary spatial direction. Lambda is a ratio of normal and surface frequency units. It can be realized through the normal profile width; it does not require a superluminal wave speed or compactification.

The product reference still needs the actual tubular geometry and supporting field. Treating a normal coordinate through a round feature as an unrestricted Cartesian product is not a completed global construction in ordinary flat space. Its curved metric and outer normal tail must be checked before identifying (16) with that space.

The complete complementary correction can be bounded

The Feshbach reduction near the finite normal cluster is

when the denominator is positive. This is O(Lambda^(-2)), not merely O(Lambda^(-1)). The bound uses the full normal resolvent and bounded V; it is not an inference from a finite spectrum. Standard isolated spectral-cluster continuation then provides nearby complete eigenvalues when the bound is smaller than their separation. The combined grading C chi_3 anticommutes with (16), preserving the paired frequencies.

The first numerical report records (19) with one common Lambda=1e26 for both assigned depth-six branches and the depth-ten control. Such a hierarchy is an explicit mathematical assumption, not a measured normal width or a derived physical scale. The finite-gamma correction in (14) remains separate and is much larger than this sample normal complement bound.

Translation does not mix the finite kernel directly

There is a further exact identity useful for inertia. Ordinary momentum preserves chirality. Its only possible nonzero within-kernel matrix elements have adjacent angular labels. Using (7), their radial integrand is a boundary derivative:

rn+1(Ul+1Vl+Vl+1Ul)−2(q−l)Vl+1Vl=−ddr[rVl+1Vl].(20) \begin{aligned} &r^{n+1}(U_{l+1}V_l+V_{l+1}U_l) -2(q-l)V_{l+1}V_l\\ &\hspace{2em}=-\frac{d}{dr}[rV_{l+1}V_l]. \end{aligned} \tag{20} ​rn+1(Ul+1​Vl​+Vl+1​Ul​)−2(q−l)Vl+1​Vl​=−drd​[rVl+1​Vl​].​(20)

Regularity and exponential decay make the boundary term zero. Hence

PpxP=PpyP=0.(21) \boxed{Pp_xP=Pp_yP=0.} \tag{21} Ppx​P=Ppy​P=0.​(21)

This removes the direct within-kernel translation path that was problematic in the earlier unrestricted oscillator carrier. It is not yet a computation of the complete particle inertia. Translation of the deformed full mode, the finite normal complement, and the energy and stress of the supporting field still contribute.

The subsequent complete normal stress calculation now evaluates those wave contributions. In the large-gamma simplex regime it finds one negative wave-inertia direction, despite a positive directional average. This result survives the large normal gap. The identity (21) therefore does not establish a physical frequency-inertia relation; the same-field source and its supporting stress are essential remaining parts of the inverse construction.

The two endpoint modes l=0 and l=q have the same radial density, with U and V interchanged. The leading small-frequency state occupies these endpoints in its two canonical components, so its leading normal density and width are independent of beta. Finite-gamma shape corrections remain. This is consistent with a common cavity profile within a depth under the stated reference geometry.
