Scalar envelope of a bound Dirac field

Updated September 9, 2026

Summary: Readjusting the scalar bound profile gives a normalized Dirac envelope potential with a derived density power, exact uniform moving branches and a specified reciprocal fluctuation spectrum.

Derive the envelope interaction from the spatial source

Consider the scalar bound profile and one free normal spatial coordinate in Normal propagation of spatial Dirac modes. The envelope describes amplitudes of the same field on that existing coordinate. It is not another physical space or a mediator particle.

Use a fixed rest polarization and center, and let the real bound radial profile adjust to a positive scalar envelope density sss. The construction below is a variational reduction to that profile family. Uniform fields give exact solutions of the stated scalar model. Varying envelopes can also drive omitted shape, center and polarization modes; the reduction does not establish their decoupling or the stability of the full field.

Let χ(t,y)\chi(t,y)χ(t,y) be the two amplitudes of the bound mode and its normal Dirac partner. In their normalized basis,

αeff=σ1,βeff=σ3,s=χ†βeffχ>0.(1) \alpha_{\rm eff}=\sigma_1,\qquad \beta_{\rm eff}=\sigma_3,\qquad s=\chi^\dagger\beta_{\rm eff}\chi>0. \tag{1} αeff​=σ1​,βeff​=σ3​,s=χ†βeff​χ>0.(1)

The microscopic scalar density is s ϕs(x)†βϕs(x)s\,\phi_s(x)^\dagger\beta\phi_s(x)sϕs​(x)†βϕs​(x), where ∫ϕs†ϕs=1\int\phi_s^\dagger\phi_s=1∫ϕs†​ϕs​=1. These component matrices arise from the full normal partner; they do not replace the bound spatial coordinates.

The normalized variational equation

For the chosen real normalized profile family, the projected connection from varying sss vanishes: its diagonal inner product is the derivative of the unit norm and its off-diagonal normal-partner inner product vanishes by angular integration. The restricted first-order action is

It gives

i∂tχ=[−iαeff∂y+βeffM(s)]χ.(7) i\partial_t\chi =\left[-i\alpha_{\rm eff}\partial_y +\beta_{\rm eff}M(s)\right]\chi. \tag{7} i∂t​χ=[−iαeff​∂y​+βeff​M(s)]χ.(7)

The fixed-center and fixed-polarization restrictions in this action must remain explicit. The full spatial equation can have components outside this profile family when sss varies. Eliminating those components requires their actual response and the corresponding dressed vertices; the absence of a projected connection is not a proof that the complement is dynamically inactive.

Its unstable or gapless modes cannot be declared harmless from the vanishing projected connection alone.

Uniform propagation with laboratory density held fixed

For a positive-frequency plane wave with normal momentum kkk, write n=χ†χn=\chi^\dagger\chin=χ†χ. Its normalized spinor gives

E2=M(s)2+k2,s=nM(s)E.(8) E^2=M(s)^2+k^2,\qquad s=\frac{nM(s)}E. \tag{8} E2=M(s)2+k2,s=EnM(s)​.(8)

Using (4), these relations are equivalent to

M4E=M∗5n,E(E2−k2)2=M∗5n,E>∣k∣.(9) \boxed{M^4E=M_*^5n,\qquad E(E^2-k^2)^2=M_*^5n,\qquad E>|k|.} \tag{9} M4E=M∗5​n,E(E2−k2)2=M∗5​n,E>∣k∣.​(9)

There is one positive solution: the left side increases from zero for E>∣k∣E>|k|E>∣k∣. At fixed laboratory density, changing momentum changes the scalar density and therefore the bound profile. This is consistent with the normal boost calculation, where holding the proper scalar density fixed instead gives a constant MMM and a laboratory density n=sE/Mn=sE/Mn=sE/M.

The total energy density is

H(n,k)=nE−nM26E.(10) \boxed{\mathcal H(n,k)=nE-\frac{nM^2}{6E}.} \tag{10} H(n,k)=nE−6EnM2​.​(10)

The subtracted term is the interaction counted twice in density times the nonlinear phase frequency. Independent variation gives

∂H∂n=E,∂H∂k=nkE=jy.(11) \frac{\partial\mathcal H}{\partial n}=E,\qquad \frac{\partial\mathcal H}{\partial k}=\frac{nk}{E}=j_y. \tag{11} ∂n∂H​=E,∂k∂H​=Enk​=jy​.(11)

Thus the action fixes the frequency, current and scalar source together. For a nonlinear plane family, the derivative of its phase frequency at fixed density is not a substitute for the energy derivative in (11).

ε0=U(s)=56sM,P=sU′(s)−U(s)=16sM.(12) \varepsilon_0=U(s)=\frac56sM, \qquad P=sU'(s)-U(s)=\frac16sM. \tag{12} ε0​=U(s)=65​sM,P=sU′(s)−U(s)=61​sM.(12)

γ2(ε0+P)−P=nE−nM26E,(13) \gamma^2(\varepsilon_0+P)-P =nE-\frac{nM^2}{6E}, \tag{13} γ2(ε0​+P)−P=nE−6EnM2​,(13)

At rest take χ0=n (1,0)Te−iMt\chi_0=\sqrt n\,(1,0)^T e^{-iMt}χ0​=n​(1,0)Te−iMt, with M=M∗n1/5M=M_*n^{1/5}M=M∗​n1/5. The scalar source derivative is a=nM′(n)=M/5a=nM'(n)=M/5a=nM′(n)=M/5. For a normal Fourier wavenumber qqq, the ordinary and conjugate fluctuation matrix is

Both values are nonnegative. Their product is q4+(4/5)M2q2q^4+(4/5)M^2q^2q4+(4/5)M2q2 and their sum is positive. The low branch satisfies ω−2=q2/5+O(q4/M2)\omega_-^2=q^2/5+O(q^4/M^2)ω−2​=q2/5+O(q4/M2), agreeing with dP/dε0=1/5dP/d\varepsilon_0=1/5dP/dε0​=1/5 from (12).

This verifies normal fluctuations of the declared reduced action. It does not establish stability of the bound field: the m=0m=0m=0 radial branches at several working depths have the separate growing modes already documented in the bound-profile note. Those degrees of freedom, together with changing centers and polarizations, remain part of a full coupling calculation.

Reciprocal scalar envelopes readjusts two nested spatial profiles together and derives their full scalar coupling matrix from one common interaction coefficient.

Spatial Dirac polarization waves restores the other rest polarizations. Their linear normal waves have a quadratic gapless branch, and certain finite polarized superpositions preserve the scalar source exactly while their bound spatial current changes.

Verification

Finite differences of the full energy verify both derivatives in (11). The normal Lorentz stress is checked against that same energy, and the complete ordinary/conjugate matrix spectrum is compared with (15). Independent finite differences of the full spatial field verify (7c)–(7d) for complex envelope amplitudes, including the nonzero residual norm and its zero projection onto the retained amplitudes.
