Geometric response of balanced normal populations

Updated September 12, 2026

Summary: Paired normal Dirac populations leave a stationary scalar source unchanged while supplying a common geometric response across target grades, including a finite kinetic gap and the complete probe self-interaction.

A stationary source need not measure a moving polarization

The complementary Dirac reduction derives a stationary source from occupied normal modes of the same field. Its full positive-space occupation has an even frequency response. There is a different preparation in the same scalar interaction: add equal populations in the positive and negative normal branches, with a nonuniform distribution over coefficient directions.

The added populations cancel in the stationary scalar current and energy. Their polarization nevertheless changes when the retained geometry moves. The resulting leading geometric response is independent of the retained mode degree for a specified common probe. The calculation below includes both Dirac signs, their positive canonical norms, a finite normal kinetic gap and the complete probe scalar self-interaction.

The normal signs are not assigned an antiparticle interpretation. Their occupied columns must represent actual orthogonal modes of the same field; their geometry and populations have not been dynamically selected. Matrix directions describe coefficients over ordinary spatial sectors, rather than extra physical dimensions or compactification.

What this establishes and what still needs selection

This supplies a common geometric response across distinct retained grades under the actual scalar projection, with canonical normalization and self-consistent normal dynamics. A stationary scalar excess can still carry the complementary mode count described in the linked mass-source note. The geometric cancellation therefore permits different stationary sources and a shared moving-polarization response within one interaction.

It is only one response component. Scalar density vertices and the full interaction energy retain their occupation and band dependence. No electric current, measured charge, encounter rate or neutrino suppression has been identified by equation (9). The chosen populations are additional physical preparation data; common Δu\Delta uΔu is not derived by the scalar kernel. At zero gap their occupied density spectra are conserved by (3), so this closed mode model cannot dynamically choose those spectra from arbitrary initial data.

Physical spatial geometry must supply the normal modes, their preparation and their overlap with other nested sectors. The sectors are nested; one particle is not a constituent inside another. The full spatial Dirac residual and finite-norm binding must be checked when passing from this mode control to particle fields. The positive-current localization condition states one relevant flat-space restriction. Neither a physical mass operator nor a complete coupling law is claimed here.
