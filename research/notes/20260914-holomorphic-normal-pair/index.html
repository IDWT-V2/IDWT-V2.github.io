Complete normal currents and the two-wave coupling

Updated September 14, 2026

conditional calculation

The gapped normal carrier admits a concrete polarized two-wave current calculation. Its large-gap limit has two distinct angular contributions: the overlap of separate self currents and a mixed current between the waves. Keeping both removes a false inference of zero interaction from a zero of the separate-current overlap.

Under common parameters, the normal response is nearly independent of the occupied surface degree within a winding. Actual surface overlaps retain additional degree and orientation dependence. This is a conditional geometric interaction on the inverse spatial carrier, not a derived physical IDWT force or a rate of particle encounters.

The operator, source and complete normal corrections are defined in the carrier, the inertia calculation, and the endpoint source response. The local quartic used here is a declared probe of those fields. Its failure to generate the prescribed normal texture remains unresolved.

Keep the complete same-field phase average

For two distinct carrier frequencies, write the field at a given position as A exp(-i omega_a t)+B exp(-i omega_b t). With the same two Hermitian normal matrices Gamma_i, define

JiA=A†ΓiA,JiB=B†ΓiB,Ki=A†ΓiB.(1) J_i^A=A^\dagger\Gamma_iA,\qquad J_i^B=B^\dagger\Gamma_iB,\qquad K_i=A^\dagger\Gamma_iB. \tag{1} JiA​=A†Γi​A,JiB​=B†Γi​B,Ki​=A†Γi​B.(1)

The quartic U=g sum_i J_i^2/2 is an interaction of this one field. Expansion and averaging over the relative oscillation phase gives the cross term

U‾AB=g(JA⋅JB+∑i∣Ki∣2).(2) \boxed{\overline U_{AB} =g\left(\boldsymbol J^A\cdot\boldsymbol J^B +\sum_i|K_i|^2\right).} \tag{2} UAB​=g(JA⋅JB+i∑​∣Ki​∣2).​(2)

The second term is interference of the two waves in the same local current. It is not an exchanged particle. Equation (2) is an algebraic phase average for distinct frequencies. At equal or resonant frequencies the relevant phases must instead remain in the dynamics. The unaveraged quartic retains them in either case.

For the normal kernel both currents in (1) vanish at leading chiral order. Inserting the complete correction eta/Lambda gives their first nonzero terms. The calculation retains all normal Fourier powers and all four normal spinor components in both members of the canonical pair.

This is not a derivation of neutrino interactions. The full depth-five geometry, physical units and cross-sector interaction still have to be supplied.

At gamma=10000, the aligned electron and muon normal source profiles differ by about 0.00328 in relative L2 norm. Their direct and mixed normal overlap coefficients are respectively about 9.20738332e6 and 9.20740815e6, close to the common endpoint value 9.20762937e6. The cross term can also be negative at other orientations; the complete quartic, including the separate self terms, remains nonnegative for positive g. Neither the sign of this cross energy nor an energy gradient alone establishes the dynamics of an encounter.

The physical normalization removes an apparent width suppression

Let the surface radius be R and the normal profile width be a. Both are ordinary spatial lengths. The normal width is a field-profile scale on an unbounded plane, not a compactified dimension. Equal physical wave speeds give Lambda=R/a. Canonical normalization of a d-dimensional product profile requires

Ψphys=a−1R−(d−2)/2ψdimensionless.(9) \Psi_{\rm phys} =a^{-1}R^{-(d-2)/2}\psi_{\rm dimensionless}. \tag{9} Ψphys​=a−1R−(d−2)/2ψdimensionless​.(9)

Its first nonzero normal current therefore scales as

Jphys=a−2R−(d−2)Λ−1j=a−1R−(d−1)j.(10) J_{\rm phys} =a^{-2}R^{-(d-2)}\Lambda^{-1}j =a^{-1}R^{-(d-1)}j. \tag{10} Jphys​=a−2R−(d−2)Λ−1j=a−1R−(d−1)j.(10)

The complete volume element contributes a^2 R^(d-2). Hence

∫Jphys2 ddx=R−d∫j2 d2r dA(11) \boxed{\int J_{\rm phys}^2\,d^dx =R^{-d}\int j^2\,d^2r\,dA} \tag{11} ∫Jphys2​ddx=R−d∫j2d2rdA​(11)

at this leading order. The apparent Lambda^(-2) suppression is exactly offset by the increasingly concentrated physical profile. If g_d is the physical quartic coefficient in frequency-times-volume units, the pair coefficient is

U‾ab=gdRdIab(Dab+Xab).(12) \overline U_{ab} =\frac{g_d}{R^d}I_{ab}(D_{ab}+X_{ab}). \tag{12} Uab​=Rdgd​​Iab​(Dab​+Xab​).(12)

Thus narrowing this normal profile alone does not weaken the physical interaction at fixed g_d and R. Taking Lambda large at fixed dimensionless quartic g instead changes the corresponding physical coupling. That distinction is necessary when comparing the normal gap with the source and response. Across different d, the physical normalization and sector measure still require a common master-field derivation; the numerical C_n values alone do not settle that relation.

Separate the two ordinary spatial profiles

The complete normal fields can also be placed at different centers. Let s be their separation along the axial normal direction, in units of the common normal width a. This axial displacement is an ordinary Cartesian translation. It is distinct from displacing the radial coordinate of the round surface, which would change the profile of the feature rather than translate the whole feature.

The separate and mixed terms are evaluated on the actual complex fields at x+s/2 and x-s/2, with all their spinor components and normal angles. For aligned normal polarization, the depth-six electron/muon comparison gives the following normal cross coefficient, divided by the common endpoint value 2 C_6 gamma^2:

Polarized coefficients are more varied than this aligned example. At relative angle pi/6 the complete cross term is negative at coincident centers, positive at separations one and two, and negative again at separation three. The separate and mixed contributions have different spatial envelopes. A sign-changing cross energy alone does not prove a bound particle pair or a stable dynamical trajectory.

Direct differentiation of the full complex spatial fields gives the gradient of this cross energy. Independent displaced-profile differences agree with it. Interchanging the two profiles leaves their cross energy unchanged and reverses its separation derivative. A common rotation of both polarized fields and the separation vector also leaves it unchanged. These checks establish reciprocity of this interaction contribution; the inertial and supporting-field terms needed for motion remain absent.
