28. Historical quadratic kernel as a candidate pre-contact force

Historical V2 contained

Sinthist=12∑d,d′gdd′∫(ξd⋅ξd′)2 [ΨˉPdΨ] [ΨˉPd′Ψ]. S_{\rm int}^{\rm hist} = \frac12\sum_{d,d'}g_{dd'} \int (\xi_d\cdot\xi_{d'})^2 \,[\bar\Psi P_d\Psi] \,[\bar\Psi P_{d'}\Psi]. Sinthist​=21​d,d′∑​gdd′​∫(ξd​⋅ξd′​)2[ΨˉPd​Ψ][ΨˉPd′​Ψ].

For d=d′=6d=d'=6d=d′=6, this gives a depth-six density-density kernel proportional to

(ξ⋅ξ′)2. (\xi\cdot\xi')^2. (ξ⋅ξ′)2.

After averaging one isotropic source distribution, historical V2 obtained

Vmf(ξ)∝g666⟨∣ξ′∣2⟩ ∣ξ∣2. V_{\rm mf}(\xi) \propto \frac{g_{66}}6\langle |\xi'|^2\rangle\,|\xi|^2. Vmf​(ξ)∝6g66​​⟨∣ξ′∣2⟩∣ξ∣2.

This is a genuine extended coordinate-space mean field and therefore could influence trajectories before exact point contact.

But it cannot simply be reintroduced as established current V2 physics, for two reasons already identified elsewhere:

  1. current V2 replaced the historical oscillator-eigenvalue story by the exact projective point-contact mass theorem;
  2. the bridge from coordinate harmonic kernel to occupied phase-ray contact remains explicitly unproved.

Therefore the historical kernel is useful here as a candidate mechanism to test, not as evidence.

28.1 Sign and geometric behavior of the historical d=6d=6d=6 pair kernel

The raw pair invariant

(ξ1⋅ξ2)2 (\xi_1\cdot\xi_2)^2 (ξ1​⋅ξ2​)2

is nonnegative and is minimized when the two vectors are orthogonal, not when their spatial centers coincide. It is therefore not a central pair potential of the form V(∣ξ1−ξ2∣)V(|\xi_1-\xi_2|)V(∣ξ1​−ξ2​∣).

Its gradient with respect to ξ1\xi_1ξ1​ is

∇ξ1(ξ1⋅ξ2)2=2(ξ1⋅ξ2)ξ2. \nabla_{\xi_1}(\xi_1\cdot\xi_2)^2 =2(\xi_1\cdot\xi_2)\xi_2. ∇ξ1​​(ξ1​⋅ξ2​)2=2(ξ1​⋅ξ2​)ξ2​.

Thus by itself it tends to change relative orientation/amplitude, not universally reduce the separation

∣ξ1−ξ2∣. |\xi_1-\xi_2|. ∣ξ1​−ξ2​∣.

The harmonic well obtained historically arose only after averaging over an isotropic source density centered on the same mode, not from a literal attractive two-particle distance law.

This is another important negative result:

the historical (ξ⋅ξ′)2 kernel is not, by itself, the missing six-dimensional electron-capture force. \boxed{ \text{the historical }(\xi\cdot\xi')^2\text{ kernel is not, by itself, the missing six-dimensional electron-capture force.} } the historical (ξ⋅ξ′)2 kernel is not, by itself, the missing six-dimensional electron-capture force.​

To produce the user's Earth-like correlation dynamically, the relevant two-body operator must depend on full relative separation, a covariant geodesic analogue, or a connection whose equations reduce that separation.
