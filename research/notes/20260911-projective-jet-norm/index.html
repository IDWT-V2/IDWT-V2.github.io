Summary: The complete projective holomorphic jet lift has a calculable kinetic norm that changes its reduced simplex coefficient, while canonical compression keeps the local scalar response bounded on any fixed smooth background.

The geometric polynomial needs its parent-field norm

One possible route to a master-field origin is to represent the lower field and its derivatives by components of a larger field. For the natural projective holomorphic jet lift, that route has an explicitly nonconstant kinetic metric:

Z(X)=X2+12X+4040.(2) \boxed{Z(X)=\frac{X^2+12X+40}{40}.} \tag{2} Z(X)=40X2+12X+40​.​(2)

If this lifted field carries the canonical time derivative, the reduced coefficient of the derivative energy is P(X)/Z(X)P(X)/Z(X)P(X)/Z(X). The metric cannot be omitted while retaining that physical lift. The following calculation derives it without discarding any component or adjusting the occupied indices.

The geometry remains conditional physical CP3 with unknown radius RRR, ordinary macroscopic spatial directions and no compactification. The finite jet carrier describes derivatives of the same spatial field. Its mathematical rank adds no physical spatial directions, and its realization as components of the master spinor is a further hypothesis.

Thus this canonical compression of the algebraic scalar source cannot equal a nonzero constant times the unbounded polynomial P(X)P(X)P(X) on all degrees of one fixed smooth background. This is a statement about the specified linear lift and fixed-background response. It does not impose a uniform bound across different self-consistent particle backgrounds, whose densities may differ. It also does not replace a nonlinear, frequency-dependent elimination of other spatial components. Those require the full complementary variation.

The geometric simplex operator remains a concrete candidate. The natural osculating-polynomial lift, by itself, does not derive it as a canonically normalized physical mass interaction.
