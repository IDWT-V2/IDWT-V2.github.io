A Convex Dispersion Rule Gives Exact Count States and Persistence

Updated October 13, 2026

The count-state construction has a direct convex forward form. Take the maximum of massive-Dirac dispersion curves fixed by the geometric count data, smooth that maximum in its energy arguments, and eliminate one strictly convex algebraic variable. This gives one compatible constitutive law across every eligible shell. Each count node retains its exact value, slope and an open massive-dispersion neighborhood.

In the preferred-time positive-wave realization, these neighborhoods become exactly affine scalar-source charts. The complete energy is nonnegative, and the existing full-spectrum argument gives conditional nonlinear persistence for nodeless fields at all fourteen maintained assignments. The finite feedback-label window is explicit: omitting it would admit extra single-shell frequencies beyond the count range.

This is a simpler effective realization, not a derivation of its constitutive premise from the master field. The ordinary sphere, sector units and count tangency data remain supplied. No new preference for the assigned depths follows from the fact that the construction works there.
