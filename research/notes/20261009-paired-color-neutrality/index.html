Paired Spatial Modes, Color Neutrality and the Mass Count

Updated October 9, 2026

A paired-mode construction can give exact non-Abelian neutrality. Its useful content is an explicit representation and invariant state, rather than cancellation inferred from a count. The same algebra gives a precise meaning to an interaction that acts on two matched parts of a field pattern. It also exposes the additional selection rule needed to associate that neutral branch with leptons and a colored branch with quarks.

All spatial dimensions below are ordinary dimensions of the nested physical manifold. A tensor product of field-mode spaces does not make an electron a composite of two quarks, identify either factor with an antiparticle, or establish a physical division of its six equivalent spatial dimensions into two preferred triples. A compatible field representation and a literal product geometry are different hypotheses.

This makes the two-sided-force proposal concrete: derive whether the common same-field interaction samples matched local actions or resolves different parts of a correlated pattern, and calculate which representation it excites. Neither an average cancellation nor availability of a singlet is sufficient to claim absence of every strong response.
