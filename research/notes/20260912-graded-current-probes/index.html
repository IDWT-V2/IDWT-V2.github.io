Updated September 13, 2026

Summary: Reduced currents give exact common-probe responses across different coherent mass grades, with reciprocal shape evolution, same-grade interference and vacuum regularity kept explicit.

Use the self-mass action for the second pattern too

All currents are evaluated at the same physical spatial point before integration. Spatial profiles, both polarizations and their phases remain part of the fields. The independent canonical grade decomposition and weights in (1) are inverse-action premises, not a derived master reduction.

A fixed probe gives a stronger exact statement

Let a probe have fixed degree MMM, arbitrary complex mode coefficients and every spinor component. Its currents only reach order MMM. Any coherent target of degree N≥MN\ge MN≥M has

ρN,k=APz,k,0≤k≤M.(4) \rho_{N,k}=A P_{z,k},\qquad 0\le k\le M. \tag{4} ρN,k​=APz,k​,0≤k≤M.(4)

Therefore its full cross energy with that probe, and its diagonal source on the target's occupied ray, are independent of NNN at the same local AAA and zzz. This does not require the probe itself to be coherent. The complete target gradient still contains BNkB_{Nk}BNk​ and depends on NNN: equal diagonal responses do not imply equal changes of shape.

For the root or smooth law Φσ(r)=μ(r2+σ2−σ)\Phi_\sigma(r)=\mu(\sqrt{r^2+\sigma^2}-\sigma)Φσ​(r)=μ(r2+σ2​−σ), linearize about a nonzero coherent target and a zero probe field. Since the probe current is quadratic in its amplitude, its complete linear source operator is

VM(x)=μ sσ(A(x)) β∑k=0Mhk,dBMk(Pz(x),k),sσ(A)=AA2+σ2.(5) \boxed{\mathcal V_M(x)= \mu\,s_\sigma(A(x))\,\beta \sum_{k=0}^Mh_{k,d}B_{Mk}(P_{z(x),k}),\qquad s_\sigma(A)=\frac{A}{\sqrt{A^2+\sigma^2}}.} \tag{5} VM​(x)=μsσ​(A(x))βk=0∑M​hk,d​BMk​(Pz(x),k​),sσ​(A)=A2+σ2​A​.​(5)

At the positive root endpoint, s0(A)=1s_0(A)=1s0​(A)=1 for every A>0A>0A>0. Equation (5) is then independent of both target degree and target density. Different nonconstant target profiles sharing the same local ray texture give the same full probe Dirac operator, including every probe coefficient and spinor component. This is stronger than equality of one expectation value. For a parallel slack target ray, its transverse probe occupation jjj has source coefficient

μ sσ(A)DMDj β,Dj=(j+dd).(6) \mu\,s_\sigma(A)\frac{D_M}{D_j}\,\beta, \qquad D_j=\binom{j+d}{d}. \tag{6} μsσ​(A)Dj​DM​​β,Dj​=(dj+d​).(6)

The contraction identity used in (6) is the first sum in equation (14) of the self-mass note. Both the spatial kinetic operator and these coefficient sectors must be retained when computing a physical response.

The target responds at second order in the small probe amplitude. That backreaction comes from the same energy; fixing the target in the linear probe equation is the stated perturbative limit. It does not remove the reciprocal variation of the full action. Nor does a weak field perturbation automatically represent a separately normalized massive particle.

Root interaction and the vacuum limit

For positive coherent currents in two different grades, the contribution of a shared order is

μhk,d[A2+B2+2ABτk+σ2−σ].(7) \mu h_{k,d}\left[\sqrt{A^2+B^2+2AB\tau^k+\sigma^2}-\sigma\right]. \tag{7} μhk,d​[A2+B2+2ABτk+σ2​−σ].(7)

Unshared orders contain only their own grade's current. At σ=0\sigma=0σ=0, subtracting the two isolated energies gives a nonpositive cross term. At alignment it vanishes; at other orientations it generally remains. In particular, the order-zero root is additive for positive currents. It must not be confused with the quartic action's uniform order-zero cross term in (2).

The endpoint also has a physical limitation. Equation (5) remains nonzero as a positive target density tends to zero, if the probe amplitude is taken to zero first. Reversing those limits gives the isolated probe problem. A globally weak tail cannot simply be assigned the same physical meaning as a finite normalized particle while ignoring this noncommuting vacuum limit.

Positive σ\sigmaσ restores decay of (5) when the target disappears. It also restores dependence on the actual target density and changes the spatial self-source. Near-equal weighted matrix elements do not establish equality of every spatially localized probe response. The separate spatial calculation retains this distinction.

Small self-source requires a separate interpretation. At a scalar-null coherent spinor with positive canonical density ρ\rhoρ, the quartic source vanishes, but its occupied real derivative has a nonzero singular value 2κDNρ2\kappa D_N\rho2κDN​ρ. Cancellation between spinor components therefore does not suppress every response. A small field norm, a small scalar balance and a small microscopic coefficient are different mechanisms.

The existing odd-depth scalar-null classes also annihilate every current in (1) when all coefficient spinors belong to the same class. They provide no self-binding or selected small nonzero mass, and a general external scalar source can drive complementary components. These checks do not yet identify the neutrino's electric or weak channels, or derive a suppression scale for the assigned depth-five states.

Verification

The later localized response calculation extends the comparison to solved spatial target profiles under one binding and self-mass action. A finite normalized lower-grade probe has identical cross energy, complete probe source and reciprocal translation force for targets with the same profile and different larger grades. Their full shape responses and frequency-proportional inertias still differ. The test does not identify the probe as an observed particle or settle a full encounter.

A different forward calculation uses the actual scalar master projection and balanced normal populations. Their stationary scalar contribution cancels while their moving polarization produces a common geometric response. It retains finite normal kinetic gaps, complete probe self-interaction and ordinary tangent rotations, without replacing the raw projection weights by shell weights.
