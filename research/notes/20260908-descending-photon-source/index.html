Descending photon source

Updated September 8, 2026

Summary: A higher-sector field sources a photon mode by projection over its bound coordinates while retaining normal propagation, with reciprocal feedback fixed by the same action.

Start with the higher-dimensional field pattern

There is one infinite physical manifold and one nested chain of ordinary macroscopic spatial submanifolds. A higher-sector particle may determine an interaction through its complete spatial field pattern. A photon response is then a particular projection of that interaction. This construction does not assume that the lower particle's bound depth sets the strength of the source.

All retained amplitudes below belong to the single field Ψ∞\Psi_\inftyΨ∞​. There are no mediator particles. A separation into target and photon amplitudes is a mode decomposition to be derived and normalized, not a postulate of independent physical fields.

The photon geometry separates two bound spatial coordinates from the perpendicular phase directions. In a fixed product chart of a containing depth b≥3b\geq3b≥3, write

x=(u,y),u∈E≃R2,y∈N≃Rb−2.(1) x=(u,y),\qquad u\in E\simeq\mathbb R^2, \qquad y\in N\simeq\mathbb R^{b-2}. \tag{1} x=(u,y),u∈E≃R2,y∈N≃Rb−2.(1)

This is a relation to the photon plane. It does not divide the higher particle's dimensions into different physical kinds. A product chart, fixed frame, and product measure are assumptions for the formulas below; curved or moving frames require their Jacobians and connection terms.

This reciprocal pairing permits a source-led, downward calculation. It does not require equal responses to unequal preparations, occupations, or incident fluxes. A genuinely time-dependent background can exchange energy with perturbations and requires its time-dependent variational equations. Reciprocity of the real Hessian is not automatically a time-reversal symmetry of a scattering experiment.

When coordinates depend on the field, vary the inclusions, frame, and kinetic terms too. Holding an action-dependent projector fixed can omit part of the source. When a selected higher solution already contains photon components, expand around that complete solution rather than counting those components twice.
