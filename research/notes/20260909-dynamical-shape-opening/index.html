Dynamical opening of spatial shape channels

Updated September 9, 2026

Summary: The full Dirac response can rotate a statically inaccessible higher-dimensional shape into a lower-sector source, producing a derived low-frequency barrier with both objects' polarizations retained.

A shape can fail to match statically and still respond dynamically

For the spatial moment interaction, a source in a higher sector acts on a lower one through its restricted shape tensor. A mixed shape with one direction perpendicular to the lower sector has zero restriction. The full field can nevertheless move that pattern into another spatial shape which does have a nonzero restriction.

This note calculates that process using the self-consistent Dirac profiles and complete quadrupole response in Dirac shape response. All coordinates belong to the same nested manifold of literal macroscopic spatial dimensions. The tensors label actual spatial patterns; their component counts do not introduce other physical dimensions.

The result is a conditional spatial coupling mechanism. It does not assign the chosen quadrupole to a photon, or identify its suppression with a measured neutrino rate.

Restriction can change the type of spatial shape

Include the lower object's response as well

Let χa,χb\chi_a,\chi_bχa​,χb​ be the two objects' own complete shape responses. For a weak cross term with coefficient ϵ\epsilonϵ multiplying their moment pairing, the leading response of the lower shape to a higher applied source hbh_bhb​ is

Both polarizations and both spatial spectra enter. The reverse response uses RT\mathcal R^TRT, obtained from the same real pair energy. Common physical rotations transform both circulation tensors, both shape bases and the inclusion together, leaving the physical prediction unchanged.

The coefficient ϵ\epsilonϵ here controls a perturbative contact expansion; it is not an independently fitted constant for each particle pair. At leading order one may use the isolated self-consistent profiles. Their static deformation changes (15) at the next order. A finite interaction requires the corresponding complete pair background, as illustrated by Self-consistent nested Dirac pair.

For a specified quadratic fluctuation energy, both responses can be solved exactly from

This gives reciprocal feedback and collective poles. Applying that finite quadratic model to a nonlinear physical pair requires its Hessian at the correct background; it is not supplied just by combining two isolated profiles at an arbitrary large contact coefficient.
