Normal propagation of spatial Dirac modes

Updated September 10, 2026

Summary: Retaining the normal Dirac partner gives exact free-direction propagation, source-preserving boosts and a full moment-response equation for collective fluctuations.

A bound sector can propagate through a containing spatial direction

Choose bound coordinates x∈Rdx\in\mathbb R^dx∈Rd and one perpendicular coordinate yyy in a containing sector of the same nested physical manifold. The coordinate yyy is another ordinary macroscopic spatial direction. For the lower field it is a free direction relative to its bound spatial profile; it is not an internal or auxiliary dimension.

Use a Clifford representation appropriate to that containing space, with Hermitian α1,…,αd,αy,β\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_d,\alpha_y,\betaα1​,…,αd​,αy​,β. The containing representation may include several copies of a lower representation. Its field components and the mode decompositions below do not add further physical coordinates.

Let a normalized full spatial bound mode obey

Hbϕ=Mϕ,Hb=−iαi∂i+βm(x),M>0,∫ϕ†ϕ dx=1.(1) H_b\phi=M\phi,\qquad H_b=-i\alpha_i\partial_i+\beta\mathfrak m(x), \qquad M>0,\qquad\int\phi^\dagger\phi\,dx=1. \tag{1} Hb​ϕ=Mϕ,Hb​=−iαi​∂i​+βm(x),M>0,∫ϕ†ϕdx=1.(1)

MMM is this model's positive bound frequency. Its use as a rest parameter in the calculation does not identify it with a measured particle mass or replace the actual electron orbit. The full bound field, including its lower radial components, is the one derived in Rotation-neutral Dirac fields.

The omitted partner carries the normal propagation

The scalar source also gives an exact zero-mode condition. Since {Hb,β}=2m(x)I\{H_b,\beta\}=2\mathfrak m(x)I{Hb​,β}=2m(x)I, a normalized bound eigenfield obeys

M⟨β⟩=∫m(x) ϕ†ϕ dx.(5a) \boxed{M\langle\beta\rangle =\int\mathfrak m(x)\,\phi^\dagger\phi\,dx.} \tag{5a} M⟨β⟩=∫m(x)ϕ†ϕdx.​(5a)

For the present nonnegative quadratic scalar profiles, the right side is strictly positive for every nonzero square-integrable field. There is therefore no localized zero-frequency bound mode in this source class. The positive branch has ⟨β⟩>0\langle\beta\rangle>0⟨β⟩>0 and its normal dispersion in (4) remains massive. The massless zero mode required by the photon construction needs its own source dynamics. Transverse localization and preservation of normal propagation alone do not establish that zero mode. Equation (5a) concerns the bound eigenproblem (1). The full real-linear fluctuation operator also varies the source and can have collective zero modes, including the translation mode. Their normal dispersion and any identification with a photon require that complete fluctuation calculation.

A normal boost preserves the scalar binding source

Collective propagation requires variation of the full source

The normal partner solves the frozen bound eigenproblem exactly. A collective fluctuation also changes the binding source. For a finite moment interaction this additional response can be retained without restricting the field to two normal amplitudes.

Let bA(x)b_A(x)bA​(x) be the full spatial source vectors at a stationary profile, and let κ\kappaκ be the real symmetric curvature matrix of its moments. Write the extra, real-linear source derivative as

δNextra[η]=∑A,BbAκABhB,hA=2Re⁡⟨bA,η⟩.(14) \delta N_{\rm extra}[\eta] =\sum_{A,B}b_A\kappa_{AB}h_B, \qquad h_A=2\operatorname{Re}\langle b_A,\eta\rangle. \tag{14} δNextra​[η]=A,B∑​bA​κAB​hB​,hA​=2Re⟨bA​,η⟩.(14)

Equations (16)–(20) supply a full-source route to the normal dispersion of collective modes. Identifying a transverse massless photon still requires solving that spectrum for the appropriate spatial solution. A zero frequency at q=0q=0q=0 alone does not establish a linear lightlike branch or its physical polarization.

The same construction applies to the nested pair under its specified projected-channel assumptions. Use both full spatial perturbation spaces and the curvature of their common energy. For the second-moment shapes, its block is

where IabI_{ab}Iab​ is the actual lower-to-higher spatial inclusion and R†\mathcal R^\daggerR† uses the Frobenius moment norm. Center variations supply the additional dipole blocks. The source column operator is block diagonal over the two projected channels; the off-diagonal curvature in (21) carries their mutual response. Equations (17)–(18) consequently retain both objects' full spatial variations and repeated source feedback. For finite pair coupling the background must be the jointly readjusted pair, so these equations cannot use two unchanged isolated profiles.

Two-frequency spatial Dirac currents combines both normal partners into an exact nonlinear field with a changing spatial current and a constant scalar source. It retains the complete boost phase and the response of its periodic background.

Periodic spatial source response extends these full resolvents to a two-frequency background, retaining the frequency sidebands of its source and both ordinary and conjugate field variations.

Collective translation waves solves the source-carrying dipole branch for selected even-dimensional chiral fields. The complete source and normal partners give exact inertia and stiffness; at bound depth two the two displacement polarizations have low-frequency speed one-half in Dirac units. A collective zero alone therefore does not identify a photon in this scalar model.

Verification

normal_dirac_transport_checks.py constructs the containing Clifford representation and directly differentiates the boosted spatial field at depths three, five and six. It checks the complete bound-plus-normal Dirac equation, the pointwise scalar source, the same self-consistent moment coefficient, density/current transformation, and the polarization-dependent centroid. Independent radial integration checks (12), and reconstruction checks (4)–(5).

Independent Cartesian Hermite regulators at depths three and five verify the full centered nonlinear source derivative, both normal resolvents, the driven response (18) and the complete characteristic identity (20). The projected squared-coordinate operators retain their full-basis matrix elements. These are finite-regulator algebra checks, not converged collective particle spectra; the full phase zero mode is retained.

The exact uniform boost does not cure a bound-profile instability. The radial stability limits of the scalar branch remain those in the profile note, and spatially varying envelopes require the additional response terms described in the envelope reduction.
