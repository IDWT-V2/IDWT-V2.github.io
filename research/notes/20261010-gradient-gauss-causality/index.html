A Positive Gradient Gauss Law Separates Static Confinement from Signal Propagation

Updated October 10, 2026

The fourth-order source window distinguishes depths three and four if the complete constrained source energy is positive and proportional to the inverse of the spatial bi-Laplacian. That dimensional result does not require a tensor Gauss law. An ordinary gauge law can produce the same static energy and ordinary charge continuity. The explicit candidate below does, but its localized physical response has a spacelike tail despite finite-speed homogeneous waves.

These calculations concern a prospective collective connection of the one field, linearized in one color direction. They introduce no exchanged particle. A full non-Abelian normal-frame action and an actual source reduction have not been derived. The linear result identifies a limitation that a completion would have to address.

A Complementary Positive Spectral Bound

Neutral Motion in the Tensor-Gauss Alternative

An Abelian double-divergence Gauss law conserves dipole moment. A neutral composite can translate rigidly because its dipole changes by translation times total charge, which is zero. That removes one overly strong mobility objection.

It does not automatically allow arbitrary internal motion. For the retained determinant color singlet,

⟨∣∑iriTi∣2⟩=23∑i<j∣ri−rj∣2. \left\langle\left|\sum_i r_iT_i\right|^2\right\rangle =\frac23\sum_{i<j}|r_i-r_j|^2. ⟨​i∑​ri​Ti​​2⟩=32​i<j∑​∣ri​−rj​∣2.

Rigid translation preserves this norm; dilation multiplies it by the squared dilation factor. Zero expectation of an individual color dipole operator is weaker than its annihilating the state. A direct collection of Abelian tensor constraints would restrict these internal changes unless other same-field degrees of freedom carry the compensating moments. A true non-Abelian covariant constraint can have different conservation laws and must be derived independently.
