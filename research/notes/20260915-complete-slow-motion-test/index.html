Research / Mass and normalization

Testing slow geometric motion with every centered deformation

Updated September 15, 2026

conditional calculation

The radial geometry has a nonzero wave source. Allowing it to move is a possible alternative to static force balance, but the proposed motion must respect the approximation used to calculate it. Including the actual moving spatial basis gives a precise test: the simplest first-order slow action predicts motion far outside its own range of validity.

This result applies to the stated Berry-only action and the tested symmetric profile. It does not rule out faster same-field dynamics, other shapes or a complete sustaining kinetic action. No formal velocity reported here is asserted to be a physical trajectory.

The small signed partner supplies the strongest constraint

The carrier has chiral form HP=(0T†T0)H_P=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&T^\dagger\\T&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)HP​=(0T​T†0​). At the real reference state, a real source has zero matrix element between the two opposite small signed partners. An imaginary source generally has a nonzero matrix element there. Thus that partner's contribution to the real–imaginary curvature is zero, even though it imposes the strongest condition (3). Its two roles cannot be inferred from curvature alone.

The result identifies what the candidate action lacks: a self-consistent sustaining response capable of keeping the occupied motion in the regime being modeled. The complete particle's positive inertia is still a joint energy-and-momentum question. The signed modes in (3) have no assigned particle–antiparticle interpretation.
