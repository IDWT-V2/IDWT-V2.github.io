What the combined result establishes

The same reconstructed action now supports a localized spatial fixed point, the selected simplex carrier frequencies, their exact canonical inertia, and a finite common-probe identity. The pair comparison retains both polarizations and reciprocal translation variation. Its continuing limitations concern the actual physical carrier, observed interaction channels, full encounter evolution and complete spatial stability.

In particular, the oscillator labels still need to arise as modes of the same physical field. Their tensor interpretation and complete spacetime transformation law cannot be inferred from the dimension count alone. The inverse action is a concrete target for that derivation, not evidence that its premises are already realized by particles.

The later rest-frame carrier calculation provides an explicit covariant tensor connection from the field's own current. Its additional kinetic term preserves these linear calculations on zero-rotation-moment rays and preserves the finite common-probe identity for such targets. For complex polarized rays it changes the kinetic form and produces a defective principal response. Those new terms cannot be omitted when assessing that completed action's general stability.

The later higher angular response gives the complete polynomial angular block classification at even depth and tests additional electron and muon blocks. Its finite spectra retain their stated radial and angular limits. The trace-current comparison also identifies a secular high-momentum response that real frequencies alone do not exclude. Adding a positive trace contraction removes that specific degeneracy and yields a positive conserved norm for the complete constant far-field operator. Its localized frequency and common-probe identities survive, while its changed Hessian requires its own tests.
