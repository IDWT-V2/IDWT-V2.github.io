Research / Mass and normalization

Size variation of a common positive response

Updated September 22, 2026

conditional calculation

A shared length among the harmonics of one depth must follow from the same spatial action as their standing frequencies. The fixed-norm response result gives a frequency shift, but it does not yet determine a length. This note varies that length for a minimal local response whose source, stiffness and kinetic normalization all scale together. The calculation supplies a necessary test for that class of candidate actions, not a general theorem about every same-field support mechanism.

One scale-covariant sector model

Work at one physical depth d≥2d\geq2d≥2. Let a common reference region or profile family have length LLL. It need not be a hard-walled cavity or a compactified dimension. For a normalized spatial pattern write

ψi,L(x)=Qi L−d/2ui(x/L),∫∣ui(y)∣2 ddy=1.(1) \psi_{i,L}(x)=\sqrt{Q_i}\,L^{-d/2}u_i(x/L),\qquad \int |u_i(y)|^2\,d^dy=1. \tag{1} ψi,L​(x)=Qi​​L−d/2ui​(x/L),∫∣ui​(y)∣2ddy=1.(1)

The canonical norm QiQ_iQi​ can differ between solutions. At this stage uiu_iui​ is held fixed during the dilation; allowing it to relax can change the result. Assume that the positive-frequency bare branch has

E0,i(Qi,L)=QicμiL,μi>0,(2) E_{0,i}(Q_i,L)=Q_i\frac{c\mu_i}{L},\qquad \mu_i>0, \tag{2} E0,i​(Qi​,L)=Qi​Lcμi​​,μi​>0,(2)

Here ULf(x)=L−d/2f(x/L)U_L f(x)=L^{-d/2}f(x/L)UL​f(x)=L−d/2f(x/L) is unitary between the reference and physical L2L^2L2 spaces. The source shape σi\sigma_iσi​ includes the actual spatial polarization contractions if the action supplies them. Its arbitrary normalization cannot be absorbed separately for each mode.

The depth-five, six and ten extrema have positive multipliers, respectively one-third, one-half and three-quarters of the bare frequency, but remain size maxima. Energy per norm and the canonical frequency differ because the eliminated response is quadratic in QiQ_iQi​; the positive energy at those maxima does not repair their negative size curvature.

For the positive response alone, unconstrained amplitude does not repair the problem. At fixed LLL, ∂Qi2Ei=−g2Ki/Ld−2<0\partial_{Q_i}^2E_i=-g^2K_i/L^{d-2}<0∂Qi​2​Ei​=−g2Ki​/Ld−2<0. A specified conserved norm removes that radial direction from the constrained tangent space; it does not select its value or supply a positive size curvature.

What an additional stabilizer must accomplish

If the complete same-field energy contains a further term Gi(Qi,L)G_i(Q_i,L)Gi​(Qi​,L), a stable common length LdL_dLd​ must satisfy, for every occupied harmonic at that depth,

∂LGi∣Ld=AiLd2−pBiLdp+1,∂L2(Ei+Gi)∣Ld>0.(9) \left.\partial_LG_i\right|_{L_d} =\frac{A_i}{L_d^2}-\frac{pB_i}{L_d^{p+1}}, \qquad \left.\partial_L^2(E_i+G_i)\right|_{L_d}>0. \tag{9} ∂L​Gi​∣Ld​​=Ld2​Ai​​−Ldp+1​pBi​​,∂L2​(Ei​+Gi​)​Ld​​>0.(9)

The same GGG must also enter the canonical multiplier and the complete energy-momentum response. A mode-independent geometric cost is not automatically sufficient: its one size derivative must equal the mode-dependent right side of (8) for all occupied harmonics. A coupling or stiffness coefficient independently chosen for each harmonic would hide the missing mechanism. A different spatial order, a fixed physical gap, nonlinear profile relaxation or a constrained off-diagonal field equation can change the scaling argument; each must be calculated from its own full action. The localized current construction, for example, uses an auxiliary spatial fixed-point functional whose value is not additional particle energy, so (4) is not its size law.

The next constructive task is to derive a common stabilizing term and solve its profile and norm equations together with (5) and (9), then check the full Hessian and reciprocal pair response. The present result shows exactly why a positive quadratic response and a common length symbol alone cannot complete that task.
