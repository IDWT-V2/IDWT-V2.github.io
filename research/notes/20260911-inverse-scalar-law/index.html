Reconstructing a scalar interaction from spatial particle fields

Updated September 11, 2026

Summary: Complete spatial fields determine a scalar interaction only when their full residual is scalar and equal-current points require the same law; unchanged Jacobi pairs cannot share the assigned simplex frequencies through such a law, while nonlinear dressed controls reconstruct both the common action and its coupling derivative.

Keep the causal kinetic equation and invert the interaction

The quadratic inverse construction matches the complete simplex frequencies, but its unrestricted polynomial dynamics fail the curved finite-propagation test. A different inverse problem keeps the original first-order spatial Dirac operator D\mathscr DD and asks for one real local scalar law:

i∂τψ=Dψ+F(J)βψ,J=ψ†βψ.(1) \boxed{ i\partial_\tau\psi=\mathscr D\psi+F(J)\beta\psi,\qquad J=\psi^\dagger\beta\psi. } \tag{1} i∂τ​ψ=Dψ+F(J)βψ,J=ψ†βψ.​(1)

Here β†=β\beta^\dagger=\betaβ†=β, β2=I\beta^2=Iβ2=I and {D,β}=0\{\mathscr D,\beta\}=0{D,β}=0. The physical geometry, connection, measure and canonical field normalization are fixed inputs. The scalar current is not identified with electric charge. All complex spinor components remain in (1).

For a smooth real FFF, this is a semilinear first-order equation. As long as its smooth solutions exist and remain locally bounded, the difference of two solutions has the original Dirac principal part and a locally Lipschitz source. The usual exterior-cone energy estimate and Gronwall inequality preserve the Dirac domain of dependence. This does not prove global existence or stable particle solutions, but it avoids replacing the kinetic equation by an unrestricted sixth-order spatial dispersion.

Equation (1) is a candidate class of same-field laws. Its inverse conditions apply to arbitrary spatial polarizations. The explicit Jacobi comparison below is a narrower fixed-profile test on physical CP3 with O(2)\mathcal O(2)O(2) and one common radius RRR. No dimensions are compactified, and the radius is not selected. Sector nesting does not require one particle field to be a restriction or constituent of another.

Frequency and rest energy still need separate identification

For a stationary solution of (1), (8) gives

ωQ−E=∫[JF(J)−U(J)] dV.(17) \boxed{\omega Q-E =\int\left[J F(J)-U(J)\right]\,dV.} \tag{17} ωQ−E=∫[JF(J)−U(J)]dV.​(17)

Thus the inverse frequency target, the total energy of a localized particle and an observed rest mass must be connected by the same physical normalization and dynamics. The current calculation reconstructs and tests a candidate scalar law; it does not establish that final physical identification.
