Finite biased support and the mode-selection boundary

Updated September 28, 2026

conditional calculation

The positive particle-sourced response fails spectral transparency when it has no independent bias. A natural extension is to add one common bias per depth. This note determines exactly what that buys and where it stops.

Orthogonality with a common bias

Let A>0A>0A>0 be the response stiffness, SiS_iSi​ the quadratic source of the selected mode iii, and bbb a depth-wide bias. Eliminating the response gives the source energy

Ueff,i=−12⟨Si+b,A−1(Si+b)⟩.(1) U_{\mathrm{eff},i} =-\frac12\langle S_i+b,A^{-1}(S_i+b)\rangle. \tag{1} Ueff,i​=−21​⟨Si​+b,A−1(Si​+b)⟩.(1)

Under a field-amplitude variation, SiS_iSi​ scales as ∣a∣2|a|^2∣a∣2. Vanishing of the resulting frequency shift requires

⟨Si,A−1b⟩=−⟨Si,A−1Si⟩.(2) \boxed{\langle S_i,A^{-1}b\rangle =-\langle S_i,A^{-1}S_i\rangle.} \tag{2} ⟨Si​,A−1b⟩=−⟨Si​,A−1Si​⟩.​(2)

Writing ui=A−1/2Siu_i=A^{-1/2}S_iui​=A−1/2Si​ and q=A−1/2bq=A^{-1/2}bq=A−1/2b, these are linear conditions

This is a common bias for the depth, not an independently fitted bias for each selected mode. It can therefore be a legitimate inverse construction if a single action derives both the source vectors and the bias.

Stationarity is not amplitude stability

The orthogonality condition cancels the first amplitude derivative, but the eliminated positive response has a fixed second derivative. Write s=∣a∣2s=|a|^2s=∣a∣2, so the selected source is suis u_isui​, and subtract the constant energy of the bias alone. The response contribution is

d2ΔUi(∣a∣2)da2∣a=1=−4∥ui∥2.(11) \left.\frac{d^2\Delta U_i(|a|^2)}{da^2}\right|_{a=1} =-4\|u_i\|^2. \tag{11} da2d2ΔUi​(∣a∣2)​​a=1​=−4∥ui​∥2.(11)

Thus the bias makes the normalized amplitude stationary but does not stabilize it. Within the response sector alone, the stationary point is a strict local maximum in the amplitude direction. A viable joint action needs a bare-field or constrained contribution whose positive curvature exceeds 4∥ui∥24\|u_i\|^24∥ui​∥2 for every selected mode in the common normalization. This is the amplitude block of the full Schur-complement condition, not a complete instability proof for a model that includes such an additional term.

This is a boundary of the common-bias transparency calculation. A physical action could select occupations through a nonlinear law, a finite band, a conserved charge sector, or a boundary condition. The failed cancellation does not establish that the next mode is absent: it could have a shifted frequency, remain unoccupied, be unstable, or describe an exotic branch. Continue that branch under the same action and test its stability and observable response. The bias and any occupation rule require a same-field derivation; choosing the bias after listing observed modes fits the spectrum.

There is also a simple incompatibility when two sources are proportional. If uj=αuiu_j=\alpha u_iuj​=αui​, equations (3) demand both ⟨ui,q⟩=−∥ui∥2\langle u_i,q\rangle=-\|u_i\|^2⟨ui​,q⟩=−∥ui​∥2 and α⟨ui,q⟩=−α2∥ui∥2\alpha\langle u_i,q\rangle=-\alpha^2\|u_i\|^2α⟨ui​,q⟩=−α2∥ui​∥2. For a nonzero source, these agree only when α=1\alpha=1α=1. A common bias cannot transparently support arbitrary rescalings of one source direction.

What this adds to the support problem

The result leaves three conditional routes:

  1. an independently present background with a derived bias and a finite mode-selection law;
  2. a constrained or indefinite response for which the positive overlap argument no longer applies; or
  3. a nonlinear same-field support whose stationary equations generate the orthogonality conditions without mode-by-mode tuning.

The first route is algebraically consistent for a finite selected set, but it introduces a new physical obligation: derive the bias and the selection mechanism together. It does not establish the mass operator, a complete particle, or a neutrino interaction rate.

Verification

biased_source_compatibility_checks.py solves the exact finite Gram systems and tests the next unselected mode at every maintained depth. The normal and optimized runs each pass 40 conditions. The selected exact report contains the source vectors, solved bias coefficients, and unselected-mode residuals.

biased_source_infinite_checks.py performs the infinite even-harmonic sums independently at 50 and 80 decimal digits. Its normal and optimized runs each pass 48 convergence, compatibility and amplitude-curvature conditions. The selected infinite-space report contains the converged norms, coefficients, condition estimates and next-mode residuals. The source components are algebraic controls; they are not measured particle densities.
