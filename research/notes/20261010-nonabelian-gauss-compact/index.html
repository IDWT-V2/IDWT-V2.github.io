Compact Non-Abelian Gauss Data Delimit the Flux-Confinement Result

Updated October 10, 2026

The linear separation bound is valid for its commuting connection sector. Allowing the full connection of the proposed action changes the prescribed-source variational problem: commutators can carry the required color density inside each source core. An explicit finite-energy construction gives a separation-independent upper bound for two opposite compact sources. A universal positive linear lower bound is therefore unavailable in this larger classical domain.

This is a constructive limitation of that action, not a disproof of the sector geometry or of a quantum color theory. The Hodge gate continues to remove an independent source at the inactive occupied depths. A connection induced by a more restrictive geometric reduction, a fully dynamical matter source, or a quantum fundamental-source sector defines a different problem and needs its own derivation.

Opposite Fixed Cores Defeat a Universal Linear Lower Bound

For each core, keep the same shape, radius, source strength and constitutive parameters. Reverse α\alphaα in the second core to reverse its source. Translate the two cores to separation R>2R>2R>2, so their supports are disjoint. Every local term in the complete Hamiltonian is then additive, and combined Gauss has no cross-commutator support. Hence

There is no displacement flux through the plane separating the balls. This upper bound excludes a positive TRT RTR lower bound in the unrestricted classical prescribed-source domain for these fixed sources. The original commuting theorem remains valid because these fields do not belong to its commuting domain.

The trials are admissible constrained Hamiltonian data, not exact static solutions. A variational upper bound requires admissible data; it does not require the trial to be a stationary solution. Global evolution in the degenerate vacuum is still unproved. The calculation also does not include the source's own kinetic and supporting energy. If localized master-field matter of fixed shape can be supplied with finite local energy, that energy must be included and checked; it has not been assumed away as a particle result here.

Center Charge and Classical Mean Color Are Different

The connection variables are adjoint and are unchanged by the SU3 center. A fundamental vector is multiplied by e2πi/3e^{2\pi i/3}e2πi/3, which no tensor product of center-neutral adjoints cancels. Therefore this classical mean-density construction does not screen a quantum fundamental's triality or construct a quantum gauge-invariant isolated quark. The source matrix HHH is adjoint-covariant data and contains less information than a fundamental quantum source state.

Ordinary integrated color components are gauge dependent; vanishing exterior field strength and finite Hamiltonian energy are the relevant verified statements here. Historical classical-source work already warns that connection contributions can change naive color screening conclusions. Sikivie and Weiss's primary paper gives examples; a subsequent primary analysis emphasizes the gauge interpretation. Their ordinary-dielectric examples are comparisons, not the derivation of this quartic-dielectric trial. The first preprint's text was accessed in full; the subsequent paper was available only as its publisher abstract, so its detailed arguments have not been used.

The same Hodge operator is recomputed at all occupied depths. It is active at three/four and zero at two/five/six/ten. At the inactive depths with a surviving normal algebra, finite energy still requires D=0D=0D=0, and the declared action still constrains its source to zero. The checker and report preserve 846 Gauss and energy checks across eight cases, plus 18 Hodge dependency checks. They verify actual SU3 matrices, source direction and normalization, divergence, complete curvature, positivity, independent shape integrals, fixed-source amplitude variations, disjoint-core Gauss and every occupied gate.
