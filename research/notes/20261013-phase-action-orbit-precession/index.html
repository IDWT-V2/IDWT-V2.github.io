A Forward Phase-Action Derivation of Projective Orbit Precession

Updated October 13, 2026

Result and Physical Scope

A common classical collective action admits the maintained signed-petal trajectory as an exact solution. Its geometric magnetic term is derived by eliminating a conserved phase, rather than appended as a separately driven precession. The result connects an actual position curve on the candidate six-dimensional CP3 to an explicit action and its conserved quantities.

The action is a candidate effective reduction, not a derived consequence of the complete IDWT master equation. Its physical collective phase, kinetic coefficient, restoring source and fixed-norm sector are premises. The construction does not identify a coefficient vector with the spatial Dirac spinor bundle. Neither physical electric charge, particle mass nor an integer orbital lock is inferred merely from the existence of this solution.

The spatial coordinates remain ordinary dimensions, and q(t) is deterministic. The phase variable is a field phase, not an additional spatial direction. No probability distribution of electron positions is used.

1. Why Orientation Holonomy Alone Is Insufficient

The preceding transport calculation moves a tangent vector along an already specified orbit. A minimal director action, for example,

admits parallel transport D_t e=0 when prepared with zero covariant director velocity. Its director equation is I D_t²e=lambda e; that preparation has lambda=0. On this branch the director term contributes no orbital force. It explains orientation transport but does not derive a precessing position curve. A dynamical coupling to the geometric connection is needed for that.

8. Scientific Status and Verification

The forward derivation establishes an exact precessing position trajectory for a common classical action, with its connection term and rate supplied by a conserved physical phase. The weighted scalar construction shows one way to retain uniform outer extension with finite reduced action. Both are conditional mathematical results.

The remaining physical derivation must select the phase action, realize the fixed-source overlap as a closed reciprocal same-field reduction, justify the field carrier and kinetic weighting, and determine the occupied states and their observable response. Radial persistence is now proved within the collective action; full-field electron persistence remains open. Exactly closed petal solutions and nearby bounded nonclosing solutions both occur. Which motions are physically admissible must be determined through their interactions and measured response; exact integer locking is not a prerequisite of the radial-persistence result.

The construction supplies conditional dynamical support for the sector picture: the proposed six-dimensional electron geometry admits actual precessing trajectories with a local-overlap restoring potential and radial trapping. The collective reduction and uniform-extension law still require a common same-field realization. The fixed-source calculation does not establish how a chemical bond changes the trajectory.
