Causal constraint on reconstructed simplex dynamics

Updated September 11, 2026

Summary: An exact commutator on the actual curved spatial geometry proves that the reconstructed all-band polynomial dynamics have no finite propagation speed, while leaving open causal nonlinear dynamics with selected simplex particle frequencies.

What the inverse frequency match does not settle

The inverse construction gives local Hermitian equations with exact frequencies ±μS(n,2m)\pm\mu S(n,2m)±μS(n,2m) on their complete first Dirac pair. Their full canonical norm is conserved. However, every member of that polynomial class fails a finite-propagation test when used as a fixed equation for all spatial modes.

This conclusion follows directly on the specified physical CPm\mathbb{CP}^mCPm geometry with line connection O(m−1)\mathcal O(m-1)O(m−1). It does not require a flat approximation, a positive-frequency projection or a group-velocity interpretation. The physical radius RRR is arbitrary and finite. These remain ordinary macroscopic spatial directions; no direction is compactified.

The result constrains an inverse dynamical ansatz. A nonlinear particle mass is not required to equal the frequency of every small disturbance of one fixed quadratic background. Causal dynamics that select several nonlinear particle states remain a different problem. No particle depth or assigned mass index is changed by this test.

What can and cannot change this conclusion

The original first-order operator has [D,t][\mathscr D,t][D,t] equal to local Clifford multiplication by dtdtdt, up to the convention for iii. Its norm is bounded by 1/R1/R1/R. This provides a direct curved-space control for the test above.

The same obstruction also applies to the direct insertion H=D+μβPm(X0)H=\mathscr D+\mu\beta P_m(X_0)H=D+μβPm​(X0​), even though that insertion keeps the original first-order kinetic term. On the test field ϕn\phi_nϕn​, its zero-form commutator is still (10) with b=Pmb=P_mb=Pm​; the ordinary Dirac commutator contributes only to the odd form. Thus promoting the polynomial mass coefficient to an operator on every spatial disturbance needs a causal completion as well. A coefficient obtained only on selected nonlinear stationary particle states is not covered by that promotion.

A fixed smooth multiplication potential cannot cancel (10); it commutes with ttt. In particular, adding the specified local scalar term g(ψ†βψ)βψg(\psi^\dagger\beta\psi)\beta\psig(ψ†βψ)βψ has vanishing linearization at the zero field. Its small-amplitude equation still has the same obstruction. For a smooth local nonlinearity F(ψ†βψ)βψF(\psi^\dagger\beta\psi)\beta\psiF(ψ†βψ)βψ, a nonzero F(0)F(0)F(0) adds only a multiplication term to that linearization.

Likewise, a fixed positive commuting kinetic metric in the inverse action gives iZf˙=ZHa,bfiZ\dot f=ZH_{a,b}fiZf˙​=ZHa,b​f and the same evolution if˙=Ha,bfi\dot f=H_{a,b}fif˙​=Ha,b​f. That normalization does not repair (15). A different same-field dynamical completion would need to change the propagation equation itself.

The scalar response calculation remains valid mathematics for its specified action. Its reciprocal coefficients and spatial residuals are useful inverse constraints, but this action cannot yet represent a fundamental causal particle interaction at unrestricted spatial frequencies.

A controlled effective reduction may retain the polynomial over a limited regime, provided its causal parent, retained modes, frequency range and approximation error are derived. A cutoff alone is not a proof of causal support. The local Dirac retarded response already illustrates how causal propagation can survive a same-field reduction when its full frequency dependence is kept. The instantaneous interaction test addresses a different source of acausality: an explicitly nonlocal instantaneous spatial potential.

Most importantly, (14) does not exclude a universal causal nonlinear equation whose distinct self-consistent particle solutions have selected simplex masses. That equation would have to generate the particle spectrum through its nonlinear spatial states and normalization, without imposing this unrestricted high-order dispersion on every disturbance.

The inverse scalar-law construction develops that alternative with the original Dirac kinetics. It tests the full spatial residual and the requirement that all particle candidates obey one function of the scalar current.

Verification

inverse_simplex_causality_checks.py checks exact finite differences for every root allocation at depths six and ten. Independent Jacobi polynomials and repeated application of the actual curved differential operator verify the full formula (10).

Physical curved quadrature and its refinement independently check the normalized recurrence. The paired matrix equation retains both frequency branches and verifies the complete norm lower bound. The original Dirac operator supplies the bounded local commutator control. Finite block contractions also check the range-partition estimate; the continuum proof is (2)–(5), not a conclusion drawn from those finite numerical examples.
