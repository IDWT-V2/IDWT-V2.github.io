A Fourth-Order Static Response Has a Three-and-Four Neutral-Pair Window

Updated October 10, 2026

A common static pole can distinguish the quark-assigned depths without inserting a separate dimensional coefficient. The result concerns a specific positive source-energy functional. It does not establish that the normal color connection has that pole, that its sources have the required mobility, or that higher sectors have no forces.

The coupled geometric wave action provides a local positive finite-speed completion of a fourth-order displacement stiffness. Its rotation and shear channels have different static poles. This distinction is central to a proposed color application.

6. The Source Constraint and Mobility Remain Physical Questions

Pretko's rank-two scalar-charge model, sections III A and IV, gives an established example in which a double-derivative Gauss constraint produces a fourth-order static source energy. It also conserves dipole moment and prevents isolated charges from moving freely through local processes. Those consequences are part of that model, not optional details to discard when borrowing its static pole. Its U(1) tensor theory is not an SU(3) sector derivation.

IDWT needs a same-field source constraint, a compatible normal-color current, complete reciprocal dynamics and the intended mobile field patterns. Positive elastic stiffness, a positive constrained source energy and the force between dynamical color patterns must be connected explicitly. The conditional dimensional window is useful evidence for investigating that construction, not an established derivation of confinement or the absence of all higher-sector forces.

Ordinary Gauss Response and Its Signal Cone

The gradient-electric Gauss comparison now realizes this positive static pole with ordinary charge continuity, without imposing dipole conservation. Its homogeneous waves have finite speed, but a conserved finite-energy quadrupole produces a nonzero gauge-invariant response outside their wave cone at every occupied depth. This candidate therefore does not yet provide a consistent physical color completion. A complementary finite positive ordinary-channel bound shows why merely adding ordinary gauge modes cannot generate a double infrared pole. The conditional dimensional window above remains distinct from these dynamical requirements.
