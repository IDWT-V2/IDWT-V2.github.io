47. Simultaneous commutant and its limited no-mixing theorem

For a generic pair with canonical E0(2)⊕E4(4)E_0^{(2)}\oplus E_4^{(4)}E0(2)​⊕E4(4)​ decomposition, consider the explicitly restricted class

[X,J1]=[X,J2]=0. [X,J_1]=[X,J_2]=0. [X,J1​]=[X,J2​]=0.

Its real dimension is six; the symmetric part is {aP0+bP4}\{aP_0+bP_4\}{aP0​+bP4​} (dimension two), and the skew part has dimension four. Every such commuting operator has P0XP4=P4XP0=0P_0XP_4=P_4XP_0=0P0​XP4​=P4​XP0​=0. The executable check constructs the Clifford pair, solves the linear commutator equations at an exact generic representative, and verifies these statements.

This is a simultaneous-commutant theorem. Covariance of a matrix-valued function X(J1,J2)X(J_1,J_2)X(J1​,J2​) under simultaneous rotations is a different condition: for example X=J1X=J_1X=J1​ is covariant but need not commute with J2J_2J2​. Consequently this calculation alone is not a classification of all covariant pair operators, nor a blanket prohibition of nonlinear or state-dependent coupling.
