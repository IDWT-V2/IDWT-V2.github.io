Spatial transport of a neutral doublet

Updated September 9, 2026

Summary: The two neutral polarizations of a balanced five-dimensional rotation carry a nonzero traceless geometric curvature when its physical null axis varies, with an exact spatial kinetic-energy decomposition.

A neutral mode can respond to changing spatial geometry

Rotation-neutral spatial modes identifies a two-component neutral subspace for the sum of two equal spatial rotation planes. This note derives how those modes respond when the rotation's physical null axis changes. Their geometric response is nonzero even though the balanced tangential rotation annihilates them.

The axis is an ordinary spatial direction in a five-dimensional tangent space of the one infinite manifold. Its orientation is described by a unit vector n∈S4n\in S^4n∈S4. This sphere is a set of possible directions, not an additional physical space. When n=n(x)n=n(x)n=n(x), its variation occurs over actual spatial positions xxx; all derivatives below are pulled back to those positions. The two complex amplitudes describe polarization of the same field Ψ∞\Psi_\inftyΨ∞​, not two extra spatial dimensions or mediator particles.

The explicit kinetic calculations use a flat Euclidean spatial chart and ordinary derivatives. A curved sector requires its metric and spin connection in the same projection.

This is a conditional projected-kinetic mechanism. It does not identify the resulting connection with the observed weak interaction or its trace with electric charge. The physical mode selection and electromagnetic current remain to be derived.

What this supplies to the coupling problem

The same higher geometric source can now support two precise statements: its balanced tangential rotation annihilates the doublet, while spatial changes of its null axis induce nonzero, noncommuting transport within it. This provides an explicit example of neutrality in one geometric operation coexisting with another response, without inserting an arbitrary second matrix to manufacture that distinction.

There are still essential physical selection questions. The zero doublet is the middle eigenspace of SSS, not its unique ground state. The scalar self-binding model admits neutral and nonneutral polarizations at the same energy. A physical constraint, isolated populated band or dynamical selection must therefore be derived before (12) closes the actual field problem. At finite gaps, omitted modes contribute corrections and possible transitions. The moving-mode equation retains their exact terms.

Furthermore, the full six-dimensional source contains a mixed contribution acting nontrivially on this doublet. Its absence from a photon response cannot be assumed. The descending source projection must identify the physical current. Zero determinant curvature in (8) is not a proof of zero electric charge, and nonzero su(2)\mathfrak{su}(2)su(2) curvature is not a calculation of weak chirality, coupling strength or scattering rate. Those identifications require the same field action and the occupied spatial particle solutions.
