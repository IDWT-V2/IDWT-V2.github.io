Persistent spatial shape barriers

Updated September 9, 2026

Summary: For orthogonal higher circulation, three spatial restriction maps classify permanent, linear and quadratic frequency barriers, with a Dirac example using one interaction coefficient.

Ask whether a shape stays inaccessible throughout the response

Dynamical shape opening shows that a higher spatial pattern can have no static restriction into the lower sector and still drive it after the higher field responds. Across the particle hierarchy, the next question is which shapes remain inaccessible, and how strongly the others open at low frequency.

Consider a higher even-dimensional sector with a specified orthogonal circulation tensor JJJ, so JT=−JJ^T=-JJT=−J and J2=−IJ^2=-IJ2=−I. The lower sector is a fixed spatial submanifold with local tangent inclusion III. Both belong to the one nested manifold of ordinary macroscopic spatial dimensions. Changing JJJ relative to this inclusion changes their physical relation; rotating both together only changes its coordinate description.

Use the normalized symmetric traceless spatial shape tensors of the full Dirac response. Write

R(C)=ITCI,K(C)=12[J,C],T=iK.(1) \mathcal R(C)=I^TCI,\qquad \mathcal K(C)=\frac12[J,C],\qquad T=i\mathcal K. \tag{1} R(C)=ITCI,K(C)=21​[J,C],T=iK.(1)

The lower output contains all symmetric shapes, including its breathing component. A higher traceless tensor need not remain traceless below. The tensor components label physical field patterns; they are not further physical dimensions or additional interaction particles.
