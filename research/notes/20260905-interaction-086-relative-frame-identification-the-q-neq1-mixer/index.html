86. Relative-frame identification: the q≠1q\neq1q=1 mixer is exactly a broken frame-rotation generator

Section 85 isolated the unwanted operator

Vz=∣1,0⟩⟨0,0∣+∣0,0⟩⟨1,0∣. V_z=|1,0\rangle\langle0,0|+|0,0\rangle\langle1,0|. Vz​=∣1,0⟩⟨0,0∣+∣0,0⟩⟨1,0∣.

The next representation-theoretic calculation identifies it exactly.

Let the two spin factors carry generators

JaO=12σa⊗I,JaH=12I⊗σa. J_a^{O}=\frac12\sigma_a\otimes I, \qquad J_a^{H}=\frac12I\otimes\sigma_a. JaO​=21​σa​⊗I,JaH​=21​I⊗σa​.

Define common and relative rotations

Ja=JaO+JaH,Ka=JaO−JaH. J_a=J_a^O+J_a^H, \qquad K_a=J_a^O-J_a^H. Ja​=JaO​+JaH​,Ka​=JaO​−JaH​.

In the coupled basis

∣1,1⟩, ∣1,0⟩, ∣1,−1⟩, ∣0,0⟩, |1,1\rangle,\ |1,0\rangle,\ |1,-1\rangle,\ |0,0\rangle, ∣1,1⟩, ∣1,0⟩, ∣1,−1⟩, ∣0,0⟩,

direct transformation gives

Kz=Vz. \boxed{K_z=V_z.} Kz​=Vz​.​

Thus the precise operator that appears whenever q≠1q\neq1q=1 is not merely an abstract singlet--triplet vector. It is the relative axial rotation of the observed and hidden spin frames.

86.1 The three relative rotations are the singlet--triplet vector

For all three axes,

Ka=JaO−JaH K_a=J_a^O-J_a^H Ka​=JaO​−JaH​

fails to commute with the locked-spin Casimir:

[J2,Ka]≠0. [J^2,K_a]\neq0. [J2,Ka​]=0.

By contrast the common rotations satisfy

[J2,Ja]=0. [J^2,J_a]=0. [J2,Ja​]=0.

Therefore the two vector operator classes have a direct geometric meaning:

  • JaJ_aJa​: common rotations of the already-locked frames;
  • KaK_aKa​: relative rotations that change their mutual identification.

The qqq ambiguity found earlier contains

−q−12Kz. \boxed{-\frac{q-1}{2}K_z.} −2q−1​Kz​.​

Hence

q≠1⟹the stationary orbit generator contains a relative-frame rotation. \boxed{q\neq1\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\text{the stationary orbit generator contains a relative-frame rotation}.} q=1⟹the stationary orbit generator contains a relative-frame rotation.​

86.2 Real six-dimensional check

At the aligned locked point,

Jlock=(0I−I0). J_{\rm lock} =\begin{pmatrix}0&I\\-I&0\end{pmatrix}. Jlock​=(0−I​I0​).

A common infinitesimal rotation has the real six-dimensional form

Ba+=(Ra00Ra), B_a^+ =\begin{pmatrix}R_a&0\\0&R_a\end{pmatrix}, Ba+​=(Ra​0​0Ra​​),

while a relative rotation is

Ba−=(Ra00−Ra). B_a^- =\begin{pmatrix}R_a&0\\0&-R_a\end{pmatrix}. Ba−​=(Ra​0​0−Ra​​).

Exact matrix calculation gives

[Ba+,Jlock]=0, \boxed{[B_a^+,J_{\rm lock}]=0,} [Ba+​,Jlock​]=0,​

but

[Ba−,Jlock]≠0. \boxed{[B_a^-,J_{\rm lock}]\neq0.} [Ba−​,Jlock​]=0.​

So the same common/relative distinction appears independently in the real vector representation and in the chiral four-spinor representation.

A common rotation belongs to the stabilizer of the selected frame link. A relative rotation moves the selected link orientation.

86.3 The scattering-lock Hessian gives the relative mode positive stiffness

The aligned quadratic lock operator derived earlier is

Klock=κ(I−I−II). K_{\rm lock} =\kappa \begin{pmatrix} I&-I\\ -I&I \end{pmatrix}. Klock​=κ(I−I​−II​).

For every three-vector vvv,

Klock(vv)=0, K_{\rm lock} \binom{v}{v}=0, Klock​(vv​)=0,

whereas

Klock(v−v)=2κ(v−v). \boxed{ K_{\rm lock} \binom{v}{-v} =2\kappa \binom{v}{-v}. } Klock​(−vv​)=2κ(−vv​).​

Thus the lock itself separates the frame motions into:

common mode: zero relative-lock cost,relative mode: stiffness 2κ. \boxed{ \text{common mode: zero relative-lock cost}, \qquad \text{relative mode: stiffness }2\kappa. } common mode: zero relative-lock cost,relative mode: stiffness 2κ.​

This is exactly the distinction between JaJ_aJa​ and KaK_aKa​.

86.4 Consequence for the qqq family

Section 85 gave

G~q=G~1+(q−1)(−12I+12Jz+Jz2−12Kz). \widetilde G_q =\widetilde G_1 +(q-1)\left(-\frac12I+\frac12J_z+J_z^2-\frac12K_z\right). Gq​=G1​+(q−1)(−21​I+21​Jz​+Jz2​−21​Kz​).

The only part of this deformation that changes the locked irreducible sector is

−q−12Kz, -\frac{q-1}{2}K_z, −2q−1​Kz​,

and KzK_zKz​ is precisely a positive-stiffness relative-frame mode of the scattering lock.

Therefore the desired q=1q=1q=1 generator is uniquely the member of the integral family with no excitation of the relative frame coordinate.

Conditionally on the stationary gross orbit being evaluated at the selected scattering-frame minimum rather than with an independently excited relative-frame normal mode,

q=1. \boxed{q=1.} q=1.​

This is substantially sharper than simply postulating singlet/triplet preservation: the forbidden mixer has now been identified with the very relative frame motion that the lock potential penalizes.

86.5 Remaining logical gap

One step still has to be justified before calling this unconditional.

A positive quadratic stiffness means the relative mode is energetically costly; it does not by itself prove that the stationary electron orbit has exactly zero excitation in that mode. A normal mode with nonzero frequency can in principle be excited.

The needed statement is therefore not

relative rotations do not exist, \text{relative rotations do not exist}, relative rotations do not exist,

but rather

the ground/stationary gross orbit is the zero-relative-mode solution of the selected frame-lock potential. \boxed{ \text{the ground/stationary gross orbit is the zero-relative-mode solution of the selected frame-lock potential}. } the ground/stationary gross orbit is the zero-relative-mode solution of the selected frame-lock potential.​

If the bound-state variational problem already defines the electron orbit as the minimum-energy stationary solution in the frame coordinate, this follows directly. If the orbit construction permits a persistent excited relative-frame mode, then q≠1q\neq1q=1 remains possible as an excited branch rather than as the ground locked orbit.
