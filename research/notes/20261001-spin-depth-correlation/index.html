Spatial Depth and the Three Spin Classes

Updated October 1, 2026

An explicit nonlinear spinor profile provides a possible connection between spatial depth and the spin classes in IDWT. Its complete spatial pattern transforms with spin one-half, and its integrated squared amplitude is finite at every maintained matter depth: three, four, five, six and ten. The same profile has a logarithmically divergent norm at depth two.

This is a concrete branch-specific correlation. It suggests that depth could affect which kinds of localized excitation are admissible. It does not yet explain why nature selects those excitations, or produce the spin-zero and spin-one particles assigned to depth two.

How This Relates to the Other Correlations

This is a third connection to investigate alongside the mass relationships and the electron orbit and observer map. Together they ask whether the same spatial framework can account for mass, orbital geometry and spin. Their evidential strength depends on which inputs they share and which outcomes follow independently.

Here the spinor premise already supplies half-angle rotation behavior. The additional result is the finite-norm threshold of a complete nonlinear spatial profile, which aligns with the assigned matter/boson depth division. That is a concrete consistency test, rather than three independently established experimental confirmations of IDWT. In particular, this flat Rd\mathbb R^dRd calculation is not yet a global solution on the proposed CP3\mathbb{CP}^3CP3 electron geometry; joining those constructions needs a compatible metric, profile and field equation.

The coordinate rotation and the angular matrix transform together. The whole solution family therefore carries exactly the spinor-label rotation law, rather than acquiring an extra orbital multiplet from its angular factor. At depths three, four, five, six and ten, its restriction to those observer rotations consists of spin-one-half copies.

This is a classical rotation property. It does not by itself establish fermionic quantum statistics or identify an observed particle. A full turn reverses the field amplitude; bilinear observables are unchanged.

Increasing depth alone does not change a direct spinor into an integer-spin field. A depth-two spinor also has half-angle behavior under rotations in its own plane. The useful new distinction comes from this profile's norm, not from a disappearance of spinor algebra in two dimensions.

The profile has finite norm and spin-one-half covariance, matching the spin requirement for the depth-five neutrino assignment. The same rule also works at neighboring depths three, four and six.

Nothing in this calculation makes depth five uniquely weakly interacting, neutral, unusually light or restricted to a fixed axis. Those properties need a coupling and frequency calculation. Using Dirac spinors here also does not settle whether physical neutrinos are Dirac or Majorana particles.

7. Relation to the Mass Operator

At fixed ggg, the profile condition gives

A=(dga)(d−1)/2,Q∝a. A=\left(\frac{d}{ga}\right)^{(d-1)/2}, \qquad Q\propto a. A=(gad​)(d−1)/2,Q∝a.

The family retains a continuous length scale. It does not select a discrete mass-frequency, the electron–muon harmonic relation, or the simplex spectrum. Its rotation result also needs rechecking when additional transforming mode coefficients are attached to the spatial profile.

The mathematical opportunity is specific: use a common field action to support this localized spinor family and determine whether scalar and vector collective modes are physically admissible at depth two. Such an action would need finite complete energy, stable evolution, selected frequencies, and the appropriate polarization constraints. The present calculation establishes the profile and rotation identities that this proposal can use.

8. Verification and Scope

The reproducible calculation and recorded results contain 69 successful checks: Clifford relations, full-profile derivatives and rotations at all six depths, radial integrals against their exact values, scalar/vector rotation algebra, orientation phases, vector polarization counts and simplex representation controls.

The analytic derivations above supply the scope of the identities; sampled matrix checks support their implementation. No time-evolution or stability test is included. The conclusion is an exact mathematical correlation for one nonlinear profile, together with candidate scalar and vector channels, rather than a completed depth-to-particle-spin law.
