Self-consistent nested Dirac pair

Updated September 9, 2026

Summary: A common scalar moment interaction determines both nested Dirac profiles and their relative deformation, with reciprocal source variation and no independent coupling coefficient for the pair.

Let both spatial profiles respond to one interaction

The single-profile calculation derives a scalar confining source from the full spatial Dirac field. Here the same interaction determines two profiles on nested spatial sectors. The higher field changes the lower source and the lower field changes the higher source; neither profile is held fixed during self-consistency.

Take Qp⊂Qp+qQ_p\subset Q_{p+q}Qp​⊂Qp+q​ and local flat coordinates x∈Rpx\in\mathbb R^px∈Rp, y∈Rqy\in\mathbb R^qy∈Rq in the containing sector. The distinction between xxx and yyy is relative to this particular lower partner. All p+qp+qp+q directions are ordinary, equivalent kinds of macroscopic spatial direction. For a six-dimensional field with a five-dimensional partner, this is a description of their nesting, not an inherent division of the six-dimensional particle's space.

Adopt orthogonal projected channels of the same field Ψ∞\Psi_\inftyΨ∞​, with the appropriate tangential Dirac kinetic terms, specified spatial measures and separately fixed unit norms. These are explicit assumptions of a projected scalar model. Nested reciprocity describes the required measures and inclusion maps; a probability-normalized extension over an infinite fiber cannot silently be replaced by a constant of unit amplitude. No additional interaction particle is introduced.

Specify the interaction below as a function of the projected channel densities. A coherent unprojected superposition also requires the transition-density terms of its full source. Integrated kinetic orthogonality alone does not remove those pointwise scalar cross terms. Connecting the projected model to that coherent field requires the corresponding source projection.

The calculation establishes stationary solutions of this projected model. It does not identify them as the actual electron orbit, a neutrino profile, or a stable two-particle bound state.

A closed stationary symmetry class

Consider real profiles whose scalar moments have the form

Mp=tpIp,Mp+q=(txIp00tyIq).(4) M_p=t_pI_p,\qquad M_{p+q}=\begin{pmatrix}t_xI_p&0\\0&t_yI_q\end{pmatrix}. \tag{4} Mp​=tp​Ip​,Mp+q​=(tx​Ip​0​0ty​Iq​​).(4)

Equations (1)–(3) imply

a=g0(tp+tx),b=g0ty.(5) \boxed{a=g_0(t_p+t_x),\qquad b=g_0t_y.} \tag{5} a=g0​(tp​+tx​),b=g0​ty​.​(5)

The lower Dirac operator has scalar mass mp+a∣x∣2m_p+a|x|^2mp​+a∣x∣2; the higher one has scalar mass mp+q+a∣x∣2+b∣y∣2m_{p+q}+a|x|^2+b|y|^2mp+q​+a∣x∣2+b∣y∣2. In particular, the coefficient along every shared spatial direction is the same for both fields. The higher field may acquire a different spatial width relative to its lower partner without assigning its directions different physical kinds.

This is a stationary symmetry class. Arbitrary complex perturbations can generate additional mixed spatial moments, so a stability calculation must include those responses rather than assuming that (4) closes all time-dependent motions.

Two-frequency fields in a readjusted nested pair extends the real stationary profiles to exact opposite-frequency components in both channels. Their currents can oscillate with independent clocks while both scalar sources retain the same self-consistency equations.

Energy and reciprocal variation provide independent checks

For each massless partner, a norm-preserving spatial dilation gives its kinetic expectation as twice its scalar source expectation. Summing the stationary equations yields

Ep+Ep+q=6Eint,E=56(Ep+Ep+q).(12) E_p+E_{p+q}=6\mathcal E_{\rm int},\qquad \boxed{\mathcal E=\frac56(E_p+E_{p+q}).} \tag{12} Ep​+Ep+q​=6Eint​,E=65​(Ep​+Ep+q​).​(12)

The total energy at the displayed scale is approximately 4.2961737654.2961737654.296173765. The full Dirac energy is not globally bounded below, so this identity does not establish a global ground state or nonlinear stability.

For independent variations of both partner fields, their cross Hessian is

δpδp+qE=g0tr⁡(IδMpITδMp+q).(13) \delta_p\delta_{p+q}\mathcal E =g_0\operatorname{tr} \left(I\delta M_pI^T\delta M_{p+q}\right). \tag{13} δp​δp+q​E=g0​tr(IδMp​ITδMp+q​).(13)

The same bilinear is obtained by differentiating either source in (3). It is the explicit reciprocal response of the two solved profiles. The general tensor form remains valid when their orientations and shape perturbations are restored; the stationary table uses the symmetry (4).

Normal propagation retains the deformed scalar source

The higher field's four real profiles also supply the normal frame needed by Reciprocal scalar envelopes. Choose an ordinary free coordinate in a sector containing the whole pair, with Clifford matrix αz\alpha_zαz​ anticommuting with the bound matrices and β\betaβ. It is an existing direction of the same physical manifold.

Write the matrix in the numerator of (6) as

P=F00I+iF10Cx+iF01Cy+F11CxCy. P=F_{00}I+iF_{10}C_x+iF_{01}C_y+F_{11}C_xC_y. P=F00​I+iF10​Cx​+iF01​Cy​+F11​Cx​Cy​.

The two anticommuting spatial matrices give the exact quaternion identity

Since βαz\beta\alpha_zβαz​ commutes with both CxC_xCx​ and CyC_yCy​, the mixed scalar normal-partner block vanishes. For an orthonormal rest-polarization matrix VVV with βV=V\beta V=VβV=V, put F=PV/Ωp−1Ωq−1F=PV/\sqrt{\Omega_{p-1}\Omega_{q-1}}F=PV/Ωp−1​Ωq−1​​ and W=(F,αzF)W=(F,\alpha_zF)W=(F,αz​F). Then

where σ\sigmaσ is (7). This identity holds for arbitrary complex normal amplitudes and every selected rest polarization. Thus their scalar density multiplies the same deformed spatial source, rather than introducing unaccounted interference terms.

Angular integration removes the mixed normal current. The two partner families have the canonical spatial norm. When any real profile parameter sss varies, the angular average of the projected derivative contains only the radial norm derivative:

∫W†∂sW dx dy=0(16) \int W^\dagger\partial_sW\,dx\,dy=0 \tag{16} ∫W†∂s​Wdxdy=0(16)

for a normalized profile family. This justifies the first-order kinetic normalization used in the two-envelope reduction.

An exact uniform normal boost preserves the scalar density pointwise, as in Normal Dirac propagation. Boosting the deformed partner therefore leaves both reciprocal scalar source matrices unchanged for a uniform pair. Varying envelope densities can still drive omitted spatial profiles through the residual described in the scalar envelope calculation; (16) does not establish full dynamical closure.

Verification

nested_dirac_pair_checks.py compares (10) directly with finite spatial derivatives of the full Clifford field for several nested depths, including arbitrary complex test coefficients. Product radial quadrature checks its moments and norm. The isotropic limit is independently compared with the central Dirac solver. Increasing radial basis size and changing both numerical widths test the anisotropic branch and the coupled self-consistency equation.

Finite differences of the frequency verify both scalar moment derivatives. Finite differences of the pair energy and of each partner's source verify (13). Several specified values of the one common coefficient test the scaling in (11). Reports have timestamped filenames and all conditions remain active under Python optimization.

The complete anisotropic normal frame is also checked pointwise for complex amplitudes, with angular normalization and real profile derivatives. Independent full spatial derivatives verify boosted propagation of the actual self-consistent deformed partner.
