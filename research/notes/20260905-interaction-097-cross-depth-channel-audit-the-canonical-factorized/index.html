97. Cross-depth channel audit: the canonical factorized normal-spinor contraction does not lock the frames

Sections 95--96 established that a second-order cross-depth process can in principle produce both the positive coarse lock and the full oriented singlet projector. The remaining danger is circularity: the explicit rank-one transition

BQ=β∣n⟩⟨SQ∣ B_Q=\beta|n\rangle\langle S_Q| BQ​=β∣n⟩⟨SQ​∣

already contains the desired oriented singlet. The next test is therefore whether the canonical 3⊂63\subset63⊂6 spinor factorization naturally supplies that channel. It does not.

97.1 The direct normal-polarization contraction is factorized

Relative to the nucleus-selected split,

Spin(3)O×Spin(3)H, Spin(3)_O\times Spin(3)_H, Spin(3)O​×Spin(3)H​,

the electron chiral carrier is

E=(2,2). E=(\mathbf2,\mathbf2). E=(2,2).

A depth-three output spinor carried by the observed factor is

N=(2,1). N=(\mathbf2,\mathbf1). N=(2,1).

Selecting a normalized hidden/normal spinor ∣χ⟩∈2H|\chi\rangle\in\mathbf2_H∣χ⟩∈2H​ gives the most direct contraction

Bχ=IO⊗⟨χ∣. \boxed{ B_\chi=I_O\otimes\langle\chi|. } Bχ​=IO​⊗⟨χ∣.​

Consequently

Bχ†Bχ=IO⊗∣χ⟩⟨χ∣. \boxed{ B_\chi^\dagger B_\chi =I_O\otimes|\chi\rangle\langle\chi|. } Bχ†​Bχ​=IO​⊗∣χ⟩⟨χ∣.​

This is a rank-two product projector. Its Pauli expansion contains only

(1,1)⊕(1,3), (\mathbf1,\mathbf1)\oplus(\mathbf1,\mathbf3), (1,1)⊕(1,3),

and its entire mixed correlation matrix vanishes:

14tr⁡[Bχ†Bχ(σa⊗σi)]=0∀a,i. \boxed{ \frac14\operatorname{tr} \left[B_\chi^\dagger B_\chi (\sigma_a\otimes\sigma_i) \right]=0 \qquad\forall a,i. } 41​tr[Bχ†​Bχ​(σa​⊗σi​)]=0∀a,i.​

So the canonical factorized restriction has no (3,3)(\mathbf3,\mathbf3)(3,3) lock content at all for a fixed normal polarization.

97.2 Eliminating a complete hidden doublet gives only a scalar shift

For an orthonormal hidden basis ∣↑⟩,∣↓⟩|\uparrow\rangle,|\downarrow\rangle∣↑⟩,∣↓⟩,

B↑†B↑+B↓†B↓=I4. \boxed{ B_\uparrow^\dagger B_\uparrow +B_\downarrow^\dagger B_\downarrow =I_4. } B↑†​B↑​+B↓†​B↓​=I4​.​

Therefore a complete or unpolarized normal-channel elimination produces

ΔHfact∝−I4, \Delta H_{\rm fact}\propto-I_4, ΔHfact​∝−I4​,

which cannot distinguish the diagonal-spin decomposition

1⊕3. \mathbf1\oplus\mathbf3. 1⊕3.

This gives a clean no-go:

the bare statement that the depth-six spinor carries a two-state normal polarization does not generate the frame lock. \boxed{ \text{the bare statement that the depth-six spinor carries a two-state normal polarization does not generate the frame lock.} } the bare statement that the depth-six spinor carries a two-state normal polarization does not generate the frame lock.​

A nonfactorizing correlation between the observed and hidden spin factors is required.

97.3 Once a relative frame is supplied, two reduced doublet channels are allowed

If the nuclear/scattering background supplies a relative frame QQQ, the product symmetry can be compared through the corresponding diagonal subgroup

Spin(3)diag. Spin(3)_{\rm diag}. Spin(3)diag​.

Then

E=(2,2)↓Spin(3)diag=1⊕3, E=(\mathbf2,\mathbf2) \downarrow_{Spin(3)_{\rm diag}} =\mathbf1\oplus\mathbf3, E=(2,2)↓Spin(3)diag​​=1⊕3,

while the depth-three carrier is a doublet

N=2. N=\mathbf2. N=2.

A transition involving a background/nuclear doublet can therefore pass through either

2⊗1→2 \mathbf2\otimes\mathbf1\to\mathbf2 2⊗1→2

or

2⊗3→2. \mathbf2\otimes\mathbf3\to\mathbf2. 2⊗3→2.

Because

2⊗1=2, \mathbf2\otimes\mathbf1=\mathbf2, 2⊗1=2,

and

2⊗3=2⊕4, \mathbf2\otimes\mathbf3=\mathbf2\oplus\mathbf4, 2⊗3=2⊕4,

there are exactly two reduced doublet channels: one through the electron singlet and one through the electron triplet.

Therefore

diagonal rotational covariance alone does not force singlet dominance. \boxed{ \text{diagonal rotational covariance alone does not force singlet dominance.} } diagonal rotational covariance alone does not force singlet dominance.​

The triplet channel is symmetry-allowed.

97.4 Exact Clebsch--Gordan normalization gives a quantitative test

Let PSP_SPS​ and PT=I−PSP_T=I-P_SPT​=I−PS​ denote the electron singlet and triplet projectors. For a normalized singlet reduced map, summing over a complete depth-three doublet gives

∑mBS,m†BS,m=2PS. \boxed{ \sum_m B_{S,m}^\dagger B_{S,m}=2P_S. } m∑​BS,m†​BS,m​=2PS​.​

For the normalized Clebsch--Gordan projection

2⊗3→2, \mathbf2\otimes\mathbf3\to\mathbf2, 2⊗3→2,

direct exact matrix calculation gives

∑mBT,m†BT,m=23PT. \boxed{ \sum_m B_{T,m}^\dagger B_{T,m}=\frac23P_T. } m∑​BT,m†​BT,m​=32​PT​.​

Let the two reduced matrix elements be aaa and bbb. If the eliminated channel has a common positive gap Δ>0\Delta>0Δ>0, the rotationally averaged second-order shift is

ΔHe=−2∣a∣2ΔPS−2∣b∣23ΔPT. \boxed{ \Delta H_e =-\frac{2|a|^2}{\Delta}P_S -\frac{2|b|^2}{3\Delta}P_T. } ΔHe​=−Δ2∣a∣2​PS​−3Δ2∣b∣2​PT​.​

The singlet is therefore the lower branch exactly when

∣a∣2>∣b∣23. \boxed{ |a|^2>\frac{|b|^2}{3}. } ∣a∣2>3∣b∣2​.​

Equality gives no singlet--triplet splitting; reversing the inequality favors the triplet and kills this lock route.

This is now a sharp falsification criterion for the projected master operator.

97.5 The canonical factorized contraction lies exactly on the no-splitting boundary

For the direct product contractions,

∑χBχ†Bχ=I4=PS+PT. \sum_\chi B_\chi^\dagger B_\chi=I_4=P_S+P_T. χ∑​Bχ†​Bχ​=I4​=PS​+PT​.

2∣a∣2=1,2∣b∣23=1, 2|a|^2=1, \qquad \frac{2|b|^2}{3}=1, 2∣a∣2=1,32∣b∣2​=1,

hence

∣b∣2=3∣a∣2. \boxed{|b|^2=3|a|^2.} ∣b∣2=3∣a∣2.​

That is precisely the equality condition

∣a∣2=∣b∣23. |a|^2=\frac{|b|^2}{3}. ∣a∣2=3∣b∣2​.

So the naive factorized 3⊂63\subset63⊂6 restriction is not merely too weak to orient the lock. It sits exactly at zero singlet--triplet preference.

A successful interaction must depart from factorization in a measurable way.

Hom⁡(E,N)≅(2,1)⊗(2,2)∗. \operatorname{Hom}(E,N) \cong (\mathbf2,\mathbf1)\otimes(\mathbf2,\mathbf2)^*. Hom(E,N)≅(2,1)⊗(2,2)∗.

Using pseudoreality of the SU(2)SU(2)SU(2) doublet,

Hom⁡((2,2),(2,1))=(1,2)⊕(3,2). \boxed{ \operatorname{Hom}((\mathbf2,\mathbf2),(\mathbf2,\mathbf1)) =(\mathbf1,\mathbf2)\oplus(\mathbf3,\mathbf2). } Hom((2,2),(2,1))=(1,2)⊕(3,2).​

There is no bare (3,3)(\mathbf3,\mathbf3)(3,3) transition representation. The full mixed lock only appears after a quadratic electron endomorphism is formed:

End⁡(2,2)=(1,1)⊕(3,1)⊕(1,3)⊕(3,3). \operatorname{End}(\mathbf2,\mathbf2) =(\mathbf1,\mathbf1) \oplus(\mathbf3,\mathbf1) \oplus(\mathbf1,\mathbf3) \oplus(\mathbf3,\mathbf3). End(2,2)=(1,1)⊕(3,1)⊕(1,3)⊕(3,3).

This independently supports the second-order route of Sections 95--96 and rules out returning to the first-order shortcut corrected in Section 94.
