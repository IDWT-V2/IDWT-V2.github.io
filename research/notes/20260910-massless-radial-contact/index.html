Massless radial scalar contact

Updated September 12, 2026

Summary: Regular flat radial scalar Dirac fields have no finite-norm stationary state for cubic or smooth saturating contact; the homogeneous root is also excluded under the stated virial hypotheses.

Consequence for the contact construction

Replacing ggg by the graph coefficient κDN\kappa D_NκDN​ cannot change this radial existence result. The graph pairing alone therefore does not supply a normalized stationary profile of form (2) when reduced to (1) on flat infinite space.

This conclusion identifies the limits of a specific spatial reduction. The cubic and smooth proofs use the flat infinite tail, zero bare mass, an integrable scalar self-potential and the stated radial form. The root case uses the additional virial hypotheses just stated. These radial arguments do not apply to the proposed global CP3\mathbb{CP}^3CP3 electron geometry, nonradial polarization patterns, additional same-field interactions or different asymptotic backgrounds. The fixed-sign Fourier argument (13) has its separate nonradial scope. The radial tail proof also does not apply to the nonlocal scalar moment model, whose self-generated quadratic mass profile grows at infinity.

The exact massive one-dimensional solution in local scalar normalization provides a positive control: adding m>0m>0m>0 creates a frequency gap and admits decaying profiles. Its fixed-norm frequency is nevertheless a nonlinear function of the contact coefficient.

The smooth spatial controls use their specified curved geometry and connections, so their existence is consistent with this flat-space boundary. Generating a self-mass coefficient and finding a viable localized spatial field remain separate requirements of the same proposed dynamics.

The positive-current localization theorem gives a separate full-field restriction for the convex matrix-current hierarchy. Its virial and scalar identity retain every coefficient and spinor component, and identify a spatially changing mass-sign projector as a possible contribution outside a fixed positive-current decomposition.
