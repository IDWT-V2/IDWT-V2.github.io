Massless radial scalar contact

Updated September 10, 2026

Summary: The flat massless cubic scalar Dirac equation has no nonzero finite-norm stationary wave in its standard regular radial ansatz, by a zero-frequency identity and an exact tail argument.

Equation and exact scope

Consider only the flat local scalar Dirac equation

i∂tΨ=[−iα⋅∇+g(Ψ†βΨ)β]Ψ,x∈Rd,d≥1,g∈R.(1) i\partial_t\Psi= \left[-i\alpha\cdot\nabla+g(\Psi^\dagger\beta\Psi)\beta\right]\Psi, \qquad x\in\mathbb R^d,\quad d\ge1,\quad g\in\mathbb R. \tag{1} i∂t​Ψ=[−iα⋅∇+g(Ψ†βΨ)β]Ψ,x∈Rd,d≥1,g∈R.(1)

There is no bare mass, prescribed background or additional kernel. The coordinates are ordinary physical spatial coordinates. This comparison does not replace the proposed curved spatial geometry of an IDWT particle.

Under precisely these assumptions, the only solution is zero. The proof separates zero and nonzero frequency.

Consequence for the contact construction

Replacing ggg by the graph coefficient κDN\kappa D_NκDN​ cannot change this radial existence result. The graph pairing alone therefore does not supply a normalized stationary profile of form (2) when reduced to (1) on flat infinite space.

This conclusion identifies the limits of a specific spatial reduction. The proof uses the flat infinite tail, zero bare mass, local cubic scalar source and the stated radial form. It does not apply to the proposed global CP3\mathbb{CP}^3CP3 electron geometry, nonradial polarization patterns, additional same-field interactions or different asymptotic backgrounds. It also does not apply to the nonlocal scalar moment model, whose self-generated quadratic mass profile grows at infinity.

The exact massive one-dimensional solution in local scalar normalization provides a positive control: adding m>0m>0m>0 creates a frequency gap and admits decaying profiles. Its fixed-norm frequency is nevertheless a nonlinear function of the contact coefficient.

Verification

Those finite integrations illustrate the mechanism; the nonexistence result is the argument (5)–(9), not a conclusion drawn from unsuccessful numerical shooting.
