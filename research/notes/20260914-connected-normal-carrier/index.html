A bounded normal carrier with linked spatial connection

Updated September 14, 2026

conditional calculation

A linked spatial texture and connection improve the inverse mass operator. Both coefficients are bounded, their radial profiles obey two common first-order equations, and the complete unwanted normal spectrum has an explicit positive gap. A bounded polarization coefficient projects to equally spaced values on the actual localized modes. The resulting small frequency has the same simplex numerator at every assigned particle grade.

These are properties of a specified spatial operator. Its coefficients have not yet been identified with the sustaining motion of the one IDWT field. In particular, the positive geometric functional below is not being assigned as an additional physical field energy. The calculation uses ordinary unbounded spatial coordinates, with no compactification or new particle interaction. Mass remains the selected frequency.

Linked radial coefficients on an ordinary plane

Let z=x+iy, r=|z| and theta=arg(z). For positive integer n, define

W=w(r)einθ,A=a(r)reθ,B=∂xAy−∂yAx=a′r.(1) W=w(r)e^{in\theta},\qquad \boldsymbol A=\frac{a(r)}r\boldsymbol e_\theta, \qquad B=\partial_x A_y-\partial_y A_x=\frac{a'}r. \tag{1} W=w(r)einθ,A=ra(r)​eθ​,B=∂x​Ay​−∂y​Ax​=ra′​.(1)

Here W is a complex matrix coefficient and A specifies a connection on field components. Their linked profile rule, in dimensionless normal units, is

w′=n−arw,a′=2r(1−w2),w(0)=a(0)=0,w(∞)=1,a(∞)=n.(2) \boxed{w'=\frac{n-a}{r}w,\qquad a'=2r(1-w^2),} \qquad w(0)=a(0)=0,\quad w(\infty)=1,\quad a(\infty)=n. \tag{2} w′=rn−a​w,a′=2r(1−w2),​w(0)=a(0)=0,w(∞)=1,a(∞)=n.(2)

The regular radial solution has 0<w<1 and 0<a<n away from the origin. Near zero, w=c_n r^n[1+O(r^2)] and a=r^2+O(r^(2n+2)). At infinity, 1-w and n-a decay exponentially. Thus W is smooth and bounded, A is regular at zero and tends to n e_theta/r at infinity, and its curvature has finite integral 2 pi n.

Equation (2) is a rescaled instance of the mathematical first-order vortex profile equations. Their full-plane existence with prescribed zeros is established by Taubes's existence theorem. For the present radial case all n zeros coincide at the origin. The connection and texture are retained as inverse spatial coefficients here; the physical interpretation of that reference is not part of this model.

The boundary term is [a+(n-a)w^2]_0^infinity=n. Consequently (2) minimizes this radial functional and gives G=2 pi n. This supplies an explicit compatibility rule for the coefficients. It does not supply their physical energy units, canonical time evolution or their response to an occupied particle mode. Nonradial configurations with separated zeros also require attention; the radial cost alone does not select coincident zeros against every such deformation.

A complete normal operator with an explicit gap

Use alpha_1=sigma_1 tensor I, alpha_2=sigma_2 tensor I, Gamma_1=sigma_3 tensor tau_1, Gamma_2=sigma_3 tensor tau_2, and chirality C=sigma_3 tensor tau_3. The self-adjoint Dirac operator is

Tensor factors are implicit. It anticommutes with C. All matrix coefficients are bounded; its principal part is the ordinary spatial Dirac derivative. A smooth change of component basis can change the phase of W and the corresponding connection together, but neither coefficient is independently discarded in this calculation.

Let Q be the block from C=+1 to C=-1. In the positive-chiral square, the off-diagonal coefficient is proportional to w'-(n-a)w/r and hence vanishes by (2). The two remaining components have opposite connection signs:

Each covariant Laplacian is nonnegative on the full plane. Therefore Q has no square-integrable zero mode, and every nonzero frequency of D has absolute value at least one. The gap statement includes the full continuum and every angular mode; it is not a finite-box spectral estimate.

The actual finite kernel

Write q=n-1. Negative-chiral zero modes have the same angular form as in the holomorphic carrier:

For l=0,...,q, the unique decaying radial solution is regular at zero. Both components are asymptotic to a constant times r^(-1/2) exp(-r). They are normalized by 2 pi integral r(U_l^2+V_l^2)dr=1. The ratio V_l/U_l obeys the ungauged ratio equation; the connection adds the same scalar radial weight to both components.

The weight n+1-a lies strictly between 1 and n+1 away from the origin. Equation (9) excludes every other integer angular label: for l=-1 or l=n the required expectation already has magnitude n+1, which no nonzero square-integrable field can attain; more distant labels require a still larger value. Together with (6) and the regular decaying solutions of (7), this gives dim ker D=n, entirely in negative chirality.

The source is orthogonal to the complete kernel by (9), and the unit gap makes its complementary inverse unambiguous. This is a full spatial identity, not a matrix fit.

Momentum and the remaining physical construction

The distinction between two spatial momenta matters. Equation (7) gives the exact projected covariant identity

PΠP=0,Π=−i∇+τ3A/2.(16) P\boldsymbol\Pi P=0,\qquad \boldsymbol\Pi=-i\nabla+\tau_3\boldsymbol A/2. \tag{16} PΠP=0,Π=−i∇+τ3​A/2.(16)

It does not give P(-i nabla)P=0. For n=6, the largest absolute adjacent canonical x-momentum entry is approximately 0.254966. The complete physical translation calculation must include the connection's transformation and sustaining motion. Reusing the old unconnected canonical response would give the wrong result. No physical particle inertia has been derived from (16), positive or negative.

The construction now has bounded linked coefficients, finite geometric cost, a complete finite kernel, a rigorous normal gap and the desired projected numerator under one shared rule. It still needs a same-field action for their joint oscillation, its total momentum and stress, and the particle source's reciprocal effect on the geometry. Simply adding the positive functional (3) to the wave energy would change the total energy-frequency accounting and would require a new stationary calculation.

Independent verification uses direct Cartesian derivatives of all four components, a second application of the full Dirac operator to arbitrary positive-chiral test fields, radial and outer-domain refinement, exact projected identities, high-precision complete inverse matrices and the analytic full-complement bound.
