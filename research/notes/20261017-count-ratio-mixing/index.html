Count Ratios, Quark Mixing and the Cumulative-Band Test

Updated October 17, 2026

The two candidates are fits to already known measurements. They give approximately 0.223607 and 0.041158. The familiar external square-root mass-ratio form is not new by itself. Within the maintained common-scale families, the proposal sharpens it to the displayed exact rational squared values. The new research question is whether the same cumulative geometry that enters the mass count also enters a correctly normalized transition interaction.

A precise projection identity

Let B1⊂B2\mathcal B_1\subset\mathcal B_2B1​⊂B2​ be real mode spaces with dimensions D1<D2D_1<D_2D1​<D2​, and P1,P2P_1,P_2P1​,P2​ their orthogonal projectors. A unit classical coefficient vector with isotropic second moment on the larger band has

⟨aaT⟩=P2D2,⟨∥P1a∥2⟩=tr⁡(P1P2D2)=D1D2. \langle aa^T\rangle=\frac{P_2}{D_2},\qquad \langle\|P_1a\|^2\rangle =\operatorname{tr}\left(P_1\frac{P_2}{D_2}\right) =\frac{D_1}{D_2}. ⟨aaT⟩=D2​P2​​,⟨∥P1​a∥2⟩=tr(P1​D2​P2​​)=D2​D1​​.

The brackets specify a classical preparation average. They do not define a probability distribution of electron positions or a Born law. Likewise the trace identity is not yet a transition amplitude. Identifying its square root with an observed mixing magnitude is the physical bridge being proposed.

For an orthonormal spatial basis ϕj(x)\phi_j(x)ϕj​(x), write the cumulative diagonal kernel as KN(x,x)=∑j≤DN∣ϕj(x)∣2K_N(x,x)=\sum_{j\le D_N}|\phi_j(x)|^2KN​(x,x)=∑j≤DN​​∣ϕj​(x)∣2. Its spatial integral is DND_NDN​. The ratio of the integrated kernels therefore reproduces D1/D2D_1/D_2D1​/D2​; the pointwise ratio generally does not. On the weighted ball it varies with position. On a homogeneous band whose diagonal kernels are constant, the ratio is pointwise constant as well. A physical contact must specify whether it samples an integrated, local or otherwise weighted response. These are different mechanisms.

Cumulative bands of all polynomial degrees through NNN supply the required nesting. A single-parity real amplitude-sphere realization has a different property: degree-NNN homogeneous polynomial restrictions contain harmonic degrees N,N−2,…N,N-2,\ldotsN,N−2,…. Opposite parities are orthogonal. The proposed mixing pairs have degrees (0,3)(0,3)(0,3), (2,19)(2,19)(2,19) and (2,71)(2,71)(2,71); they are all opposite-parity pairs. Their direct inner-product contact is consequently zero in that particular realization. Under the proposed direct overlap mechanism, the fitted nonzero mixing favors a genuinely cumulative realization. It does not rule out every sphere construction: a different interaction could mix parity, but would require its own derivation.

What the same rule fails to explain

The analogous up/top candidate gives

∣Vub∣=151215450=181030≃0.003513. |V_{ub}|=\sqrt{\frac{15}{1215450}} =\frac1{\sqrt{81030}}\simeq0.003513. ∣Vub​∣=121545015​​=81030​1​≃0.003513.

Against the used benchmark 0.00382 this is about 8% low. The down-side third-generation candidate fails much more severely, and bottom has no established ordinary integer-count assignment. No bottom count is invented to complete the table. A two-angle approximation gives ∣Vcd∣≃∣Vus∣|V_{cd}|\simeq|V_{us}|∣Vcd​∣≃∣Vus​∣ and ∣Vts∣≃∣Vcb∣|V_{ts}|\simeq|V_{cb}|∣Vts​∣≃∣Vcb​∣; exact three-generation unitarity does not make these equalities exact or determine the CP phase.

Applying the same squared ratio rule to neutrino mass-state counts 2002,11628,657802002,11628,657802002,11628,65780 gives approximately 0.172,0.177,0.0300.172,0.177,0.0300.172,0.177,0.030, compared with the used PMNS values 0.307,0.561,0.0220.307,0.561,0.0220.307,0.561,0.022. These are failed fits. The successful quark comparisons do not supply a general mixing matrix, the CP-violating phase or a neutrino mixing mechanism.

The exploratory candidate set contained the plain and square-root values of every ratio below one among fourteen maintained counts: 182 candidates per mixing element. A crude log-range estimate gave about 0.3 accidental hits within a 1% window per target. This is not a calibrated significance test; the ratios are correlated and the choice of family relationships carries a selection history. Cross-family numerical hits were not adopted as mechanisms. The credible contribution is two compact targets with a testable geometric bridge and explicit failures, not a discovery probability.

Bottom and the adjacent-depth hypothesis

The same inversion makes the bottom difficulty sharper. Its mass at the supplied down-sector unit corresponds to roughly 890 counted contributions, whereas a mixing denominator inferred from the approximate two-angle relations is DsDc/Du=35420/3≃11807D_sD_c/D_u=35420/3\simeq11807Ds​Dc​/Du​=35420/3≃11807. These are fitted targets for two different observables, not an identified band with a fractional number of modes. In the unchanged residual-free occupation action, a participation count CmC_mCm​ and origin weight p0p_0p0​ obey

Hμ≥Cm[1+2(1−Cmp0)2]. \frac H\mu\ge C_m[1+2(1-C_mp_0)^2]. μH​≥Cm​[1+2(1−Cm​p0​)2].

A clock equal to its participation count therefore needs Cmp0=1C_mp_0=1Cm​p0​=1. The proposed mass/mixing mismatch cannot simply be assigned to one undressed state of that action. The bound is in the carrier's actual unit μ\muμ, not automatically the down unit. Changed projections, dressed states or changed actions are possible research routes; this note does not adopt an unreviewed repair.

Likewise the pairs 3/4 and 5/6 suggest an adjacent-depth charged-current hypothesis, but a depth-ten tau has no assigned partner at nine or eleven. The mass fit and a universal depth-change-by-one law cannot both be asserted from these data. Adjacency is a conjecture suggested by two pairs, not an established selection rule.

Reproduction and next test

The arithmetic follows directly from the mass assignments and scale law. The exact trace calculation is reproducible by choosing any nested orthonormal bases and summing the retained squared components; the ball version additionally requires its actual kernel and integration measure. The original calculation used exact rational checks for the band ratios and parity controls. The finite candidate search and its failed comparisons are retained in the source-pinned research record.

The useful next calculation is a reciprocal same-field transition vertex on a cumulative spatial carrier, with its kinetic metrics and contact weighting varied as part of the action. It must yield these magnitudes while accounting for the third generation, bottom, unitarity and CP phase. A mode-space projection alone does not do that work.
