Source frequencies in spatial coupling

Updated September 9, 2026

Summary: The full field source, spatial observables and changing mode patterns carry different temporal frequencies, whose relation to normalized response determines whether a slow bound oscillation weakens coupling.

Identify the clock carried by the interacting spatial pattern

A low bound oscillation frequency can affect coupling only through the source actually sampled by the other object. In particular, the frequency in a spatial susceptibility need not equal the phase frequency of the unperturbed bound field. The distinction can be established directly from the written source, without assigning a weak coefficient to depth five.

All fields below live on the one nested manifold of ordinary macroscopic spatial dimensions. Mode indices and amplitude components describe fields over that space. They introduce neither further spatial directions nor interaction particles. A finite expansion illustrates the source algebra; it is an exact solution of the nonlinear field equation only when its profiles and amplitudes also satisfy that equation.

A pure occupied mode can still have nonzero transition matrix elements to other modes. The absence of a pre-existing off-diagonal mean density is not the absence of a response to an applied source.

Include the response scale and normalization

The frequency barriers are expansions about zero forcing frequency with fixed profiles and regular response functions. An amplitude proportional to ω\omegaω or ω2\omega^2ω2 is small relative to the corresponding spectral and source scales. Those coefficients can change when the particle's bound scale changes.

The zero-bare-mass scalar moment branch gives an exact control. Let Dλϕ(x)=λd/2ϕ(λx)D_\lambda\phi(x)=\lambda^{d/2}\phi(\lambda x)Dλ​ϕ(x)=λd/2ϕ(λx). For unit spatial norm, dilating a complete bound solution gives

A two-frequency background has additional periodic response coefficients and must be treated separately. Different physical particle scales could still provide a small frequency ratio; their selected profiles and normalization must establish it.

Conditions for the proposed neutrino mechanism

The proposed five-dimensional application requires a selected spatial mode whose actual source has no unsuppressed static contribution, followed by a derived slow source clock in the frame of its interaction partner. The full response must include both polarizations and all accessible ordinary, conjugate and spatial channels. An incoming mode also has to satisfy its own field equation; Incoming spatial shape modes checks that distinction explicitly.

A physical photon additionally has a massless propagating branch. Its outgoing Green function can have poles where a regular static susceptibility does not. The low-frequency power of a gapped shape response cannot by itself determine the normalized photon amplitude or its emitted or scattered flux.

V2's neutrino spectrum provides proposed mass indices and a conditional scale reconstruction. The projected mass-operator construction still leaves the depth-five normalization to be derived. Neither the frequency rules nor a chosen small normalization complete that task.
