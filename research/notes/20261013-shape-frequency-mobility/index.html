Updated October 13, 2026

A localized field profile and translation symmetry do not by themselves establish particle inertia or a moving solution. For the Gaussian-window pattern action, a uniform phase gradient has a positive restricted kinetic response, yet the complete field equation excludes the corresponding differentiable Gaussian traveling family. Two explicit changed phase actions supply exact motion. The second follows a branch's actual positive stationary frequency and includes the full functional response of that frequency.

These are classical fields on ordinary shared R10\mathbb R^{10}R10. Density is squared field amplitude, not position probability; amplitude and phase are variables of one field, not additional physical dimensions or exchanged particles. An internal profile and its moving center remain distinct, including for an electron with a literal deterministic trajectory in six ordinary spatial dimensions. All ten center-motion directions are permitted independently of the pattern's active-depth classification. Neither observed-particle identification nor a local Lorentz-covariant field theory follows from the results below.
