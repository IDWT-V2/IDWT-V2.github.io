Source and scope

Adapted from IDWT_04_Gravity.md, §3.10.2–3.10.5c, especially the explicit functional, near line 315. Status: checked algebra within a flat, scalar, fixed-norm mean-field model; not a derivation of the V2 master dynamics.

Functional and stationary equation

For real coordinates x,y in R^d, coupling g>0, kinetic coefficient set to one, and Q = integral |Psi|^2 fixed, retain the source functional

E[Ψ]=∫∣∇Ψ(x)∣2 dx+g2∬(x⋅y)2∣Ψ(x)∣2∣Ψ(y)∣2 dx dy. E[\Psi]=\int |\nabla\Psi(x)|^2\,dx +\frac g2\iint(x\cdot y)^2|\Psi(x)|^2|\Psi(y)|^2\,dx\,dy. E[Ψ]=∫∣∇Ψ(x)∣2dx+2g​∬(x⋅y)2∣Ψ(x)∣2∣Ψ(y)∣2dxdy.

Variation of E-mu Q gives

[−Δ+g∫(x⋅y)2∣Ψ(y)∣2 dy]Ψ(x)=μΨ(x). \left[-\Delta+g\int(x\cdot y)^2|\Psi(y)|^2\,dy\right]\Psi(x)=\mu\Psi(x). [−Δ+g∫(x⋅y)2∣Ψ(y)∣2dy]Ψ(x)=μΨ(x).

For an isotropic density, its second moment is integral y_i y_j |Psi(y)|^2 dy = Q delta_ij <r^2>/d. Thus V(x)=lambda |x|^2, with lambda=g Q <r^2>/d. The normalized oscillator ground state has <r^2>=d/(2 sqrt(lambda)), so

λ=(gQ/2)2/3,Ψ(x)=Q λd/8π−d/4exp⁡(−λ ∣x∣2/2). \lambda=(gQ/2)^{2/3},\qquad \Psi(x)=\sqrt Q\,\lambda^{d/8}\pi^{-d/4} \exp(-\sqrt\lambda\,|x|^2/2). λ=(gQ/2)2/3,Ψ(x)=Q​λd/8π−d/4exp(−λ​∣x∣2/2).

The historical formula lambda=(g/2)^(2/3) is the Q=1 specialization. Choosing Q=1 is not a derivation of quantum charge discreteness.

Transfer conditions

  • This produces an isotropic stationary solution, not a proof that it is the unique physical background or that its full fluctuation spectrum is stable.
  • The eigenvalue mu includes the full mean-field potential; the interaction appears with one-half its double integral in E. Do not equate mu Q with E.
  • When expanding this functional in oscillator modes, retain the kinetic matrix and quartic term, or explicitly subtract the background potential already included in oscillator eigenvalues. Otherwise it is double-counted.
  • A resonant-only quartic mode sum requires a justified rotating-wave or time-averaging reduction; index-resonance restrictions do not follow merely from expanding the static functional in a basis.
  • The formula as written uses a fixed origin. A co-moving centroid prescription must be defined and varied consistently before claiming translation covariance.
  • This closure does not identify oscillator eigenvalues with cumulative state counts, select occupied particle indices, or supply a physical lock frequency.
