The angular response required for spectrally transparent support

Updated September 17, 2026

conditional calculation

The current-supported spatial equation has a precise remaining metric force. This note works backward from that force. It asks what response a supporting sector would have to produce if it were to leave the selected frequency unchanged on the round reference geometry.

The result is a diagnostic and an inverse construction. It does not yet derive the response from the one field Ψ∞\Psi_\inftyΨ∞​, establish a nonlinear supported particle, or repair the finite-chain mass-unit drift. It does identify why another function of scalar curvature is insufficient and what additional angular operator is required.

Domain, collective modes and positivity

On a scalar harmonic of degree ℓ\ellℓ,

Ld(ℓ)=−1+ℓ(ℓ+d−1)d.(7) \mathcal L_d(\ell) =-1+\frac{\ell(\ell+d-1)}d. \tag{7} Ld​(ℓ)=−1+dℓ(ℓ+d−1)​.(7)

Thus

Ld(0)=−1,Ld(1)=0,Ld(ℓ)>0(ℓ≥2).(8) \mathcal L_d(0)=-1, \qquad \mathcal L_d(1)=0, \qquad \mathcal L_d(\ell)>0\quad(\ell\ge2). \tag{8} Ld​(0)=−1,Ld​(1)=0,Ld​(ℓ)>0(ℓ≥2).(8)

The highest-weight densities used in the present controls have only even scalar harmonics. Their nonconstant metric force begins at ℓ=2\ell=2ℓ=2, so the inverse in (2) is well defined and positive on precisely that angular subspace. The ℓ=0\ell=0ℓ=0 radius must be balanced separately. The ℓ=1\ell=1ℓ=1 kernel contains the conformal collective directions and cannot be hidden by an inverse; it is absent from these centered even densities.

This projected inverse is much better behaved than an inverse on the full scalar space. A quadratic auxiliary response with spatial stiffness ⟨χ,Ldχ⟩\langle\chi,\mathcal L_d\chi\rangle⟨χ,Ld​χ⟩ is positive for ℓ≥2\ell\ge2ℓ≥2, but it would have a negative radial direction and a zero degree-one direction if those modes were retained. Any dynamical realization must therefore treat radius and collective position or orientation explicitly rather than declaring the full inverse stable.

A hyperbolic completion on the positive angular subspace can use

χ¨+c2R2Ldχ=source.(9) \ddot\chi+\frac{c^2}{R^2}\mathcal L_d\chi=\text{source}. \tag{9} χ¨​+R2c2​Ld​χ=source.(9)

Equation (9) only shows that the required static angular kernel admits a causal linear completion on that projected subspace. It does not show that χ\chiχ is an allowed component of Ψ∞\Psi_\inftyΨ∞​, or that its coupling and complete energy are positive.

What the construction does and does not solve

The response (4) cancels the complete first angular force without changing the reference frequency. It therefore demonstrates that first-order support and frequency preservation are mathematically compatible; the previous angular obstruction is a restriction on local scalar-curvature support, not a theorem against every patterned response.

The price is explicit. The construction contains a reference curvature, a projected inverse angular operator, and two operator-valued sources. Their origin in the one field is not derived. If χ\chiχ is introduced as an independent response field, the model violates the stated one-field premise. If it is obtained by eliminating a mode, that mode, its kinetic normalization, its coupling, and the backreaction produced by elimination must all be derived from the same action. Equations (12)--(15) show that a zero-background stable quadratic mode cannot do the job. Merely naming χ\chiχ a mode of the field would not accomplish this.

The cancellation also makes only the first variation vanish. It supplies no positive second variation of the joint field and geometry, no nonlinear existence result, and no common compatible radius. Because it is spectrally transparent by construction, it leaves the exact finite-chain drift of ω/S(n,d)\omega/S(n,d)ω/S(n,d) unchanged and cannot repair the failed shared electron--tau unit. Supporting geometry and spectral selection remain two simultaneous requirements.

The productive next test is therefore narrower than another geometry scan: seek an actual retained same-field channel whose Schur complement produces the two responses in (6), then compute the full joint Hessian. The candidate must retain the positive ℓ≥2\ell\ge2ℓ≥2 stiffness, handle the radius and collective modes separately, and preserve the common mass operator across depths 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, and 10.
