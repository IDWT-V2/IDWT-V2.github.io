Scalar gap with canonical normalization

Updated September 11, 2026

Summary: A specified global scalar interaction admits a self-consistent gap and an exact collective response frequency, while canonical normalization determines whether a cumulative mode count enhances either quantity.

One scalar response from the same field

The nonlinear spatial reduction determines how complementary modes alter a proposed linear mass coefficient. A separate constructive question is whether a summed occupied-mode response can itself generate a count-dependent coefficient. The following model makes that question exactly solvable, including its dynamical response.

Verification and physical consequence

scalar_gap_normalization_checks.py checks the exact shell sums and six factorial moments, gap thresholds, bounds and asymptotic orders. Complex controls retain one to four kinetic pairs, up to three degenerate polarizations per pair, all conjugate variations, and common coordinate covariance. Independent energy derivatives, the complete field spectrum, driven scalar response and nonlinear time evolution verify the finite model's equations.

The calculation provides a self-consistent scalar coefficient and a collective response pole from one specified interaction. It also locates the normalization needed for a count enhancement. A physical simplex mass law still requires a master-derived kernel, a justified occupation and canonical norm, viable spatial particle states, and a rest-mass identification. This comparison neither establishes nor overturns the assigned particle dimensionalities.
