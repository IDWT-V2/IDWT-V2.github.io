Passive Hodge Interference and the Clock That Controls Coupling

Updated October 9, 2026

Two coherent orientations of one field contact can implement the symmetric Hodge filter without destroying energy. A positive wave-network action realizes that mathematical transfer. Its all-sector checks reveal two additional requirements: the physical origin of the response time matters, and a static zero does not guarantee cancellation of an oscillating color-volume interaction.

The network is an explicit inverse construction with prescribed coherent paths and contact maps. It is not a derived particle geometry, an observed force or a compactification. All channels represent modes of the same field along ordinary spatial paths. No electron three-plus-three decomposition is used.

6. Coherence and Robustness

The selected zero requires adding amplitudes before squaring. If the two contact routes become distinguishable, their incoherent powers do not cancel. With path amplitudes α=(1+ϵ)/2\alpha=(1+\epsilon)/2α=(1+ϵ)/2, β=(1−ϵ)/2\beta=(1-\epsilon)/2β=(1−ϵ)/2 and extra DC phase ϕ\phiϕ, the odd-channel static gain is

This floor couples the nonzero static density, which was absent from the ideal derivative channel. An exchange symmetry could protect equal routes, but reciprocity alone does not impose equal splitting or zero extra phase. The source must also be formed before lower-dimensional projection if it is to retain the complete-pattern Hodge rule; a network diagram alone does not derive that ordering.

The useful outcome is a physical distinction that can now be tested: an instantaneous graded self-contact, a passive delayed orientation interferometer and a fast-mode-induced gyroscopic action share some mathematics but give different finite-frequency and nonlinear responses. Their coefficients, time scales and omitted modes cannot be exchanged without rederivation.
