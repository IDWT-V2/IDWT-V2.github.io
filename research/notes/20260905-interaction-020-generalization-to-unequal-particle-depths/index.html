20. Generalization to unequal particle depths

Let particles have depths a<ba<ba<b. Locally write

Qb≃Qa×Fb/a Q_b\simeq Q_a\times F_{b/a} Qb​≃Qa​×Fb/a​

for the purpose of the cross-depth contact theorem.

The lower-depth particle is cylindrical across the fibre:

ρa↑b(x,y)=ρa(x). \rho_a^{\uparrow b}(x,y)=\rho_a(x). ρa↑b​(x,y)=ρa​(x).

The local interaction is therefore

Iab=∫Qaρa(x)[∫Fb/aρb(x,y)dμF(y)]dμa(x). I_{ab} =\int_{Q_a}\rho_a(x) \left[ \int_{F_{b/a}}\rho_b(x,y)d\mu_F(y) \right]d\mu_a(x). Iab​=∫Qa​​ρa​(x)[∫Fb/a​​ρb​(x,y)dμF​(y)]dμa​(x).

This has a profound consequence for collision geometry:

a lower-depth particle does not carry an impact parameter in directions in which it is intrinsically free/cylindrical. \boxed{ \text{a lower-depth particle does not carry an impact parameter in directions in which it is intrinsically free/cylindrical.} } a lower-depth particle does not carry an impact parameter in directions in which it is intrinsically free/cylindrical.​

The higher-depth particle's extra coordinates are integrated, not matched to an independently localized coordinate of the lower-depth particle.

Thus unequal-depth interaction can be geometrically less coincidence-suppressed than full same-depth contact.

This is not a statement that unequal-depth coupling is stronger. The polarization invariant and dynamical coefficient may suppress or forbid it completely. It is only a statement about coordinate incidence.

20.1 Adjacent weak pairs

For the quark weak transition

4↔3, 4\leftrightarrow3, 4↔3,

there is one extra coordinate on the depth-four side. Pure marginalization removes its localization requirement, but the CKM audit proves that marginalization alone cannot reproduce the observed mode transitions. Therefore weak conversion requires

full cross-depth incidence+mode-changing operator. \text{full cross-depth incidence} + \text{mode-changing operator}. full cross-depth incidence+mode-changing operator.

For

5↔6, 5\leftrightarrow6, 5↔6,

the depth-five neutrino mode is cylindrical across the one additional charged-lepton direction from the higher-depth viewpoint. Again there is no naive one-coordinate hit penalty; selection is representation/dynamics, not random coincidence.

For

5↔10, 5\leftrightarrow10, 5↔10,

the depth-five mode spans five additional tau-sector directions. The full marginal theorem still preserves its total contact weight. Hence the large depth difference does not by itself make the tau weak channel exponentially or power-law rare.

This matches an important qualitative fact: weak transitions involving particles assigned very different depths are observed at ordinary weak-interaction rates rather than being annihilated by enormous geometric coincidence factors.
