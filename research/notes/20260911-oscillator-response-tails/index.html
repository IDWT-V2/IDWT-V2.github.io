Four normal directions retain a shared-mode-dependent divergence

Codimension four is relevant to a ten-to-six flat spatial comparison, although the occupied particle modes and their physical kernel have not been identified with this oscillator example. For fixed shared degrees k≠lk\ne lk=l, use the same total cutoff NNN in both responses. Their paired cutoffs differ only by a bounded integer. Equation (9) therefore gives

GN,k−GN,l=c082ν(bl−bk)log⁡(N/2)+O(1)=c08(l−k)4νlog⁡(N/2)+O(1).(10) \boxed{G_{N,k}-G_{N,l} =\frac{c_0^8}{2\nu}(b_l-b_k)\log(N/2)+O(1) =\frac{c_0^8(l-k)}{4\nu}\log(N/2)+O(1).} \tag{10} GN,k​−GN,l​=2νc08​​(bl​−bk​)log(N/2)+O(1)=4νc08​(l−k)​log(N/2)+O(1).​(10)

The common linear growth cancels, but the displayed logarithm does not. Subtracting one cutoff-dependent constant times the identity changes neither side's difference, so it cannot make every shared-mode response finite. This is an additive-subtraction statement, not a prohibition of all effective theories. Further direct terms or a different source and spectrum would need to follow from the same action and normalization.

The divergence is not automatically a short-distance singularity. Here the cylindrical source dual is constant in the normal coordinates and is not in their untruncated L2L^2L2 space. High oscillator levels also explore increasing spatial extent. The mode cutoff does not by itself separate large-distance and short-distance contributions.
