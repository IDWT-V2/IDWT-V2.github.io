Isotropic polarization comparison

Updated September 8, 2026

Summary: Uniform five-dimensional null-axis and six-dimensional complex-structure tensors have identical photon-plane overlap distributions under an explicitly isotropic preparation.

The preparation is part of the model

Use the two-plane diagnostic from photon plane coupling:

Cd=∥PEJdk∥2,∣k∣=1,E⊂k⊥,dim⁡E=2.(1) C_d=\|P_EJ_dk\|^2, \qquad |k|=1,\qquad E\subset k^\perp,\qquad \dim E=2. \tag{1} Cd​=∥PE​Jd​k∥2,∣k∣=1,E⊂k⊥,dimE=2.(1)

The real skew tensors are the fixed, normalized candidates already used in the dimensional magnetic kernel:

Choose kkk uniformly on the sphere in each containing spatial space. Conditional on kkk, choose EEE uniformly among two-planes in k⊥k^\perpk⊥. This is a mathematical ensemble of relative preparations. It does not follow from nesting, transversality, or the particle's depth. An actual observer, binding geometry, or preparation may restrict this ensemble.

Both objects are polarized in each preparation. Fixing JdJ_dJd​ chooses the target's frame; it does not replace its polarization by an average. Independent isotropic rotations of both objects give the same relative ensemble. Correlations between their polarizations would change it.

All coordinates remain ordinary spatial directions of one manifold. Equation (2) describes a specified uniform rotational response tensor; it does not establish the full shape or field solution of either particle.

Six dimensions

For every kkk, J6kJ_6kJ6​k is a unit vector perpendicular to kkk. The space k⊥k^\perpk⊥ has dimension five. Its squared projection onto a uniformly oriented two-plane has the beta law

C6∼Beta⁡(1,32).(3) \boxed{C_6\sim\operatorname{Beta}\left(1,\frac32\right).} \tag{3} C6​∼Beta(1,23​).​(3)

To obtain this law, rotate the plane to the first two axes and express a uniform direction as a vector of five independent standard Gaussians divided by its norm. The numerator is the sum of two squared Gaussians; the remaining three supply the complementary denominator. The gamma ratio derivation and normalization are given in full in isotropic projection fractions.

Interpretation

This exact equality identifies which information a uniform isotropic tensor model retains. To obtain a distinctive five-dimensional response, the calculation needs additional derived spatial information: a restricted relative preparation, spatially varying response patterns, the allowed transition spectrum, or other terms of the same field's action. Those structures can be tested before any orientation average is taken.
