3. Electron-electron scattering is therefore not the same as electron-electron full-depth contact

Two electrons both have assigned depth six. A naive collision picture would ask whether their full Q6Q_6Q6​ coordinates coincide.

But ordinary electromagnetic scattering can instead proceed through the depth-two electromagnetic channel. Schematically,

6⟶2⟵6, 6\longrightarrow2\longleftarrow6, 6⟶2⟵6,

with interaction functional

Iee(2)=∫Q2Jˉe1→2(x)Kem(x,x′)Jˉe2→2(x′) dμ2(x)dμ2(x′). I_{ee}^{(2)} = \int_{Q_2} \bar J_{e_1\to2}(x) K_{\rm em}(x,x') \bar J_{e_2\to2}(x') \,d\mu_2(x)d\mu_2(x'). Iee(2)​=∫Q2​​Jˉe1​→2​(x)Kem​(x,x′)Jˉe2​→2​(x′)dμ2​(x)dμ2​(x′).

Consequently, the empirical fact that electron beams scatter readily does not by itself imply that two electron centers frequently achieve genuine six-dimensional coincidence.

This changes the interpretation of a possible collider test.

The observable electromagnetic Moller-scattering rate probes the projected d=2d=2d=2 interaction channel. A genuinely new effect from literal six-dimensional electron overlap would have to be separated from that ordinary projected interaction.

The experimentally interesting comparison is therefore

projected carrier interactionversusfull-depth contact/excitation. \boxed{ \text{projected carrier interaction} \quad\text{versus}\quad \text{full-depth contact/excitation}. } projected carrier interactionversusfull-depth contact/excitation.​

A rare full-depth process could coexist with an unsuppressed electromagnetic scattering rate.
