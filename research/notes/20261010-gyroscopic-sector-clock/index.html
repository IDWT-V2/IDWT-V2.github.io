Ordinary Spatial Rotation Planes and a Constant Coupling Clock

Updated October 10, 2026

An isotropic spatial rotation plane provides an alternative to separately matching displacement and rotation stiffnesses in the bending-wave action. Its two circular branches have an exactly constant frequency difference. This gives a concrete candidate for a slow coupling clock beneath a high particle mass-frequency. The result uses ordinary spatial displacement and rotation; it does not reinterpret physical dimensions as internal states.

The gyroscopic coefficient must be supplied by an actual background pattern of the same field. Holding that pattern fixed defines a conditional wave calculation. Its origin, reciprocal backreaction and support remain physical questions. The mixed-curvature action supplies one explicit positive same-field completion; retaining its entire spectrum shows when a constant-clock approximation is valid.
