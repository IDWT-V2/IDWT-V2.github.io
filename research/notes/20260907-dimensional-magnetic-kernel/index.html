Dimensional magnetic kernel comparison

Updated September 8, 2026

Physical premise (IDWT): There is one infinite-dimensional physical manifold. All sectors form one nested chain of submanifolds within it. Particle depth counts ordinary, literal macroscopic spatial dimensions. “Observed” and “unobserved” describe an observer's access to those dimensions, not different kinds of physical dimension. See spatial meaning and mathematical scope.

Summary: The constrained magnetic kernel distinguishes five-dimensional null-axis polarization from six-dimensional complex-structure polarization, while the scalar-exchange bridge explicitly supplies a five-dimensional vector-channel escape route.

Same kernel, different spinor moments

There is a genuine zero-response direction in five dimensions. No rescaling of the coupling removes it. More generally every skew matrix in an odd-dimensional real space is singular, since det⁡J=det⁡(−J)=(−1)ddet⁡J\det J=\det(-J)=(-1)^d\det JdetJ=det(−J)=(−1)ddetJ. Four-dimensional invertible skew matrices exist, so this parity result alone does not establish exactly six.

Relation to the spectral discriminator

The spectral comparison assumed an isotropic unrestricted bivector cost. For an isotropic envelope, the integrated magnetic projector merely rescales that coupling, preserving the corresponding gap multiplicities. For an anisotropic envelope the selected field is generally not proportional to JJJ.

Consequently the six-dimensional frozen-field excited gaps need not remain triply equal for an anisotropic envelope, even though its joint field-plus-ray minimum family remains CP3. These are different statements. Do not demand the isotropic-source singlet-triplet spectrum of every anisotropic magnetic preparation.

Physical scope and next test

This calculation tests a concrete part of the response kernel already in the archive. It omits orbital magnetic energy, electric response, radiation, spatial profile relaxation, nonuniform polarization, and the nucleus. Anisotropy should next be generated by the coupled envelope/nucleus solution, rather than chosen only as a diagnostic Fourier weight.

The useful discriminant is polarization response to controlled envelope anisotropy, or a preparation-resolved directional magnetic response. Whether the electron exhibits either specified response still needs a derived observable map. The mathematics does not identify an electron from CP3 geometry or an averaged cloud alone.
