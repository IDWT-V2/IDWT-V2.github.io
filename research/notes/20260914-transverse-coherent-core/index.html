Research / Mass and normalization

Separating the transverse modes of a mass core

Updated September 14, 2026

conditional calculation

The complete finite current hierarchy has a useful decomposition around one coherent particle core. Its many coefficient components do not each require a separate stability calculation. Every component transverse to the occupied coefficient ray has a complex-linear, self-adjoint spatial equation. The same is true of every initially empty lower grade. Only the occupied coefficient block retains the real/conjugate partner response studied in the spatial shape calculation.

This result assumes a fixed component frame and a spatially constant coherent coefficient ray. It concerns the formal linearized equation on the complete ordinary spatial domain. It does not prove nonlinear stability, settle the responding frame, or derive a physical spatial carrier for the prescribed simplex weights. Mass is the particle's selected oscillation frequency in equivalent units throughout.

Initially empty lower grades

Suppose M<NM<NM<N is initially empty. Its first current variation is zero because the current is quadratic in that grade's field. Its complete mass Hessian is consequently

DSmass,M,j[η]=ω0DM,d(1−q+qDj,d)B0η,0≤j≤M.(9) D S_{{\rm mass},M,j}[\eta] =\omega_0D_{M,d}\left(1-q+\frac q{D_{j,d}}\right)B_0\eta, \qquad 0\le j\le M. \tag{9} DSmass,M,j​[η]=ω0​DM,d​(1−q+Dj,d​q​)B0​η,0≤j≤M.(9)

There is no bjb_jbj​ term. Equations (6) and the shape projector still apply. These blocks are also complex-linear and self-adjoint, and they do not linearly force the occupied grade. In particular their coefficients are independent of the larger target grade. This is a spatial response version of the common-target identity.

The formal finite-tower linearization through the highest occupied grade therefore consists of the one occupied coefficient block, the transverse blocks (7), and the lower-grade blocks (9). Empty channels above the highest occupied grade can have a Lipschitz source without a derivative at zero; this decomposition does not assert otherwise. A spatially varying coefficient ray or a responding frame requires its connection terms to be examined separately.
