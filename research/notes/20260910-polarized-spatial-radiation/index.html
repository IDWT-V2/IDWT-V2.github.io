Polarized spatial radiation

Updated September 10, 2026

Summary: Both complex pulse polarizations determine an exact outgoing quadrupole source, whose full spatial spectral measure fixes radiated energy and a low-frequency suppression law.

A response channel of the same spatial field

The spatial displacement action uses a declared stationary component field with two ordinary bound coordinates, an existing propagation coordinate yyy, size λ\lambdaλ and tension T\mathcal TT. Two weak incoming displacement pulses are X=A(u)+B(v)X=A(u)+B(v)X=A(u)+B(v), where u=t−yu=t-yu=t−y, v=t+yv=t+yv=t+y. Their derivatives a=A′a=A'a=A′ and b=B′b=B'b=B′ have independent real spatial waveforms and complex Fourier polarizations.

The local collision calculation gives conservative reciprocal delays. The full spatial field can also carry energy away in its complementary quadrupole modes. This note calculates that channel to leading order in the weak incoming slopes. It assumes no independent incoming complementary excitation. These modes belong to the same component field; no mediator particle is added. Its identification with the physical master field remains conditional.
