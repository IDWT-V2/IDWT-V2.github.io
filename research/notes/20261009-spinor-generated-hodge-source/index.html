The Spinor Field Generates the Middle-Hodge Source

Updated October 9, 2026

The middle-form source used by the Hodge-contact candidate can be constructed directly from spatial spinor components of the same field. It need not be introduced as an unrelated tensor field. A common Clifford bilinear generates the complete linear middle-form carrier at every comparison depth, and its selfadjoint Hodge filter retains the previously calculated parity rule.

This establishes the source carrier, rather than a physical color symmetry. One spinor squared obeys nonlinear constraints; spatial rotations do not become an independent color action. The construction uses commuting classical field amplitudes. It does not identify the bilinear with an occupied particle state or impose a Grassmann quantum-field interpretation.

3. The Same Field Source Has the Checked Hodge Parity

Applying H=(∗+∗†)/2H=(*+*^\dagger)/2H=(∗+∗†)/2 gives

Independent Color Can Reside in the Ordinary Normal Spinor Factor

An explicit full ambient Clifford comparison supplies a different three-component carrier: the degree-two spinor factor of the ordinary six-normal space. It retains a complete four-dimensional Dirac spinor and gives an independent color action at depths three/four, with standard pair-Casimir contractions and a real metric normal connection. This normal triplet is not the symmetric-square spin-one source above. The same fixed projection loses added kinetic directions at five/six and ten, while a depth-two triplet remains kinematically possible. Joining the instantaneous Hodge filter to this independent color carrier requires a complete common source and action; matching component counts alone does not perform that join.

A Lower-Order Hermitian Color-Current Alternative

The Hermitian adjoint-current construction uses a bilinear with one conjugate spinor and separate normal color matrices. Its Hodge-filtered norm is fourth order in field amplitude, compared with the twelfth-order three-source holomorphic volume above. Both are proposed interactions with different source symmetries and dynamics. The new current has exact spin-dependent Fierz and pair formulas, preserves independent color at three/four, and retains the full kinetic field. A real sawtooth gives its density source, whereas a positive-frequency complex comb gives a different density spectrum. Their response tables must be calculated separately.
