Normalized oscillator modes

Updated September 10, 2026

Physical premise (IDWT): There is one infinite-dimensional physical manifold. All sectors form one nested chain of submanifolds within it. Particle depth counts ordinary, literal macroscopic spatial dimensions. “Observed” and “unobserved” describe an observer's access to those dimensions, not different kinds of physical dimension. See spatial meaning and mathematical scope.

Source and scope

Transcribed from Appendix B §20–20a, with representation-theory overclaims omitted. The associated historical code includes overlap_J near line 2282. These are scalar flat-space oscillator modes, not automatically V2 spinor, polarization, or orbital-section states.

Oij=∬(x⋅y)2∣χi(x)∣2∣χj(y)∣2 dx dy=⟨r2⟩i⟨r2⟩jd. O_{ij}=\iint(x\cdot y)^2|\chi_i(x)|^2|\chi_j(y)|^2\,dx\,dy =\frac{\langle r^2\rangle_i\langle r^2\rangle_j}{d}. Oij​=∬(x⋅y)2∣χi​(x)∣2∣χj​(y)∣2dxdy=d⟨r2⟩i​⟨r2⟩j​​.

Isotropy must hold for the actual density, for example an s-wave or a uniform shell mixture; an arbitrary individual oscillator eigenstate need not have it. With one mode fixed, this overlap grows linearly with the other shell index, unlike the degree-d cumulative count. It is a density-density expectation, not a normalized charged-current amplitude.

For actual coherent transitions use off-diagonal moments such as integral chi_i^*(x) x_a x_b chi_j(x) dx, retaining phases, angular indices, selection rules, and any spinor vertex.

∑k=0Nga(k)gb(N−k)=ga+b(N). \sum_{k=0}^{N}g_a(k)g_b(N-k)=g_{a+b}(N). k=0∑N​ga​(k)gb​(N−k)=ga+b​(N).

The generating functions multiply as (1−z)−a(1−z)−b=(1−z)−(a+b)(1-z)^{-a}(1-z)^{-b}=(1-z)^{-(a+b)}(1−z)−a(1−z)−b=(1−z)−(a+b). The a=b=3a=b=3a=b=3 example does not give the electron an inherent 3+3 split or prove colour neutrality. The simplex band moments instead average shell observables across a whole cumulative band.

Constant-background overlaps

A uniform continuation and an oscillator ground state have different overlap weights. For the physicists' Hermite polynomials in a dimensionless coordinate,

∫−∞∞H2m(y)e−y2/2 dy=2π(2m)!m!,∫−∞∞H2m+1(y)e−y2/2 dy=0. \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}H_{2m}(y)e^{-y^2/2}\,dy =\sqrt{2\pi}\frac{(2m)!}{m!},\qquad \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}H_{2m+1}(y)e^{-y^2/2}\,dy=0. ∫−∞∞​H2m​(y)e−y2/2dy=2π​m!(2m)!​,∫−∞∞​H2m+1​(y)e−y2/2dy=0.

To derive this, integrate the generating function e2ty−t2=∑jHj(y)tj/j!e^{2ty-t^2}=\sum_jH_j(y)t^j/j!e2ty−t2=∑j​Hj​(y)tj/j! against e−y2/2e^{-y^2/2}e−y2/2. The result is 2πet2\sqrt{2\pi}e^{t^2}2π​et2.

Thus overlap against a constant background removes odd modes by parity, but every even mode has a nonzero overlap. Normalized oscillator modes add nonzero prefactors and do not change these zeros. The constant is a generalized background on the line, not a normalizable oscillator state. A ground-state bra would supply another Gaussian and a different orthogonality relation.

This distinction matters for the cylindrical continuation premise: a low-degree insertion and parity alone do not exclude excitation in several added coordinates when the relevant modes are even. The actual vertex and integration weight must be supplied.

Finite spatial operators

The complete source marginal uses these normalized constant-background integrals in a finite matrix; its singular strengths follow a weighted Hockey-Stick identity. Physical bilinear currents must be formed before that linear map is applied. The spatial evaluation kernel uses the same paired-occupation weights at the oscillator center and fixes the distinction between spatial point contact and projective ray contact.

Review checks
