Normalized oscillator modes

Updated September 8, 2026

Physical premise (IDWT): There is one infinite-dimensional physical manifold. All sectors form one nested chain of submanifolds within it. Particle depth counts ordinary, literal macroscopic spatial dimensions. “Observed” and “unobserved” describe an observer's access to those dimensions, not different kinds of physical dimension. See spatial meaning and mathematical scope.

Density overlap versus transition matrix element

For isotropic normalized densities in the same R^d,

Oij=∬(x⋅y)2∣χi(x)∣2∣χj(y)∣2 dx dy=⟨r2⟩i⟨r2⟩jd. O_{ij}=\iint(x\cdot y)^2|\chi_i(x)|^2|\chi_j(y)|^2\,dx\,dy =\frac{\langle r^2\rangle_i\langle r^2\rangle_j}{d}. Oij​=∬(x⋅y)2∣χi​(x)∣2∣χj​(y)∣2dxdy=d⟨r2⟩i​⟨r2⟩j​​.

Isotropy must hold for the actual density, for example an s-wave or a uniform shell mixture; an arbitrary individual oscillator eigenstate need not have it. With one mode fixed, this overlap grows linearly with the other shell index, unlike the degree-d cumulative count. It is a density-density expectation, not a normalized charged-current amplitude.

For actual coherent transitions use off-diagonal moments such as integral chi_i^*(x) x_a x_b chi_j(x) dx, retaining phases, angular indices, selection rules, and any spinor vertex.
