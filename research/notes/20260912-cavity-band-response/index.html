Spatial and moving-cavity response

Updated September 12, 2026

Summary: Exact oscillator matrix elements connect cumulative state counts to polarized spatial response and energy-transfer distributions, while moving scalar and Dirac modes show why a frozen cavity overlap is only one part of coupling.

A spatial operator with a specified meaning

Let PN,dP_{N,d}PN,d​ project onto the normalized isotropic oscillator modes of total degree at most NNN in ddd ordinary spatial coordinates. Their common oscillator length is LLL and their count is DN=(N+dd)D_N=\binom{N+d}{d}DN​=(dN+d​). The operator

A(k)=eik⋅x,η=∣k∣2L22,(1) A(k)=e^{ik\cdot x},\qquad \eta=\frac{|k|^2L^2}{2}, \tag{1} A(k)=eik⋅x,η=2∣k∣2L2​,(1)

is a Fourier component of a declared scalar density probe. It is unitary on the complete spatial Hilbert space. Its compressed matrix can lose weight to modes outside a selected band.

These are conditional spatial controls. Positive density is not automatically electric charge or a Dirac scalar. A band trace represents specified equal weights; a coherent field instead requires its actual complex coefficients. The calculation neither changes the particle depth assignments nor adds compactified coordinates.
