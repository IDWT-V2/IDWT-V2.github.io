7. Correlation enters only where full higher-dimensional geometry is retained

For a projected interaction at depth aaa, unobserved coordinates have been marginalized and the relevant overlap is on QaQ_aQa​.

For a full-depth interaction, however, the conditional geometry matters. Let

Xb(t),Yb(t)∈Qb X_b(t),Y_b(t)\in Q_b Xb​(t),Yb​(t)∈Qb​

be two same-depth trajectories. The observer sees

πbaXb(t),πbaYb(t). \pi_{ba}X_b(t), \qquad \pi_{ba}Y_b(t). πba​Xb​(t),πba​Yb​(t).

A complete theory must determine

P ⁣(dist⁡Qb(Xb,Yb)∣dist⁡Qa(πXb,πYb)). P\!\left( \operatorname{dist}_{Q_b}(X_b,Y_b) \mid \operatorname{dist}_{Q_a}(\pi X_b,\pi Y_b) \right). P(distQb​​(Xb​,Yb​)∣distQa​​(πXb​,πYb​)).

There are three qualitatively distinct regimes:

  1. independent fibres: visible approach does not reduce unobserved separation;
  2. partially locked fibres: visible approach reduces but does not eliminate unobserved separation;
  3. diagonally locked/full correlation: minimizing projected separation dynamically selects small full separation as well.

The conditional electron–nucleus frame model supplies a mathematical example of the third type. It chooses an observer-relative coordinate grouping

V6=O⊕H≅R3⊕R3, V_6=O\oplus H\cong\mathbb R^3\oplus\mathbb R^3, V6​=O⊕H≅R3⊕R3,

within six ordinary spatial dimensions of the same kind. This is not an inherent physical split in the electron. The model additionally assumes a bound configuration with symmetry reduction

SO(3)O×SO(3)H⟶SO(3)diag. SO(3)_O\times SO(3)_H \longrightarrow SO(3)_{\rm diag}. SO(3)O​×SO(3)H​⟶SO(3)diag​.

At the locked orbit the mixed block satisfies

Q∈SO(3), Q\in SO(3), Q∈SO(3),

so the two three-frames are not independent. This supplies a geometric correlation between the two projected separations.

The sign of the actual binding coefficient κ\kappaκ remains uncalculated, so V2 has a mathematically explicit correlation mechanism but not yet a dynamical proof that nature selects it.
