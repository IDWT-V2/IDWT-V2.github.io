Scalar response of reconstructed simplex dynamics

Updated September 11, 2026

Summary: Reconstructed actions with identical simplex frequencies redistribute a fixed total scalar response between frequency branches, while their common local scalar interaction gives distinct spatial self-sources and reciprocal phase-sensitive mode transfer.

Compare the same interaction on the inverse actions

The inverse dynamics construction gives a family of local Hermitian Hamiltonians with the same complete simplex frequencies, using the geometric simplex polynomial. Coupling can test more than that spectrum. Here every candidate is given the same specified scalar interaction of the one physical field:

E[ψ]=⟨ψ,Ha,bψ⟩+g2∫J(x)2 dν(x),J=ψ†βψ.(1) \boxed{ E[\psi]=\langle\psi,H_{a,b}\psi\rangle+ \frac g2\int J(x)^2\,d\nu(x),\qquad J=\psi^\dagger\beta\psi. } \tag{1} E[ψ]=⟨ψ,Ha,b​ψ⟩+2g​∫J(x)2dν(x),J=ψ†βψ.​(1)

Use the canonically normalized physical spinor, with time-kinetic metric equal to the identity, and unit-volume spatial measure dνd\nudν. The general normalization rule applies if a different physical lift is specified. Equation (1) uses one real coefficient ggg, not an independent coefficient for every pair of occupied modes. Its relation to the frequency scale μ\muμ is not derived by the inverse construction.

The scalar current in (1) is a test of the written scalar interaction. It is not identified with electric charge. Both frequency branches and every form component of the field remain available; neither frequency sign is assigned an antiparticle interpretation.

The calculations use the specified actual spatial CP3 geometry with line connection O(2)\mathcal O(2)O(2). Its physical radius remains unknown, and no dimensions are compactified. The two-mode example below is a common-center spatial symmetry control, not a complete encounter of separated particles. Sector nesting does not imply particle containment.

The complete curved causality test excludes finite propagation for these fixed polynomial actions at unrestricted spatial frequencies. Their coupling calculations below remain conditional inverse constraints. A causal completion or a controlled effective regime has not been derived for them.
