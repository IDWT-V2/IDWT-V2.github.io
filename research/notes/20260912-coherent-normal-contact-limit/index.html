Coherent normal sources and the contact limit

Updated September 12, 2026

Summary: A canonically normalized coherent normal source approaches the full rank-one contact operator, while exact positive-source moments quantify the required concentration and divergent scalar self-energy cost.

A field realization of a narrow coherent source

The coherent contact construction has the required simplex eigenvalue when a point current on the occupied mode ray is assumed. A direct question is whether the smooth scalar kernel can approach that operator through an actual canonically normalized mode field. It can, with a substantial cost in the same interaction.

The finite source is a stationary mode preparation

The aligned coherent pair establishes the stationary and stability properties of the corresponding positive-density component action. A simple bound extends its finite energy maximum to the scalar Dirac current when the spatial kinetic operator is zero.

Retain every complex spinor component and both scalar signs. For each family, its scalar ray current jLj_LjL​ and positive norm density pLp_LpL​ obey ∣jL∣≤pL|j_L|\le p_L∣jL​∣≤pL​. At fixed canonical norms J,qJ,qJ,q,

For the last inequality, resolve each positive density into its spinor rows and apply the normalized symmetric-product bound to every pair of coefficient tensors. The single-family sharp coherent bound gives the two self-terms; the cross-product bound gives CNMJqC_{NM}JqCNM​Jq.

Aligned coherent tensors with one common scalar sign attain (10), with arbitrary allowed spinor polarizations in that sign space. The complete scalar gradients preserve both occupied tensors and give (9). Opposite common scalar signs give the same energy maximum; no antiparticle interpretation is assigned to them.

The equality conditions are common coherent coefficient direction and common scalar sign. On the finite product of the fixed-norm spheres, conservation of energy and both norms therefore gives nonlinear orbital stability of this entire maximizing set. This includes the scalar-sign and spinor variations in the declared zero-spatial-kinetic mode system. It establishes neither capture into that set nor stability of omitted spatial modes. In particular, it is not a finite-energy theorem for the singular M→∞M\to\inftyM→∞ limit.

A populated background also needs a selection rule

For a fixed collection of isotropic normal currents CM=qMIMC_M=q_MI_MCM​=qM​IM​, their scalar ray symbol is the constant Qf=∑MqMDMQ_f=\sum_Mq_MD_MQf​=∑M​qM​DM​. The cross source at every retained grade is consequently

The sum is finite, or explicitly convergent. It is independent of NNN. Populating a different normal band M=NM=NM=N for each target supplies a different preparation; a master derivation must explain that matching. The concentrated coherent source (1) is a distinct possibility with fixed canonical norm and an explicit contact limit, but it incurs the self-energy cost just calculated.

These results sharpen the physical operator problem. A viable reduction must supply its normal preparation, account for the complete energy, retain the target self-term and solve the spatial field equations. The rank-one projection itself now has an explicit finite-source approximation and quantitative bounds, without establishing a physical mass operator or changing any assigned particle dimensionality.
