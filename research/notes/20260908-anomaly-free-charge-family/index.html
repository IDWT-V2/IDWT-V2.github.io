Anomaly-free charge family

Updated September 8, 2026

What is being checked

This note gives a conditional consistency test for a complete specified charge table. It concerns representations of observed four-dimensional chiral fields, not additional physical spatial dimensions, and does not derive those fields from the nested spatial sectors.

Use only left-handed Weyl fields, conjugating right-handed fields:

Field SU(3)×SU(2)SU(3)\times SU(2)SU(3)×SU(2) Abelian charge
QQQ (3,2)(\mathbf3,\mathbf2)(3,2) qqq
ucu^cuc (3‾,1)(\overline{\mathbf3},\mathbf1)(3,1) uuu
dcd^cdc (3‾,1)(\overline{\mathbf3},\mathbf1)(3,1) ddd
LLL (1,2)(\mathbf1,\mathbf2)(1,2) lll
ece^cec (1,1)(\mathbf1,\mathbf1)(1,1) eee

The mixed non-Abelian, mixed gravitational, and cubic Abelian conditions are

2q+u+d=0,3q+l=0, 2q+u+d=0,\qquad 3q+l=0, 2q+u+d=0,3q+l=0, 6q+3u+3d+2l+e+n=0,6q3+3u3+3d3+2l3+e3+n3=0. 6q+3u+3d+2l+e+n=0, \qquad 6q^3+3u^3+3d^3+2l^3+e^3+n^3=0. 6q+3u+3d+2l+e+n=0,6q3+3u3+3d3+2l3+e3+n3=0.

The cubic SU(3)SU(3)SU(3) anomaly cancels between two fundamentals and two antifundamentals. There are four weak doublets counting colours, satisfying the usual even-doublet condition. These checks assume precisely this field content and chirality assignment.

A family of exact solutions

For one scalar doublet HHH of charge hhh, invariance of the ordinary Yukawa terms QHucQHu^cQHuc, QH†dcQH^\dagger d^cQH†dc, LH†ecLH^\dagger e^cLH†ec, and LHνcLH\nu^cLHνc requires, respectively,

q+h+u=0,q−h+d=0,l−h+e=0,l+h+n=0. q+h+u=0,\quad q-h+d=0,\quad l-h+e=0,\quad l+h+n=0. q+h+u=0,q−h+d=0,l−h+e=0,l+h+n=0.

The weak-index contractions use the invariant alternating tensor where needed; these equations display the Abelian charge sums. Combining them with l=−3ql=-3ql=−3q gives the family

(q,u,d,l,e,n)=(q,−q−h,−q+h,−3q,3q+h,3q−h). (q,u,d,l,e,n)=(q,-q-h,-q+h,-3q,3q+h,3q-h). (q,u,d,l,e,n)=(q,−q−h,−q+h,−3q,3q+h,3q−h).

Every displayed anomaly vanishes for arbitrary q,hq,hq,h. In particular, the cubic sum reduces to

6q3+3(−q−h)3+3(−q+h)3+2(−3q)3+(3q+h)3+(3q−h)3=0. 6q^3+3(-q-h)^3+3(-q+h)^3+2(-3q)^3 +(3q+h)^3+(3q-h)^3=0. 6q3+3(−q−h)3+3(−q+h)3+2(−3q)3+(3q+h)3+(3q−h)3=0.

In the field order (Q,uc,dc,L,ec,νc)(Q,u^c,d^c,L,e^c,\nu^c)(Q,uc,dc,L,ec,νc), define the conventional vectors

Y=(16,−23,13,−12,1,0),B−L=(13,−13,−13,−1,1,1). Y=\left(\frac16,-\frac23,\frac13,-\frac12,1,0\right), \qquad B-L=\left(\frac13,-\frac13,-\frac13,-1,1,1\right). Y=(61​,−32​,31​,−21​,1,0),B−L=(31​,−31​,−31​,−1,1,1).

Then the general displayed family is X=aY+b(B−L)X=aY+b(B-L)X=aY+b(B−L), with a=2ha=2ha=2h, b=3q−hb=3q-hb=3q−h, by direct substitution in each entry. The two-parameter freedom and its relation to the neutrino charge are also described in Allanach, Davighi and Melville, §3.1. The calculation here is an explicit sufficient family, not a classification of every spectrum or of every solution without the Yukawa assumptions. For a compact global U(1)U(1)U(1), compatible charge lattices impose further integrality conditions; rational examples already demonstrate nonuniqueness.

Imposing n=0n=0n=0 removes bbb in this family. Choosing h=1/2h=1/2h=1/2 then yields

(q,u,d,l,e,n)=(16,−23,13,−12,1,0). (q,u,d,l,e,n)=\left(\frac16,-\frac23,\frac13,-\frac12,1,0\right). (q,u,d,l,e,n)=(61​,−32​,31​,−21​,1,0).

Conjugation restores the usual right-handed charges. Neutrino neutrality and the normalization are extra conditions, or must follow from an independently derived geometric charge map. They do not follow solely from having three colours. Conversely, dropping νc\nu^cνc while calling the neutrino Dirac would omit a field from this anomaly calculation.

V2's existing determinant-trivial gauge carrier gives a particular charge assignment by a separate construction. The freedom here neither changes that assignment nor inserts a new gauge field into V2. It specifies what anomaly cancellation alone can establish, leaving the physical carrier, chirality, and occupied-mode bridge to be derived.
