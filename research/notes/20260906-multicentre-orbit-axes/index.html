Multicentre orbit axes

Updated September 10, 2026

Physical premise (IDWT): There is one infinite-dimensional physical manifold. All sectors form one nested chain of submanifolds within it. Particle depth counts ordinary, literal macroscopic spatial dimensions. “Observed” and “unobserved” describe an observer's access to those dimensions, not different kinds of physical dimension. See spatial meaning and mathematical scope.

Summary: Three-petal motion gives exact trigonal axes and a distinct 6D rotation gives tetrahedral axes; under bound-within/free-without, projected nuclear crossing is an encounter rather than an unobserved-depth miss.

The corrected molecular question

Molecular nuclei should not be inserted as generic fixed perturbations. The stronger proposal is that they settle along directions selected by a shared electron trajectory. The electron follows Hamiltonian motion in six coordinates; in a weak-force interval this approaches a straight line, while nuclear encounters bend it. Molecular shape may then be a closure and force-balance condition constrained by conservation of total momentum, energy, and the six-dimensional angular-momentum bivector.

There are two different apparent encounters:

  1. the orbit can project through a central nucleus while carrying nonzero coordinates unsupported by that nucleus;
  2. an outer nucleus can lie along a petal axis without lying at the electron's instantaneous radius.

The actual signed three-petal branch, rather than a modulus-folded six-lobe image, therefore supplies the trigonal axes directly.

Flexible nuclear three-planes

Let a nucleus carry a rank-three orthoprojector PaP_aPa​ in the electron's six-coordinate space. A minimal anisotropic distance family is

Allowing PPP to relax instantaneously with no orientation cost does not retain an anisotropic force. A rank-three plane in six dimensions can always be chosen either to contain the one separation vector Δq\Delta qΔq or to lie wholly perpendicular to it. Therefore

For η>0\eta>0η>0 the relaxed interaction is only a constant rescaling of 1/∣Δq∣1/|\Delta q|1/∣Δq∣. In the selected cylindrical limit η=0\eta=0η=0, the minimum is zero and the ideal Coulomb kernel becomes singular: a freely reorienting plane can turn until it intersects the electron. Complete cost-free flexibility is therefore unusable in either case. Finite inertia or stiffness is essential.

The physically useful interpretation of flexibility must be finite response. The nuclear plane needs rotational inertia, an orientation stiffness relative to its lower-depth structure, or a nonzero response time. It can then lag the electron, store angular momentum, and return it through backreaction. This is the same kind of dynamical susceptibility already isolated by the response modules, now applied to molecular closure. A fixed projector and an instantaneously minimized projector are the two controls on either side of that finite-response problem.
