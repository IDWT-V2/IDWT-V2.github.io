Why the reduced stability test misses a mode

Let AAA be the active four-coordinate space with two visible and two hidden coordinates. Linearization about q,p∈Aq,p\in Aq,p∈A and C∈so(A)C\in\mathfrak{so}(A)C∈so(A) splits the full 27-variable system into blocks of sizes

27=14+6+6+1.27=14+6+6+1.27=14+6+6+1.

The 14-block is the original active orbit. Each remaining spatial coordinate contributes its position, momentum, and four mixed source components. The last component rotates the two inactive directions and is constant to linear order. The blocks decouple exactly because the potential depends only on the full and visible squared radii and all inactive base coordinates vanish. Random-state checks give exactly zero off-block entries and active-block agreement to 2.23×10−162.23\times10^{-16}2.23×10−16.

At h=0.0128h=0.0128h=0.0128, the growing mode belongs to the third visible direction and its source components, not the third hidden direction. Its six-block has the reciprocal real pair 1.1384713,0.878370841.1384713,0.878370841.1384713,0.87837084, one unit-circle pair, and two unit multipliers. The hidden-side six-block has only unit-modulus multipliers within numerical accuracy. Omitting the third visible perturbation therefore removes a physically relevant instability from this diagnostic.
