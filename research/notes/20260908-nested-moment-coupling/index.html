Nested moment coupling

Updated September 8, 2026

Summary: The dot-square kernel on centered isotropic nested subspaces gives a minimum-depth Gram matrix of full rank, despite factorized bare coefficients.

Applying the specified quadratic kernel

This calculation applies the existing cross-sector moment kernel to an arbitrary finite nested chain. It is a flat spatial benchmark, or a tangent-chart calculation with the flat kernel explicitly adopted. It does not replace the actual curved sector geometry or derive its global interaction law.

Use a finite containing spatial space RD\mathbb R^DRD, with isometric embeddings Ed:Rd→RDE_d:\mathbb R^d\to\mathbb R^DEd​:Rd→RD whose images are nested. Set Pd=EdEdTP_d=E_dE_d^{\mathsf T}Pd​=Ed​EdT​. All these directions are ordinary spatial directions in the same containing manifold; the projectors express relations between subspaces, not an inherent split in any particle. Embedding a sector's moment tensor in this way is a coordinate identification inside the kernel. It does not assign a delta-sheet density to the particle or remove the lower-sector field's specified cylindrical continuation through the containing directions.

Md=∫xxTρd(x) dx. M_d=\int xx^{\mathsf T}\rho_d(x)\,dx. Md​=∫xxTρd​(x)dx.

Which projection would give rank one

The decomposition into the trace and traceless part in the containing space is

Pd=dDID+P~d,tr⁡P~d=0. P_d=\frac{d}{D}I_D+\widetilde P_d,\qquad \operatorname{tr}\widetilde P_d=0. Pd​=Dd​ID​+Pd​,trPd​=0.

Orthogonality in the Frobenius inner product gives

Ade=deD+tr⁡(P~dP~e),tr⁡(P~dP~e)=min⁡(d,e)−deD.(8) A_{de}=\frac{de}{D} +\operatorname{tr}(\widetilde P_d\widetilde P_e),\qquad \operatorname{tr}(\widetilde P_d\widetilde P_e)=\min(d,e)-\frac{de}{D}. \tag{8} Ade​=Dde​+tr(Pd​Pe​),tr(Pd​Pe​)=min(d,e)−Dde​.(8)

The first term has rank one. The second is a positive semidefinite Gram matrix and is generally nonzero. If the largest retained depth is DDD, its last row is zero and it has rank N−1N-1N−1: the remaining traceless projectors are independent, as follows from their successive spatial shells.

Keeping only the trace response would produce Gdetrace=uduede/DG^{\rm trace}_{de}=u_du_e de/DGdetrace​=ud​ue​de/D, with one common response. This is a definite additional projection of the interaction. It is not implied by each profile being isotropic on its own sector, because PdP_dPd​ is not isotropic on the entire containing space when d<Dd<Dd<D.

Equation (8) identifies the extra physical condition a scalar common-anchor mechanism would need in this model: the actual interaction must select the trace channel, or otherwise make the normalized response profiles proportional. It must also explain what happens to the traceless response, preserve the kinetic normalization, and establish the relation to the complete occupied mass operator.
