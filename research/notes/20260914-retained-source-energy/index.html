The complete energy of a retained spatial source

Updated September 14, 2026

conditional calculation

Mass is the selected particle frequency in equivalent units. A proposed same-field realization must also supply its corresponding inertial response. Once a sustaining mode is retained dynamically, its spatial kinetic energy and interactions belong in that calculation.

Stationary identity for a quartic action

Consider canonical Dirac fields Φj=e−iωjtϕj(x)\Phi_j=e^{-i\omega_jt}\phi_j(x)Φj​=e−iωj​tϕj​(x) with finite integrals and vanishing boundary terms. Write their complete energy as E=T+U2+U4E=T+U_2+U_4E=T+U2​+U4​, where TTT is the spatial Dirac kinetic energy, U2U_2U2​ is quadratic in the fields, and U4U_4U4​ is quartic. Here the symbols denote integrated quantities. Let Qj=∫ϕj†ϕjQ_j=\int\phi_j^\dagger\phi_jQj​=∫ϕj†​ϕj​.

Multiplying each stationary field equation by its conjugate and summing gives Euler's homogeneous-function identity,

∑jωjQj=T+U2+2U4,E=∑jωjQj−U4.(1) \sum_j\omega_jQ_j=T+U_2+2U_4, \qquad \boxed{E=\sum_j\omega_jQ_j-U_4.} \tag{1} j∑​ωj​Qj​=T+U2​+2U4​,E=j∑​ωj​Qj​−U4​.​(1)

Additional binding terms must contribute their actual homogeneous variations; a smoothed or fractional binding term cannot silently be counted as quartic. Equation (1) is not a formula for particle mass from amplitudes. It is a consistency test on the energy of the specified stationary solution and its canonical normalization.

For a pure cross interaction between target AAA and retained source BBB, let the target's other energy terms vanish on its solved branch. Then ωaQa=U×\omega_aQ_a=U_\timesωa​Qa​=U×​, while the normal equation gives ωbQb=Tb+U×\omega_bQ_b=T_b+U_\timesωb​Qb​=Tb​+U×​ if it has no other terms. The complete energy is therefore

E=ωaQa+Tb.(2) E=\omega_aQ_a+T_b. \tag{2} E=ωa​Qa​+Tb​.(2)

The algebraic homogeneous comparison has Tb=0T_b=0Tb​=0. A spatial source does not acquire this identity merely by reproducing the same target source matrix. Its kinetic expectation must be evaluated, along with any binding contributions omitted from the hypotheses of (2).

Spatial scaling supplies another necessary condition

Suppose the geometry is ordinary flat Rd\mathbb R^dRd, the coefficients are spatially constant, and all currents and boundary terms allow the norm-preserving variation ϕj(x)↦λd/2ϕj(λx)\phi_j(x)\mapsto\lambda^{d/2}\phi_j(\lambda x)ϕj​(x)↦λd/2ϕj​(λx). T+dU4=0,E=U2−(d−1)U4,∑jωjQj=U2−(d−2)U4.(3) T+dU_4=0,\qquad E=U_2-(d-1)U_4, \quad \sum_j\omega_jQ_j=U_2-(d-2)U_4. \tag{3} T+dU4​=0,E=U2​−(d−1)U4​,j∑​ωj​Qj​=U2​−(d−2)U4​.(3)

In particular, a massless canonical Dirac action with a nonnegative local quartic potential cannot have a nontrivial finite stationary solution of positive energy under these scaling hypotheses when d>1d>1d>1. This does not apply to the earlier inverse binding action, to a nonzero background without a justified subtraction, or to a different spatial geometry. It identifies which extra physical structure a proposed localized positive-frequency realization must account for.

Equations (1)–(3) follow directly from the stated action and admissible variations. A numerical stationary calculation should check the full equations, their energy identity and their spatial virial independently.
