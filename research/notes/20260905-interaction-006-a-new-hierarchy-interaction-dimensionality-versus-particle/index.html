6. A new hierarchy: interaction dimensionality versus particle dimensionality

The preceding results suggest classifying an interaction not only by which particles it connects, but by the depth aaa on which its effective overlap is evaluated.

For particles of depths bbb and ccc:

interaction codimension=(b−a)+(c−a). \boxed{ \text{interaction codimension} =(b-a)+(c-a). } interaction codimension=(b−a)+(c−a).​

This number does not automatically suppress the interaction because marginalization can remove the extra coordinates exactly. Instead it measures how much higher-dimensional information is discarded before the two currents meet.

This suggests a physical interpretation:

  • low-aaa interactions are insensitive to much of the particles' detailed higher-dimensional configuration;
  • high-aaa interactions retain more of that configuration and can therefore be more geometrically selective;
  • full-depth contact (a=b=ca=b=ca=b=c) is maximally sensitive to relative higher-dimensional position/orientation.

This gives a possible reason why a low-depth carrier can mediate a ubiquitous long-range force while genuinely full-depth restructuring is rare.
