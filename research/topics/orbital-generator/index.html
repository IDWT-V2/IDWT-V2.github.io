  • Localized polarization stationarity — With the same localized Gaussian envelope and magnetic kernel, uniform five-dimensional polarization has a nonzero spatial first variation whereas six-dimensional pure polarization is stationary.
  • Frozen magnetic texture test — A frozen-protocol test admits spatial polarization textures into the existing six-dimensional magnetic kernel and treats negative energy curvature as a failure of the uniform preparation.
  • Dimensional magnetic kernel comparison — The constrained magnetic kernel distinguishes five-dimensional null-axis polarization from six-dimensional complex-structure polarization, while the scalar-exchange bridge explicitly supplies a five-dimensional vector-channel escape route.
  • Accurate cylindrical correction — Accuracy-controlled fixed-Casimir shooting revisits the rejected cylindrical candidates with analytic derivatives and independent endpoint checks.
  • Dimensional necessity from response — A fixed-preparation hidden-response rank theorem gives a conditional six-dimensional lower bound, while exact five-dimensional dynamics and averaging counterexamples identify what the existing constructions do not require.
  • Five-dimensional CP3 selection and spectral discrimination — A five-dimensional spinor-bivector model also selects a CP3 minimum family, but its exact spectral sum rule excludes the six-dimensional singlet-triplet gap within the linear-bivector operator class.
