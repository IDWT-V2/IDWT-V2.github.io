operator-tools

Self-contained mathematical tools for V2 interaction calculations. Research index lists the notes across all topics.

  • Causal constraint on reconstructed simplex dynamics — A full curved-space commutator excludes a finite propagation speed for the fixed all-band polynomial class; causal nonlinear particle spectra remain open.

  • Reconstructing dynamics with exact simplex frequencies — A complete inverse polynomial class gives exact paired frequencies and retains complementary spinor kinetics, with the action fixed by its canonical norm.

  • Deriving the simplex polynomial from spatial covariant derivatives — The first purely holomorphic differential annihilator on the specified CP3 line bundle has an adjoint square with the exact simplex spectrum, giving a geometric construction of the polynomial and a local extension to its complete Dirac pair.

  • Inheriting the projective mass candidate — The normal bundle supplies the connection needed by the CP3 simplex candidate, but complete form degrees and normal Dirac derivatives prevent an automatic common mass construction at depths six and ten.

  • A local simplex operator on projective spatial spinors — A local polynomial in the squared Dirac operator gives exact simplex values and a Hermitian scalar insertion on a specified CP3 spinor family, with its full additional spectrum, bundle assumptions and kinetic contribution retained.

  • Transfer of the simplex operator to spatial spinors — A complete Killing-spinor frame transfers the scalar simplex operator to a local spinor operator, but its two Dirac branches and the real action distinguish this spectrum from an algebraic scalar mass.

  • A conformal spatial operator with the simplex spectrum — The critical conformal scalar operator on a round even-dimensional sphere has exactly the normalized simplex spectrum, while its spinor realization, spatial geometry and master interaction remain additional requirements.

  • Green function marginal — Full flat-fibre integration of a normalized Laplace Green function gives the lower-dimensional Green function, while restriction to a slice does not.

  • Projected bilocal variation — Varying an exchange-symmetric interaction between projected densities retains the channel projector and one kernel convolution in each mean-field term.

  • Colour singlet criterion — Vanishing mean colour charge is weaker than the singlet condition; the positive total quadratic Casimir distinguishes them exactly.

  • Angular kernel parity — The dot-square angular integral has an exact finite spectrum, while its density-sourced Hartree potential acts by multiplication and preserves parity.

  • Dirac heat curvature — The untwisted spatial Dirac heat kernel has local relative coefficient minus scalar curvature over twelve, with an explicit round-sphere spectral benchmark.

  • Anomaly-free charge family — The one-family anomaly equations admit a hypercharge and B-minus-L family when a right-handed neutrino is included; selecting observed charges needs additional conditions.

  • Interaction operator tools — Self-contained tools for spatial projection, localized profiles, interaction operators, and conservation laws.

  • Gaussian contact profiles — Normalized Gaussian spatial densities give an exact contact overlap, force, and covariance rule under marginalization onto shared coordinates.

  • Confining oscillator domain — The harmonic spatial operator has a closed energy form, compact resolvent, and an explicit eigenbasis whose Gaussian tails justify specified boundary limits.

  • Fibre-integrated stress — Integrating a conserved stress tensor over ordinary spatial fibres yields a lower-depth balance law with explicit metric, boundary-flux, and sampling terms.

  • Spectral analysis tools — Exact tools for recurrence, continuum response, spectral robustness, Gaussian profile geometry, simplex regularization, and statistical calibration.

  • Spectral survival recurrence — Pure-point spectral evolution has a positive mean return weight and arbitrarily accurate recurrences, while an integrable continuous spectral density permits decay of the return amplitude.

  • Continuum mode memory — Eliminating a coupled continuum produces an exact memory kernel, with exponential decay available only under additional weak-coupling and spectral assumptions.

  • Bounded spectral perturbations — A bounded self-adjoint perturbation preserves compact resolvent, bounds every ordered eigenvalue shift, and preserves isolated spectral-cluster rank under an explicit gap condition.

  • Gaussian mode geometry — Normalized positive Gaussian profiles have an exact parameter-space metric determined by their mean and covariance, with zero Berry curvature for this real family.

  • Simplex spectral zeta — Explicit analytic continuation of the one-eigenvalue-per-index simplex tower gives its value and derivative at zero, including the normalization dependence of its zeta determinant.

  • Isotropic projection fractions — An isotropically prepared spatial direction has a beta-distributed squared projection onto a fixed subspace, with explicit moments and observer-relative momentum bookkeeping.

  • Fixed-null calibration — A specified null model permits exact combination of independent match probabilities, explicit treatment of probability floors, and honest finite-simulation uncertainty.

  • Shared time coordinate — All nested spatial sectors use one time coordinate; this premise neither equates proper times nor supplies a metric or causal theorem.

  • Centroid-relative self-binding — Centering the auxiliary scalar moment functional yields an explicit translation-covariant self-binding model with zero net self-force.

  • Cylindrical barrier separation — A barrier continued cylindrically has no route around it through the added coordinates; a separated Hamiltonian reduces each transverse mode to a longitudinal crossing problem.

  • Nested sector dynamics — Cylindrical function lifts require a projection, and exact inheritance of dynamics additionally requires an invariant domain and an intertwining operator relation.

  • Simplex reciprocal sum — Reciprocal simplex counts have an exact telescoping sum and truncation remainder, providing a conditional trace check for the V2 count spectrum.

  • Simplex band level moments — Uniform counting over a cumulative oscillator band gives exact mean and variance of the excitation level, without selecting a physical occupation or mass correction.

  • Weighted mode evaluation — A weighted coefficient norm bounds point evaluation exactly when the corresponding inverse-weighted evaluation sum is finite; counts alone do not establish that condition.

  • Simplex heat trace — The tower with one eigenvalue per simplex index has an explicit heat-trace bound and convergence domain, distinct from the spatial dimension of a sector operator.

  • Round-sphere Dirac counts — The round-sphere Dirac spectrum has a dimension-dependent spinor multiplicity, giving two times the simplex count on S3 and four times it on S5.

  • Nested spatial tools — Tools for common time, centred self-binding, cylindrical barriers, and inherited evolution on nested spatial sectors.

  • Counting mode tools — Exact counting and spectral tools with explicit operator, normalization, and occupation assumptions.

  • Coulomb exponential profiles — Normalized exponential profiles give exact Coulomb integrals, a two-particle variational bound, and finite-range contact estimates.

  • Zonal hybrid geometry — Spherical-harmonic addition gives exact directed-state overlaps and conditional trigonal, tetrahedral, and octahedral orthogonality.

  • Cyclic resonance spectrum — A nearest-neighbour amplitude ring has exact Fourier resonances, paired levels, and a calculable response to alternating couplings.

  • Dipole dispersion response — Second-order dipole coupling yields an attractive inverse-sixth-power energy with a coefficient fixed by the specified excitation response.

  • Radial confinement bounds — The radial spatial measure fixes the centrifugal term, while a Hardy bound gives an explicit sufficient condition excluding levels below a saturating threshold.

  • Chiral pseudoscalar mass term — A specified leading chiral action makes pseudoscalar mass squared proportional to light-quark masses, with the response scale left as matched input.

  • Resonant quartet dynamics — A conservative quartic amplitude interaction has exact action invariants and a detuning-controlled exchange law under specified pump preparation.

  • Oscillator rotation branching — Harmonic-polynomial decomposition gives exact angular multiplicities for a flat oscillator shell and its restriction to an observer-selected spatial three-plane.

  • Resonance and profile tools — Tools for Coulomb profiles, angular functions, cyclic and quartic resonance, dispersion response, radial bounds, and pseudoscalar symmetry protection.
