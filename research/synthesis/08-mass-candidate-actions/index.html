Current mass-operator candidates

Updated November 10, 2026

conditional synthesis

The mass problem is to explain how one same-field law produces and sustains the known particle frequencies, their spatial structures and their interactions. Frequency already expresses mass in equivalent units, mc2=ℏωmc^2=\hbar\omegamc2=ℏω. An assigned frequency, a normalized coefficient vector, a supported core and a complete finite-energy particle are different achievements. Current constructions establish several of these steps conditionally, but no action below yet supplies the whole chain.

The candidates include common-fundamental portion actions, count-feedback actions, finite current hierarchies and material-plus-vacuum constructions. The newer portion route has compact support and a proposed relativistic cluster Hamiltonian; the older routes supply explicit count-frequency equations, polarization-sensitive responses, reference cores, local exits and constructed exteriors. Each has its own kinetic law, geometry and domains. A theorem for one does not establish the same result for another.

Physical spacetime has infinitely many ordinary spatial dimensions plus time. Matter occupies up to ten spatial dimensions; all remaining spatial directions are unbound for every particle. Field components and coefficient spaces do not add physical dimensions. A lower-depth pattern requires a confinement mechanism. No compactification, Kaluza–Klein tower or mediator particle is used. Classical internal field oscillations do not replace an electron's literal six-dimensional trajectory around a nucleus; its proposed external orbit using four spatial directions is a separate motion question. See physical premises, spatial carriers and orbits and binding.
