2. The occupied quark bases

The committed up-type occupied modes are

(nu,nc,nt)=(3,20,72), (n_u,n_c,n_t)=(3,20,72), (nu​,nc​,nt​)=(3,20,72),

at depth

du=4, d_u=4, du​=4,

while the down-type sector contains

nd=1,ns=4, n_d=1, \qquad n_s=4, nd​=1,ns​=4,

and the bottom state is the critical adjacent-band pair

(nb,−,nb,+)=(16,17). (n_{b,-},n_{b,+})=(16,17). (nb,−​,nb,+​)=(16,17).

Its mass is

mb=μ3S(16,3)S(17,3). m_b =\mu_3\sqrt{S(16,3)S(17,3)}. mb​=μ3​S(16,3)S(17,3)​.

Thus the physical three-generation spaces may be written schematically as

Fu=span⁡{∣u⟩,∣c⟩,∣t⟩}, \mathcal F_u =\operatorname{span}\{|u\rangle,|c\rangle,|t\rangle\}, Fu​=span{∣u⟩,∣c⟩,∣t⟩},

Fd=span⁡{∣d⟩,∣s⟩,∣b⟩}. \mathcal F_d =\operatorname{span}\{|d\rangle,|s\rangle,|b\rangle\}. Fd​=span{∣d⟩,∣s⟩,∣b⟩}.

The first two down states are ordinary occupied coherent bands. The bottom state is already exceptional because it is represented by a two-band singular-value construction. Any CKM derivation must respect that distinction rather than replacing the bottom state by an arbitrary ordinary mode.

5. Why the mass hierarchy does not determine CKM

The simplex mass laws determine diagonal mass eigenvalues:

Mu=μ4diag⁡(S(3,4),S(20,4),S(72,4)), M_u =\mu_4\operatorname{diag} \left( S(3,4), S(20,4), S(72,4) \right), Mu​=μ4​diag(S(3,4),S(20,4),S(72,4)),

while the down sector has the schematic form

Md=μ3diag⁡(S(1,3),S(4,3),S(16,3)S(17,3)). M_d =\mu_3\operatorname{diag} \left( S(1,3), S(4,3), \sqrt{S(16,3)S(17,3)} \right). Md​=μ3​diag(S(1,3),S(4,3),S(16,3)S(17,3)​).

These spectra fix eigenvalues but not the relative orientation of the up and down eigenbases.

For any unitary matrices UuU_uUu​ and UdU_dUd​,

Mu↦UuMuUu†, M_u \mapsto U_uM_uU_u^\dagger, Mu​↦Uu​Mu​Uu†​,

Md↦UdMdUd† M_d \mapsto U_dM_dU_d^\dagger Md​↦Ud​Md​Ud†​

preserves the eigenvalue spectra. The charged-current mixing matrix is

VCKM=Uu†Ud. V_{\rm CKM}=U_u^\dagger U_d. VCKM​=Uu†​Ud​.

This is the usual up-row/down-column W+W^+W+ convention. The down-row/up-column matrix V−V_-V−​ used for the preceding cross-sector kernel is its adjoint, V−=Ud†UuV_-=U_d^\dagger U_uV−​=Ud†​Uu​; the two must not be given the same matrix label.

Therefore the successful simplex mass hierarchy alone cannot determine CKM. A separate geometric object fixing the relative orientation of the two three-dimensional flavor frames is mathematically necessary.

Any proposal that constructs CKM solely from the six quark masses, with no independently derived cross-sector operator, adds an extra ansatz rather than following from the present V2 mass theorem.

8. A finite matrix problem

The CKM calculation can be reduced to a finite operator computation.

Let the up-sector normalized occupied states be

∣U⟩=(∣3,4⟩,∣20,4⟩,∣72,4⟩), |U\rangle = \left( |3,4\rangle, |20,4\rangle, |72,4\rangle \right), ∣U⟩=(∣3,4⟩,∣20,4⟩,∣72,4⟩),

and let the down-sector states be

∣D⟩=(∣1,3⟩,∣4,3⟩,∣b,3⟩), |D\rangle = \left( |1,3\rangle, |4,3\rangle, |b,3\rangle \right), ∣D⟩=(∣1,3⟩,∣4,3⟩,∣b,3⟩),

where ∣b,3⟩|b,3\rangle∣b,3⟩ is the normalized singular vector of the 161616--171717 bottom block.

Define

Kij=⟨Di∣P3D∞P4∣Uj⟩. K_{ij} = \langle D_i| P_3D_\infty P_4 |U_j\rangle. Kij​=⟨Di​∣P3​D∞​P4​∣Uj​⟩.

Remove the derived universal polarization and weak-coupling factors and use the independently derived kinetic metrics and mass-basis rotations:

VCKMV2=(Ld†Zd−1/2KZu−1/2Lu)†. \boxed{ V_{\rm CKM}^{\rm V2} =\left(L_d^\dagger Z_d^{-1/2}KZ_u^{-1/2}L_u\right)^\dagger. } VCKMV2​=(Ld†​Zd−1/2​KZu−1/2​Lu​)†.​

1. An explicit globally admissible bundle

Let L=O(1) on CP5 and choose

Eg=(L−2⊗C3)⊕(L3⊗C2). E_g=(L^{-2}\otimes\mathbb C^3)\oplus(L^3\otimes\mathbb C^2). Eg​=(L−2⊗C3)⊕(L3⊗C2).

The determinant is L^(-6+6)=the trivial line. Hermitian metrics and a chosen parallel determinant trivialization reduce its frame bundle to S(U(3) times U(2)). This is a real global construction, not local trace subtraction on the tangent bundle. The group is the usual (SU(3) times SU(2) times U(1))/Z6 subgroup of SU(5).

The integer block charges (-2,3) are the primitive solution of 3a+2b=0, up to simultaneous sign reversal. Dividing by six gives the existing hypercharge generator Y=diag(-1/3,-1/3,-1/3,1/2,1/2). The choice of degree-one L and of this auxiliary carrier is an additional construction, not a background selection derived from the master action.

For a unitary connection a on L and traceless unitary connections A_c,A_w on the displayed trivial multiplicity bundles, a compatible connection is

∇g=diag⁡(d−2aI3+Ac, d+3aI2+Aw). \nabla^g=\operatorname{diag}(d-2aI_3+A_c,\ d+3aI_2+A_w). ∇g=diag(d−2aI3​+Ac​, d+3aI2​+Aw​).

The expression is interpreted in local line frames with their transition functions; the global tensor-product connection is what is defined. Its trace is zero and its mixed blocks vanish. With A_c=A_w=0, its actual holonomy is only a U(1) subgroup; the allowed structure group is not a proof that every non-Abelian gauge interaction is dynamically generated.

4. Fibre multiplicities are not zero-mode multiplicities

If one were additionally to use the ordinary spin Dirac operator on CP5 twisted by a line L^q, its index would be

ind⁡(D⊗Lq)=χ(O(q−3))=(q+25), \operatorname{ind}(D\otimes L^q)=\chi(\mathcal O(q-3)) =\binom{q+2}{5}, ind(D⊗Lq)=χ(O(q−3))=(5q+2​),

where the binomial is the polynomial extension for negative arguments and K^(1/2)=O(-3). For the charges q=(-4,-3,0,1,2,6) this gives (-6,-1,0,0,0,56), not one mode per representation. This is a conditional diagnostic of a particular twisted-Dirac interpretation, not a compactification premise imposed on V2. It prevents declaring a complete particle spectrum merely from the 16-dimensional exterior fibre.
