Same-field coupling across sectors

Updated November 5, 2026

conditional synthesis

IDWT seeks interactions between actual spatial patterns of Ψ∞\Psi_\inftyΨ∞​, with both partners responding under one law. The field occupies the single physical spacetime with ten ordinary spatial directions plus time. A particle's depth describes its dimensional involvement; it does not create a separate containing world or an internal substitute for position.

There are no mediator particles or particle-exchange force mechanisms in this description. A response kernel obtained by eliminating some modes is a conditional reduction of the same field's equations. Its mathematical resemblance to a familiar screened inverse operator does not establish an exchanged particle or a physical Yukawa mechanism.

The guiding geometric hypothesis is changing compatibility. Each pattern may deform or reorient the other toward greater compatibility or toward incompatibility during an encounter. Both signs are allowed. Shared spatial support supplies access, while polarization, relative phase and spatial response can prevent effective engagement. Automatic alignment, an imposed binary matching rule and a separately fitted coefficient for every particle pair do not follow from the hypothesis. The generalized coupling framework states the common variational structure and its existing spatial controls.

Neutrinos and the depth-two control

The photon is the explicit depth-two transverse comparison, with linear, circular and elliptical polarization retained. Higher-depth massive patterns need not obey the same transverse restriction. Coupling may be driven by higher-dimensional operations whose response descends to lower patterns; a photon-led two-plane formula is not the only possible same-field law.

The full photon transition calculation sums target-polarization changes for a specified spatial Pauli term. A selected incident polarization can be dark, but the inclusive response of that probe has a positive bound across its tested depths. Under its stated ensemble, five- and six-dimensional comparisons give identical mean response, EG=(4/5)I2\mathbb E G=(4/5)I_2EG=(4/5)I2​. The depth-five working hypothesis instead asks whether one phase-locked standing pattern folded through five ordinary directions has a comparatively fixed encounter axis and narrow orientation/phase acceptance. Its two apparent rotation-plane views are not independent fitted oscillators. The dense center need not dominate contact; directional outer waves can matter. These remain hypotheses for the complete spatial equations. A fixed sector orientation, a chosen neutral projector or a special depth-five coefficient does not test them. Depths 2,3,4,6,102,3,4,6,102,3,4,6,10 provide necessary same-law comparisons, including whether an apparent effect is merely generic to odd depth.

A neutrino's zero ordinary electric charge does not set every interaction to zero. Neither a low mass unit nor a small unnormalized vertex supplies an observed encounter probability. Extended weak tails are permitted, but their actual response and total conserved energy must be calculated rather than inferred from their existence.

The common physical target

The target is a family of different selected mass-frequencies with groups of related physical response under one field law and one canonical normalization. Actions that reproduce the same masses but arbitrary unrelated responses exhibit underdetermination, rather than deriving that pattern. Self-coupling may sustain localization and set the clock; pair coupling must use the same field dynamics, including each supporting stress and the response of both objects.

The calculations in this volume establish conditional reciprocity, normalization constraints, complete polarization data, geometric selection examples and several dynamical response mechanisms. They do not identify every occupied particle pattern or derive its physical charge, complete force law or rate. They also do not replace the electron's literal external trajectory around a nucleus with an internal mode calculation. The missing bridge is a normalized, spatially viable common field solution and its reciprocal interactions, assessed at all maintained depths with the same assumptions.
