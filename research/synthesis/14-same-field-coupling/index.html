Physical scope

All directions belonging to a given particle are of the same spatial kind. A decomposition relative to another particle's submanifold describes their relation; it does not give either particle an inherent division into different kinds of space.

It assumes specified spatial maps, measures, mode subspaces, and an interaction channel. It does not require accepting a particular list of global sphere or projective topologies. Actual embeddings and their domain conditions must be supplied wherever a proposed topology is used.

IDWT has no mediator particles. The source profiles, response modes, and representation indices below describe the one field on this physical space. Coupling starts with its direct interaction terms. Eliminating some of its modes is a conditional reduction of those equations, not a particle-exchange mechanism.

The working coupling hypothesis includes a changing geometric match: each particle can drive the other's spatial pattern toward greater compatibility or toward incompatibility during an encounter. Bilateral polarization coupling separates that physical target from a restricted orientation benchmark whose relative overlap remains constant. Both the change in compatibility and its effect on subsequent interaction must follow from the same dynamics.
