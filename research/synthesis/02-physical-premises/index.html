Physical premises and nested spatial sectors

Updated September 24, 2026

conditional synthesis

Depth counts ordinary physical spatial dimensions in one nested manifold. A particle pattern may rotate independently of its sector. Lower-dimensional sectors are nested, but particle solutions at different depths need not be restrictions of one another.

The equations and numerical values below retain the assumptions of their linked source notes. Results from different actions are not combined into one physical mechanism.

2. Bound coordinates and free coordinates

For nested depths a<ba<ba<b, write the higher coordinate space locally as

Ξb≃Ξa×Fb/a, \Xi_b\simeq\Xi_a\times F_{b/a}, Ξb​≃Ξa​×Fb/a​,

The lower-depth particle has no profile that must be aimed at a point of the extra fibre. It spans that fibre because nothing in its spatial mode distinguishes one fibre point from another.

The same state is compatible with, but not forced by, a free outer Hamiltonian. In a direction with no binding potential,

Hfree=−ℏ22μ∂y2. H_{\mathrm{free}} =-\frac{\hbar^2}{2\mu}\partial_y^2. Hfree​=−2μℏ2​∂y2​.

3. The cross-depth contact theorem

Let ρa(x)\rho_a(x)ρa​(x) be a density or current carried by the depth-aaa mode, and let ρb(x,y)\rho_b(x,y)ρb​(x,y) be one carried by the depth-bbb mode. From the higher-depth side, the lower-depth quantity is cylindrical:

ρa↑b(x,y)=ρa(x). \rho_a^{\uparrow b}(x,y)=\rho_a(x). ρa↑b​(x,y)=ρa​(x).

For an allowed interaction local on Ξb\Xi_bΞb​, the overlap is

Iab=∫Ξadμa(x)∫Fb/adμF(y) ρa(x)ρb(x,y). I_{ab} = \int_{\Xi_a}d\mu_a(x) \int_{F_{b/a}}d\mu_F(y)\, \rho_a(x)\rho_b(x,y). Iab​=∫Ξa​​dμa​(x)∫Fb/a​​dμF​(y)ρa​(x)ρb​(x,y).

Define the pushforward, or marginal, of the higher-depth density:

ρˉb→a(x)=(πba)∗ρb(x)=∫Fb/aρb(x,y) dμF(y). \bar\rho_{b\to a}(x) = (\pi_{ba})_*\rho_b(x) = \int_{F_{b/a}}\rho_b(x,y)\,d\mu_F(y). ρˉ​b→a​(x)=(πba​)∗​ρb​(x)=∫Fb/a​​ρb​(x,y)dμF​(y).

Fubini's theorem then gives

Iab=∫Ξaρa(x)ρˉb→a(x) dμa(x). \boxed{ I_{ab} = \int_{\Xi_a} \rho_a(x)\bar\rho_{b\to a}(x)\,d\mu_a(x) }. Iab​=∫Ξa​​ρa​(x)ρˉ​b→a​(x)dμa​(x)​.

This is the cross-depth contact theorem. The higher-depth particle contributes through its complete marginal over the extra coordinates, and the cylindrical lower-depth extension covers the entire fibre. The overlap therefore carries the full marginal weight.

If ρb\rho_bρb​ is a normalized nonnegative density, its whole weight survives the pushforward:

∫Ξaρˉb→a dμa=∫Ξbρb dμb=1. \int_{\Xi_a}\bar\rho_{b\to a}\,d\mu_a = \int_{\Xi_b}\rho_b\,d\mu_b =1. ∫Ξa​​ρˉ​b→a​dμa​=∫Ξb​​ρb​dμb​=1.

This is the precise sense in which contact is full: the interaction integrates the extra coordinates over the complete fibre.

For a nonlocal or filtered interaction, replace the pointwise product by a kernel on the shared coordinates:

Iab[K]=∫Ξa×Ξaρa(x)Kab(x,x′)ρˉb→a(x′) dμa(x)dμa(x′). I_{ab}[K] = \int_{\Xi_a\times\Xi_a} \rho_a(x)K_{ab}(x,x')\bar\rho_{b\to a}(x') \,d\mu_a(x)d\mu_a(x'). Iab​[K]=∫Ξa​×Ξa​​ρa​(x)Kab​(x,x′)ρˉ​b→a​(x′)dμa​(x)dμa​(x′).

Unequal depth determines how the modes meet. The kernel, coupling constant, and representation content determine what they do when they meet.

4. Noncompact normalization

On an infinite fibre, the cylindrical pullback πba∗χa\pi_{ba}^{*}\chi_aπba∗​χa​ can be used as a translation-invariant generalized state or as a field/observable. These uses need distinct normalizations:

  • a cylindrical field or observable pulled back from Ξa\Xi_aΞa​;
  • a generalized momentum eigenstate with delta normalization; or
  • a box-normalized approximation, whose density contains an explicit inverse fibre-volume factor.

The particle's normalization over its spatial support remains

∫Ξa∣χa(x)∣2dμa(x)=1. \int_{\Xi_a}|\chi_a(x)|^2d\mu_a(x)=1. ∫Ξa​​∣χa​(x)∣2dμa​(x)=1.

The cross-depth interaction of a cylindrical field is finite when the higher-depth density supplies an integrable fibre factor. This does not make it equal to the overlap of two probability-normalized states. In a box of fibre volume VFV_FVF​, a normalized uniform extension has density ρa(x)/VF\rho_a(x)/V_Fρa​(x)/VF​; its overlap with a fixed normalized ρb\rho_bρb​ is Iab/VFI_{ab}/V_FIab​/VF​, tending to zero as the box grows. Cancelling that factor requires a specified flux, source-density, or coupling normalization in the action. It does not follow from Fubini's theorem. The contact formula above uses the cylindrical-field convention, normalized on the physical spatial sector.

5. Reciprocity

Let the interaction part of the action be

Sab=gab∫dt Iab. S_{ab}=g_{ab}\int dt\,I_{ab}. Sab​=gab​∫dtIab​.

For the local form, functional differentiation gives

δSabδρa(x)=gabρˉb→a(x), \frac{\delta S_{ab}}{\delta\rho_a(x)} =g_{ab}\bar\rho_{b\to a}(x), δρa​(x)δSab​​=gab​ρˉ​b→a​(x),

and

δSabδρb(x,y)=gabρa(x). \frac{\delta S_{ab}}{\delta\rho_b(x,y)} =g_{ab}\rho_a(x). δρb​(x,y)δSab​​=gab​ρa​(x).

The lower-depth mode feels the higher-depth mode through the latter's complete marginal. The higher-depth mode feels the lower-depth mode uniformly across the additional fibre. These are two variations of the same interaction term, so reciprocity follows when the action and kernel have the corresponding exchange symmetry. Both objects remain in their respective sectors.

In IDWT's one-field language, ρa\rho_aρa​ and ρb\rho_bρb​ are sector components of one field Ψ∞\Psi_\inftyΨ∞​. A cross-sector term is that field coupling two of its own depth-resolved structures on their shared coordinates.

Literal macroscopic spatial dimensions

IDWT has one infinite-dimensional physical manifold, M∞M_\inftyM∞​. Every particle sector is a nested spatial submanifold of that same manifold:

Qa↪Qb↪M∞,a<b. Q_a\hookrightarrow Q_b\hookrightarrow M_\infty,\qquad a<b. Qa​↪Qb​↪M∞​,a<b.

This inclusion relates spatial sectors. Individual particles are field structures associated with the sectors; it does not place one particle inside another or make it a constituent of another. In particular, an electron state need not be the restriction of a tau state. A restriction of bundles compares the available field components across sectors; identifying particular particle solutions through that restriction is an additional dynamical assumption.

Depth ddd counts ordinary, literal macroscopic spatial dimensions of QdQ_dQd​. They are all the same kind of physical dimension. Their coordinates specify position, separation, and motion. A sector is not an internal state space, a compactified auxiliary space, or a configuration-space substitute for physical space. There are no additional physical dimensions outside M∞M_\inftyM∞​.

For the electron and tau, this means Q6↪Q10Q_6\hookrightarrow Q_{10}Q6​↪Q10​: the electron's six spatial dimensions are six of the tau's ten. All ten are the same kind of dimension for the tau. The electron is “flat” relative to it, meaning lower-dimensional, not that the tau has an inherent six-plus-four division. The same relationship holds between three-dimensional nuclei and six-dimensional electrons. “Flat” here describes relative dimensionality, not a claim of zero metric curvature.

The photon-transversality construction illustrates this at every containing depth. The photon retains two directions of bound oscillation. There is one perpendicular direction within a three-dimensional sector, four within a six-dimensional sector, and eight within a ten-dimensional sector. These are descriptions relative to the photon; they do not create different kinds of dimensions in the containing sector.

IDWT attributes the three-dimensional experience of ordinary matter and observers to the three-dimensional nuclear sector. This general premise does not require a physical division of any higher-depth particle into different kinds of dimensions. The observer map must describe what the observer's interactions register; its dynamics remains to be derived.

For example, the electron has six ordinary macroscopic spatial dimensions. In the proposed geometry, Q6=CP3Q_6=\mathbb{CP}^3Q6​=CP3 is its actual spatial submanifold. Three of those dimensions are shared with us. That fact makes none of the six physically different in kind, and does not give the electron an inherent 3+33+33+3 split. Grouping coordinates by an observer's access is a choice of description, not a division in the electron. Frame-lock models that use such a grouping have additional assumptions; they do not establish the general origin of three-dimensional experience.

This states the physical premise; it does not declare the proposed metric, embedding, lock, or observer response mathematically derived or experimentally established. The following vocabulary applies throughout the research record:

8.1 Lower-order normal geometry

Tangential compression sets normal covectors to zero. The normal connection and normal Clifford products survive in the lower-order part:

8.2 The theorem's hypotheses

The result is conditional on three geometric facts:

  1. PdP_dPd​ is a smooth zeroth-order bundle projector;
  2. sector restriction means tangential compression with normal covectors set to zero;
  3. the projected spinor subbundle is invariant under tangential Clifford multiplication.

Tangential Clifford invariance gives a full-rank elliptic symbol. Its direct verification for every sector is the diagnostic that the projected geometry retains all ddd spatial directions.
