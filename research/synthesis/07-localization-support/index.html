Localization and same-field support

Updated November 10, 2026

conditional synthesis

A particle candidate must be more than a frequency assigned to a mode. It must be a spatial field pattern whose own dynamics sustain its shape, stresses and internal motion. Several IDWT constructions now establish useful parts of that problem: bounded localization under a specified inverse action, smooth departures from an exact material core, and genuine decaying vacuum exteriors. These results belong to their respective actions. Their combination is a set of constraints on a physical mechanism, not a theorem that one complete observed particle has been constructed.

Physical spacetime has infinitely many ordinary spatial dimensions plus time. Matter occupies up to ten spatial dimensions; all remaining spatial directions are unbound for every particle. A pattern's depth describes its dimensional involvement in that one space, not a component count or a compactified direction. A localized ten-dimensional radial field does not acquire physical depth three merely because its carrier has the down assignment. Translation along a direction of exactly uniform extension leaves that pattern unchanged; a change of its orientation is a different operation.

All supporting variables used below are declared components or effective descriptions of the one field Ψ∞\Psi_\inftyΨ∞​. No mediator particles, particle-exchange force, Kaluza–Klein tower or compactified spatial direction is introduced.

For an electron, its internal field structure remains distinct from its physical position and literal deterministic motion around a nucleus. The maintained baseline is a trajectory in four ordinary spatial directions. A stationary density or a squared field norm below is not a quantum orbital or a Born position law. See the physical premises and orbits and binding.

What has to be localized

Three notions must be kept separate.

  • Finite internal support or decay: the complete field energy and relevant charges are integrable on the actual physical slice, with the stated tails included.
  • Persistence: evolution remains localized for an appropriate time. An invariant exact profile, bounded evolution near a family, a finite control interval and a full nonlinear stability theorem are different results. Short-lived states need not be infinitely stable.
  • Physical depth confinement: the dynamics explain why a pattern has the claimed lower-dimensional involvement in the shared containing space. Assigning a subspace, a frame, or a band of coefficients does not by itself derive that confinement.

The phrase “bound within, free without” permits several conditional realizations. Under exact coordinate independence in additional noncompact directions, a lower-depth field has a cylindrical extension. Such a field can have finite overlap with an integrable higher-depth partner, but it is not automatically a finite-total-energy localized particle on the whole ambient slice. A box-normalized constant extension carries an inverse outer-volume factor. A finite-norm profile localized in all ten directions is a different ansatz whose relation to physical depth must be stated. The bound-within/free-without module gives the normalization distinction and the marginal-overlap formula.
