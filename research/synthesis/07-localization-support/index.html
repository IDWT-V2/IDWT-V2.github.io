One interaction can also control a pair encounter

For two pointwise orthogonal field components with densities ρi,ρj\rho_i,\rho_jρi​,ρj​, the same nonlinear contact that supplied (3) has the mixed density

ΔUij=−gρiρj+hρiρj(ρi+ρj).(7) \Delta U_{ij} =-g\rho_i\rho_j +h\rho_i\rho_j(\rho_i+\rho_j). \tag{7} ΔUij​=−gρi​ρj​+hρi​ρj​(ρi​+ρj​).(7)

This follows by subtracting the two isolated densities from U(ρi+ρj)U(\rho_i+\rho_j)U(ρi​+ρj​). The coefficients are exactly the same ones that enter each self-energy and its localization equation. Yet the integrated pair response also depends on spatial overlap, polarization, relative phase when the components are not orthogonal, and reciprocal deformation. In particular, little overlap with ordinary matter can give rare encounters even if a pattern's own sustaining coupling is not small. This is a possible route for a neutrino-like weak encounter rate; it has not been calculated for an IDWT neutrino state.

The more specific neutrino hypothesis is that its effective self-coupling itself is small, making its localized oscillation frequency small. That can be tested, but low coupling cannot simply be taken to zero in (3): below the finite threshold (4), this particular scale model has no localized stationary radius. At depth five with a=C=Q=1a=C=Q=1a=C=Q=1, the threshold is Bfold=1.10616B_{\rm fold}=1.10616Bfold​=1.10616. A weakly coupled but localized neutrino would therefore need either an effective coefficient still above its profile-dependent localization threshold or a different same-field support mechanism. The toy coefficient is not a physical neutrino coupling, and the observed small neutrino mass is constrained by upper bounds and oscillation differences, not a measured positive absolute value for every state.

An extended neutrino pattern is another distinct possibility. If one spatial carrier obeys ωd=αdc/Rd\omega_d=\alpha_d c/R_dωd​=αd​c/Rd​, where αd\alpha_dαd​ is its derived dimensionless eigenvalue, then

RνRe=αναeωeων.(8) \frac{R_\nu}{R_e} =\frac{\alpha_\nu}{\alpha_e} \frac{\omega_e}{\omega_\nu}. \tag{8} Re​Rν​​=αe​αν​​ων​ωe​​.(8)

The large observed electron-to-neutrino frequency contrast could then reflect a large support ratio, a small spectral factor, or both. This relation is conditional on each state having a meaningful characteristic radius and a common propagation speed; a diffuse or differently localized neutrino need not obey that simple carrier law. An extended physical support is also distinct from the width of a neutrino wave packet during propagation.

Equation (1) gives a useful limit on one simple explanation of such extension. At depth five, the magnitude of a local density-square self-contact relative to the gradient estimate is gBQ/(AR4)gBQ/(A R^4)gBQ/(AR4). Making ggg weaker and RRR larger suppresses that ratio in both ways. Thus this particular local contact cannot by itself hold a very broad, weakly coupled state. A supporting interaction with a different scale law, a suppressed gradient term, or a different spatial solution would have to be derived from the same field. This is a restriction on the fixed-profile contact model, not on the proposal that neutrinos may be much larger.

An isolated composite can likewise have rest energy that differs from the sum of its components' bare rest energies because its complete interacting field contains kinetic, stress and interaction energy. This is consistent with the measured proton-mass problem and its lattice QCD decomposition, but no IDWT proton or neutron structure has been solved here. A positive interaction term alone does not determine whether binding raises or lowers the composite's total energy relative to physically isolated constituents.

The proposed strong inter-quark coupling has a clear conditional test in (7). Where the two densities satisfy h(ρi+ρj)>gh(\rho_i+\rho_j)>gh(ρi​+ρj​)>g, that local mixed term is positive: the interaction contribution to a composite can exceed the sum of the isolated self terms. Where the inequality reverses it is negative. Thus the same nonlinear interaction can be attractive in one density range, helping localization, and add positive cross energy at another. The total proton or neutron mass would still require its complete spatial kinetic energy, supporting stress and field solution. The couplings and density ranges for actual quark patterns have not been derived in IDWT.

The direct test of the proposal is therefore one canonically normalized same-field action with a finite, stable particle profile; the action must give its selected frequency, full positive inertial response, and reciprocal pair response without an independently fitted interaction rule. The two-term scale obstruction and three-term example identify what such a derivation must address, rather than providing the mass operator themselves.
