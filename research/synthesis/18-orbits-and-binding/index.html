The alpha exponent and the prediction

Alpha decay, in which the quarks survive and the nucleus comes apart, needs no tunnelling or imaginary time: the fourth direction is the natural path for that motion. Across 142 even-even alpha emitters, spanning 30 decades of half-life, the exponent computed from the three-direction Coulomb barrier and the net electric charge has a slope of 0.956 against the expected 1, with an rms scatter of 0.98 decades.
