Spatial and dynamical stability

Updated November 5, 2026

conditional synthesis

A spatial pattern can have positive high-frequency inertia and causal characteristics while its lower-frequency shape still grows. A purely imaginary linear spectrum can coexist with secular drift unless neutral modes and the evolution norm are controlled. A stable pattern on a prescribed closed ball need not remain localized when its support and exterior are varied. Stability results must therefore identify the action, background, varied fields, domain, norm and duration they actually cover.

IDWT does not require every particle candidate to be infinitely stable. Electron-like candidates need substantial persistence; short-lived branches may be localized resonances. A negative energy direction or a growing linear disturbance is not yet a decay process: its timescale, outgoing channels and complete energy, momentum and charges remain to be calculated. Conversely, an energy-minimum theorem is a sufficient stability condition, not a universal definition of a particle.

Physical spacetime has infinitely many ordinary spatial dimensions plus time. Matter occupies up to ten spatial dimensions; all remaining spatial directions are unbound for every particle. Field components, harmonic coefficients and perturbation spaces do not add physical dimensions or a second physical manifold. Internal pattern stability does not replace the electron's deterministic six-dimensional external trajectory around a nucleus. No probabilistic position law or mediator particles are introduced.

This comparison changes the energy law from the critical first-order Dirac branch, so their successful properties cannot be combined as if one solved particle already possessed both.

The real-spatial span calculation is another distinct trial. Its positive arclength coefficient in a chosen co-rotated preparation does not prove relaxed stable elasticity under arbitrary orientation changes. The contact and path account gives the exact trial functional, reduced path equation and remaining endpoint and outgoing-evolution requirements.

A Sampled Trial Maximum and an Exact Saddle Are Different Claims

The bent-profile control checks an exact trial-energy identity on a prescribed family. Its sampled quadratic bounce has the largest of seven amplitudes, but the exact arc functional has a nonzero first derivative there. This has an independent analytic proof. The measured trial agreement is retained without promoting it to a solved graph saddle or a relaxed field solution.

The later finite tanh envelopes are likewise specific preparations inside periodic boxes, not actual contact-ended boundaries. Their fixed initial-density threshold lengths, cell-density rms widths and hump counts must retain their definitions. Both stated grand-potential drift bounds fail, the short norm bound fails, and no refinement study is supplied. The frozen observations are useful controls; they do not establish a universal finite-length instability rule or a physical lifetime.
