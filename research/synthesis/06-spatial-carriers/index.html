Spatial carriers and compatible geometry

Updated November 1, 2026

conditional synthesis

A spatial carrier specifies what actually varies across physical space: the field components, their spatial derivatives, their orientation and the measure used to compute energy and contact. It must do more than reproduce a dimension or a binomial count. It must allow the proposed field pattern to coexist with other patterns in one higher-dimensional space and remain meaningful through its supporting region and exterior.

IDWT uses ten ordinary spatial dimensions plus time in one shared spacetime. Flat ten-dimensional space is the spatial baseline. Particles may be described as confined to layers or nested submanifolds of that same space, but neither the layer analogy nor an assigned depth supplies the confining dynamics. The six-dimensional electron is not a constituent of the ten-dimensional tau. Translation in a direction of uniform extension leaves a pattern unchanged; distinguishable center motion occurs where its structure varies.

Earlier finite-multiplet constructions contain all fourteen maintained prefixes; common occupation and phase interactions preserve their specified reference fields, and actual spatial gradients enter a volume-preserving shape law. These remain conditional comparison constructions. The current physical proposal instead has literal portions at one fundamental, with mode coefficients describing their combination. The new geometry case explains how carrier compatibility differs from selecting a physical depth.

What a physical carrier still has to establish

The useful common result is compatibility: the same count and carrier rules can accommodate the supplied preparations across depths two, three, four, five, six and ten. That is distinct from selecting those depths or indices. A minimal real-spinor clock criterion supplies a more selective comparison: under the additional requirement of one isolated irreducible real spin module, the allowed complex/quaternionic clock types occur at precisely those depths within one through ten. Restriction of a larger common module can restore repeated clock carriers at other depths, so isolation is a physical assumption still to derive.

A complete spatial construction must join the following requirements under one action: physical dimensional involvement, a spatial operator and measure, occupied components and internal phases, localization and its exterior, independent orientations and translations, and reciprocal contact between actual fields. Geometry choices must coexist in one ordinary manifold. It is insufficient to optimize an unrelated metric at each depth and combine the answers afterward, but neither a spherical nested chain nor a projective topology is a universal premise.

The supporting dynamics must also distinguish changes of the internal pattern from the motion of its center and the observer's registration. A time-averaged density does not become an electron position law. A finite carrier, a successful spectral identity or a local reference solution does not yet establish observed particle identity, complete positive inertia, persistence or the full physical mass operator.
