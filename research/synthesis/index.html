Condensed research

This reading sequence brings the current calculations into twenty manageable documents. Each document includes selected equations and results from the maintained research. Source links lead to the full record; the condensed set does not reproduce abandoned research routes.

  1. Research map and current status — The physical mass operator remains the central open problem. This reading set follows the active spatial, current, support, coupling and inertia calculations. Mathematical existence within a stipulated action is kept distinct from a derived same-field law or an observed particle.

  2. Physical premises and nested spatial sectors — Depth counts ordinary physical spatial dimensions in one nested manifold. A particle pattern may rotate independently of its sector. Lower-dimensional sectors are nested, but particle solutions at different depths need not be restrictions of one another.

  3. Spatial operator and contact toolkit — The simplex factor must arise from an actual physical spatial operator or a justified effective reduction. Mode counting, one eigenfrequency, a contact trace, and a pair vertex are different objects.

  4. Same-field actions and canonical normalization — A candidate mass and coupling mechanism must follow from one varied action with its canonical norm, retained modes and complete energy. The calculations here are conditional action constructions; they do not establish a unique master-field reduction.

  5. Simplex spectrum and sector assignments — Observed frequencies provide the targets. The assigned spatial depth and harmonic index determine a proposed cumulative mode count, while the physical frequency unit and occupation rule require dynamics.

  6. Spatial carriers and compatible geometry — A carrier must live in the physical sector and support the proposed spectrum, polarization and spatial variation. Candidate geometries at different depths must coexist in one nested chain before their field equations are compared.

  7. Localization and same-field support — The same-field interaction may sustain a finite spatial pattern and set its oscillation frequency. A radial minimum is only one necessary part of a stationary spatial field; angular stress and the response of the sustaining structure matter.

  8. Current mass-operator candidates — The finite component carrier, inverse current law and density-current simplification are candidate routes to the assigned frequencies. Their exact finite-chain and current calculations should be read with their stated geometric and normalization assumptions.

  9. Common scales across particle sectors — One operator must account for frequencies at all maintained depths without fitting each particle independently. Radius, gap, spatial conversion and canonical norm must be compared under the same assumptions.

  10. The competing outer depth-ten branch — The extra depth-ten branch is a candidate state of the same harmonic, not an established particle identification. Its local frequency, retained same-field response and shifted-radius checks are useful even though one fitted law does not reproduce every known sector.

  11. Spatial and dynamical stability — Stability claims depend on which field, polarization and geometric perturbations were varied. The current-action component and angular results and the conformal background calculation belong to specified, distinct actions.

  12. Complete energy, inertia and causal motion — Mass is the selected oscillation frequency in equivalent units, while positive inertia requires the momentum response of the complete particle and its sustaining field. Partial wave momentum and an assigned frequency do not settle that response.

  13. Photon geometry and physical polarization — Depth-two light supplies the explicit transverse polarization control. A massive higher-depth sector may have a different polarization rule; the proposed photon zero and its radiative protection remain separate questions.

  14. Same-field coupling across sectors — Coupling is compatibility and reciprocal response of actual spatial patterns in the one field. A response coefficient, pair vertex, encounter probability and rest frequency require separate normalizations before they can be related.

  15. Pair geometry and reciprocal encounters — Both particles carry orientation, phase and a responding spatial field. Fixed overlap is a snapshot; a complete encounter calculation must include the reciprocal motion of both patterns and preserve the common field law.

  16. Neutrino frequency and interaction controls — Neutrino patterns may have different physical support sizes and nonzero but rare encounters. Oscillation measurements constrain mass differences; a particular positive absolute mass is not imposed by this synthesis.

  17. Composite and hadronic mass — A composite frequency and complete energy can contain a substantial interaction contribution. The normal response, quark-pattern coupling and canonical normalization must be calculated from one action before identifying a nucleon mass mechanism.

  18. Orbits, binding and observed spatial patterns — A projected picture, a circulating coefficient ray and an actual spatial orbit are distinct. The available orbit calculations make the observer map explicit and test a conditional Coulomb-driven spatial pattern.

  19. Flavor, charge and gauge geometry — Mass assignments alone do not produce a mixing matrix or a physical gauge field. Candidate projected operators and bundle data give finite tests whose physical source and normalization remain to be derived.

  20. Observational comparisons and remaining proofs — A candidate law must pass simultaneous frequency, normalization, spatial-state, stability and response tests. This document keeps the maintained depth controls and explicit proof obligations together without promoting an inverse fit into a physical derivation.
