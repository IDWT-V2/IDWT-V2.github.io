Condensed research

This twenty-volume sequence explains the current model, its important equations and representative results. Particle patterns occupy nested layers of one higher-dimensional physical space. The mass-count prescription, conditional action constructions and their unresolved physical bridges are kept distinct. Detailed derivations remain linked from each volume.

  1. Research map and current status — A map of IDWT V2's physical premises, constrained particle counts, dimensional evidence and same-field constructions, with the remaining spatial-state and normalization requirements kept explicit.

  2. Physical premises and nested spatial sectors — IDWT investigates one field in ordinary higher-dimensional physical space. Particle depth, literal electron motion, internal field structure, mathematical mode descriptions and detector registration have distinct roles; spatial support and same-field dynamics must connect them.

  3. Spatial operator and contact toolkit — Real spatial counts, flat-ball count clocks, finite-speed parents and conditional contact operators, with kinetic normalization, supporting-state requirements and their distinct scopes.

  4. Same-field actions and canonical normalization — Common varied actions connect real mode counts, particle clocks, supporting strain and reciprocal response, while global localization, complete normalization and the master-field realization remain open.

  5. Simplex spectrum and sector assignments — The cumulative simplex count organizes fourteen massive-state assignments under shared scale constraints. Exact assignment theorems and real spatial count clocks make that structure testable, while physical units, occupied cutoffs and complete same-field selection remain conditional.

  6. Spatial carriers and compatible geometry — Real spatial fields can realize the simplex count, coherent prefixes and variable physical shapes, but a coefficient carrier does not by itself derive spatial confinement, occupied states or a complete particle.

  7. Localization and same-field support — Constructed same-field actions support localized count patterns and exact core or exterior solutions, while their physical confinement, active connection, complete normalization and persistence remain separate requirements.

  8. Current mass-operator candidates — Reconstructed count actions and a common material-plus-vacuum action retain the assigned clocks and supply conditional core, local-exit and exterior results. A complete spatial state with the required total charge, raw energy, inertia and physical depth remains unproved.

  9. Common scales across particle sectors — The maintained count law separates exact frequency multipliers from a conditional numerical sector-scale prescription. A common physical action must produce those units, spatial support and complete energy normalization without fitting each particle independently.

  10. The competing outer depth-ten branch — The extra depth-ten branch is a candidate state of the same harmonic, not an established particle identification. Its local frequency, retained same-field response and shifted-radius checks are useful even though one fitted law does not reproduce every known sector.

  11. Spatial and dynamical stability — Complete spatial stability, bounded linear evolution and local causal principal health are established for specified common actions, with nonlinear support, endpoint transmission and physical lifetimes still separate problems.

  12. Complete energy, inertia and causal motion — Mass is the selected oscillation frequency in equivalent units, while positive inertia requires the momentum response of the complete particle and its sustaining field. Partial wave momentum and an assigned frequency do not settle that response.

  13. Photon geometry and physical polarization — The extended-connection photon hypothesis, its physical transverse plane and polarization mathematics, with same-field projection, timing, momentum and protected masslessness kept distinct.

  14. Same-field coupling across sectors — Reciprocal interactions must follow from one field action applied to both spatial patterns. Complete polarization, phase, kinetic normalization and response spectra distinguish an allowed overlap from a force, a rate or a derived mass scale.

  15. Pair geometry and reciprocal encounters — Both particles carry orientation, phase and a responding spatial field. Fixed overlap is a snapshot; a complete encounter calculation must include the reciprocal motion of both patterns and preserve the common field law.

  16. Neutrino frequency and interaction controls — The maintained depth-five counts constrain neutrino mass-squared splitting ratios independently of one scale. Electric neutrality, nonzero weak response, flavor mixing, spatial support and encounter rates remain distinct same-field requirements.

  17. Composite and hadronic mass — Exact spectral reduction, canonical response freedom and conditional geometric guide dynamics identify the ingredients of a same-field composite mass calculation, while a complete nucleon frequency and localized supporting state remain unsolved.

  18. Orbits, binding and observed spatial patterns — Literal six-dimensional electron motion, four-direction external orbit examples, their projected shapes and reciprocal nuclear response are distinct parts of the binding problem; useful exact trajectories do not yet select atomic locks or molecular states.

  19. Flavor, charge and gauge geometry — Conditional charge and color geometries, real-spinor clock selection and fitted count-ratio mixing, with canonical transition normalization and the missing same-field dynamical bridges explicit.

  20. Observational comparisons and remaining proofs — Current mass, depth, spatial-state and interaction evidence is assessed against one common action, complete energy and charges, literal trajectories and empirical response. Conditional core and exterior theorems sharpen the remaining global connection and normalization requirements without completing the physical mass operator.
