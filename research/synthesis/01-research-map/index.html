Open physical source

The physical source of the existing Coulomb-plus-precession construction remains unresolved: the electron–nucleus interaction must supply the precession generator and its rate relative to the orbit clock. Producing a similar petal curve alone does not establish that physical identification. The scalar-potential and nuclear-response models below are diagnostics along possible routes, not established required ingredients.

The lower-dimensional-nucleus follow-up now derives an exact reduced Hamiltonian trajectory directly on the CP3 sector represented by J. For a fixed nuclear three-plane and invariant binding energy V(s), the cross-plane block precesses while s is conserved. Thus a nonzero binding slope can supply precession away from exact alignment, but a positive energy slope alone is not dynamical capture into the lock. This result does not yet include a separate relative-orbit clock or derive the physical petal readout; it leaves the action scale and kernel slope explicit.

An inverse nuclear orientation kernel now realizes that binding slope: K(u,J)=gamma(u)||P(u)JP(u)||²/2. With a fixed common nuclear plane, its normalized marginal is mean(gamma) s. The same energy supplies both electron and nuclear orientation variations. An inverse formula reconstructs V(s) from a specified precession-rate function, but a single orbit leaves infinitely many kernels compatible. The full fixed petal generator instead requires an oriented linear source; a bare nuclear plane does not select that source under its unbroken SO(3) times SO(3) symmetry. These are inverse compatibility results, not a first-principles nuclear current or binding law.
