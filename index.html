Overview

Infinite-Dimensional Wave Theory starts from a space with infinitely many ordinary spatial directions, all of the same kind as the three we see, plus time. One field fills that space, and every particle is a stable pattern of it. A pattern is bound in a fixed number of directions, its depth, and is free in all the others. Matter is bound in at most ten.

Within its depth ddd, a pattern sits at a whole-number level nnn. Its mass is the scale shared by that depth times the number of points in a simplex with nnn points along each edge in ddd directions:

m=μd(n+d−1d)m=\mu_d\dbinom{n+d-1}{d}m=μd​(dn+d−1​)

With the electron mass as its single measured input, this rule gives the W, Z and Higgs bosons, five quarks, the muon and the tau. Nine of these ten masses fall within one percent of measurement, and the top quark within 2.2 percent.

The electron orbits the nucleus through four spatial directions at a fixed distance, so each shell is a three-sphere. The angular patterns on that shell give capacities of 2, 8, 18 and 32 electrons, and their subshell content matches every one of the seven periods of the periodic table.

The levels connect the families. Each charged lepton’s level is a quark level plus a neutrino level: electron 13 = up 3 + neutrino 10, muon 35 = charm 20 + neutrino 15, and tau 23 = down 1 + neutrino 22.

The depth-five counts fix the neutrino masses at 1.53, 8.90 and 50.4 meV in normal order. Their sum, 60.8 meV, lies below the current cosmological bound.
